Cincinnati is a major city in southwestern Ohio. In UAP research it is known as the academic home of Dr. Eric Wang, a Chinese-born aeronautical engineer who earned his degrees at the University of Cincinnati and taught there before he was recruited into classified government research. Wang is one of the more specifically identified figures connected to early post-war UAP reverse engineering efforts.

## Dr. Eric Wang

Dr. Eric Wang earned his advanced degrees in aeronautical engineering at the University of Cincinnati and taught there before moving into secret government work. He later worked at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Dayton, Ohio, less than 60 miles from Cincinnati. Researcher accounts associate him with early US efforts to analyze and exploit recovered non-human technology. His training in aeronautics and his classified work at Wright-Patterson place him among the engineers who fed the alleged early UAP reverse engineering programs.

## Broader Ohio UAP Context

Cincinnati is part of an Ohio region that is disproportionately represented in early UAP research. Wright-Patterson AFB's Foreign Technology Division in nearby Dayton was a principal repository for crashed UAP materials, according to multiple whistleblowers. Their accounts include Senator Barry Goldwater's documented inquiries about the facility's "Blue Room." The Battelle Memorial Institute, which conducted classified UAP material analysis, has its flagship facility in Columbus.
