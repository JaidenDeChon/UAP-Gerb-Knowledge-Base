Vandenberg Air Force Base (now Vandenberg Space Force Base) is a US military installation in Santa Barbara County, California, about 60 miles north of Santa Barbara. It is a major launch site for the Western Test Range and is used for satellite launches into polar orbit and for ICBM test flights. In UAP research, Vandenberg is best known as the site of the [[Vandenberg Air Force Base UFO Film Incident]] of September 14, 1964, when optical instrumentation filmed a disc-shaped craft firing directed energy beams at a dummy nuclear warhead during an ICBM test.

## Western Test Range and Nuclear Testing

Vandenberg's Western Test Range extends over the Pacific Ocean. During the Cold War it was used heavily for ICBM test launches, since missiles fired from there flew a path that avoided populated areas. Every nuclear payload and ballistic missile launched from Vandenberg had to be documented optically and by instruments. Dedicated units did this work with high-powered telescope and camera systems that could photograph missile details from 160 miles away.

## The 1964 UFO Film Incident

On September 14, 1964, Lieutenant [[Robert Jacobs]] commanded the optical instrumentation unit assigned to document a test launch of a dummy ICBM warhead from Vandenberg's Western Test Range, near [[Big Sur, California]]. The test was designed to evaluate chaff decoy systems intended to deceive Soviet air defenses. As the warhead separated at the fringe of space, an unidentified disc-shaped craft entered the camera frame, matched the warhead's speed and fired four directed energy beams at it. The warhead fell off its trajectory.

Within days, government agents confiscated the film and ordered Jacobs to stay silent. Jacobs's commanding officer, Major [[Florence J. Mansman]], later corroborated the incident in writing. Jacobs testified under oath to [[AARO]] in February 2023. The full account is at [[Vandenberg Air Force Base UFO Film Incident]].

## Other Historical Notes

In wider UAP discussion, Vandenberg is also cited as a place where classified aerospace programs were carried out. It launched reconnaissance satellites, including early launches in the Keyhole program, and its position on the California coast gave it a strategic advantage for putting satellites into polar orbit that Area 51 and similar inland facilities could not offer.
