Dugway Proving Ground (DPG) is a United States Army test and evaluation facility in Tooele County, Utah, about 85 miles southwest of Salt Lake City in the Great Salt Lake Desert. It covers more than 1,250 square miles (800,000+ acres) of remote terrain. It was founded in 1942, after the Pearl Harbor attacks, to test chemical and biological defense systems. DPG operates under the US Army Test and Evaluation Command (ATEC), and its main acknowledged mission is testing biological and chemical weapons, nuclear-biological-chemical (NBC) survivability, and aerospace systems. Often called "Area 52" by UFO researchers, Dugway became widely known in the late 1990s as the alleged successor to Area 51's clandestine UAP programs, after the Nevada site received heavy public exposure.

## History and Official Mission

Dugway was established in 1942 to test US and Allied chemical and biological defense systems, after the 1941 Pearl Harbor attacks created an urgent need to improve military capabilities. Its official mission includes nuclear, biological, and chemical survivability testing and training of defense materiel, using chemical and biological agents and simulants. The Utah proving ground uses state-of-the-art laboratories and chambers to carry out testing and training under environmentally controlled conditions.

DPG has taken part in the global war on terrorism with Special Forces mission rehearsals, classified testing, and urgent-need testing of biochemical equipment. The site also has a history of supporting test operations for unmanned aerial systems. According to a 2021 US Army overview of its mission and capabilities, Dugway employs 2,120 people in total: 784 contractors, primarily Army and Air Force; 622 primary mission and installation contracting command personnel, including on-site security; and 486 military or defense agency civilians.

## Partner Agencies and Contractors

Dugway's partners include many US military, government, and intelligence agencies: the US Army, the US Navy, SOCOM (Special Operations Command), the 388th Range Squadron out of Hill Air Force Base, the Departments of Justice, Homeland Security and Interior, NASA, and three universities, among them Utah State University. Utah State runs a University Affiliated Research Center (UARC) called the Space Dynamics Laboratory for the Missile Defense Agency at Dugway. This is significant because UAP researchers assert that UARCs and Federally Funded Research and Development Centers (FFRDCs) are important contributors and project managers for joint corporate-government UAP legacy programs.

Major contractors at Dugway include C Martin Company, which handles facility operations, maintenance, and range operations and employed witness [[MS (Dugway Witness)|"MS"]] from 2009-2013; Battelle Memorial Institute, which does chemical and biological defense information analysis, facility design, and program management; and Jacobs Solutions, which provides range testing and lab services.

## Major Range and Test Facility Bases (MRTFBs)

Dugway houses two of the Department of Defense's 23 designated Major Range and Test Facility Bases (MRTFBs). These bases are the "critical core" of DOD test and evaluation infrastructure, with over 10 million acres, 25,000+ employees, and over $4 billion in acknowledged funding each year.

The West Desert Test Center (WDTC) is the main Army MRTFB at Dugway, led by Army Test and Evaluation Command (ATEC). Its operations are centered at Ditto Technical Center and also include Avery Technical Center and Michael Army Airfield. According to a 2013 GAO report, about 77% of acknowledged work at WDTC was done for the Joint Services Chemical and Biological Defense Program (CBDP).

The southern area of the Utah Test and Training Range (UTTR) is an Air Force MRTFB shared between Dugway and nearby Hill Air Force Base, and Hill's 388th Range Squadron runs part of it. The southern range includes Michael Army Airfield, which the 388th uses for air testing and training.

Because the Army and Air Force share this MRTFB structure, researchers have theorized that a joint-service UFO reverse engineering program operates between WDTC and UTTR. The idea resembles witness "Ed's" claims of a joint program between the Edwards Air Force Base 412th Test Wing and the Nevada Test and Training Range.

## Key Facilities

Avery Technical Center is a 40-acre area bordering Michael Army Airfield. It was historically used for radiobiological warfare studies and for handling radioactive materials for the Atomic Energy Commission, and it has an internal rail system for moving radioactive materials. Its main buildings include Test Operations (Building 1010), Classified Storage (Building 1016), Photo Operations and Maintenance (Building 1012), General Storage (Building 1038), and the Avery Tunnel Entrance (Building 102). Although Avery is a major facility, it is conspicuously absent from DPG visitor guides.

Michael Army Airfield is shared between Dugway and Hill Air Force Base's 388th Range Squadron. It is used for aerospace testing, including the Lockheed Martin X-33 hypersonic vehicle program, and lies within the West Desert Test Center.

[[Ditto Technical Center]] holds the main operations of the West Desert Test Center, including the Garrison building where witness "MS" was interrogated after his 2009 encounter.

Granite Peak is a restricted sub-installation within Dugway, surrounded by 388th Range Squadron infrastructure such as radar stations and threat emitter systems. Granite Peak is alleged to sit above the primary underground facility.

## Structure and Infrastructure

Dugway is among the largest restricted military installations in the continental United States. Three mountain ranges, salt flats, and desert surround the site. Its geography and isolation give it natural security, and keep it free from urban encroachment, light pollution, and excess acoustic and electronic noise. Armed personnel and "black op helicopters" that intercept approaching civilians protect the site, which makes it ideal for clandestine and classified military test and evaluation.

Satellite imagery analysis has identified what appears to be a small, inconspicuous surface building in the desert between German Village and Avery Technical Center. According to witness "MS's" source "GP," a senior DOD chemist working in legacy operations at Dugway, this entrance is for personnel only (no logistics, supplies, or equipment). All materiel is supplied internally through underground tunnels that connect to other facilities.
