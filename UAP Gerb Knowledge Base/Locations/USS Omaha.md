USS Omaha (LCS-12) is an Independence-class littoral combat ship of the United States Navy, homeported in San Diego. On the night of 15 July 2019 her crew filmed an unidentified spherical object that flew over the sea in a warning area off San Diego and then descended into the water. The map pins the ship at a representative point offshore of San Diego, where that event took place.

## The 2019 Sphere Video

The footage was recorded on a phone in the ship's Combat Information Center, filming an infrared display. It shows a dark sphere moving across the screen, stopping, and easing down into the ocean. It was the last of a series of unknown contacts around the ship that night. No wreckage was recovered, and a submarine reportedly searched the area without finding anything. Filmmaker Jeremy Corbell released stills in May 2021 and the video soon after. The Pentagon later confirmed that the footage Corbell had leaked was authentic.

## Audio Comparison with the Flyby Footage

The video's soundtrack is the ambient noise of the Combat Information Center. In his analysis of the [[Flyby Footage]], UAP Gerb compares that footage's muffled background to this shipboard audio, as one possible source of the sound: a screen recording made somewhere similar, rather than the original cockpit audio. The comparison is complicated by the fact that the flyby footage is a [[Second-Generation Recording]], whose audio belongs to the room it was recorded in.

The video's auto-generated captions render the ship's name as "USS nits". This note was first created as USS Nitze (DDG-94), a destroyer with no connection to the event; the 2019 sphere video was filmed aboard USS Omaha.
