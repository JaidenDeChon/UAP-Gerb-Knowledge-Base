Avery Technical Center is a 40-acre restricted facility in Dugway Proving Ground's West Desert Test Center in Utah, next to Michael Army Airfield. It is Army land leased for the exclusive use of the Air Force's 388th Range Squadron, from Hill Air Force Base, a Dugway tenant since 1978. Its buildings were designed in the early 1950s for radiological studies, and its radiological warfare laboratories irradiated food with spent fuel elements from the Atomic Energy Commission; today it is the administrative area that supports air testing and training for the 388th Range Squadron. Although it is a major installation, it is left out of Dugway Proving Ground visitor guides. It was laid out around an internal rail system built to carry radioactive materials, parts of which are still visible. Avery became widely discussed in UAP research after witness "MS's" 2009 encounter with an alleged extraterrestrial craft in an unmarked building there.

## History and Official Function

Avery Technical Center was set up for radiological studies and research into handling radioactive material. In the 1950s its radiological warfare laboratories were used mainly to study dosimetry and to irradiate food with gamma rays from spent Atomic Energy Commission fuel elements, according to the 2012 assessment. Gerb calls them "radiobiological warfare laboratories for the Atomic Energy Commission". Today, in the words of the 2012 environmental assessment, "Avery is the administrative area, which provides support to air testing/training for the 388th RANS", the 388th Range Squadron from Hill Air Force Base. About 100 Department of Defense and contract personnel support the squadron on Dugway, providing environmental services, equipment maintenance, fire fighting and emergency response, security and utilities for air testing and training on the Utah Test and Training Range. Dugway Proving Ground as a whole is the Defense Department's primary chemical and biological defense testing center.

## Infrastructure and Key Buildings

Avery Technical Center was organized in the 1950s around an internal rail system for moving radioactive material, and the 2012 environmental assessment notes that "portions of the rails are still visible today". The rail line draws interest because of allegations of underground tunnel connections and craft transport. The facility has many numbered buildings, though many developed or unclassified structures are not marked or detailed in public documentation.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said Avery uses an internal rail system to transport radioactive materials, as if it were still in service. The 2012 environmental assessment (page 30) describes the rail system as part of the 1950s layout, with only portions of the rails still visible.

Identified buildings include:
- Building 1010 (Test Operations): built in 1952, listed in the 2012 environmental assessment as the "Operation headquarters for the 388th". In the 1950s it held the area's radioactive "hot cell". Gerb bets it was the "ghost building" where witness MS encountered the hovering disc craft in 2009; MS described the ghost building as having no numbers or insignia, and the inside as a clean room.
- Building 1016 (Classified Storage): storage for classified materials and equipment.
- Building 1012 (Photo Operations and Maintenance): photographic documentation and maintenance work.
- Building 1038 (General Storage): general-purpose storage.
- Building 1002 (Avery Tunnel Entrance): built in 1952 and listed in the 2012 environmental assessment as "Not used by the 388th". Gerb gives the number as 102. It is sometimes taken as a possible link to the alleged deep underground military base beneath Granite Peak, though no document says so.

## Absence from Public Documentation

Avery Technical Center is missing from Dugway Proving Ground visitor guides and from many US Army and ATEC documents, even though it is a major 40-acre facility next to Michael Army Airfield. Nearby facilities such as Ditto Technical Center, by contrast, are documented in detail. The gap suggests that Avery's classification level or operational sensitivity is higher than that of other acknowledged DPG installations.

## Witness MS 2009 Encounter

In 2009, C Martin Company contractor [[MS (Dugway Witness)|"MS"]] was assigned to read electrical meters across Dugway facilities as part of government billing operations. This routine task led him to enter an unmarked building at Avery—the "ghost building" with no numbers or insignia, believed to be Building 1010 (Test Operations).

As soon as MS entered, armed guards (believed to be Industrial Security/INDC personnel) held him at gunpoint. Inside what he described as a clean room, he saw about six lab technicians in white coats, believed to be ATCH and Battelle Memorial Institute scientists, standing around a hovering disc-shaped craft about 10 feet tall and 20 feet in circumference. The craft had these features:

- Hovering 10-12 inches off the ground under its own power
- No visible propulsion system or audible noise
- Brushed metal appearance with absolutely no seams, fasteners, or visible construction joints
- Appeared to be manufactured from a single seamless piece
- A conical bottom section that appeared to float independently from the main craft
- Accompanied by a flat-screen electronic display showing what appeared to be a mapping/navigation system

To MS, the craft appeared to be in the process of being dismantled or studied, with its conical bottom section removed and in view. He was immediately detained and taken to the Garrison building at Ditto Technical Center, where men in black suits interrogated him for six hours.

## Internal Rail System and Underground Connections

Avery's documented internal rail system was originally designed to transport radioactive materials, and portions of the rails are still visible. It has drawn more interest because of allegations about Dugway's deep underground military base. Building 1002 (Avery Tunnel Entrance, which Gerb calls 102), together with GP's claims that craft and materials are moved through underground rail connections, suggests that Avery's rail system may be the main way UAP-related materiel travels between surface facilities and the alleged underground complex beneath Granite Peak.

The hypothesis draws on the facility's past radiobiological research capabilities, its clean room infrastructure, classified storage and internal rail transport system, and its absence from public documentation. On this view, Avery is more than an acknowledged administrative and support area for the 388th Range Squadron and may be a primary site for studying and reverse-engineering technologies of unknown origin.
