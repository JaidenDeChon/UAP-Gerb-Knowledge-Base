La Jolla is a wealthy coastal neighborhood of San Diego, California. It is home to major research institutions, including the University of California San Diego (UCSD) and the Scripps Institution of Oceanography. In UAP research, La Jolla matters as the headquarters location of [[SAIC]] (Science Applications International Corporation), a defense and intelligence contractor that Gerb identifies as a central node in alleged U.S. UAP legacy programs.

## SAIC Headquarters

[[SAIC]] was founded in 1969 by [[J. Robert Beyster]]. It built its early corporate identity in La Jolla through classified defense science contracts, which made the city the operational home of one of the most influential defense contractors in U.S. history. The SAIC headquarters building in La Jolla is described as having two large bronze Egyptian statues holding pyramids outside it. Gerb refers to this unusual architectural choice for a defense contractor when discussing SAIC's alleged connections to programs dealing with non-human technology.

SAIC's corporate structure, its revolving-door hiring from the defense-intelligence bureaucracy and its long-running access to sensitive programs have made it a focus of research into UAP legacy programs. Gerb characterizes SAIC as a "body shop" model contractor able to provide high-clearance personnel and technical services across government portfolios, an arrangement well suited to sheltering compartmented research activities from standard oversight mechanisms. The company's early growth came from classified defense science contracts and aggressive recruitment of senior officials from the military and intelligence community. This gave it a workforce that already held security clearances and knew how classified programs were structured.

## SAIC's UAP-Related Connections

Gerb's research into SAIC identified several specific connections to alleged UAP legacy programs.

The [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] is an alleged account of a senior government official being denied access to a restricted reverse-engineering program. It names a contractor operating a Special Access Program with UAP-related materials. Gerb has proposed SAIC as a plausible candidate among the contractors the memo describes, citing SAIC-adjacent career paths and board-level overlap among former DoD SAP governance officials.

SAIC had a large contract presence at [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane|Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane]], a facility that witness [[Randy Anderson]] cited as the site of an alleged Off-World Technologies Division. Gerb argued that this contract presence fits structurally with Anderson's account of controlled access to unusual technology demonstrations at Crane.

On the gatekeeping allegations around [[AARO]], the video argues that [[Sean Kirkpatrick]]'s tenure there reflected an institutional minimization of whistleblower testimony. It also argues that SAIC-adjacent career networks contributed to the office managing information rather than disclosing it.

Drawing on research by [[Catherine Austin Fitts]], Gerb argued that accounting vulnerabilities in DoD and HUD contracting created pathways for black-budget spending that map plausibly onto long-term clandestine R&D infrastructure. In that black-budget architecture, he placed SAIC at a major node because of its size, clearance levels and classified contract scope.

## Significance in UAP Research

The La Jolla headquarters establishes SAIC as a defense intelligence giant based in Southern California. It is geographically close to [[San Diego, California, USA|San Diego]]'s U.S. Navy installations, to [[Lockheed Martin]]'s Southern California operations, and to major scientific research institutions including UCSD and Scripps Institution of Oceanography. SAIC's alleged connections to the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] context, its contract presence at Crane, and former board member ties to figures like [[Bobby Ray Inman]] (who directed UAP-adjacent referrals per the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]] context) make its La Jolla headquarters a symbolic reference point in discussions of the defense contractor ecosystem allegedly surrounding UAP programs.
