CIA Headquarters in Langley, Virginia is the main campus of the Central Intelligence Agency and the home of its Directorate of Science and Technology (DS&T). In UAP research it matters mainly as the place where NASA mission specialist [[Bob Echler]] met CIA DS&T Deputy Director [[Everett Heinman]] in 1989 to ask about recovered non-human craft.

## The 1989 Heinman-Echler Meeting

In 1989, Echler contacted retired Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]] (a former Director of Naval Intelligence, Director of the NSA, and Deputy Director of the CIA) to ask whether recovered UFO vehicles might be made available for scientific research. Inman sent Echler to Everett Heinman at CIA DS&T as "the best person to ask" about recovered craft in operational condition. Echler then traveled to CIA Headquarters in Langley to meet Heinman.

At the meeting, Heinman denied any knowledge of UFOs or UAP legacy programs. In 2022 the researcher RGH UFOs contacted him independently and asked specifically about Inman's referral. This time Heinman gave no flat denial and said only that he was "far removed from that area of work". Gerb reads this as an ambiguous partial confirmation, noting that a direct denial would have been the expected answer if no such programs existed.

## Significance in Legacy Program Research

Gerb presents the Langley meeting as direct evidence that Admiral Inman knew specifically which CIA officials managed recovered craft programs. As the CIA's technical intelligence arm, the DS&T would be a logical place to house a program handling physically recovered non-human technology. The video argues that Heinman's whole career in DS&T, together with his 2022 non-denial, keeps him of interest to anyone mapping how oversight of UAP legacy programs is organized.

The site also fits a wider pattern of gatekeeping by the CIA as an institution. In 2011, CIA DS&T Deputy Director [[Glenn Gaffney]] allegedly blocked the [[Kona Blue]] technology transfer that would have moved recovered non-human materials from [[Lockheed Martin]] to the AATIP/Kona Blue research initiative.
