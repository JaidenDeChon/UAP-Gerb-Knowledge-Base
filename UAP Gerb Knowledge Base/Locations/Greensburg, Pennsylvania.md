Greensburg is the county seat of Westmoreland County, Pennsylvania, about 7 miles west of the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|Kecksburg crash site]]. It figures in the Kecksburg case as the home of radio station WHJB and of the state police barracks where US Army and Air Force personnel were seen on the night of December 9, 1965.

## Role in the Kecksburg Incident

On December 9, 1965, WHJB radio in Greensburg became the first media outlet to broadcast news of the Kecksburg incident. It sent a reporter to the scene, who was turned away by military personnel manning roadblocks. The station's coverage started the wider press attention that made Kecksburg one of the most widely documented UAP crash retrieval cases in Pennsylvania history.

The state police barracks in Greensburg was a coordination point on the night of the incident. Witnesses and investigators reported seeing Army and Air Force personnel at the barracks. That was unusual for what was reportedly a routine meteor event, and researchers cite it as evidence that the military response to the Kecksburg object was planned in advance, not improvised.

## Stan Gordon's Investigation

Stan Gordon, the foremost investigator of the Kecksburg case, also worked out of Greensburg. Over several decades he documented witness testimonies and compiled the most comprehensive evidentiary record of the incident. Gordon's work, later shared with Leonard Stringfield, is the main research foundation for the Kecksburg crash retrieval thesis.
