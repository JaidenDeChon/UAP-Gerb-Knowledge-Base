Fort Riley is a United States Army installation near Junction City, Kansas. In UAP research it is known as the place where retired Army Lieutenant Colonel [[Philip J. Corso]] claimed to have personally seen a non-human biological entity in the summer of 1947.

## Corso's 1947 Encounter

On or about July 6, 1947, Corso was serving as post duty officer at Fort Riley. He claims that he came across a crate in a storage area that held a non-human body preserved in a bluish liquid. In his sworn affidavit, Corso described the entity as approximately four feet tall, with an oversized incandescent light bulb-shaped head, almond-shaped eye sockets, four-fingered hands, thin legs, and no facial hair or eyebrows. Gerb treats the affidavit as substantially more credible than the later co-written book *The Day After Roswell*. Skull features were arranged in a small circle on the lower part of an enlarged cranium.

Corso later determined that the crate was part of a convoy of five trucks carrying biological entities from an airfield in New Mexico westward along Route 40 through Fort Riley, with [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] cited as the final destination. His official military records confirm that he was posted at Fort Riley on that date. Gerb sees this as a meaningful detail that corroborates the timing of his account.

## Credibility Context

The Fort Riley observation is one of the parts of Corso's broader testimony that Gerb assesses as relatively credible. Gerb distinguishes between Corso's original manuscript *Dawn of a New Age*, which gives this account in an unembellished form, and the co-written 1997 bestseller *The Day After Roswell*. He argues that co-author Bill Burns inserted fabrications and exaggerations into the published version. Corso's sworn affidavit about the Fort Riley encounter is treated as evidence that should be taken seriously, even though other parts of his public claims are approached with skepticism.

A 1964–65 FBI background check on Corso returned negative characterizations, and the Eisenhower Library confirmed he never attended an NSC meeting despite claiming membership. Gerb weighs these points against the Fort Riley affidavit without dismissing it outright.
