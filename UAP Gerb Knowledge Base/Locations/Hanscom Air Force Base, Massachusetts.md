Hanscom Air Force Base is a United States Air Force installation located in Bedford, Massachusetts, approximately 17 miles northwest of Boston. It serves as the home of the Air Force Research Laboratory's Information Directorate and hosts a range of defense electronics, command and control, and avionics research programs. In UAP research, Hanscom appears as a posting in the career of witness "Ed," a redacted USAF officer whose testimony is analyzed by UAP Gerb in connection with alleged reverse engineering programs at [[Edwards Air Force Base]].

## Ed's Hanscom Period (2002–2005)

Witness "Ed" (Witness 11063 in [[Steven Greer]]'s Disclosure Project archive) is described as a US Air Force officer who held postings at Nellis Air Force Base, Hanscom, Edwards, and the Pentagon. UAP Gerb's analysis of Ed's testimony identifies the Hanscom period (approximately 2002–2005) as a phase of Ed's career involving conventional foreign military asset analysis in collaboration with MIT — which maintains close institutional ties with Hanscom through the Lincoln Laboratory, a federally funded research and development center on the base campus.

This phase of Ed's career is distinguished from his subsequent Edwards posting as involving terrestrial systems rather than direct non-human technology testing. The Hanscom period serves as part of the broader professional biography that positions Ed credibly as an Air Force officer with legitimate access to sensitive aerospace programs, preceding his alleged read-in to the reverse engineering programs at Edwards.

## Institutional Context

Hanscom's electronics and systems research mission — and its close ties to MIT Lincoln Laboratory — make it a plausible staging ground for personnel who would later move into more sensitive reverse engineering programs. Air Force Research Laboratory partnerships and specialized test and evaluation roles at Hanscom fit within the broader ecosystem of Western Range facilities and personnel pipelines described in UAP Gerb's analysis of the Hidden Wing program structure.
