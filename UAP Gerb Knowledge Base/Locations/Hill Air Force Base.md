**Hill Air Force Base** is a major United States Air Force installation in northern Utah, near Ogden, about 30 miles north of [[Salt Lake City, Utah|Salt Lake City]]. It is named after Major Ployer Peter Hill, who died in 1935 test-flying the Boeing Model 299, the B-17 prototype. Hill hosts fighter wings and [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)]]'s Ogden Air Logistics Complex, and it runs the [[Utah Test and Training Range]] (UTTR), the Air Force's range in Utah's West Desert. In UAP research Hill is significant for its shared use of [[Dugway Proving Ground]]: the Air Force range's south range and [[Michael Army Airfield]] are used by both installations.

## Relationship with Dugway

The UTTR is administered from Hill by the unit long known as the [[388th Range Squadron]]. The range's south range overlaps Dugway, and the 388th uses Michael Army Airfield and the adjacent [[Avery Technical Center]] for air testing and training. It also runs radar and threat-emitter sites on and around [[Granite Peak, Dugway|Granite Peak]]. A July 2012 environmental assessment covered the Air Force's continued exclusive use of Army land at Dugway.
