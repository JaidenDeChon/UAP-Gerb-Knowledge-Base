Fargo is the largest city in North Dakota. It is home to Hector International Airport (formerly Hector Airport) and was the site of the 1948 Gorman Dogfight, one of three classic UFO incidents that year that Project Sign investigators cited as proof that UFOs were real. In the encounter, a North Dakota National Guard pilot carried out a sustained aerial pursuit of an anomalous light over the city.

## The Gorman Dogfight (October 1, 1948)

On the evening of October 1, 1948, Second Lieutenant George F. Gorman of the North Dakota Air National Guard was flying a P-51 Mustang on a cross-country training flight over Fargo. After observing a small Piper Cub 500 feet below him, Gorman noticed another object to the west: a small blinking light that left no wing silhouette. At 9:07 p.m. he contacted air traffic control at Hector Airport and was told there was no other traffic in the area.

Gorman accelerated to 350–400 mph to pursue the object, which repeatedly outmaneuvered him. On one pass the two nearly collided, and Gorman got a clear look at the object. It was a simple ball of light about 6–8 inches in diameter, and as it accelerated it stopped blinking and grew brighter. In later attempts to chase it, at altitudes of up to 14,000 feet, Gorman's Mustang stalled while the object kept climbing. He gave up the pursuit at 9:27 p.m. From Hector Airport, air traffic controller L.D. Jensen independently watched the object through binoculars.

Gorman swore a legal affidavit on October 23, 1948, stating: "I am convinced that there was definitely thought behind its maneuvers." Project Sign later checked Gorman's P-51 for radiation and found it measurably more radioactive than other fighters. The radioactivity was initially attributed to the plane's proximity to an "atomic powered object."

## Project Sign Investigation

Captain Edward J. Ruppelt, director of Projects Sign, Grudge, and Blue Book, named the Gorman Dogfight as one of three 1948 incidents that "proved to Air Force intelligence specialists that UFOs were real." The official Air Force explanation was that Gorman had chased a lit weather balloon or the planet Jupiter. Investigators widely rejected it, finding the maneuverability described incompatible with a prosaic object.
