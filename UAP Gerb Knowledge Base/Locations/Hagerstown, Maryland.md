Hagerstown is a city in western Maryland. It comes up in UAP research through the 1950s Del Rio, Texas UFO crash cases, as the location of the US Marine Corps Metallurgy Laboratory where USAF Reserve Colonel [[Robert Willingham]] submitted a fragment he had retrieved from the crash site of an alleged disc-shaped craft near [[Langtry, Texas]].

## The Willingham Fragment and the USMC Metallurgy Lab

In 1955 Willingham saw a UAP traveling at approximately 2,000 mph, which then crashed near Langtry, Texas, on the Mexican border. He went to the crash site himself and retrieved a small piece of anomalous metal. The fragment was described as rigid, unyielding, extremely lightweight and grayish-silver, with small holes in a honeycomb pattern along one side that suggested heat dissipation structures. Willingham found that a torch could not cut or deform it.

Willingham submitted the fragment to a USMC Metallurgy Lab in Hagerstown, Maryland. When he later tried to follow up on the results of the analysis, the laboratory denied having any record of the tests or of the officer who had accepted the sample. After that, a USAF intelligence general warned Willingham not to discuss what he had seen "down on the border."

## Pattern of Document Disappearance

The Hagerstown episode fits a pattern that recurs in UAP crash retrieval testimony: physical materials and their documentation are submitted through official channels and then disappear without trace, while the people who received them deny all knowledge. Gerb places it alongside similar patterns in other cases, including the disappearance of all FOIA-requested documents related to the [[Aztec, New Mexico]] crash, and presents them as part of a broader institutional practice of evidence suppression.

The metallurgical properties Willingham described (extreme light weight, heat resistance and a honeycomb pattern) are consistent with descriptions of anomalous material from other alleged crash retrieval cases documented in the UAP Gerb knowledge base.
