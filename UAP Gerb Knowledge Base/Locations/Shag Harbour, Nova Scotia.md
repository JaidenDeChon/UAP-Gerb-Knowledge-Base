Shag Harbour is a small fishing village on the southwestern tip of Nova Scotia, Canada. In the UAP context it is best known for a well-documented 1967 incident in which multiple witnesses, including Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP) officers, saw an unidentified object crash into the harbor. The case is one of the few UAP incidents to receive an official government investigation, carried out by Canada's Department of National Defence.

## The 1967 Shag Harbour Incident

On the night of October 4, 1967, multiple witnesses, among them local residents and RCMP officers, saw a large object with flashing amber lights descend at roughly 45 degrees and hit the surface of Shag Harbour. They first assumed it was a crashing aircraft and alerted the authorities. Canadian Coast Guard and RCMP units responded, but they found no conventional aircraft wreckage, only a trail of yellowish foam on the water's surface.

Canada's Department of National Defence investigated the event. The incident remains one of the few officially acknowledged cases in which a government agency concluded that an unidentified object entered the water without being identified as any known aircraft or vessel. It is frequently cited as one of the best-documented cases of a trans-medium UAP or USO (unidentified submerged object) event.

## Relation to Shelburne Harbor

Shag Harbour is close to [[Shelburne Harbor, Nova Scotia]], which lies approximately 30 miles to the northeast. Shelburne Harbor was the site of a separate encounter in 1960, when Royal Canadian Navy divers observed disc-shaped craft on the seabed during a joint US-Canadian minesweeping exercise. The two Nova Scotia incidents are distinct UAP/USO events, seven years apart but close together on the map.
