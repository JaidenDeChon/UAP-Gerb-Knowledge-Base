Holloman Air Force Base is a US Air Force installation in the Tularosa Basin of southern New Mexico, about six miles southwest of [[Alamogordo, New Mexico|Alamogordo]] and next to [[White Sands Missile Range]]. It opened in 1942 as Alamogordo Army Air Field. In 1947 Air Materiel Command made it its main site for testing pilotless aircraft and guided missiles, and on 13 January 1948 it was renamed for Colonel George V. Holloman, a pioneer of guided missile research. Its host unit today is the 49th Wing, which trains F-16 pilots.

## In Gerb's videos

Holloman comes up in Gerb's videos in two ways: as the setting of an alleged landing of a non-human craft, and as one of the New Mexico sites in the early record of green fireballs and missile research.

### The alleged landing

Gerb refers to the story of a landing at Holloman several times without examining it in depth, and his videos give two dates for it.

- In *UFO Whistleblowers Vol.1*, he mentions the story that President [[Dwight D. Eisenhower]] met extraterrestrials who had landed at Holloman in 1954 and reached an agreement with them. He asks whether this is the kind of deal [[David Grusch]] declined to discuss.
- In his Corso video, Gerb cites [[Jacques Vallee]]'s *Forbidden Science: Pacific Heights*, in which [[Eric Davis]] says former president George H. W. Bush told him the 1954 Holloman landing, and the deal Eisenhower is said to have made there, really happened.
- In his Edwards video, Gerb places the landing in 1957, describing a saucer that touched down at Holloman and occupants who walked out to meet Air Force personnel. He compares it with the account of Dr. Epigoni in [[Project Heaven]], in which beings left a landed craft, lay down and seemed to switch themselves off, and with the [[Craft Donation Theory|craft donation theory]].
- In his Dugway video, Gerb retells a story from [[James Fox]]. In the 1970s producers Allan Sandler and Robert Emenegger, then making a UAP documentary, were shown film of a landing at Holloman by two Air Force men and licensed to use it, but the licence was soon withdrawn. One of the two men, Colonel Weinbrenner, reportedly said on his deathbed that Utah is where the alien bodies are stored.
- In his video on the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], Gerb notes that Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] asked about a Holloman landing, along with Roswell and [[Majestic 12]], and that the committee declined to discuss it.

### Green fireballs and missile work

- Gerb's Sandia video names Holloman with Los Alamos, the White Sands Proving Ground and Sandia Base as the places [[Project Twinkle]] studied green fireballs. A month before [[Project Blue Book]] began in 1952, the Air Force recommended that Twinkle not be declassified.
- His Sarbacher video quotes page 33 of the Twinkle report: after the phenomenon was seen following a V-2 launch on 31 August 1950, plans were made for aircraft from Holloman to chase the objects and photograph them at close range.
- His Northrop Grumman and Lockheed Martin videos place [[BDM International|BDM]] at [[Fort Bliss]], White Sands and Holloman, working on missile guidance, applied optics, electronic instrumentation and radiation physics. TRW bought BDM in 1997, and Northrop Grumman bought TRW in 2002. Gerb has linked all three companies to UFO legacy programs.
