Big Sur is a rugged stretch of the central California coast on the Pacific Ocean, south of the Monterey Peninsula. In UAP research it is known as the area where [[Robert Jacobs]]'s optical instrumentation unit, based at [[Vandenberg Air Force Base]], operated during the September 14, 1964 missile test that produced the famous Vandenberg UFO film incident.

## The 1964 Vandenberg Missile Test

On September 14, 1964, Lieutenant [[Robert Jacobs]] commanded a 100-man optical instrumentation unit at [[Vandenberg Air Force Base]]. Its job was to film every nuclear payload and ballistic missile launch from the Western Test Range near Big Sur, and its cameras could pick out the "nuts and bolts" of a missile from 160 miles away. That day the unit was to document the launch of a dummy ICBM warhead designed to separate from [[Missile Chaff Decoy|chaff countermeasures]] released ahead of it. The decoy was meant to draw Soviet air defenses away from the actual payload.

The cameras near Big Sur filmed a disc-shaped craft, described as two saucers pressed together with a hemisphere on top, as it intercepted the dummy warhead at the edge of space. The craft fired four directed energy beams at the warhead and knocked it off its trajectory. During the intercept it traveled at an estimated 6,000–8,000 mph.

## Aftermath and Cover-Up

The film was suppressed at once. The next day Major [[Florence J. Mansman]] summoned Jacobs and showed him the relevant part of the film. Three men in gray suits were present, whom Mansman identified as the Director of the Office of the Chief Scientist and two government agent assistants. Mansman ordered Jacobs to describe the events as "laser tracking strikes", a cover story that was technically impossible, since no practical laser tracking system capable of such a feat existed in 1964. The men in gray suits cut the UFO footage from the reel with scissors, put it in a briefcase and left. Mansman warned Jacobs of "dark consequences" for any security breach. Jacobs kept quiet for 18 years, reasoning that he had never signed a non-disclosure agreement, and then disclosed the incident publicly in 1982.

Mansman later corroborated Jacobs's account in a signed letter to Paramount Pictures. On February 10, 2023, decades after the incident, Jacobs testified about it under oath to [[AARO]] and [[Sean Kirkpatrick]]. That makes it one of the few UAP cases in which a witness has given official testimony in the current disclosure era.

## Significance

At the Big Sur optical instrumentation site, film of a UAP interacting with a U.S. nuclear weapon was captured, then immediately classified and physically confiscated. The incident is part of Gerb's wider analysis of the connection between UFOs and nuclear weapons. That analysis identifies a documented pattern of UAP appearing at nuclear weapons systems and disabling or interfering with them, across multiple decades and facilities. One example is the 1967 [[Malmstrom Air Force Base]] incident, in which all 10 Minuteman ICBMs were rendered inoperable at the same time.
