Shelburne Harbor is a sheltered harbor on the southwestern coast of Nova Scotia, Canada. In 1960 it was the site of a USO (unidentified submerged object) encounter: Royal Canadian Navy (RCN) divers taking part in a joint US-Canadian minesweeping exercise reported seeing two disc-shaped craft on the seabed, with what appeared to be occupants trying to repair one of them. Rear Admiral Timothy Gallaudet cites the case as a significant historical USO event in his 2024 white paper on USOs.

## The 1960 Incident

During a joint US-Canadian minesweeping exercise in Shelburne Harbor, RCN divers went down to the seabed and found two disc-shaped craft resting on the bottom. According to their accounts, later relayed in Rear Admiral Timothy Gallaudet's 2024 white paper *Beneath the Surface*, they saw what appeared to be occupants working to repair one of the craft. The divers reportedly filmed the encounter.

Rear Admiral Gallaudet has said he found the story implausible at first. He stated that later disclosures by US military whistleblowers changed his view and led him to include the case in his white paper on USO phenomena and maritime UAP research priorities.

## Relation to Shag Harbour

Shelburne Harbor is about 30 miles northeast of [[Shag Harbour, Nova Scotia]], the site of the famous 1967 UAP impact into the water. Researchers studying USO patterns in the northwestern Atlantic have noted that the two cases, a 1960 underwater disc encounter and a 1967 impact, fall within the same 30-mile stretch of coast.

## Significance

The Shelburne Harbor case came seven years before the Shag Harbour incident and is much less well known. Gallaudet, a retired US Navy rear admiral, cited it in a formal white paper, which gives it unusual institutional credibility. If the RCN divers' footage was preserved, it would be among the earliest physical documentation of a USO.
