Cape Race is a remote headland at the southeastern tip of the Avalon Peninsula in Newfoundland, Canada. In UAP research it is known as the place where, in 1887, Captain R.F. Moore observed a luminous fireball offshore that showed the characteristics of an unidentified submerged object (USO).

## The 1887 Moore Sighting

In 1887 Captain R.F. Moore reported seeing an object like a fireball in the waters off Cape Race. The sighting is recorded in historical maritime records, and UAP researchers cite it as an early example of trans-medium or USO phenomena: objects seen entering, leaving or moving through the surface of the ocean. The cape sits where the North Atlantic shipping lanes converge, and its waters have long been rough and foggy. Together these made it a busy corridor for maritime observation.

## Historical Maritime Context

During the age of steam, Cape Race was one of the most important navigational landmarks in the North Atlantic and the main landfall for trans-Atlantic ships approaching North America. It also had a prominent wireless telegraph station, which received distress calls from the RMS Titanic in 1912. The heavy shipping traffic and the trained observers in the region give added weight to historical USO reports from the area.
