Shag Harbour is a small fishing community in Shelburne County, Nova Scotia, Canada, on the southwestern shore of the province. It is the site of the [[Shag Harbour Incident]], a 1967 UAP case that ranks among the most thoroughly documented and officially acknowledged UAP events in Canadian history, and among the most significant internationally. This wiki uses Shag Harbour as a benchmark case when comparing crash retrieval incidents outside the U.S., alongside the [[Varginha Incident]] in Brazil and the [[1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval|Coyame Incident]] in Mexico, as documented in [[Videos/The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash/summary|The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash]].

## The Shag Harbour Incident (1967)

On the night of October 4, 1967, multiple civilian witnesses and Royal Canadian Mounted Police officers saw a large object with amber lights descend at a shallow angle into the waters of Shag Harbour. It made a bright flash and left a yellowish foam on the surface. The object was reportedly 60 feet in diameter. The Canadian government opened an official investigation and recorded the incident in government records as a genuine unidentified flying object. The Royal Canadian Navy searched underwater, and no wreckage was recovered.

Later investigation by UAP researchers established that Canadian and U.S. naval assets kept sonar contact with an underwater object in the days after the initial crash, and that a separate submarine was also detected in the area. The object reportedly moved underwater to a spot near a classified naval facility at Government Point, then left in a northeasterly direction and rose out of the water. The Canadian government's own records classify the incident as unexplained.

## Significance as a Comparative Case

Gerb cites Shag Harbour as one of several internationally significant UAP incidents outside the United States that involve alleged object recovery or government involvement. The Coyame and Varginha cases are characterized by alleged biological containment and direct military involvement in recovering entities or craft. Shag Harbour differs from them: it is notable for documented government acknowledgment, multiple credible civilian and law enforcement witnesses, and the absence of a satisfactory official explanation.

The case is geographically distinct from [[Shag Harbour, Nova Scotia]], a related page catalogued separately in this wiki. It is also distinct from the 1960 [[Shelburne Harbor USO Incident]], in which Royal Canadian Navy divers taking part in a joint U.S.-Canadian minesweeping exercise reported seeing disc-shaped craft on the seabed. Gerb treats the proximity of these two significant Canadian maritime UAP incidents, in the same coastal region of Nova Scotia, as meaningful for USO (Unidentified Submerged Object) research more broadly.
