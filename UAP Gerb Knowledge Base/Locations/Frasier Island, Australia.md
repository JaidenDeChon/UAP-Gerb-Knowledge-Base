Frasier Island (also known as K'gari) is a large sand island off the southeastern coast of Queensland, Australia. In UAP research it is known as the site of a 1966–67 sighting of unidentified submerged objects (USOs), reported by pilot C. Adams and cameraman Les Hendy. The sighting came at a time when the Australian government was beginning to take UAP phenomena more seriously.

## The Frasier Island USO Sighting (1966–67)

In 1966–67, pilot C Adams and cameraman Les Hendy observed four or five mysterious objects near Frasier Island, including a main cigar-shaped object and smaller craft accompanying it. The witnesses said the objects went under the water without disturbing the surface. The same trait is reported in several other credible USO accounts and is considered one of the more physically remarkable aspects of the wider transmedium craft phenomenon.

Gerb examined this case as part of a comprehensive historical survey of USO sightings, from the era of Christopher Columbus through the late 20th century, drawing on maritime logs, military records and Project Blue Book files. The Frasier Island encounter is noted for sharing an important distinguishing feature with the [[1987 HMNZS Southland USO Incident|HMNZS Southland]] case near New Zealand: the surface stayed completely undisturbed as the objects went under. Entering the water without the expected displacement, wake or cavity is flagged again and again in independent accounts with multiple witnesses as a trait of genuine transmedium craft, one that misidentified conventional aircraft, missiles or natural phenomena cannot explain. No known human-made vessel can pass from air into water at speed without leaving visible effects on the surface, so this reported trait is one of the most physically diagnostic details in the USO literature.

## Australian Context

As the UAP Gerb survey video notes, the Frasier Island sighting happened while the Australian government was beginning to take UAP phenomena seriously. Australian Blue Book records and Soviet files from the same era reportedly described similar USO encounters, including Tic Tac-shaped craft, cigar-shaped objects and fireballs seen coming out of the water. Independent USO sightings by Australian, Soviet and American witnesses all describe objects rising from or sinking into the sea, and this wider pattern is cited as evidence against explaining the phenomenon purely as misidentification or collective delusion.

## Significance in USO Research

The Frasier Island case is one of a cluster of mid-20th-century maritime sightings that Gerb examines in his survey of historical USO cases. It is part of a wider body of research that draws on maritime logs, military records and Project Blue Book files to argue that the volume and consistency of credible historical USO reports suggest the oceans may be central to understanding the UAP phenomenon. The suppression of maritime reporting through directives such as [[OPNAV 3820]] and [[JANAP 146C]] is cited as a likely reason why cases like the Frasier Island sighting did not enter the official Blue Book record.
