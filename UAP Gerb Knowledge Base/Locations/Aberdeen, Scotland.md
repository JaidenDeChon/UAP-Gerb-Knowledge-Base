Aberdeen is a coastal city in northeastern Scotland, United Kingdom, at the mouths of the rivers Dee and Don on the North Sea coast. It is Scotland's third-largest city and a major center of the North Sea oil industry. In UAP research, Aberdeen is the geographic reference point used to place the alleged 1991 North Atlantic retrieval of an unidentified submerged object (USO), which a witness named Mark described on Art Bell's *Coast to Coast AM* on 19 April 2002.

## Role in the 1991 Retrieval Case

The alleged 1991 USO retrieval took place about 250 nautical miles (roughly 288 standard miles) west of Aberdeen, in waters consistent with the Rockall Trough. The trough is a deep-water feature east of the Rockall Plateau, where the ocean is more than 8,000 feet deep. A crew sent from Virginia with a Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicle (DSRV) dove to a depth of just under 1.5 miles and recovered a triangular, charcoal-gray craft about 68–70 feet long. Its underside had no visible windows, rivets, seams or mechanical features, and its hull carried repeating geometric markings. A marine archaeologist had estimated that the craft rested on the seafloor for 30 to 40 years before it was recovered.

Gerb used Aberdeen as a fixed reference point to estimate roughly where the recovery happened. Allowing for an error of plus or minus 20%, the estimated recovery area reaches as far as about 345 standard miles west of Aberdeen, which takes in the deep waters of the Rockall Trough.
