San Diego is a major coastal city in southern California. It is home to one of the largest U.S. Navy installations in the world and is the base of operations for the USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group. In UAP research, the city is most closely associated with the [[2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter (Tic Tac)]], the most documented and officially acknowledged modern UAP case in United States military history.

## The 2004 Nimitz Tic Tac Encounter

On November 14, 2004, U.S. Navy FA-18F Super Hornet pilots from the USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group, operating off the coast of San Diego, encountered a white, oblong, Tic Tac-shaped unidentified craft approximately 40 feet long. Commander [[David Fravor]], then the commanding officer of Strike Fighter Squadron 41 (VFA-41 Black Aces), was among the pilots who saw it. The craft hovered above a patch of roiling whitewater on the ocean, then accelerated away at a speed no known aircraft could match. Later analysis suggested the whitewater may have indicated a larger object submerged below the surface, acting as a transmedium vehicle. Fravor also noted that the objects (plural) appeared to have jammed the radar and sensory equipment of the Navy jets during the engagement.

The encounter was recorded on the now-famous FLIR1 gun-camera video, which the U.S. Department of Defense officially released in April 2020. In Fravor's account, the craft had no visible wings, engines or exhaust, and it changed direction instantaneously in a way inconsistent with any known aerodynamic platform. Gerb sees the active radar jamming Fravor reported as part of a broader pattern across multiple military UAP cases, including the 1979 [[Manises UFO Incident]] and the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]], in which anomalous craft used sophisticated electronic countermeasures against military aircraft.

## Debate Over the Object's Origin

The 2004 Nimitz encounter is a focal point in the ongoing debate over whether the craft was non-human technology or an advanced human-made aircraft. Gerb has examined both possibilities.

Under the non-human hypothesis, the craft's observed capabilities, including extreme speed, no visible propulsion signature and an apparent response to the approaching aircraft, are consistent with the non-human craft described in multiple independent witness and whistleblower accounts.

The other hypothesis points to Lockheed Skunk Works. Ross Coulthart stated categorically that the Tic Tac is Lockheed Martin technology, and Steven Greer similarly claimed the Nimitz Tic Tac originated from Lockheed Skunk Works. Gerb has theorized that the Tic Tac may have been testing the reaction times, speeds and methods of unknowing U.S. military pilots during the Nimitz exercises, which he describes as "unwitting testing on military personnel."

Gerb has also noted that human-made and non-human Tic Tac craft may both exist at the same time. The channel calls this distinction the "Human-made vs. Non-human Tic Tacs" debate.

## Navy Connection

San Diego is the home port of the USS Nimitz and a hub of U.S. Navy Pacific Fleet operations, which makes it central to research into UAP legacy programs. Gerb has identified the [[US Navy]] as a key institution in alleged UAP retrieval and reverse engineering programs, with the [[Office of Naval Intelligence]] and the classified [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] (NURO) playing central roles. San Diego is also close to [[Lockheed Martin]]'s operations in Southern California and to classified research facilities elsewhere in the region, which adds to its significance for this research.
