Howard Air Force Base (Howard AFB) is a former United States Air Force installation on the western bank of the Panama Canal, on the Pacific side, next to the city of Panama. During the Cold War it was the leading US military hub for operations throughout Central and South America, and for that reason it has been theorized as a staging location for a dedicated South American UAP crash retrieval team.

## Role as US SOUTHCOM Hub

For much of the Cold War, Howard AFB was the headquarters for US Southern Command (SOUTHCOM) air operations and the main US military air gateway to the whole of South and Central America. Operations under SOUTHCOM jurisdiction routinely staged personnel and equipment through Howard. These included counter-narcotics missions such as Operation Laser Strike in Peru, which involved up to 20 agencies.

## Theorized UAP Retrieval Staging

Researchers who have theorized about how a dedicated UAP crash retrieval team could have deployed rapidly to the 1997 [[Crash Site, Northern Peru|Peru crash site]] have named Howard AFB as the most plausible place for it to stage from. The base was close to South American airspace, was a SOUTHCOM logistics hub and had access to the classified contractor and intelligence elements that took part in Operation Laser Strike, which makes it a logical forward base for a rapid-reaction recovery unit.

Under the 1977 Panama Canal Treaties, Howard AFB was formally transferred to Panamanian control in 1999. It is now known as Marcos A. Gelabert International Airport.
