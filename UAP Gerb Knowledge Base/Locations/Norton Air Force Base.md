Norton Air Force Base was a United States Air Force installation in San Bernardino, California, about 60 miles east of Los Angeles. It was a major hub for Strategic Air Command and Air Mobility Command until it closed in 1994 under the Base Realignment and Closure (BRAC) process. In UAP research, Norton is cited as the departure point for the November 1988 classified aerospace exhibit at which [[Brad Sorenson]] allegedly saw three Alien Reproduction Vehicle Flux Liners.

## 1988 ARV Air Show Event

In November 1988, a major public USAF air show was held at Norton Air Force Base. During the Thunderbirds flyby display, [[Brad Sorenson]] was escorted away from the main public exhibit by his client, whom [[Mark McCandlish]] identified with high confidence as [[Frank Carlucci]], then-Secretary of Defense. Sorenson and other selected individuals boarded a military Boeing 727 that flew north from Norton to [[Air Force Plant 42]] (AFP 42) in Palmdale, California, where the private classified exhibit was held in a Skunk Works hangar. The public air show at Norton was cover for transporting about 30 senior individuals to the classified Palmdale briefing. See [[Norton Air Force Base Air Show]] for the full account of the event, including the exhibit's three disc-shaped "Mama," "Papa," and "Baby Bear" [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|Alien Reproduction Vehicles]], and the fact that then-Senator [[Alan Cranston]] was among the attendees.

Lieutenant Colonel John Williams, an electrical engineer who managed construction projects for Military Air Command at Norton from 1981 to 1982, separately said he knew of a facility within Norton Air Force Base so secret that even the base's wing commander was not allowed access. Rumors at the base held that this facility housed a UAP. McCandlish noted that this claim was consistent with the broader Norton-to-Palmdale geography of the ARV program.
