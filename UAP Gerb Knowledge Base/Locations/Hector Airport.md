Hector Airport is a civilian airport near [[Fargo, North Dakota, USA|Fargo, North Dakota]]. It was the main air traffic control facility monitoring the [[Gorman Dogfight]] on October 1, 1948. Air traffic controller [[L.D. Jensen]], working in the airport's tower, was an independent ground witness to Second Lieutenant [[George F. Gorman]]'s 27-minute aerial pursuit of an unidentified luminous object.

## Role in the Gorman Dogfight

On the evening of October 1, 1948, at approximately 9:07 p.m., Gorman saw an unusual blinking light and contacted Hector Airport air traffic control to ask about other aircraft in the region. Jensen confirmed that the only other traffic in the area was a Piper Cub that had already been identified. This established that the object Gorman was watching was not a known aircraft.

During the pursuit, Jensen watched through binoculars from the airport as the object passed overhead, which gave independent confirmation from the ground that the object existed. He confirmed its presence and movement, but reported that he could not make out the blinking glow Gorman described from his cockpit.

## Significance in Project Sign Investigation

The airport was central to the [[Project Sign]] investigation, because its air traffic control was in direct communication with Gorman during the encounter and Jensen had watched the object independently. The airport's records and Jensen's testimony provided ground-based data that corroborated what Gorman saw from the air.

The 27-minute pursuit ended at 9:27 p.m., and Gorman returned to Hector Airport. His P-51 Mustang was then tested for radiation as part of the Project Sign investigation. It was found to be measurably more radioactive than other fighters, though this was later attributed to reduced atmospheric shielding at the high altitude Gorman reached during the chase.
