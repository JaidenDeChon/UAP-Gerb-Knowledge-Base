USAF Radar Detachment 5 was a little-documented United States Air Force radar installation at [[Iquitos, Peru|Iquitos]] in the northern Peruvian Amazon, one of the secondary radar sites of the 1990s counter-narcotics [[Operation Laser Strike]]. UAP Gerb proposes it as the base from which Lance Corporal [[Jonathan Weygandt]] and his fellow [[Marine Air Control Group 28]] Marines set out in 1997 to secure the site of the [[Peru UFO Crash Incident|crashed craft]]. The video's transcript renders the name "USF" or "Raider" Detachment 5; the host says he could find only one public mention of the unit from the 1990s onward.

## The case for Iquitos

Operation Laser Strike ran from three sites with TPS-43 radars: [[Pucallpa]], the primary and publicly acknowledged site; Andoas; and Iquitos. Most researchers have assumed Weygandt was at Pucallpa. The host argues for Detachment 5 instead:

- **The flight in.** Weygandt says his unit flew into [[Lima, Peru|Lima]] by C-130 and then took a multi-hour flight, with one stop, to its base. Pucallpa is about 300 miles from Lima, under an hour at C-130 cruise speed; Iquitos is more than 630 miles away, close to two hours, which with a stop fits "several hours".
- **Secrecy.** A Marine the host contacted refused to discuss his base because the operation is still classified. Pucallpa has been publicised as a Laser Strike site since at least 2001, so the host reads the refusal as pointing elsewhere.
- **The mission.** Weygandt says his radar tracked narcotics traffic from Bolivia, and 1998 maps of narco routes pass directly through Iquitos.
- **The neighbours.** Iquitos also hosted the [[MARFORLANT Riverine Training Team]] and the Navy's [[NAMRU South, Lima, Peru|NAMRU South]] (formerly NAMRU-6), which the host links to the black-clad operators and to the permanent, multinational base where Weygandt was held.

The host's travel-time reconstruction, from Weygandt's timings, places the crash within roughly 90 miles of Iquitos, about 5 to 10 miles from the Colombian or Brazilian border (Weygandt himself said the Bolivian border, which the host believes was a slip). Weygandt was later driven, not flown, back to his base and kept there with Air Force personnel for three weeks before returning to Cherry Point.

## Outreach to the detachment

The host wrote to several Air Force personnel he associates with Detachment 5 and the 649th Combat Logistics Support Squadron, to ask whether MACG-28 Marines guarded the site, whether a Department of Energy team passed through or borrowed its aircraft, and whether stories circulated of a Marine who vanished for two days. As of the video, none had replied, and others he reached repeated that parts of the operation remain classified.
