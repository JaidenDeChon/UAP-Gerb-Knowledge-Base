A remote area in Catron County, west-central New Mexico, identified as the site of an alleged secondary flying saucer crash in 1947. That crash was separate from the primary [[Roswell, New Mexico|Roswell]] incident but happened at roughly the same time. Witnesses described a seamless craft with no visible rivets, seams, or weld joints, which is consistent with descriptions of non-human construction methods reported in other UAP recovery cases.

## The 1947 Secondary Crash

The San Augustine crash is considered a companion event to the Roswell incident of July 1947. Unlike the Foster Ranch scatter field associated with Roswell, the San Augustine craft reportedly came down intact or nearly intact, so witnesses could see how it was built. Those who viewed it specifically remarked that its exterior had no seams, rivets, or weld joints. That absence has been noted across multiple alleged recovery cases as a hallmark of non-human metallic construction.

The San Augustine Plains are a high-altitude grassland basin at about 6,500 feet. The area is isolated enough that a downed craft could remain undetected for some time before military recovery teams arrived.

## Relation to Roswell

There are two theories about how the 1947 New Mexico incidents relate. In one, a single craft broke apart as it came down, scattering debris at the Foster Ranch while the main body came to rest near San Augustine. In the other, the incidents involved two separate craft from the same event. Researchers including Stanton Friedman examined the San Augustine claims alongside the primary Roswell investigation.
