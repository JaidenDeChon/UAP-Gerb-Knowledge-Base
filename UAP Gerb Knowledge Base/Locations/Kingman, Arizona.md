Kingman is a city in, and the county seat of, Mohave County in northwestern Arizona. It lies at the southern end of the Cerbat Mountains, which rise immediately to its north, with the Hualapai Valley to the north-east and the Hualapai Mountains to the south-east. In UFO research it is the namesake of the alleged [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]], in which an unidentified oval craft is said to have come down in the desert "in the vicinity of Kingman" on the night of 20–21 May 1953.

## The 1953 crash retrieval

According to the sworn 1973 affidavit of engineer [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], he and 15 other [[Atomic Energy Commission]] specialists working on [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]] were assembled at [[Indian Springs Air Force Base]] in Nevada, flown to [[Phoenix, Arizona|Phoenix]], and driven for about four hours in a bus with blacked-out windows to the crash site. The affidavit places the site only near Kingman; no more precise location has been published. The city sits roughly 150 miles south-east of the Nevada test range where Upshot-Knothole's eleven shots were fired in the spring of 1953, a proximity UAP Gerb reads as part of the wider [[UFO-Nuclear Connection]].

Kingman lies on historic Route 66, on the main road between Las Vegas and Phoenix. The crash is also known in later literature through the account of [[Bill Uhouse]], who claimed the recovered craft became the model for a flying disc simulator.
