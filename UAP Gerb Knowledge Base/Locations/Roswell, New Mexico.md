A city in Chaves County, southeastern New Mexico, and the site of the most famous alleged UAP crash retrieval in history. In July 1947, wreckage of an unidentified object was found on the nearby Foster Ranch. The Army Air Force first put out a press release acknowledging a "flying disc", then changed the official explanation to a weather balloon. Material from the crash is alleged to have been transferred to Wright-Patterson Air Force Base and later studied by the Battelle Memorial Institute.

## The 1947 Crash

On or around July 3–4, 1947, rancher Mac Brazel discovered unusual debris scattered across a field northwest of Roswell. Major Jesse Marcel, intelligence officer of the 509th Bomb Group at Roswell Army Air Field, was dispatched to collect the material. Marcel later described recovering an exceedingly light metal that retained its shape after deformation. Those properties are consistent with nitinol, a nickel-titanium shape-memory alloy. Battelle Memorial Institute was contracted to study nitinol in 1949 at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, more than a decade before it was "officially" discovered in 1961.

## Battelle Memorial Institute Analysis

The alleged connection between Roswell crash material and Battelle's 1949 nickel-titanium alloy research (contract 33-38-3736 with Wright-Patterson AFB) is a central part of the Roswell evidence put together on this channel. Battelle researcher E.J. Center, who left the institute in 1957, allegedly told family members that he had worked on "parts retrieved from a flying saucer." MUFON reported this connection in the summer of 1994, about sixteen years before Center's 1949 nitinol research was declassified in 2010.

## Jesse Marcel and the Cover-Up

At the direction of General Roger Ramey, Jesse Marcel was publicly photographed posing with balsa wood and aluminum foil, which was described as weather balloon material. Researchers characterize this as deliberate press suppression. Marcel later disclosed the substitution himself, saying the debris he recovered did not match the materials in the photographs.

A secondary alleged crash site is at [[San Augustine, New Mexico]], where a craft described as seamless, with no rivets or weld joints, reportedly came down.

## Legacy

Many UAP researchers consider the Roswell case the foundational event of the modern UFO legacy program. Several whistleblower accounts describe technology seeding from Roswell, meaning the transfer of recovered materials to defense contractors for reverse engineering. Philip J. Corso's book *The Day After Roswell* refers to it explicitly.
