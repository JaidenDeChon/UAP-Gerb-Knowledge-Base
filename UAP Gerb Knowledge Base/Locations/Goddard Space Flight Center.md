Goddard Space Flight Center is a major NASA facility in Greenbelt, Maryland, about 12 miles northeast of Washington, D.C. It is named after the American rocket pioneer Robert H. Goddard and is one of NASA's main centers for Earth science research, space exploration missions, and the development and operation of scientific satellites.

## J. Allen Hynek's 1967 NORAD UFO Disclosure

On January 6, 1967, Dr. [[J. Allen Hynek]], scientific adviser to [[Project Blue Book]], took part in a discussion at Goddard Space Flight Center. There he made a significant public disclosure about military UFO tracking.

Hynek revealed that [[NORAD]] (North American Aerospace Defense Command) had been tracking UFOs since 1957, and that every single military UFO case from the continental United States in the Project Blue Book files was designated as a NORAD case.

He said this at a NASA facility, to an audience that included space scientists and researchers. The statement confirmed that NORAD was tracking and cataloging unidentified objects with defense surveillance systems, in parallel with Project Blue Book and far more seriously, while Blue Book publicly portrayed UFO investigation as dismissive and limited. The disclosure indicated that space-based and ground-based aerospace defense networks had been systematically monitoring UFOs for a decade, and that this data was never fully integrated into Project Blue Book's public findings.

## Significance

As a civilian NASA facility focused on space science, Goddard Space Flight Center gave Hynek a semi-public forum. There he could make statements about military UFO tracking that might not have been possible at a Defense Department venue. The disclosure helped establish the historical record of NORAD's long-running UFO monitoring program, which has continued through systems like the [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites that detect [[Fast Walkers]].
