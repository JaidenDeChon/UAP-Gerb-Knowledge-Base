Frenchman's Flat (also referred to as Frenchman Flat) is a dry lakebed basin within the [[Nevada National Security Site (Nevada Test Site)|Nevada Proving Ground]] (later renamed the Nevada Test Site, now the Nevada National Security Site) in Nye County, Nevada. In the 1950s it was one of the main above-ground nuclear weapons test areas for the [[Atomic Energy Commission]] (AEC) and the U.S. military, and it hosted atmospheric detonations during the early Cold War nuclear testing program.

## Role in Operation Upshot-Knothole

Frenchman's Flat was an important site for [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]], a series of 11 nuclear test shots in early 1953. [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]] (initially disclosing under the pseudonym [[Fritz Warner]]) was stationed at Frenchman's Flat on May 20, 1953. He was an AEC project engineer, measuring blast effects on the different types of structures built for the nuclear tests. There he received a telephone call from [[Edward Bushnell Doll|Ed Doll]], the physicist and project director for the test series, telling him about a special assignment the next day. That assignment took him to the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]] site.

Stansel's diary entry for that day, which Gerb cites in his timeline video "80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering", describes it in his own words: "Spent most of the day on Frenchman's Flat surveying cubicles and supervising welding of plate girder bridge sensor which cracked after last shot. Drank brew and eve read funny call from Dr. Doll about 1000. I'm going on a special job tomorrow."

The facility's remote desert location, heavy military security, and presence of AEC-cleared personnel made it a natural staging point for covert operations requiring scientific and engineering expertise under strict compartmentalization.
