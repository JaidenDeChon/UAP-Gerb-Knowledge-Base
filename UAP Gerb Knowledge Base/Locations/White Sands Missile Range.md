White Sands Missile Range (WSMR) is the US Army's largest military installation, covering approximately 3,200 square miles of desert in southern New Mexico. It was established in 1945 as White Sands Proving Ground to test V-2 rockets captured from Nazi Germany under [[Operation Paperclip]], and WSMR has since been the nation's primary overland test range for ballistic missiles, rockets, and weapons systems. The world's first nuclear weapons test, Trinity, took place on the range in July 1945.

## UAP Legacy Program Context

White Sands Missile Range is alleged to be one terminus of an underground transportation network that connects classified military facilities across the American Southwest. Gerb cites an unnamed Army officer who served in the 2010s and described traveling by underground high-speed train from a fort in Texas to White Sands. The officer reported that the train's speed and acceleration were so extreme that they reminded him of his fear of flying. His testimony is presented as firsthand corroboration of an underground rail system linking DUMB (deep underground military base) facilities.

The range lies near [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]] (approximately 100 miles to the north) and [[Sandia National Laboratories]]/[[Kirtland Air Force Base, New Mexico|Kirtland Air Force Base]], and has its own long history as a testing site for advanced weapons technologies. Together these make it a natural candidate for DUMB-related activities in [[Bill Hamilton]]'s network map, which identifies multiple installations in southern New Mexico.

## Operation Paperclip Roots

White Sands has direct ties to Operation Paperclip and the transfer of Nazi scientific expertise to the United States. German scientists, including Wernher von Braun and colleagues from Peenemünde, began conducting rocket tests at White Sands in 1946. The Paperclip link is noted in the DUMB context because the same program that brought Nazi rocket scientists to White Sands also brought engineers who specialized in underground construction, including Xavier Dorsch, to inform early US DUMB programs.
