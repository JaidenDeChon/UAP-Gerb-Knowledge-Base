White Sands Missile Range (WSMR) is the US Army's largest military installation, covering approximately 3,200 square miles of desert terrain in southern New Mexico. Originally established as White Sands Proving Ground in 1945 to test V-2 rockets captured from Nazi Germany under [[Operation Paperclip]], WSMR has served as the nation's primary overland test range for ballistic missiles, rockets, and weapons systems. The range hosted the world's first nuclear weapons test — Trinity — in July 1945.

## UAP Legacy Program Context

White Sands Missile Range is alleged to be a terminus node in an underground transportation network connecting classified military facilities across the American Southwest. An unnamed Army officer who served in the 2010s — cited by UAP Gerb — described traveling via underground high-speed train from a fort in Texas to White Sands, reporting that the speed and acceleration of the train were so extreme that it reminded him of his fear of flying. This testimony is presented as firsthand corroboration of an underground rail system connecting DUMB facilities.

The range's proximity to [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]] (approximately 100 miles to the north), [[Sandia National Laboratories]]/[[Kirtland Air Force Base]], and its own long history as a testing site for advanced weapons technologies make it a natural candidate for DUMB-related activities in [[Bill Hamilton]]'s network map, which identifies multiple installations in southern New Mexico.

## Operation Paperclip Roots

White Sands has direct connections to Operation Paperclip and the transfer of Nazi scientific expertise to the United States. German scientists, including Wernher von Braun and colleagues from Peenemünde, conducted rocket tests at White Sands beginning in 1946. This connection to Paperclip is noted in the DUMB context because the same program that brought Nazi rocket scientists to White Sands also brought engineers specializing in underground construction — including Xavier Dorsch — to inform early US DUMB programs.
