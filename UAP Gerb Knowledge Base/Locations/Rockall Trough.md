The Rockall Trough is a deep underwater basin in the northeastern Atlantic Ocean, located east of the Rockall Plateau and west of the British Isles. It extends roughly from northwest of Ireland to northwest of Scotland and reaches maximum depths of approximately 2,000 to 3,000 meters (6,500 to 9,800 feet). In UAP research, the Rockall Trough is significant as the most likely geographic site of the alleged 1991 North Atlantic USO retrieval described by witness Mark, who reported his DSRV crew operating at just under 1.5 miles of depth approximately 250 nautical miles west of [[Aberdeen, Scotland]].

## The 1991 Retrieval

UAP Gerb calculated that 250 nautical miles (approximately 288 standard miles) due west of Aberdeen corresponds to the area of the northeastern Atlantic near the Rockall Trough, where depths consistently exceed 8,000 feet — consistent with Mark's reported operational depth of just under 1.5 miles. Factoring in a generous error margin of plus or minus 20% in the radius estimate extends the potential recovery area to approximately 345 standard miles from Aberdeen, still consistent with Rockall Trough depths. This specificity of depth and bearing distinguishes the case from vague USO reports and gives investigators a defined geographic search area.

The waters of the Rockall Trough and the broader Rockall Plateau area have historically been of strategic interest to naval powers, with the region lying along key submarine transit routes between the North Atlantic and British home waters.
