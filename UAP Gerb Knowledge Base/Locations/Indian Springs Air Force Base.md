Indian Springs Air Force Base is a military installation in the Mojave Desert of southern Nevada, about 45 miles northwest of Las Vegas. Now called Creech Air Force Base, it lies close to the [[Nevada National Security Site (Nevada Test Site)|Nevada Proving Ground]], where the [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]] nuclear tests were conducted in 1953.

In [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]]'s account of the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]], the 16 [[Atomic Energy Commission]] specialists recruited to investigate the downed craft first gathered at Indian Springs. There, Stansel and the other personnel handed their personal valuables to military police. They were then flown to Phoenix, Arizona, and taken from there to the crash site by blacked-out bus.

## The O'Donnell Account

Senior [[EG&G]] manager [[Alfred O'Donnell]] told journalist [[George Knapp]] in the 1990s that a flying saucer recovered in New Mexico, together with a live being, was kept at Indian Springs until 1955 and then moved to a facility adjacent to [[Area 51]]. In his investigation of [[Sandia National Laboratories]], Gerb proposes that the move followed the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]] and put the material in Nevada Test Site installations likely run by Sandia. He notes that the base, now Creech Air Force Base, operates extensively today at the Sandia-managed [[Tonopah Test Range (TTR)|Tonopah Test Range]].
