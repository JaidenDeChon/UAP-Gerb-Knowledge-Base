Indian Springs Air Force Base is a military installation located in the Mojave Desert of southern Nevada, approximately 45 miles northwest of Las Vegas. Now known as Creech Air Force Base, it sits in close proximity to the [[Nevada National Security Site (Nevada Test Site)|Nevada Proving Ground]] where [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]] nuclear tests were conducted in 1953.

In [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]]'s account of the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]], Indian Springs served as the initial assembly point for the 16 [[Atomic Energy Commission]] specialists recruited to investigate the downed craft. At Indian Springs, Stansel and the other personnel surrendered their personal valuables to military police before being transported by aircraft to Phoenix, Arizona, and subsequently by blacked-out bus to the crash site.

## The O'Donnell Account

Senior [[EG&G]] manager [[Alfred O'Donnell]] told journalist [[George Knapp]] in the 1990s that a flying saucer recovered in New Mexico, together with a live being, was kept at Indian Springs until 1955 and then moved to a facility adjacent to [[Area 51]]. In his investigation of [[Sandia National Laboratories]], UAP Gerb proposes that the move followed the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]] and put the material in Nevada Test Site installations likely run by Sandia. He notes that the base, now Creech Air Force Base, today operates extensively at the Sandia-managed [[Tonopah Test Range (TTR)|Tonopah Test Range]].
