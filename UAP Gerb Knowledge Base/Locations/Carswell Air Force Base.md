Carswell Air Force Base (AFB) is a US Air Force installation in Fort Worth, Texas. During the Cold War it was a major Strategic Air Command (SAC) base and housed bomber units, including B-47 and B-52 wings. It is named after Major Horace S. Carswell Jr., a Medal of Honor recipient from World War II.

## UAP Connections

### 1955 Willingham Mission

In spring 1955, USAF Reserve Colonel [[Robert Willingham]] and a group of F-86 Saber pilots launched from Carswell AFB on a Cold War simulation mission escorting B-47 and B-52 bombers on a New York–Texas–Washington State routing. During this mission, Willingham and other pilots saw a high-speed unidentified object that later crashed near [[Langtry, Texas]]. The mission call sign was "Willie Eddie" and Willingham's serial was CD1195. After the sighting, Willingham returned to Carswell and submitted his oral and written report, then left privately for the crash site.

### 1957 RB-47 Incident

Gerb cites Case 5 of the [[Condon Report]], which describes a fall 1957 encounter between "a B-47" on an electronic counter-measures training mission and a light that the crew saw, ground radar returned and the aircraft's ECM receiver registered at about 2,800 mHz. In Gerb's telling a large bright fireball, alternating red and white, pursued the RB-47 for over 600 miles. He dates it to 19 September 1957 and places the aircraft at Carswell, but the report names no base or crew, and later accounts identify it as an RB-47 of the 55th Strategic Reconnaissance Wing at Forbes Air Force Base in Topeka, Kansas, and date the flight to 17 July 1957 from Blue Book records. Gerb calls the ground radar "Carswell radar". The report says the light appeared to the pilot, whom Gerb names as Lewis D. Chase, to be "a glowing body as big as a barn", moving "at a velocity far higher than airplane speeds". Gerb says intelligence personnel removed all ECM graphic and radar data after landing; in the report that is the pilot's recollection, disputed by the co-pilot and the monitoring officer, and the study concluded the data "apparently never existed".

### El Indio Debris Connection

UFO researcher [[Todd Zechel]] reportedly obtained a declassified USAF document stating that a foreign object recovered on December 6 or 7, 1950, was transported to Carswell AFB. That date is consistent with the [[El Indio, Texas]] crash date in the alleged [[Majestic 12]] Eisenhower Briefing Document. No public proof of this document has been produced.
