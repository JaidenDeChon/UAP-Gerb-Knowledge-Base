El Indio is a small community in Maverick County, Texas, on the Rio Grande about 75 miles southeast of [[Del Rio, Texas]]. Guerrero, Mexico, in the state of Coahuila, lies across the river. The area is the reported site of a UAP crash retrieval on December 6, 1950, which is mentioned in the alleged [[Majestic 12]] Eisenhower Briefing Document. It is one of two incidents known together as the "Del Rio cases."

## The 1950 Crash

According to the Eisenhower Briefing Document, a UFO of similar origin to the one in the 1947 [[Roswell Crash|Roswell Incident]] hit the Earth at high speed on December 6, 1950, in the area between El Indio and Guerrero, Mexico. It struck fast enough that the craft was described as nearly obliterated on impact. That is a major difference from the nearby 1955 crash at [[Langtry, Texas]], where three substantial pieces of wreckage remained intact. The recovered materials were reportedly taken to the Atomic Energy Commission's facility at [[Sandia National Laboratories]] in Albuquerque, New Mexico.

In a 1995 article in *Omni* magazine, journalist Dennis Stacy described his investigation, from 1990 to 1994, into the El Indio claim. Stacy found a local resident, Senor Flores, who had been working his family's land on the river north of El Indio when a ball of fire fell from the sky and crashed on a neighboring ranch, starting a grass fire. A day or two later, a military contingent arrived from Piedras Negras, blocked off the site and hauled something away by truck. Flores could not confirm whether the military vehicles were American, Mexican or a mix.

## Contextual Evidence

On December 6, 1950, the same date given in the MJ-12 document, a nationwide US military alert was called at 10:30 AM Eastern Time. It described "a large number of unidentified aircraft approaching northeastern US at high altitude", and interceptors were dispatched. [[General Ramey]] cancelled the alert at 1:16 PM. On December 8, 1950, a confidential memo sent to [[J. Edgar Hoover]] placed Army intelligence on high alert for any data on "flying discs."

## Distinction from the Langtry/Willingham Case

Four points of evidence separate the 1950 El Indio incident from the 1955 crash that Colonel [[Robert Willingham]] witnessed near [[Langtry, Texas]]:
1. The MJ-12 document describes the craft as nearly incinerated, while Willingham saw three large intact sections.
2. Willingham was serving in the Korean War in December 1950 and could not have been the witness.
3. El Indio is 75 miles southeast of Del Rio, while Langtry is 60 miles northwest, in the opposite direction.
4. Willingham's account refers to DEW (Distant Early Warning) radar systems, which were not put in place until 1952.
