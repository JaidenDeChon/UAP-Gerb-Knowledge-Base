Tonopah Test Range (TTR), also known as Area 52 and sometimes Area 54, is a highly classified weapons testing range of the Department of Energy and the Department of Defense. It lies in the northern part of the Nevada Test and Training Range, about 140 miles northwest of Las Vegas, in the remote Cactus Flat Valley of the Great Basin Desert. The range has been publicly accused of housing recovered UAP craft and of operating as a reverse-engineering RDT&E site for exotic propulsion systems. It has also been accused of serving as a critical node in alleged alien reproduction vehicle programs. From 1993 to 2017, Tonopah was managed by [[Lockheed Martin]] through its subsidiary Sandia Corporation, which administered [[Sandia National Laboratories]].

## Establishment and Official Mission

The Atomic Energy Commission (AEC) established Tonopah Test Range in 1957 as a permanent ballistics test range for unarmed nuclear weapon shapes. After AEC surveys for an ideal isolated location, the site was recommended in 1956 by the Naval Air Special Weapons Facility (now the Naval Air Warfare Center Weapons Division, based mainly at [[China Lake Naval Air Weapons Station|China Lake]]).

Official mission statements describe TTR as "The test range of choice for all national security missions". They also call it a provider of "research and development test support for the Department of Energy's weapons programs", offering a "unique test environment for use by other government agencies and their contractors".

The range has classified storage depots, multiple airfields (one major and several minor or abandoned ones) and numerous hangars. It also has facilities for robotics testing, smart transportation testing (pre-programmed or remote-controlled vehicles), infrared testing and rocket development. According to UAP researchers, it has deep underground military base (DUMB) facilities as well.

## Lockheed Martin Management Era (1993-2017)

From 1993 until 2017, Tonopah was managed by Sandia Corporation, a subsidiary of [[Lockheed Martin]]. Martin Marietta acquired the management contracts for [[Sandia National Laboratories]] in 1993. When Martin Marietta merged with Lockheed Corporation in 1995 to form Lockheed Martin, the merged company continued to administer Sandia.

UAP researchers consider these 24 years of Lockheed control over Tonopah highly significant:
- Tonopah was managed by the same defense contractor that has been accused of holding recovered UAP craft materials since the 1950s.
- [[Edgar Fouche|Edgar Fouché]] stated that [[Sandia National Laboratories]] and [[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory]] reverse-engineered the propulsion systems for the [[TR-3B]] alien reproduction vehicle.
- The TR-3B allegedly became operational in 1994, one year after Lockheed's Sandia subsidiary took control of Tonopah.
- Lockheed could have used Tonopah's isolation, its dual DOE/DoD oversight and its classified storage facilities to exploit UAP material away from the Air Force's traditional oversight structures.

In 2017, management passed to National Technology and Engineering Solutions of Sandia LLC, a subsidiary of Honeywell International.

## Edwards 412th Test Wing and Operational Security

Like [[Area 51]] Groom Lake, Tonopah Test Range is operated by a detachment of the [[Edwards Air Force Base]] 412th Test Wing MRTFB (Major Range and Test Facility Base). Because the operations there are classified, the personnel, commanders and unit designations at Tonopah are classified too, which gives the range a "mini Area 51" operational security posture.

The town of Tonopah, Nevada (population 1,938 as of 2023) offers little local infrastructure, which keeps the range isolated. Unlike Area 51 workers, some Tonopah workers were allowed to live in nearby communities. Site 4 employees, however, had to take commuter flights from Las Vegas on Janet Airlines, the same red-striped aircraft that carry Area 51 workers. This rule for Site 4 staff suggests tighter compartmentalization there than TTR's standard security protocols require.

## Site 4 / Area 54 and Bob Lazar S4 Comparison

Tonopah has an acknowledged Site 4, which appears on unclassified Sandia TTR maps under several labels:
- Site IV (in Roman numerals)
- S-4
- Site 4 / 54 (possibly indicating "Area 54")

The Air Force 554th Range Squadron manages this part of TTR in support of the Tonopah Electronic Combat Range (TECR). Site 4 is in the eastern section of TTR designated the TEWR (Tonopah Electronic Warfare Range) HQ compound.

Some researchers have alleged that Bob Lazar fabricated Area 51 S4 by borrowing from Tonopah's Site 4. UAP researchers generally reject this theory:
- Lazar described S4 as lying near [[Papoose Lake]], with hangars built into mountainsides, which does not match the location or layout of Tonopah Site 4.
- Tonopah Site 4 appears on unclassified maps, whereas Lazar described S4 as a completely unacknowledged facility.
- The two sites are not mutually exclusive, and both could serve UAP-related functions.

Private sources have told UAP researchers that Tonopah Site 4 is "directly involved in UFO operations," though the details remain classified.

## Advanced Group Six and DARPA Exotic Propulsion Programs

In 2021, investigative journalist [[Richard Dolan]] reported information leaked by a source familiar with the classified UAP Task Force report presented to Congress. According to this source, highly classified programs building prototypes of exotic propulsion were under way at Tonopah and [[Area 51]]. They included:
- Energy pulse propulsion systems
- Ion propulsion systems
- Anti-gravity propulsion systems
- Antimatter propulsion systems
- Ramjet hydrogen propulsion systems
- Compressed nuclear propulsion systems
- Advanced uses of exotic elements for energy research

These programs were allegedly led by [[DARPA]] and managed by a classified group called "Advanced Group Six". No other reference to this group has been found. Funding sources included confidential black budget funds and private contractor IRAD (Independent Research and Development), the same funding mechanism David Grusch identified for UAP legacy programs.

According to Dolan's source: "Prototypes of flying crafts utilizing the above technology are being flown at Area 51 and Tonopah Air Force Base test range. However, none of the experimental crafts have flown outside the Nellis Test and Training Range."

If this is accurate, it suggests that:
- Tonopah is an active RDT&E site for reverse-engineered or human-adapted exotic propulsion.
- [[Lockheed Martin]], which managed Tonopah from 1993-2017, would have had direct oversight of such programs and possibly the authority to fund them through IRAD.
- Keeping the craft inside the Nevada Test and Training Range prevents civilian or foreign intelligence assets from observing them.

## Deep Underground Military Base (DUMB)

UAP researchers theorize that Tonopah contains deep underground facilities for:
- Secure storage of recovered UAP craft (in the classified storage area, surrounded by double barbed wire, that is visible on maps)
- Material exploitation laboratories shielded from overhead reconnaissance
- Testing reverse-engineered propulsion systems in controlled underground environments

The range was founded by the Atomic Energy Commission (AEC) and has a dual DOE/DoD oversight structure. Together these would have given legal cover to classify underground facilities under the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]], the same mechanism alleged to "black hole" UAP materials as transclassified foreign nuclear information.

## Naval Involvement and Sand Dollar Connection

In its early years, the Navy's involvement at Tonopah went well beyond the Naval Air Special Weapons Facility's initial selection of the site:
- The Naval Special Weapons Facility conducted special weapons testing at TTR together with the AEC.
- This mission grew from equipping naval aircraft with nuclear weapons to submarine-launched ballistic missile systems, including Polaris, Poseidon and Trident.
- The Polaris submarine program was led by Admiral William F. Raborn, who later served on the boards of Wackenhut and SAIC, both companies implicated in UAP programs.
- Chief scientist [[John P. Craven]] stated that the 1964 Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP) was created to serve Project Sand Dollar, a clandestine program to retrieve "militarily sensitive hardware and items with national security importance" from the seafloor.
- UAP researchers have directly associated Sand Dollar with maritime UFO crash retrieval operations.
- The DSSP's Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicles (DSRVs), Mystic and Avalon, were created by Lockheed.

Naval operations at Tonopah ended in 1993, the same year Lockheed Martin's Sandia subsidiary took over management of the range. The timing raises the question of whether Lockheed's takeover brought previously separate Navy and Air Force UAP programs under the management of a single contractor.

## F-117 Nighthawk and Acknowledged Black Programs

Tonopah's role as a test site for the F-117 Nighthawk stealth fighter is publicly acknowledged. The F-117, designed by Lockheed Skunk Works, operated in secret at Tonopah for nearly a decade before it was disclosed to the public. This shows that TTR can hide revolutionary aerospace technology from the public and from foreign intelligence, a capability that would apply to even more exotic craft.

## 2007 Lockheed Radio Telemetry to Palmdale Skunk Works

Beginning in 2007, Lockheed radio telemetry data was detected being transmitted from TTR coordinates 7 miles west of the south end of the runway to 1011 Lockheed Way, Building 601, Palmdale, California. That is the address of Lockheed Martin Aeronautics Corporation in the Antelope Valley, home to [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works]].

This direct telemetry link between Tonopah test operations and Skunk Works headquarters suggests that:
- Data from TTR flight tests was relayed in real time to engineers in Palmdale.
- Whatever was being tested needed engineering oversight at the Skunk Works level.
- The specific coordinates (7 miles west of the south end of the runway) may mark a particular test pad or facility.

## Relationship to Other Nevada Sites

Tonopah is one part of the wider Nevada Test and Training Range network, which also includes:
- [[Area 51]] Groom Lake, an acknowledged UAP testing site according to multiple witnesses
- Area 51 S4 (alleged, near Papoose Lake), the facility where Bob Lazar claimed UAP craft were examined
- Nellis Air Force Base, the primary NTTR command center
- Nevada National Security Site (formerly the Nevada Test Site), the DOE's nuclear weapons testing site

Private sources describe Tonopah as a place where "quite a bit of the interesting stuff is stored". The phrasing suggests recovered UAP craft or materials rather than conventional weapons systems.
