A United States Air Force installation in Ward County, North Dakota. It is a nuclear-capable Strategic Air Command base and home to B-52 Stratofortress bombers and Minuteman III intercontinental ballistic missiles. In UAP research, Minot AFB is known for the events of 24 October 1968, when missile-site personnel on the ground and the crew of a B-52 saw an unidentified light or object, the B-52's radar tracked it, and the bomber's UHF radio briefly failed. Gerb, relaying [[Kevin Knuth]], calls it a "multisensor military detection of a UFO at a nuclear military base".

## The 1968 Minot AFB Incident

In the early hours of 24 October 1968, personnel in the Minuteman missile complex around Minot reported one, and at times two, bright lights; one maintenance man described a light that followed his truck to a missile site and hovered there. A ground controller asked the crew of a B-52 on approach to look for orange glows, and the navigator watched an object on radar pace the bomber. Both UHF radios failed to transmit during the close radar encounter, and the radarscope was filmed. Retired Air Force captain [[Kevin Randle]] adds ground and weather radar returns.

In Gerb's Knuth video, base personnel describe an egg-shaped object with skin like molten lava that held about 3 miles from the descending B-52, then closed 2 miles within a single 3-second radar sweep, which Knuth works out at 209 G and Mach 12 (about 9,200 mph). Gerb says the case was reported under [[USAF Regulation 200-2]] and never reached the [[Project Blue Book]] files. In fact AFR 80-17 had replaced 200-2 in 1966, and Blue Book did receive the case: the base investigating officer, Lt. Col. Arthur Werlich, phoned it in the same day, and Lt. Col. Hector Quintanilla submitted a final report on 13 November 1968. He put the ground sightings down to the star Sirius and the B-52, the radar contact and radio loss to a plasma like ball lightning, and the airborne sighting to Vega, a ground light or plasma, and planned no further investigation.

The Minot incident fits a documented pattern of UAP encounters at nuclear weapons storage and delivery sites throughout the Cold War. Similar events occurred at [[Malmstrom Air Force Base]] and other ICBM installations, where UAP were observed hovering over or near missile launch facilities and sometimes temporarily disrupted missile readiness systems.

## Strategic Significance

Because Minot AFB is both a strategic bomber base and an ICBM installation, UAP encounters there are particularly significant. UAP have been reported at nuclear-capable facilities again and again, and this has led researchers to theorize that non-human intelligence showed a deliberate interest in humanity's nuclear weapons programs.
