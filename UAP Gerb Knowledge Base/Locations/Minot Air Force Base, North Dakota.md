A United States Air Force installation in Ward County, North Dakota. It is a nuclear-capable Strategic Air Command base and home to B-52 Stratofortress bombers and Minuteman III intercontinental ballistic missiles. In UAP research, Minot AFB is known for a 1968 incident in which several independent sensors detected an egg-shaped UFO at the same time: airborne radar, ground radar, visual observation and a ground tracking station. That makes it one of the UAP encounters on record corroborated by the most kinds of sensor.

## The 1968 Minot AFB Incident

In 1968, several detection systems operating in parallel picked up an egg-shaped or disc-shaped UFO near Minot Air Force Base. B-52 crew members in flight saw the object, airborne radar tracked it, ground-based radar detected it, and personnel at a ground tracking station observed it independently. Because this independent sensor data came from a nuclear-capable installation, the case carried significant weight in Air Force UFO investigation records.

The Minot incident fits a documented pattern of UAP encounters at nuclear weapons storage and delivery sites throughout the Cold War. Similar events occurred at [[Malmstrom Air Force Base]] and other ICBM installations, where UAP were observed hovering over or near missile launch facilities and sometimes temporarily disrupted missile readiness systems.

## Strategic Significance

Because Minot AFB is both a strategic bomber base and an ICBM installation, UAP encounters there are particularly significant. UAP have been reported at nuclear-capable facilities again and again, and this has led researchers to theorize that non-human intelligence showed a deliberate interest in humanity's nuclear weapons programs.
