Salzburg is the fourth-largest city in Austria. It lies in the west of the country, on the northern edge of the Alps. In this wiki it appears as the starting point of [[TAE (Trabajos Aéreos y Enlaces)|TAE]] flight JK 297, the Spanish commercial aircraft at the center of the [[Manises UFO Incident]] of November 11, 1979. That incident is considered the first recorded case in aviation history of a commercial flight being grounded because of a UFO encounter.

## Connection to the Manises UFO Incident

On November 11, 1979, TAE flight JK 297 left Salzburg for [[Las Palmas, Spain]], with a scheduled refueling stop at [[Mallorca, Spain]]. The aircraft was a Super Caravelle carrying 109 passengers. Its crew was Captain [[Francisco Javier Lerdo de Tejada]], co-pilot [[José Ramón Zuazu]] and flight mechanic [[Francisco Javier Rodriguez]].

After leaving Mallorca, the plane resumed its southbound route over the western Mediterranean. At approximately 23:05 hours, while it was cruising at 23,000 feet, flight mechanic Rodriguez warned the crew of two powerful red lights ahead and to the left of the aircraft. Captain Lerdo de Tejada radioed [[Barcelona Air Traffic Control]], which confirmed that no other aircraft were operating on or near the flight path. As the lights drew closer, the crew could make out two powerful sources of light with no visible solid body attached. When the captain changed altitude to avoid a possible collision, the objects matched the new trajectory and stayed between half a mile and 5 miles from the aircraft.

With no safe way to evade the objects, Captain Lerdo de Tejada abandoned the flight path and made an emergency landing at [[Manises Airport, Valencia, Spain|Manises Airport]] in Valencia. It was the first time in aviation history that a commercial flight was grounded because of a UFO encounter. Just before landing, the crew detected three more UFO radar signatures, each estimated at approximately 200 meters in diameter. Airport personnel and Marines stationed at the nearby [[Manises Air Force Base, Spain|Manises Air Force Base]] independently witnessed the anomalous lights.

## The Military Escalation

The emergency landing set off a response from the Spanish Air Force. At about 00:40 on 12 November, Captain [[Fernando Cámara]] was scrambled from [[Los Llanos Airbase, Spain|Los Llanos Airbase]] in a Mirage F1 fighter jet to intercept the objects. Cámara had to accelerate to Mach 1.4 (1,074 mph) to make visual contact with a craft shaped like a truncated cone, which displayed bright, changing colors. He tried to close in and lock onto the object with an infrared missile, but his avionics were completely jammed, and his onboard systems warned that he was being targeted by continuous wave missile radar. The UFO then accelerated toward mainland Africa. Cámara returned to base with dangerously low fuel after nearly 90 minutes of pursuit.

The incident reached the [[Spanish Parliament]] in September 1980, where it was officially dismissed as "a series of freak optical illusions". Cámara publicly rejected that conclusion. In particular, he disputed the claim that electronic interference from the nearby [[US 6th Fleet]] could account for what he witnessed and experienced.

## Significance in Aviation UAP Research

The route from Salzburg to Las Palmas determined flight JK 297's position and heading over the western Mediterranean at the time of the encounter. Gerb calls the [[Manises UFO Incident]] a benchmark case because a single incident combines testimony from several civilian witnesses, independent corroboration from military ground personnel, visual contact by a military pilot and documented effects of electronic countermeasures. The incident is also cited alongside the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]] as evidence of a recurring pattern: anomalous craft showing electronic warfare capabilities across multiple independent military encounters. In the Tehran case, Major [[Parviz Jafari]] of the Imperial Iranian Air Force likewise suffered total avionics failure as he approached a UAP in an F-4 Phantom 2.
