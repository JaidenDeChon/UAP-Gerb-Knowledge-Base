**Coyame** is a small town in the state of Chihuahua in northern Mexico, in barren, sparsely populated desert southwest of the border crossing at Presidio, Texas. It gives its name to the [[1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval]], one of the most extensively documented alleged UAP crash retrieval cases outside the United States.

## The 1974 Incident

On August 25, 1974, an unidentified object tracked by US Air Defense radar at 2,200 knots and 75,000 feet altitude disappeared from radar screens near Coyame after apparently colliding mid-air with a civilian aircraft. According to the [[Denb Report]], Mexican military forces located both crash sites in the barren region near the town but died under mysterious circumstances before they could return with the recovered disc. A [[CIA]]-coordinated recovery team staged at [[Fort Bliss]], Texas, then covertly entered Mexican airspace, retrieved a 16-foot silver disc from the stopped Mexican convoy, destroyed the site and bodies with high explosives, and transported the object to Atlanta, Georgia.

The area is remote. It lies over 500 miles from the point over the Gulf of Mexico where the object was first detected, and about 60 miles from the nearest populated area, which made it an ideal place for a covert cross-border recovery. The Denb Report notes the crash site was "over a barren and sparsely populated area of northern Mexico," which let the US recovery team operate without civilian witnesses.

## Field Investigation

UAP researchers [[Noe Torres]] and [[Ruben Uriarte]] conducted field investigations in the Coyame region and reported finding alleged aircraft debris near the supposed crash site. In 2012, at a festival in nearby Presidio, Texas, witnesses including school teacher Johnny Chambers reported seeing a strange fireball (a "big explosion in the sky") on the Mexican side of the border near the Coyame area in summer 1974. They said military helicopters returned to the US from Mexico within a day.

## Geographic Context

Coyame lies near the US-Mexico border and close to [[Fort Bliss]], a major US Army installation in El Paso, Texas, which put it within the operational range of rapid response teams. The crash is among the retrieval cases the [[UAP Caucus]] listed for Congress's attention.
