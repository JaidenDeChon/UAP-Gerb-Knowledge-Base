San Francisco is a major coastal city in northern California and the home of the *San Francisco Chronicle*. In this wiki it matters as the base of journalist [[J. P. Cahn]] and as the place [[Silas Newton]] traveled to meet him. That meeting became a turning point in the suppression of public discussion about the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery]].

## J. P. Cahn and the San Francisco Chronicle

[[J. P. Cahn]] was a journalist at the *San Francisco Chronicle* whose 1952 exposé effectively labeled the Aztec crash story a hoax by publicly discrediting [[Frank Scully]], [[Silas Newton]], and the anonymous scientist Newton had referred to as "Dr. G." In [[Videos/The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval/summary|The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval]], Gerb does not treat the article as genuine investigative journalism that resolved the Aztec case. He treats it as a possible instrument of deliberate disinformation, or at minimum of targeted character destruction, designed to suppress a legitimate crash retrieval account.

Newton traveled to San Francisco to meet Cahn, and the meeting proved consequential. Cahn reportedly stole an artifact Newton showed him and had it independently tested; it was found to be common aluminum, and Cahn then used the result to discredit Newton's claims. Gerb notes that the items Newton had with him at this meeting were likely replicas, not the original recovered materials from Dr. G's collection. In his view, Cahn's test of a substitute object proved nothing about whether the actual Aztec crash materials were authentic.

In 1949 Cahn had tried to buy Scully's flying saucer story but failed to reach terms. Gerb points to this earlier commercial interest as context for judging whether Cahn's 1952 exposé came from disinterested investigative journalism or from a more adversarial motive. The *San Francisco Chronicle* article that came out of Cahn's investigation became one of the main sources through which the public record labeled the Aztec case a fraud. Gerb argues that this verdict was premature at best and deliberately misleading at worst.

## Significance to the Aztec Disinformation Theory

As the home of the *Chronicle* and the site of Newton's meeting with Cahn, San Francisco is at the center of the disinformation hypothesis Gerb puts forward about the Aztec case. Under this theory, the military may have known that Newton and GeBauer were circulating the Aztec story and may have told Newton to spread it widely, as a Cold War-era strategy for managing information. The fraud prosecution of Newton and GeBauer that followed Cahn's exposé would then not be a legal resolution of genuine wrongdoing. It would be a way to discredit the two most visible sources for the Aztec case and to close off further public investigation under the cover of a criminal finding.
