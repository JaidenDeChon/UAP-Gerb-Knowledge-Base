Campeche is a state on the southwestern Yucatan Peninsula of Mexico, bordering the Gulf of Mexico. In UAP research it is known as the area where the Mexican Air Force used infrared equipment to film eleven unidentified aerial objects in March 2004, during a routine anti-drug trafficking patrol. The incident was one of the most internationally covered military UAP cases of the early 2000s.

## The 2004 Mexican Air Force Incident

On March 5, 2004, a Mexican Air Force Merlin C26A patrol aircraft equipped with an infrared FLIR camera was conducting a narcotics surveillance mission over the state of Campeche when its crew filmed eleven separate luminous objects. Three of the eleven objects also showed up on the aircraft's radar at the same time. The rest were visible on infrared but not on radar. Gerb notes that this combination of sensor readings does not fit a simple flare or balloon explanation.

Pilot [[Magdaleno Castanon]] stated that military jets had been scrambled to pursue the objects and that the objects appeared to react to being followed, which he took as a sign that they were aware of the pursuit. The Mexican Department of Defense later released the footage to the public, making this one of the few cases in which a national military has officially disclosed infrared UAP footage.

## Skeptical Assessment

Gerb notes that skeptic [[Michael Shermer]] proposed that the objects were burnoff flares from offshore oil platforms in the Gulf of Mexico. Gerb acknowledges that this explanation has not been conclusively ruled out, and he rates the Mexico incident as more likely to have a prosaic explanation than the other cases in the same video. Those are the 1969 Finnish Pori Airport incident and the 1980 Peruvian La Joya Airbase intercept, both of which Gerb considers more likely to be genuine UAP encounters.

The detail that most complicates the flare hypothesis is that three objects appeared on radar while eight did not. Offshore platform flares are fixed heat sources, so they would be expected to behave the same way on every type of sensor.
