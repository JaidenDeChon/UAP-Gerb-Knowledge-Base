The Lyndon B. Johnson Space Center (JSC) is NASA's centre for human spaceflight in Houston, Texas, home to mission control and astronaut training. Founded in 1961 as the Manned Spacecraft Center, it was renamed for President Lyndon B. Johnson in 1973. In UAP research it is best known for former NASA photographic specialist [[Donna Hare]]'s allegation that its Building 8 held a laboratory where unidentified objects were airbrushed out of high-resolution imagery before release.

## Building 8 and the McKinnon hack

According to UAP Gerb's "The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 2)", Scottish hacker [[Gary McKinnon]] acted on Hare's claim during his 2001–2002 intrusion into US military and NASA systems. He says he reached machines in Building 8 and found folders titled "raw" and "processed" holding images of around 250 megabytes. In the "raw" folder he began downloading one that appeared to show a metallic white, tubular craft with domes around its centre and at its ends, and no rivets or seams, set against a blue-and-white planet. His connection could manage only about a megabyte every five minutes; before the image finished, he says, his cursor began to move on its own and he was cut off. Gerb finds some credibility in the account, and links it to [[Chris Mellon]]'s comments that 4K satellite footage of UAP exists but is classified or tampered with.

## Other connections

Orbital-debris scientist [[Nicholas Johnson]], who worked at JSC as NASA's Chief Scientist for Orbital Debris, features in the Kecksburg case, and space journalist [[James Oberg]] worked in the centre's mission control for more than two decades.
