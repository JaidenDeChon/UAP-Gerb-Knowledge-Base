The Cheyenne Mountain Complex is a deep underground military installation near Colorado Springs, Colorado, built into and beneath Cheyenne Mountain at a depth of about 2,000 feet. The US Army Corps of Engineers directed its construction during the Cold War. It is the main acknowledged example of a Deep Underground Military Base (DUMB) in the United States, and it houses the air defense operations center of the North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD).

## Physical Description

The complex covers 5.1 acres. Its six tunnels, each three stories high and 40 feet wide, hold 15 three-story buildings made of steel plates, all set on spring mounts to absorb the shock of a nearby nuclear detonation. The facility is designed to sustain itself and is hardened against blasts so that it can survive a near miss by a nuclear strike.

## Construction Context

The Cheyenne Mountain facility was built in the 1960s. In a 1989 speech, Army Corps of Engineers Deputy Director [[Lloyd A. Duscha]] mentioned the Corps' part in building Cheyenne and also acknowledged "other projects of similar scope which I cannot identify". The remark confirmed that several classified underground installations of similar size exist but remain secret. It is cited as evidence that the Cheyenne Mountain facility is not the full extent of US deep underground military construction.
