Ent Air Force Base was a United States Air Force installation in Colorado Springs, Colorado. From 1948 to 1975 it was the headquarters of the Air Defense Command (ADC), the command responsible for defending North American airspace. In research into UAP legacy programs, the base matters because Harry B. Cooper was posted there between 1957 and 1963. A commendation signed by General [[Curtis LeMay]] records his service there running printing management and the camera section of Air Defense Command's publishing division. Researcher [[Ryan S. Wood]] describes it as a commendation for work on the "USAF UFO program" and believes Cooper was looking at photographs of UFOs and printing classified documents; the commendation itself does not mention UFOs.

> Earlier versions said Cooper is documented as photographing UFOs and printing classified documents as part of the USAF UFO program. That is Ryan S. Wood's reading; the LeMay commendation describes printing-management and camera-section work and says nothing about UFOs.

## Harry B. Cooper and the LeMay Commendation

The base's place in UAP research rests on a signed commendation from General Curtis LeMay to Harry B. Cooper. As read out in [[Videos/The Majestic-12 Documents With Ryan S. Wood/summary|The Majestic-12 Documents With Ryan S. Wood]], it says Master Sergeant Cooper distinguished himself by meritorious service as the NCO in charge of the printing management branch and the NCO in charge of the camera section, publishing division, command directorate of administrative services, Headquarters Air Defense Command, Ent Air Force Base, from 30 September 1957 to 1 June 1963. Wood calls it a commendation "for his outstanding work at the USAF UFO program" and takes it to mean Cooper was looking at photographs of UFOs and printing classified documents. The commendation's own wording describes print-shop and camera work at Air Defense Command headquarters and does not mention UFOs.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions quoted the commendation as praising "outstanding work as NCO in charge of camera section, publishing division" and said it confirmed Cooper photographed UFOs. "Outstanding work" and the UFO program are Wood's words; the citation describes meritorious service in printing management and the camera section.

Harry B. Cooper also matters because he is the father of Timothy Cooper, who later received the largest known cache of alleged [[Majestic 12]] documents. The material reached Timothy Cooper by post from Sacramento, California, as items put into his mailbox by hand without postage, and by post from the Fort Meade FOIA office. Researcher Ryan S. Wood identifies the LeMay commendation to Harry Cooper as strong circumstantial evidence linking the elder Cooper to the institutional infrastructure through which Majestic documents might plausibly have originated.

## Air Defense Command Headquarters

As the headquarters of Air Defense Command from 1948 onward, Ent AFB was at the center of U.S. military monitoring of, and response to, unidentified aerial incursions during the most active documented period of UAP sightings over U.S. territory. Because the ADC's mission was to track and intercept unknown aerial objects, it falls directly among the institutions that Gerb identifies as having had routine contact with UAP phenomena as part of their work, and therefore as likely nodes in the classification and compartmentation of UAP information during the 1950s and 1960s.

Wood treats Cooper's printing and camera work at ADC headquarters, and the LeMay commendation for it, as evidence of a UAP-connected function at Ent Air Force Base. The commendation documents the job, not a UFO connection; that link rests on Wood's interpretation.
