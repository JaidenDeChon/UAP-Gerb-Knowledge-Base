The Persian Gulf USO Flap is a series of three documented [[Maritime Light Wheel]] encounters reported in the Persian Gulf between 1879 and 1901. They make the region the densest cluster of light wheel observations in the historical record. All three cases involved multiple credible witnesses aboard naval or commercial vessels, and two of the three were formally reported to the British Admiralty or equivalent hydrographic authorities. Together the cases are some of the best-documented instances of the maritime light wheel phenomenon.

## Encounter 1: HMS Vulture (May 15, 1879)

On May 15, 1879, JE Pringle of the HMS Vulture reported the encounter to the British Admiralty through the ship's hydrographer, Captain Evans. Pringle described "luminous waves or pulsations in the water moving at great speed" that passed beneath the ship. Pringle also observed two objects to the east and west of the vessel, which he described as revolving wheels with illuminated spokes:

- Spoke length: approximately 25 feet
- The two wheels were rotating in equal and opposite directions
- The 19th-century English phrasing leaves it unclear whether the wheels were fully submerged, but the initial pulsations were clearly beneath the surface

Analysts consider the counter-rotation, with two wheels spinning in opposite directions at the same time, inconsistent with any natural optical phenomenon driven by a single source of bioluminescent disturbance. If the same pressure wave drove both wheels, they would be expected to rotate in the same direction.

## Encounter 2: SS Patna (May 1880)

The British India Company steamer Patna reported an encounter approximately one year after the Vulture case. The captain, a Mr. Manning, a third officer, and others observed two enormous luminous wheels appear simultaneously on each side of the ship at approximately 11:30 p.m. A witness described the scene:

> "The wheels were whirling around; the spokes of which seemed to brush along the ship. The spokes would be 200 to 300 yards long and resembled the birch rods of the dame school. Each wheel contained about 16 spokes and although the wheels must have been some 5 to 600 yards in diameter, the spokes could be distinctly seen all the way around."

The men said the spokes looked like the beam of a bull's-eye lantern flashed horizontally along the water surface by someone standing in a boat. Two wheels appeared at once on opposite sides of the ship, with spokes long enough to seem to brush the vessel. That suggests either an extremely large structure or an optical interaction with the ship's position.

## Encounter 3: SS Kila (April 4, 1901)

Captain Hosan of the steamship Kila reported the final Persian Gulf light wheel encounter:

> "Vast shafts of light suddenly appeared. Shaft followed shaft upon the surface of the sea, but it was only a faint light, and in about 15 minutes died out, having appeared suddenly and having died out gradually."

The shafts revolved at an estimated speed of approximately 60 mph. Their sudden appearance and gradual fading set this case apart from the Patna and Vulture encounters, whose witnesses described more stable, ongoing wheel structures.

## Significance

These encounters, clustered in the Persian Gulf over a 22-year period, are the most documented geographic concentration of maritime light wheel phenomena. Between them, the formal report to the British Admiralty in the Vulture case, the detailed description from several witnesses in the Patna case and the estimated rotational speed in the Kila case give a more complete observational record than most individual USO events from the same era. The pair of counter-rotating wheels Pringle observed in 1879 is considered particularly significant, because a natural bioluminescent explanation would struggle to account for it.
