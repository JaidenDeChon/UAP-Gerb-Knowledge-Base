The Maury Island Incident was a reported UFO encounter on June 27, 1947, in which [[Harold Dahl]], a harbor patrolman and conservation worker on [[Maury Island, Washington]], claimed to have seen six large donut-shaped craft hovering above his boat in Puget Sound. One of the craft discharged debris that killed his dog and injured his son. The incident matters mainly as the founding narrative of the [[Men in Black]] phenomenon. The following morning an unknown man visited Dahl, showed inexplicable foreknowledge of the encounter and warned him to remain silent.

## The Encounter

At approximately 2:00 p.m. on June 27, 1947, Dahl was near the eastern shore of Maury Island when he observed six objects hovering approximately half a mile above the water. They were described as donut-shaped, roughly 100 feet in diameter, with a dark outer ring and a metallic appearance. One craft appeared to be in distress, and the others gathered around it. The distressed craft discharged two types of material onto Dahl's boat: a white foam-like substance and heavier dark metallic fragments. One piece struck and killed his dog; another burned his son's arm. Dahl claimed to have photographed the craft and retained debris samples.

## The Man in Black Visit

The following morning, June 28, 1947, Dahl was approached at his home by an unknown man in a black suit driving a black 1947 Buick. The man took him to breakfast at a local diner and, without any prompting, accurately recounted the details of the previous day's encounter. He then said:

> "What I have said to you is proof to you that I know a great deal more about this experience of yours than you will want to believe."

He warned Dahl that speaking publicly would bring negative consequences. This exchange is universally cited as the founding incident of the [[Men in Black]] archetype in UFO lore.

## The B-25 Crash

Dahl's supervisor Fred Crisman contacted publisher Raymond Palmer, who alerted Army Air Forces investigators. Captains Davidson and Brown flew from Hamilton Field to retrieve debris. On their return flight, their B-25 caught fire and crashed near Kelso, Washington, killing both officers. Two sergeants aboard parachuted to safety. Because the plane was carrying the alleged debris, the crash gave the case a conspiratorial angle that has persisted in UFO research.

## Credibility and Legacy

The incident remains highly contested. Some Air Force investigators suspected fabrication. Even so, the Men in Black story, in which a suited stranger with impossible foreknowledge silences a witness, originates here. It became one of the most persistent and culturally influential elements of UFO folklore.
