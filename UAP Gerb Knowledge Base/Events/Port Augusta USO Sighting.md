The Port Augusta USO Sighting took place in 1947 near [[Port Augusta, South Australia]]. Two separate groups of witnesses independently saw multiple oblong or egg-shaped objects rise from the sea and move from northwest to southeast. The case stands out because the independent accounts corroborate each other, and because the witnesses described a quivering motion as the objects emerged. That detail links it to the wobbling motion noted in the [[MV Marala North Atlantic Sighting]] and other documented [[Unidentified Submerged Object (USO)]] encounters.

## Incident Description

Two independent sets of witnesses saw the same or similar events.

In the first group, Frederick Walter and Emma Flav watched five gray oblong objects rise from the sea and move from northwest to southeast.

In the second, two railroad workers independently reported five white or light pink egg-shaped objects rising from the sea and moving in the same direction. They added that the objects quivered as they emerged.

The two accounts agree on the count of five objects, the direction of travel and the location. That agreement suggests a genuine simultaneous event rather than parallel misidentifications.

## Significance

Gerb points to the quivering or gyrating motion during emergence as a recurring characteristic. The MV Marala case (1950) described a rotary and wobbling motion, and the SS Morgantown Victory case (1966) described the object as having a gyrating quality. Similar descriptions appear in Australian reports through the 1960s. Because the same motion turns up in independent cases separated by time and geography, it is treated as a potential physical signature of the transmedium emergence process.
