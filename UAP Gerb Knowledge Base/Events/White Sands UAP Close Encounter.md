The **White Sands UAP Close Encounter** is an alleged face-to-face meeting between retired US Army Lieutenant Colonel [[Philip J. Corso]] and a non-human being at [[White Sands Missile Range]], New Mexico, in 1957 or 1958. Corso said that the being asked him telepathically to take down the radar that was keeping its craft on the ground, and that he did so. He described the encounter near the end of his manuscript *Dawn of a New Age* and told it privately to several researchers.

## The account

According to the manuscript, Corso was then commanding the ballistic missile firing range at Red Canyon, part of White Sands. Driving alone through an isolated area, he took shelter from the heat in an abandoned mine and there met the being, which he later drew. It communicated telepathically: it wanted to leave the area, but active radar from nearby installations was stopping its craft from departing. Corso asked what he would get in return for lowering the radar coverage, and the being answered, "A new world if you can take it." Back at his installation, Corso said, he had the radar shut down, and the craft left. He reportedly thought about the being's words for the rest of his life.

Corso and some Roswell researchers held a wider theory that radar interfered with the navigation of such craft and could cause crashes.

## Retellings

Corso told the story to [[John B. Alexander]], [[Jacques Vallee]] and [[George Knapp]], and a drawing of the being appears in an old interview shown on Knapp's *Weaponized*. He also recorded an 8mm home movie for his grandchildren, meant to be shown only after his death. Attorney [[Daniel Sheehan]], who said he was allowed to see it, called the film "extraordinarily persuasive". In Sheehan's retelling, the encounter took place in July 1945 near the Alamogordo atomic test site, during a lunch break, when Corso was a security officer. The manuscript instead places it at Red Canyon in 1957-58, and UAP Gerb treats Sheehan's date as a mistake.

## Place in the Corso testimony

UAP Gerb calls this the part of Corso's account that troubles him most. Corso's 1998 affidavit does not mention it, and in the manuscript it appears only about 120 to 140 pages in. Gerb first read it as a dream or a metaphor, a lesson for Corso's grandchildren, until he learned how many people Corso had told it to as fact. He also notes that the being is described with a noticeably different physiology from the body Corso said he saw at [[Fort Riley, Kansas]] in 1947. Gerb sees it as an example of a common pattern in which witnesses add dramatic experiences long after their first disclosures.
