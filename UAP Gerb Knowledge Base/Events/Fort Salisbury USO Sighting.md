The Fort Salisbury USO Sighting occurred in 1902 in the Gulf of Guinea, Africa, when four men aboard the ship Fort Salisbury witnessed an immense cigar-shaped object sink into the ocean. The object was estimated at nearly 200 meters long and 300 meters wide, which would make it larger than any submarine ever built. As it went under, the witnesses saw distinct colored lights at both ends.

## Incident Description

Four witnesses aboard the Fort Salisbury watched the object descend into the ocean. It was cigar-shaped and about 200 meters long and 300 meters wide. It had two orange lights at the front end and two blue-green lights at the other end, and it passed under the ship as it sank below the surface. The sighting took place in the Gulf of Guinea, off the coast of Africa.

## Assessment

Gerb acknowledges that the size, location and era could point to a submarine, since the case occurred while submarine technology was developing rapidly. The reported dimensions, however, are assessed as anomalous. The early German U-boats of the time, the most advanced submarines of the period, measured about 41 meters in length, so the reported Fort Salisbury object was nearly five times longer. The colored lights, orange at one end and blue-green at the other, do not match any known submarine navigation or running light arrangement of that era.
