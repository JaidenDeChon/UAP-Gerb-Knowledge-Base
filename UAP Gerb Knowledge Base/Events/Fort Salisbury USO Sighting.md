The Fort Salisbury USO Sighting occurred in 1902 in the Gulf of Guinea, Africa, when four men aboard the ship Fort Salisbury witnessed an immense cigar-shaped object sink into the ocean. Gerb gives the object as nearly 200 meters long and 300 meters wide, which would make it larger than any submarine ever built. As it went under, the witnesses saw distinct colored lights at both ends.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions stated the 200-by-300-meter size in the page's own voice. The figures are Gerb's, and as given they make the object wider than it is long, which sits oddly with a cigar shape; no second source was found to settle the width. One UFO-data compilation (the RR0 1902 chronology) instead quotes a crew member estimating the length at 150 to 180 meters.

## Incident Description

Four witnesses aboard the Fort Salisbury watched the object descend into the ocean. It was cigar-shaped; Gerb gives its size as about 200 meters long and 300 meters wide. It had two orange lights at the front end and two blue-green lights at the other end, and it passed under the ship as it sank below the surface. The sighting took place in the Gulf of Guinea, off the coast of Africa.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions stated the size in the page's own voice. It is now credited to Gerb, since the width he gives is larger than the length and no second source confirms either figure.

## Assessment

Gerb acknowledges that the size, location and era could point to a submarine, since the case occurred while submarine technology was developing rapidly. The reported dimensions, however, are assessed as anomalous. The early German U-boats of the time, the most advanced submarines of the period, measured about 41 meters in length, so the reported Fort Salisbury object was nearly five times longer. The colored lights, orange at one end and blue-green at the other, do not match any known submarine navigation or running light arrangement of that era.
