The 1947 Roswell Crash was an incident in the summer of 1947 near Roswell, New Mexico, in which the U.S. military reportedly recovered unidentified debris. According to many witnesses and researchers, it also recovered one or more craft and non-human occupants. The incident began when rancher W.W. "Mac" Brazel found unusual debris scattered across a large field on the J.B. Foster Ranch, about 75 miles north of Roswell. Brazel reported the find to local authorities, and personnel were sent to recover the material from [[Roswell Army Airfield|Roswell Army Air Field]] (RAAF), home of the 509th Bomb Wing, which was then the world's only nuclear-capable bombardment unit.

On July 8, 1947, RAAF public information officer Walter Haut issued a press release stating the Army had recovered a "flying disc." Within hours the military walked the announcement back, releasing a new statement that identified the debris as a conventional weather balloon. Decades later, the U.S. Air Force issued reports (1994, 1997) attributing the incident to debris from Project Mogul, a classified balloon-based program designed to detect Soviet nuclear tests. At the request of Representative [[Steven Schiff]], the General Accounting Office searched government records on the crash from March 1994 to June 1995. Its report of 28 July 1995, the [[GAO Roswell Report (1995)]], found only two 1947 records, both describing a radar-tracking balloon, and found that Roswell Army Air Field's outgoing messages from October 1946 through December 1949 had been destroyed with no record of who destroyed them or under what authority.

## Key Witnesses and Officials

Many military and civilian witnesses have maintained over the decades, against the official explanations, that the recovered material was anomalous and that non-human occupants were present. They include:

- Major Jesse Marcel, the RAAF intelligence officer who recovered the debris and later stated it was unlike any conventional material he had encountered
- [[Thomas J. DuBose|Brigadier General Thomas DuBose]], who stated he received orders from Washington to change the official story
- Multiple morticians and base medical staff who reportedly received inquiries about small, non-standard caskets and specialized preservation procedures

## Connection to the Nuclear Weapons Establishment

UAP researchers often point out how close the Roswell incident was to the center of the U.S. nuclear weapons complex. [[Roswell Army Airfield|Roswell Army Air Field]] was the home of the 509th Bomb Wing and lay about 150 air miles from [[Sandia National Laboratories]] at Sandia Base, the nation's leading nuclear weapons engineering facility. [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]], where the atomic bombs had been designed, was also part of the regional complex.

[[Leslie Groves]], who directed the [[Manhattan Project]] and had been appointed chief of the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] in February 1947, is alleged to have been on site as part of the crash response. Researchers argue that because he commanded the nuclear and advanced-weapons infrastructure of the American Southwest, Groves would have been a natural, or even required, point of contact for any anomalous recovery at or near RAAF.

The world's most sensitive nuclear programs were concentrated in the New Mexico–Tennessee–Nevada corridor, and the crash came just two years after the Trinity test and one year after the Atomic Energy Act of 1946 established civilian nuclear oversight. From this, researchers argue that the nuclear establishment was set up to absorb and classify any recovered anomalous materials under the Act's broad "Restricted Data" provisions, which would have placed them outside normal oversight from the start.
