The Voronezh UFO Incident is a reported extraterrestrial encounter that occurred on September 27, 1989, in a city park in Voronezh, Russia, which was then part of the Soviet Union. Multiple children and at least one adult, a police officer, were witnesses. The case drew media attention across the Soviet Union and prompted an official government scientific inquiry. It stands out for the witnesses' unusually specific descriptions of both a craft and a non-human entity, and for the physical traces reportedly found at the scene.

## The Incident

A group of children playing football in a public park in Voronezh saw a pink glowing object in the sky. The object turned into a deep red ball approximately 3 meters (10 feet) in diameter. The sphere circled the football pitch, vanished, and then reappeared, hovering at low altitude.

Witnesses reported that a tall humanoid entity, approximately 3 meters (10 feet) in height, emerged from the craft. It wore bronze-colored boots and had a disc-shaped object on its chest. Witnesses described the entity's head as abnormally flattened or "smushed" directly onto its shoulders with no visible neck. A robot-like figure was with it.

According to witness accounts, the entity produced a device that fired a beam at a 16-year-old boy, and the boy temporarily disappeared. He reappeared some time later. The entity and robot then returned to the craft, which departed.

A local police sergeant, Sergey Matveyev (name approximated), separately reported observing a "flying body in the sky" around the same time as the children's encounter.

## Official Response

The Soviet Scientific Commission ordered an official inquiry into the incident. Investigators examined the alleged landing site but found no concrete physical evidence of a craft landing. Radioactive cesium was detected in the area, though the quantity was assessed as insufficient to confirm a craft landing. Microorganism sampling and spectrochemical measurements revealed nothing abnormal.

Soviet media covered the story extensively. Observers have noted that the incident occurred during Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev's glasnost reforms, which loosened press restrictions. Some have suggested this helped make the coverage sensational, which may have amplified unverified details.

## Assessment

The evidence in the case remains ambiguous. The children's accounts were consistent with one another, an adult police witness independently corroborated an aerial anomaly, and the Soviet government found the incident serious enough to warrant scientific investigation. However, no physical evidence survived to conclusively establish the presence of a craft or entity.
