The Sol Foundation 2023 presentation by Kevin Knuth was physicist [[Kevin Knuth]]'s talk on the physics of UAP at the inaugural symposium of the [[Sol Foundation]], held at Stanford University in November 2023. The other speakers included [[Karl Nell]], [[David Grusch]] and [[Gary Nolan]]. Building on his 2019 paper with [[Robert M. Powell]] and [[Peter Reali]], "Estimating Flight Characteristics of Anomalous Unidentified Aerial Vehicles", Knuth put numbers to the accelerations, speeds, power, light output and fields reported in well-documented cases, and argued that together they amount to a coherent physical signature. Gerb broke the talk down in his video "The Physics of UFOs– Dr. Kevin Knuth".

## Content

As Gerb relays it, the talk ran as follows.

- Knuth opened with caution. He said he is skeptical of people who assume they have seen an alien spacecraft, but also finds it surprising that academia claims to "know our physics" when general relativity and quantum mechanics remain un-unified. Unfamiliar engineering, he said, can look a lot like anomalous physics.
- He presented UAP as a class of phenomena through seven photographs, including the 1958 Trindade Island disc ([[1958 Trindade Island UFO Photographs]]), the 1971 Lake Cote photograph from Costa Rica, and images from Weyauwega (2003) and [[Green Bay, Wisconsin|Green Bay]] (2007), Wisconsin.
- Using the [[2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter (Tic Tac)]], he measured two of the [[Five UAP Characteristics (AATIP)|five observables]] associated with [[Luis Elizondo]]: instantaneous acceleration, and hypersonic velocity without signatures. For a drop from 28,000 feet to sea level in about 78 seconds he found a lower bound of about 5,000 G (Gerb gives 5,400), and about 1,100 gigawatts of power for an assumed 1,000 kg craft.
- He cited [[Hermann Oberth]]'s 1954 lecture, which reported more than 50 radar measurements of flying saucers at about 19 km/s (42,000 mph).
- Other cases he reviewed were the 1968 B-52 encounter at [[Minot Air Force Base, North Dakota|Minot Air Force Base]] (209 G, Mach 12) and the [[1986 Japan Airlines UFO Incident]] (about 269,000 mph). He also raised the missing energy release when a craft stops: the Tic Tac, Knuth said, should have produced an explosion equal to about 250 Tomahawk cruise missiles.
- His estimates of light output and fields were 2,000 to 30,000 megacandelas, from a 1953 Canadian Air Force photograph analysed by Bruce Maccabee and reported by [[Jacques Vallee]]; electric fields of about 3 × 10⁶ V/m to stop car engines; and magnetic fields producing a Faraday effect that polarizing filters can photograph. The [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]] was the example of avionics failing near a craft.
- He closed with transmedium objects, which move between air and water: the [[Aguadilla Puerto Rico Trans-Medium UAP Incident|Aguadilla footage]], the [[1987 HMNZS Southland USO Incident]], and a list of eight early cases.
