The 1958 Trindade Island UFO photographs are a sequence of images of a Saturn-shaped disc taken on 16 January 1958 by Brazilian photographer [[Almiro Baraúna]] from the deck of the Brazilian Navy ship *Almirante Saldanha*, anchored off Trindade Island in the South Atlantic, about 600 miles off the Brazilian mainland. With some 48 people reportedly on deck to see the object, the case is one of the best-known multi-witness photographic sightings of the 1950s, and physicist [[Kevin Knuth]] included it among the seven images that open his 2023 [[Sol Foundation 2023 Presentation by Kevin Knuth|Sol Foundation talk]] on UAP physics.

## The sighting

According to the account UAP Gerb summarizes, Baraúna and many of the other people on deck, including Lieutenant Homero, saw a gleaming object fly toward the island and pass behind Desejado Peak. Seconds later it reversed direction and came back closer, lower and noticeably faster. Baraúna photographed it as it went. The object was described as gray, metallic and solid-looking, surrounded by a greenish haze or mist, with a ring running through its midsection, like a flattened version of the planet Saturn.

Brazilian president Juscelino Kubitschek released the photographs to the public in February 1958.

## In UAP Gerb's coverage

In his breakdown of Knuth's talk, Gerb describes the Trindade images as depicting a classic flying disc with sharp edges, not unlike the Calvine photograph, and presents them alongside the 1971 Lake Cote photograph from Costa Rica and the Wisconsin photographs of 2003 and 2007 as the deliberately chosen, well-documented images Knuth used to introduce UAP as a class of phenomena.
