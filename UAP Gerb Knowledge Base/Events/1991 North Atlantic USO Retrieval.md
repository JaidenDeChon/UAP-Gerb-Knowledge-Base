The 1991 North Atlantic USO Retrieval is an alleged US Navy recovery of a triangular, apparently non-human craft from the seafloor, at just under a mile and a half deep, about 250 nautical miles from [[Aberdeen, Scotland]], in the early summer of 1991. It is known from a single witness, a retired Marine Corps lieutenant colonel identified only as Mark. He described it on Art Bell's *Coast to Coast AM* radio show on 19 April 2002, saying he took part as a member of a [[Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicle (DSRV)|Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicle]] crew.

## The witness

Mark said he served at Camp Pendleton with the 1st Air Delivery unit and then at Marine Corps Air Station Futenma, Okinawa, where he did weekend rescue dives with a Navy crew. Through a friendship with a Navy lieutenant who later commanded a DSRV unit, he joined that unit in Virginia. There he operated optical and electronic recognition equipment and worked on the mechanical arms and rigging used to recover objects. The unit handled deep-sea rescue and recovery, including downed aircraft and "any anomaly that we wanted to look at".

## The account

- **Detection.** A survey vessel detected a radioactive signature on the seafloor. A second vessel's side-scan sonar found an object smaller than any known nuclear submarine. Authorities feared a foreign submarine or aircraft carrying a weapon or nuclear power source.
- **First dive.** The DSRV crew, steamed out from Virginia, found at just under 1.5 miles an object that looked more like an aircraft than a submarine. It was nosed into the mud with the rear two-thirds exposed: a charcoal-grey triangle, rounded at the rear corners, with no windows, cockpit, hatch, rivets, seams or bolts. Repeating geometric characters, "hieroglyphic like" but matching no known script, were set into the skin.
- **Survey.** After 12 hours on the surface the crew returned with a marine archaeologist, who sketched the craft and judged from the silt build-up that it had lain there for 30 to 40 years.
- **Recovery.** On the third day, on the fourth dive, thrusters cleared the silt and the craft was rigged to lift rear first, with its weight estimated from an F-14. It rose two and a half to three times faster than expected, suggesting it was extremely light. Its underside was completely smooth. Once raised, it measured about 68 to 70 feet, 6 to 8 feet longer than an F-14.
- **Aftermath.** Within four hours the crew was steamed off-site, without a debrief, warnings or additional non-disclosure agreements beyond those signed on joining the unit. Bell received Mark's sketches, and Mark offered to connect him with the naval officer.

## Analysis by UAP Gerb

UAP Gerb places the site in the Rockall Trough, the only water deep enough within about 345 statute miles of Aberdeen (allowing ±20% on Mark's estimate). Because the acknowledged DSRVs *Mystic* and *Avalon* were rated to only just over a mile, he suggests an unacknowledged Deep Submergence Search Vehicle from the [[Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP)|Deep Submergence Systems Project]] was used. He links the radioactive signature to [[Project Sanddollar|Sand Dollar]]'s interest in nuclear-emitting objects on the seafloor. He compares the markings with the hieroglyphic-style band reported on the craft in the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval]] and with images [[Daniel Sheehan]] says he saw in Project Blue Book files. The archaeologist's estimate places the crash between 1951 and 1961, which the host notes matches the alleged Majestic-12 Special Operations Manual's description of triangular craft as new and rare in the early 1950s. The host is still trying to identify Mark and the commanding officer and to locate the sketches.
