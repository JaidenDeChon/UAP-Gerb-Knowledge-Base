The SS City of Alako Hawaiian Islands Sighting occurred on November 7, 1959, when three witnesses aboard the SS City of Alako, traveling from Yokohama to Los Angeles just north of the Hawaiian Islands, observed a bright object streak across the water, emit three distinct flashes, and submerge beneath the ocean surface. [[Project Blue Book]] classified the case as a meteor. Gerb argues that this explanation is inconsistent with the object's observed trajectory and behavior. The video points out that the three flashes before the object went under also appear in independent Australian government reports of unidentified submerged objects (USOs) from years later.

## Incident Description

Three crew members aboard the SS City of Alako were approximately four miles north of the ship when they observed the object. Events unfolded in this order:

1. A bright light or object was seen streaking across the water at low altitude, traveling on a low pursuit angle along the surface
2. The object emitted three distinct flashes
3. The object submerged beneath the ocean surface
4. The SS City of Alako changed course to investigate the area
5. Nothing was found at the location

The main reason to question the Blue Book explanation is the object's low pursuit angle. It traveled nearly parallel to the ocean surface, where a meteor would have descended steeply.

## Blue Book Classification and Critique

Blue Book classified the sighting as a meteor. Gerb's critique rests on the physics of the object's path. A meteor on a low pursuit angle would be an exceptionally rare atmospheric grazing event, and even then a meteor would not skim the ocean and then submerge beneath the surface. The object apparently traveled along the water for an extended period and then deliberately submerged, which implies controlled behavior incompatible with a ballistic trajectory.

## Cross-Case Detail: Three Flashes

Gerb identifies the three distinct flashes before submersion as a recurring signature. An Australian government report from 1967 independently documented a different case, in which lights emerged from the sea, rose to a great height, curved and returned to the ocean. That report noted that three distinct flashes accompanied the return to the sea. Because the three-flash detail appears in independent reports eight years and thousands of miles apart, it is treated as a potentially meaningful pattern rather than coincidence.
