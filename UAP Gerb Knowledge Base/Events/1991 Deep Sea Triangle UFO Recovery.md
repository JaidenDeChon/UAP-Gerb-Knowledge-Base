The 1991 Deep Sea Triangle UFO Recovery is an alleged undersea craft retrieval operation described by a retired Marine Corps Lieutenant Colonel named Mark in a live call-in appearance on Art Bell's *Coast to Coast AM* on 19 April 2002. Mark claimed that in early summer 1991, a US Navy Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicle (DSRV) crew to which he belonged was dispatched from Virginia to approximately 250 nautical miles west of [[Aberdeen, Scotland]] — in waters consistent with the [[Rockall Trough]] — where they recovered a triangular, non-human craft from nearly 1.5 miles of depth. The case is notable for its operational specificity, the consistency of the craft's description with other reported UAP retrieval accounts, and the witness's credibility as a US Marine officer with direct DSRV service.

## Background and Dispatch

Mark served at 22nd Area Camp Pendleton with the First Air Delivery Unit before being stationed at Futema USMC Air Station in Okinawa, Japan, where he began rescue diving with a Navy crew on weekends. He formed a friendship with a Navy lieutenant who eventually became commander of a DSRV unit, leading to Mark's reassignment to Virginia as part of that unit. Mark's role on the DSRV was to operate computer equipment on the optical and electronic recognition side and to work on mechanical aspects of the vessel including its mechanical arms and rigging systems.

In early summer 1991, a survey vessel operating in the North Atlantic detected a radioactive signature on the seafloor. A second vessel equipped with side-scan sonar confirmed a craft resting on the bottom, but its dimensions were too small for any known nuclear submarine. Naval authorities initially suspected a downed foreign nuclear submarine, possibly Soviet or North Korean, potentially carrying a nuclear weapon or nuclear power source. Mark's DSRV crew was dispatched from Virginia.

## The Craft

At just under 1.5 miles of depth, the DSRV crew contacted the object and immediately recognized it was not a submarine but shaped more like an aircraft. The craft was partially buried nose-first in silt and mud. The exposed rear two-thirds revealed a triangular form with rounded rear corners — "rounded as it came to the corner," with a flat rear edge. It was charcoal gray in color and approximately 60 to 70 feet long.

The craft had:
- No windows or cockpit
- No rivets, seams, or bolts
- No visible mechanical systems, piping, or exhaust on its underside (visible as the craft was raised)
- Repeating geometric markings in the skin itself — not any known human language or script, not matching Russian, Arabic, or any trained reference, described as "hieroglyphic-like" but consisting of geometric shapes rather than figurative characters
- A smooth, featureless underside

Mark and his lieutenant commander friend privately concluded the craft was extraterrestrial but did not relay this assessment to the surface, fearing psychiatric evaluation and potential communications interception. The surface LPH vessel ordered the DSRV crew to observe, photograph, and determine what they had.

## Investigation and Recovery Operations

The operation took place over three days and involved four dives:

1. **First dive**: Initial contact, observation, and photography
2. **Second dive (12 hours after first)**: A marine archaeologist was brought down and made sketches. The archaeologist examined the silt and mud accumulation on the craft and estimated it had been in place for at least 30 to 40 years — placing the crash between approximately 1951 and 1961. The archaeologist made notes and brought his own personal computer.
3. **Third dive**: Additional observation and temperature/current monitoring
4. **Fourth dive (third day)**: Retrieval operation. Thrusters were used to remove silt and mud from the craft's midsection. The crew made size and weight estimations based on F-14 dimensions as the closest comparator. When rigged and lifted, the craft ascended two and a half to three times faster than anticipated — indicating anomalously low mass for its size. As the craft rose, the underside was fully visible and completely smooth. The recovery was completed.

Within approximately four hours of recovery, Mark and his entire crew were steamed off-site. No debriefing was conducted. No additional NDAs were required beyond those the DSRV crew had signed upon assignment to the unit.

## Craft Dimensions and Weight

The craft was determined to be 6 to 8 feet longer than an F-14 Tomcat (which is approximately 62 feet), making it 68 to 70 feet in total length. Its anomalously low apparent mass — ascending far faster than predicted for a vehicle of that size — was noted by Mark as one of the key indicators that it was not a conventional aircraft.

## Corroborating Details

Several details of Mark's account parallel elements reported in other UAP retrieval cases:

- **Hieroglyphic-style geometric markings**: Similar markings have been reported on the bronze acorn-shaped craft recovered at the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval]], where witnesses described writing around the lower lip of the craft. [[Daniel Sheehan]] has also stated he observed such markings in classified Project Blue Book files.
- **No mechanical features on underside**: Consistent with other descriptions of non-human craft that lack conventional propulsion exhaust or mechanical systems.
- **No cockpit or windows**: Consistent with recovered craft descriptions from multiple sources in [[Leonard Stringfield]]'s crash retrieval status reports.
- **Smooth metal with no seams**: Consistent with the "skin and inner bulkhead bonded at an atomic level" description given by [[Nat Kobitz]] of material he examined at Wright-Patterson AFB.

## Operational Context

The 1991 retrieval fits the naval UFO legacy program structure described by journalist Christopher Sharp in September 2024: CIA DS&T, [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] (NURO), SOCOM, the US Navy, and [[Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution]] deep submergence vehicles working in concert to retrieve undersea craft. UAP Gerb also notes the discrepancy between the acknowledged operational depth of DSRV units Mystic and Avalon (approximately one mile) and the 1.5-mile depth of this operation, suggesting the operation may have used an unacknowledged DSSV-type vessel built under the [[Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP)]] but never publicly acknowledged.

Art Bell received original sketches of the craft from the naval lieutenant commander involved in the operation. Mark indicated he would help Bell connect with the lieutenant commander. Both Bell and the lieutenant commander are now deceased, complicating further investigation.
