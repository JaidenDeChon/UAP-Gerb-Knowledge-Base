The **1955 Langtry UFO Crash** is an alleged UAP crash and retrieval in spring 1955 near [[Langtry, Texas]], just south of the Rio Grande on the Mexican side of the border. Most of what is known about it comes from US Air Force Reserve Colonel [[Robert Willingham]], who claimed he saw the object come down and later visited the crash site privately with his companion [[Jack Perkins]]. It is one of two separate crashes reported near Del Rio in the 1950s, and it is often confused with the [[1950 El Indio UFO Crash|El Indio incident]] of December 6, 1950.

## The Sighting

On a spring day in 1955, Colonel Willingham and other F-86 Sabre pilots were escorting B-47 and B-52 bombers from [[Carswell Air Force Base]] in Fort Worth, Texas, on a simulated Cold War training exercise. Willingham's call sign was Willie Eddie (serial CD1195). During the mission, the squadron heard by radio that [[DEW Radar Systems|DEW radar systems]] in Canada had picked up fast-moving unidentified signatures heading south toward Texas.

Willingham saw a bright object approaching at an estimated 2,000 mph, about four times the F-86's top speed of 685 mph. He said its surface looked like "magnesium steel." In the debrief afterwards, other pilots, including George Smithson, confirmed that they had seen the object too. It made a 90-degree turn southwest toward the Mexican border, then was seen throwing off sparks as it tilted to a 45-degree angle. Willingham speculated that the sudden turn may have damaged the craft's structure. When the object dropped out of sight, Willingham, who knew the region well, estimated that it had crashed near Langtry, Texas.

## The Crash Site Visit

Willingham asked Denver flight control for permission to investigate the estimated crash site, and received it. After returning to base and filing his first report, he flew out privately in a 1947 Aeronca Champion with his friend [[Jack Perkins]], an electronics engineer from Pennsylvania and a colleague from the Civil Air Patrol.

At the site, Willingham and Perkins saw:
- A disc-shaped craft broken into roughly three sections
- A main body, a flattened disc 20–25 feet across, split into two large pieces, one of them partly buried in a sandy mound
- A separate dome-shaped section 12–15 feet long, lying about 50 feet from the main body
- A 300-foot skid mark across the ground left by the impact
- Debris scattered across the site, from small fragments up to pieces "as big as a good sofa chair"
- Mexican soldiers guarding a perimeter around the wreckage but making no attempt to recover it

A Mexican officer from Mexico City, Lieutenant Martinez, noticed Willingham's US Air Force fatigues and came over to him. Martinez gave Willingham limited access to the site but would not let Perkins come near. Martinez said: "We can't let anyone close to it. The American Air Force will be here very soon to clean this all up."

## Retrieved Material

Before leaving, Willingham took a small fragment of material from the site. He described it as:
- Rigid and unyielding, but extremely light
- Grayish-silver
- Marked along one side with small holes in a honeycomb pattern, which Willingham speculated were there to dissipate heat
- Resistant to bending, and to cutting with a blowtorch
- Similar to magnesium steel

Willingham spent four days trying to test the material but took no photographs of it. He then sent the fragment to a USMC Metallurgy Lab in Hagerstown, Maryland. When he later contacted the lab to follow up, he was told that no such person (the major who had received it) had worked there and that no records of the tests existed. Later still, a USAF intelligence general warned Willingham not to discuss what he saw "down on the border."

## The Bodies Contradiction

The biggest problem for Willingham's credibility is that he contradicted himself about non-human bodies. In [[Noe Torres]] and [[Ruben Uriarte]]'s 2008 book *The Other Roswell: UFO Crash on the Texas Border*, Willingham is quoted as saying: "I never saw bodies. I was not able to get close enough, and I don't know what happened after I left."

In a July 24, 2012 interview with radio host [[Jeff Rense]], however, Willingham said he had sneaked around the perimeter to look inside the dome section and had seen three to four non-human bodies. He described them as small, with large heads and broomstick-thin arms, unclothed, and "in a lot of different pieces." Author Noe Torres repeated the claim in a 2023 presentation, saying there were bodies at the crash site.

Gerb calls this contradiction a "massive issue" for Willingham's credibility. Willingham blamed other inconsistencies in his account, such as which aircraft he was flying and the year of the event, on memory problems from a Korean War head injury. Gerb does not find that explanation enough to account for a detail as significant as whether non-human bodies were there or not.

## Independent Witness: William Drager

[[William Drager]], a former US Air Force member who served as a Spanish interpreter, was hired to work on a Japanese documentary about a UFO crash near the Mexican border that involved a colonel. Drager contacted a Mexican general who commanded the border region. In private, the general said he knew of the incident: "Yes, I know about that. I don't have any papers or documents to prove it, but due to my position I know about that." On camera, however, he denied all knowledge and dodged questions about it. Drager described the general as "beyond reproach" and a "military celebrity" in Mexico, and he gave a formal affidavit describing the exchange.

## Credibility Assessment

Researcher [[Kevin Randle]] questioned Willingham's Air Force service record in his book *Crash: When UFOs Fall from the Sky*. The National Personnel Records Service in St. Louis, Missouri, could not provide Willingham's service documents. A catastrophic fire there on July 12, 1973, however, destroyed most records for personnel who served between 1947 and 1963. Co-author Noe Torres claims to have over 50 documents that confirm Willingham's service, but he has not released them publicly.

Willingham's 1965 newspaper account, his 1977 affidavit (obtained by researcher [[Todd Zechel]]) and the 2008 book also disagree in other ways:
- The affidavit says Willingham was flying an F-94, while the book says it was an F-86 Sabre.
- The 1965 article dates the event to 1948, while the book and later sources give 1955.

Willingham put these discrepancies down to memory problems from his Korean War head injury.

## Supporting Evidence

### The Condon Report Case 5

The [[Condon Report]], a 1969 study funded by the Air Force and designed to debunk UFO phenomena, includes a case from September 19, 1957 (Case 5) that also involved an aircraft based at [[Carswell Air Force Base]]. A large, bright fireball that alternated between red and white pursued an RB-47 for over 600 miles. Several radar systems and the aircraft's own electronic countermeasures equipment detected it. When the plane landed, intelligence personnel removed all of the graphic and radar data. The case shows a pattern of UFO encounters involving Carswell-based aircraft, followed by the confiscation of data.

### USAF Document on B-47 UFO Sightings

In an unclassified document dated July 18, 1955, USAF Captain Lefty Meehan asked the commander of USAF Security Services in San Antonio for data on UFO sightings from the Northeast Air Command and the Northwestern United States, specifically sightings by B-47 jets. The request came only months after Willingham's encounter and concerned the same type of aircraft and the same region, which gives his account some circumstantial support.

### May 14, 1954 Marine UFO Encounter

On May 14, 1954, one year before Willingham's sighting, a flight of Marine Reserve jets led by Major Charles Scarboro was about six miles west of Dallas, Texas, when the pilots spotted 16 unidentified signatures in groups of four. The objects alternated between white and orange, much as Willingham described. Scarboro and another pilot, Jorgenson, tried to intercept them, but the signatures sped away and escaped the Marines.

## Distinction from the 1950 El Indio Incident

Four points separate the 1955 Langtry crash from the [[1950 El Indio UFO Crash]] of December 6, 1950:
1. The MJ-12 document describes the 1950 craft as nearly incinerated, while Willingham saw three large intact sections at Langtry.
2. Willingham was still serving in the Korean War in December 1950.
3. El Indio, Texas, is 75 miles southeast of Del Rio, while Langtry is 60 miles northwest of it.
4. Willingham's account mentions [[DEW Radar Systems|DEW radar systems]], which were not put in place until 1952.

## Eric Davis Endorsement

In a June 2018 *Coast to Coast AM* interview with journalist [[George Knapp]], physicist [[Eric Davis]] said: "Yeah, they've got—I would say, you know, if you're going to throw your bets on Roswell, your bets really good. Del Rio, Texas—that was a 1950s case. That was another one." His endorsement of Del Rio as a highly credible crash retrieval case is cited as context for both the 1950 and 1955 incidents.
