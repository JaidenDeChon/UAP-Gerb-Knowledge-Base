In 1968, a circular metal disc crashed in Nepal, and the US government responded through several agencies: the Defense Intelligence Agency, [[Project Moon Dust]] and [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. Declassified State Department cables document the incident, making it one of the few cases in which official government documents confirm Moon Dust's UFO collection activities. A cover story identified the object as a satellite, even though NASA and NORAD data show a 100% successful satellite launch rate in 1968, which means no missing satellite could account for the crashed disc.

## Discovery and Initial Response

Local authorities discovered the circular metal disc in Nepal in 1968. The Royal Nepalese Army photographed the object and showed the photos to US officials, an indication that the disc's anomalous characteristics warranted international attention.

The Defense Intelligence Department immediately began monitoring the crashed object. This suggests US intelligence had detection capabilities that alerted it to the crash shortly after it happened.

## Project Moondust Involvement

Four months after the discovery, a State Department cable titled "Moon Dust" went to three agencies:

- The [[Defense Intelligence Agency]] (DIA)
- The [[USAF 1127th Field Activities Group|1127th USAF Field Activities Group]] (Moon Dust's operational command)
- The [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] Foreign Technology Division

The cable called a diplomatic visit to Kathmandu, Nepal a "fishing expedition for info on space objects," which indicates that US officials wanted access to the crashed disc to examine it and possibly retrieve it.

## Satellite Cover Story Discrepancy

Later cables show that a possible cover story was put together identifying the object as a crashed satellite. NORAD and NASA data for 1968, however, show a 100% successful satellite launch rate, with no missing or failed satellites that year.

This discrepancy suggests that:

- the object was not a satellite, despite the cover story
- US agencies were building plausible deniability for an object they knew had a different origin
- the "crashed satellite" narrative was meant for public or diplomatic consumption and hid the true nature of the disc

## Retrieval Status Unknown

The documents end without saying whether US entities retrieved the circular metal disc. The involvement of Moon Dust and Wright-Patterson suggests that collection was at least attempted, but what finally happened to the object remains classified or is not documented in the available FOIA releases.

## Circular Disc Description

The object was described as a "circular metal disc", which matches the classic UFO shape and sets it apart from conventional satellite designs, which are typically cylindrical or box-shaped or have solar panel arrays. The circular disc suggests one of the following:

- non-human technology consistent with historical UFO descriptions
- an experimental terrestrial craft with an unconventional aerodynamic design
- a foreign aerial vehicle of unknown origin

## Multi-Agency Coordination

The Nepal incident shows the agencies that supported [[Project Moon Dust]] operations and what each did:

- DIA: intelligence monitoring and assessment
- 1127th Field Activities Group: operational command and field deployment
- Wright-Patterson AFB: technical analysis and material storage
- State Department: diplomatic coordination with foreign governments

Working through these agencies, Moon Dust could respond to foreign UFO crashes with the same institutional resources used to recover sensitive US or Soviet space assets.

## Significance

The 1968 Nepal circular disc crash is documentary evidence that Moon Dust investigated UFO crashes as well as space debris. It shows that US intelligence could detect crashes of anomalous objects worldwide. The discrepancy in the satellite cover story suggests deliberate deception about the object's true nature. The case also confirms that Moon Dust worked directly with Wright-Patterson AFB, the alleged repository for UFO crash materials.
