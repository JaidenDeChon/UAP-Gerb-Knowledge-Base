The **1950 El Indio UFO Crash** is an alleged UAP impact and retrieval operation on December 6, 1950, in the area between [[El Indio, Texas]] and Guerrero, Mexico, in the state of Coahuila. The incident is described in the alleged [[Majestic 12]] Eisenhower Briefing Document, and independent witness testimony collected by journalist [[Dennis Stacy]] has corroborated it. It is one of two separate crashes near [[Del Rio, Texas]] in the 1950s and is often confused with the 1955 [[Langtry, Texas]] incident involving Colonel [[Robert Willingham]].

## The Eisenhower Briefing Document Account

The MJ-12 Eisenhower Briefing Document was allegedly prepared in 1952 to brief President-Elect Dwight D. Eisenhower. According to the document, a UFO of similar origin to the 1947 [[Roswell Crash|Roswell Incident]] impacted the Earth at high speed on December 6, 1950, along the Texas-Mexico border between El Indio and Guerrero. The craft hit the ground so fast that it was nearly obliterated. Recovered materials were reportedly taken to the [[Atomic Energy Commission]]'s facility at [[Sandia National Laboratories]] in Albuquerque, New Mexico, for study.

The document's authenticity is disputed. Gerb says it likely mixes disinformation with factual information, which is also the view of researcher Stanton Friedman.

## Dennis Stacy Investigation and Witness Testimony

In a 1995 *Omni* magazine article titled "Crash at El Indio," journalist Dennis Stacy documented a four-year field investigation (1990–1994) into the crash. Stacy found a local resident, Senor Flores, who had been working on his family's land north of El Indio when a ball of fire fell from the sky, crashed on an adjoining ranch, and ignited a grass fire. A day or two later, a military contingent arrived from Piedras Negras, Mexico, blocked off the location, and hauled something away by truck. Flores could not confirm whether the military vehicles were American, Mexican, or a combination of both.

Stacy speculated that Flores might instead have been describing a Civil Air Patrol plane crash on January 16, 1944. The different month (January rather than December) and the eyewitness description of a "ball of fire" suggest these were separate events.

## December 6, 1950 Nationwide UFO Alert

On December 6, 1950, the same day as the alleged crash, the US military issued a nationwide alert at 10:30 AM Eastern Time about "a large number of unidentified aircraft approaching northeastern US at high altitude." An estimated 40 signatures were detected at 32,000 feet, traveling south from the northwest. General Ramey, the same general who gave the Roswell weather balloon explanation, cancelled the alert at 1:16 PM.

Two days later, on December 8, 1950, a confidential FBI memo sent to [[J. Edgar Hoover]] put Army intelligence on high alert for any data on "flying discs." Because the alert, the alleged El Indio crash and the FBI memo came so close together, researchers have speculated that they may be connected.

## Sandia National Laboratories and AT&T Connection

If the Eisenhower Briefing Document is accurate, the El Indio materials went to [[Sandia National Laboratories]] in the early years of [[AT&T]]'s management of the facility (1949–1993). [[Steven Greer]]'s Disclosure Project archives include two redacted witness entries that describe transfers of extraterrestrial or non-human intelligence technology to AT&T specifically. These give circumstantial support to the chain of custody the document implies. In 1993, management of Sandia passed to [[Lockheed Martin]].

## Distinction from the 1955 Langtry Incident

Four points of evidence separate the 1950 El Indio crash from the 1955 [[Langtry, Texas]] incident witnessed by Colonel [[Robert Willingham]]:

1. Condition of the craft: the MJ-12 document describes the El Indio craft as nearly incinerated, while Willingham said he saw three large intact sections of wreckage.
2. Timeline: Willingham was still serving in the Korean War in December 1950 and was wounded by a mortar on December 26, 1950, so he could not have been the Del Rio witness.
3. Geography: El Indio, Texas is 75 miles southeast of Del Rio, and Langtry, Texas is 60 miles northwest of it, in the opposite direction.
4. Radar: Willingham's account mentions DEW (Distant Early Warning) radar systems, which were not in place until 1952. That does not fit a 1950 date.

## Lou Elizondo Reference

Former AATIP director Lou Elizondo has mentioned the El Indio crash in public statements. This adds to the case's credibility as a subject of ongoing government interest.
