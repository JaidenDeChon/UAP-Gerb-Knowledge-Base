The 1976 Tehran UFO Incident was a major military UFO encounter over Tehran, Iran, on September 19, 1976. Two Imperial Iranian Air Force F-4 Phantom II jets were scrambled to investigate a brilliant object that people in Tehran had reported by telephone and that the air force's Brigadier General Yousefi then saw for himself. The incident is notable for electromagnetic effects on the aircraft's systems, for smaller objects that detached from the main UAP, and for confirmed tracking by [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites. The main official record is the defense attaché's report to the DIA, the [[DIA Tehran UFO Report (1976)]].

> [!edit] Corrected 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions called the jets Grumman F-4s and said radar had picked up the object before the scramble. The F-4 is a McDonnell Douglas aircraft, and the attaché's report says the first jet was sent after phone calls from Shemiran and Yousefi's own sighting; the only radar contact it reports is the second F-4's.

## The Encounter

Major [[Parviz Jafari]] was vectored toward a large UAP. As he approached, an orb-like object detached from the main craft. The avionics on Jafari's aircraft were severely disrupted: his weapons control panel went offline when he tried to fire an AIM-9 Sidewinder missile at the approaching orb, and communications were jammed. The electromagnetic effects stopped when the orb returned to the main object.

Several trained military observers watched the UAP for a sustained period, the second F-4's radar locked on to it, and it interfered electromagnetically with military aircraft systems. Together these made the encounter one of the most compelling military UFO cases of the 1970s.

> [!edit] Corrected 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions said ground radar confirmed the object. The [[DIA Tehran UFO Report (1976)|attaché's report]] records a radar lock by the second F-4's backseater at 27 nautical miles and no ground radar contact; the tower saw the cylinder-shaped object only after the pilot told it where to look.

## DSP Satellite Detection and Tracking

[[Lee Graham]] and [[Ron Regehr]], satellite sensor technicians at [[Aerojet]] in California, established through their research that United States military [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites tracked the Tehran UFO Incident. During their investigation they found a computer printout from the time of the Iranian UFO overflight, which confirmed that the DSP satellite had detected an anomalous object in Iranian airspace.

The printout showed:
- 238 scans of the object
- 39.7 minutes of continuous tracking (DSP satellites scan once every 10 seconds)
- the notation "possible SR" (likely Strategic Reconnaissance)

The data established that US space-based surveillance systems monitored the incident in real time, which confirmed the encounter independently of the Iranian Air Force reports.

## Technical Analysis Debate

UFO researcher [[Brad Sparks]], an early co-founder of [[Citizens Against UFO Secrecy (CAUS)|Citizens Against UFO Secrecy]], analyzed the DSP detection data. He questioned whether the satellite had tracked the UFO itself or had instead detected the Iranian F-4 jets scrambled to pursue it (Gerb, relaying the question, calls them "Mirage F4s"). He also raised concerns about an "out of focus graphic image" that gave the date but no specific time, which made it hard to match the DSP detections precisely to the phases of the aerial encounter.

> [!edit] Corrected 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions called the jets "Mirage F-4" jets. The attaché's report shows they were F-4 Phantoms; "Mirage" is Gerb's slip in the video.

Whatever the answer, DSP tracking data for the incident exists, and getting access to it required substantial classification review. That shows US military space surveillance systems monitored the event and that the data remains sensitive decades later.

## Fast Walker Designation

The Tehran incident is cited as one of the earliest documented cases tied to the [[Fast Walkers|Fast Walker]] designation, which [[NORAD]], [[US Northern Command]], and [[US Space Force]] use for objects detected entering or leaving Earth's atmosphere. The incident helps trace the Fast Walker term back to at least 1976.

## Significance

The 1976 Tehran UFO Incident brings together several kinds of evidence:
- Trained military pilot observations
- Multi-aircraft encounters
- A radar lock by the second F-4
- Electromagnetic interference with weapons and communications systems
- Independent tracking by US military satellites
- Sustained observation over an extended period (39.7 minutes of satellite tracking)

> [!edit] Corrected 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions listed ground radar confirmation. The only radar contact in the attaché's report is the second F-4's airborne lock.

Graham and Regehr's work brought the DSP satellite confirmation to light decades after the incident. It demonstrates that many historical UFO cases may have matching classified satellite detection records that are still withheld from the public. The case also shows how hard it is to get complete DSP data: even researchers with technical backgrounds must work from fragmentary printouts rather than full mission records.

## Related Cases

The Tehran incident has been mentioned alongside other cases involving similar craft, including encounters in which conical or orb-like objects detached from a main craft. Some researchers have noted parallels with other military jet encounters with UAPs that showed electromagnetic effects.
