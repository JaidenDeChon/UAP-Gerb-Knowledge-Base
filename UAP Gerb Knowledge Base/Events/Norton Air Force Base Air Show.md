The **Norton Air Force Base Air Show** was a public USAF air show held on November 12, 1988, at the now-defunct [[Norton Air Force Base]] in San Bernardino, California. The public event served as cover for a private, classified aerospace exhibit at [[Air Force Plant 42]] in Palmdale, to which a small group of senior attendees was covertly transported — among them industrial designer and inventor [[Brad Sorenson]], whose account of the exhibit forms one of the central pieces of evidence in the [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|Alien Reproduction Vehicle]] disclosure narrative.

## The Public Air Show and Covert Transfer

Sorenson attended the public Norton air show alongside a client later identified with high confidence by aerospace artist [[Mark McCandlish]] as [[Frank Carlucci]], then-US Secretary of Defense. During the USAF Thunderbirds flyby, Sorenson was escorted away from the main public exhibit by his client and, along with roughly 30 other senior individuals — described as sharply dressed men aged 45 to 60, believed by Sorenson to be senior DoD, intelligence community, or contracting officials — boarded a military Boeing 727 that flew north from Norton to Air Force Plant 42 in Palmdale, where the classified exhibit was held inside a Skunk Works hangar. The only attendee Sorenson recognized by sight was then-Senator [[Alan Cranston]] (D-CA), who was a sitting US Senator at the time.

## The Classified Exhibit

The private exhibit displayed a range of prototype and mature aerospace vehicles, including the losing prototype to the B-2 stealth bomber, small hovercraft-like vehicles, and a remote-piloted, pumpkin-seed-shaped craft called the Aurora, reportedly armed with 120 or more nuclear warheads and described by military personnel as capable of destroying any Soviet city with a population over one million in under two hours. Behind a partition sat the centerpiece of the exhibit: three flying saucer-shaped craft that reminded Sorenson of submarines, nicknamed **"Mama," "Papa," and "Baby Bear"** for their differing sizes, which hovered off the ground with no visible means of support while remaining perfectly proportional to one another. As an industrial designer, Sorenson was struck by what he considered sloppy construction and a seemingly crude, lead-paint-like finish on the craft.

An attending three-star general narrated video footage of one of the discs "flying" and jittering low over a dry lake bed, executing skips that appeared to defy conventional aerodynamics before shooting vertically into the sky. Program officials described the craft as [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|Alien Reproduction Vehicles]] — reverse-engineered "Flux Liner" copies of recovered non-human technology — and indicated they were seeking additional Congressional funding to build more of them, during the same period the [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)|Strategic Defense Initiative]] was receiving massive appropriations.

## Significance

The Norton/Palmdale exhibit is treated by UAP researchers as one of the most detailed firsthand accounts of an alleged Alien Reproduction Vehicle program, corroborated independently by [[Mark McCandlish]]'s technical illustrations based on Sorenson's descriptions and by Sorenson's 1990 on-record interview with [[William B. Scott]] of Aviation Week and Space Technology. The presence of a sitting US Senator and the then-Secretary of Defense at the classified portion of the exhibit is cited as evidence that the briefing was intended, at least in part, to solicit continued high-level government funding for the program.
