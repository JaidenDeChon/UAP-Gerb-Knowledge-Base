The **Norton Air Force Base Air Show** was a public USAF air show held on November 12, 1988, at [[Norton Air Force Base]] in San Bernardino, California, a base that has since closed. The public event was cover for a private, classified aerospace exhibit at [[Air Force Plant 42]] in Palmdale. A small group of senior attendees was covertly taken there, among them the industrial designer and inventor [[Brad Sorenson]]. His account of the exhibit is one of the central pieces of evidence in the [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|Alien Reproduction Vehicle]] disclosure narrative.

## The Public Air Show and Covert Transfer

Sorenson attended the public Norton air show with a client whom aerospace artist [[Mark McCandlish]] later identified, with high confidence, as [[Frank Carlucci]], then-US Secretary of Defense. During the USAF Thunderbirds flyby, the client escorted Sorenson away from the main public exhibit. Sorenson and roughly 30 other senior figures boarded a military Boeing 727 that flew north from Norton to Air Force Plant 42 in Palmdale, where the classified exhibit was held inside a Skunk Works hangar. He described the others as sharply dressed men aged 45 to 60 and believed they were senior DoD, intelligence community or contracting officials. The only one he recognized by sight was then-Senator [[Alan Cranston]] (D-CA).

## The Classified Exhibit

The private exhibit showed a range of prototype and mature aerospace vehicles. They included the prototype that lost out to the B-2 stealth bomber, small hovercraft-like vehicles, and a remote-piloted, pumpkin-seed-shaped craft called the Aurora. The Aurora was reportedly armed with 120 or more nuclear warheads, and military personnel described it as capable of destroying any Soviet city with a population over one million in under two hours. The centerpiece of the exhibit sat behind a partition: three flying saucer-shaped craft that reminded Sorenson of submarines. They were nicknamed "Mama," "Papa," and "Baby Bear" for their different sizes, were perfectly proportional to one another, and hovered off the ground with no visible means of support. As an industrial designer, Sorenson was struck by what he considered sloppy construction and by the craft's finish, which looked crude, like lead paint.

A three-star general at the exhibit narrated video footage of one of the discs "flying" and jittering low over a dry lake bed. It made skips that appeared to defy conventional aerodynamics, then shot vertically into the sky. Program officials described the craft as [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|Alien Reproduction Vehicles]], reverse-engineered "Flux Liner" copies of recovered non-human technology. They indicated they were seeking more Congressional funding to build more of them, at a time when the [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)|Strategic Defense Initiative]] was receiving massive appropriations.

## Significance

UAP researchers treat the Norton and Palmdale exhibit as one of the most detailed firsthand accounts of an alleged Alien Reproduction Vehicle program. In their view it is independently corroborated by [[Mark McCandlish]]'s technical illustrations, which were based on Sorenson's descriptions, and by Sorenson's 1990 on-record interview with [[William B. Scott]] of Aviation Week and Space Technology. The presence of a sitting US Senator and the then-Secretary of Defense at the classified part of the exhibit is cited as evidence that the briefing was meant, at least in part, to win continued high-level government funding for the program.
