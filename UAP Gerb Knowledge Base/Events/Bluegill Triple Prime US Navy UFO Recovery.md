The Bluegill Triple Prime UFO Recovery is an alleged incident in which the United States Navy recovered a non-human craft disabled during the Bluegill Triple Prime nuclear test in October 1962, conducted as part of Operation Fishbowl — a series of high-altitude nuclear detonations in the Pacific Ocean above Johnston Island. The event was disclosed by Harold Malmgren, a late presidential adviser, and is cited in UAP research as one of the earliest documented instances of a US Navy USO (unidentified submerged object) retrieval operation.

## The Incident

On or around 26 October 1962, the Bluegill Triple Prime test involved a nuclear warhead detonated at high altitude over the Pacific. According to Malmgren, the electromagnetic pulse or blast effects of the detonation accidentally disabled a nearby UFO, which fell into the waters below. A Navy search and recovery operation was launched. Historical logs from the USS Safeguard, an auxiliary rescue and salvage vessel, record the Safeguard and other vessels — including the USS Engage — steaming to the water crash location, searching in a square grid pattern, and employing divers to recover a "pod." During the net recovery of this pod, the Safeguard temporarily lost power and the pod was released for helicopter pickup.

## Significance in the Naval UFO Program Timeline

The Bluegill Triple Prime recovery is cited by UAP Gerb for its timing: the incident occurred in October 1962, just two years before [[John P. Craraven]] was tasked in 1964 to establish the [[Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP)]] — the program that produced the Navy's DSRV units and dramatically expanded undersea recovery capabilities. UAP Gerb argues the DSSP may have been developed in direct response to the demonstrated need for better recovery capability illustrated by the Bluegill Triple Prime incident and similar encounters.

The recovery also establishes that the US Navy was aware — at least as early as 1962 — of the possibility that nuclear tests could disable anomalous aerial objects and that recovery protocols for such objects existed within active naval operations. The USS Safeguard's logs constitute contemporaneous documentation of a recovery operation for an unknown "pod" consistent with a non-human craft.
