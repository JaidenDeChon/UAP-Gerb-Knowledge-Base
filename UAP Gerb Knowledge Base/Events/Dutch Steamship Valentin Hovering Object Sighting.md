The Dutch Steamship Valentin Hovering Object Sighting took place in 1910 in the South China Sea. Captain Brier of the Dutch steamship Valentin, together with his first and second mates and his first engineer, saw an object that appeared to be a horizontal wheel rotating rapidly above the water surface. The case is documented in records of the [[Danish Meteorological Institute]]. It is one of the [[Maritime Light Wheel]] encounters in which the phenomenon is described as clearly aerial, hovering above the ocean instead of beneath it.

## Incident Description

Captain Brier and three senior officers of the Valentin all saw the same object and described it this way:

> "It looked like a horizontal wheel rapidly turning."

In most maritime light wheel cases the structure is described as submerged beneath the ocean surface, but the Valentin account has the wheel hovering *above* the water. This places the case in the [[Trans-Medium Vehicle|transmedium]] category, since the same type of structure seen submerged in other encounters is seen here in the air.

## Significance

The aerial description matters for the Maritime Light Wheel phenomenon as a whole. If the same rotating wheel structure is seen both submerged (as in the [[SS Bintang Light Wheel Sighting]], the [[Persian Gulf USO Flap]], and other cases) and hovering above the ocean surface (as in the Valentin case and the [[SS Shahian Circular Object Sighting]]), that is evidence of transmedium operation, and not of a phenomenon confined to the water. With four witnesses, the captain and three officers, the observation has strong corroboration.
