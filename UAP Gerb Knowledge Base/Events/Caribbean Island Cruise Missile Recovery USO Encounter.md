The Caribbean Island Cruise Missile Recovery USO Encounter is a military incident described by [[Luis Elizondo]] in a 2022 interview with NASA astronaut Terry Virts. It involves two separate sightings, on cruise missile recovery operations in consecutive months, of a large, dark, circular unidentified submerged object rising from the depths of the [[Puerto Rico Trench]] near a Caribbean island.

## Background

According to Elizondo, the Navy test-fires cruise missiles in the area, and a helicopter regularly flies out from a Caribbean island to recover them. When a missile runs out of fuel it splashes into the ocean and sinks, later rising back to the surface, where the crew fetches it so its telemetry can be analysed. Elizondo places the site over the [[Puerto Rico Trench]], which he calls water 22,000 feet deep and "the second deepest part of the ocean". In fact the trench is the deepest part of the Atlantic, reaching about 27,500 feet (8,376 m) at the Milwaukee Deep, and deeper trenches exist in the Pacific.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the Navy launches the missiles from helicopters and that the trench is about 22,000 feet deep, "the second deepest body of water on Earth". Elizondo says only that the helicopter recovers the missiles, and the depth and ranking are his; the trench's deepest point is about 27,500 feet ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Deep); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Milwaukee-Depth)).

## First Encounter

On the first recovery mission, the helicopter crew was pulling the missile up from the ocean surface when they saw a large dark circular object rising toward the surface from below. It was described as round and circular, approximately the size of a small island, and black or dark in color. The object rose to just below the surface and stayed submerged. The crew watched it with alarm, but they completed their mission and left.

## Second Encounter

On the following month's recovery mission, the same or a similar object appeared as the crew lowered a Navy frogman on a rope to attach it to the missile. As the frogman descended, the object began rising from below. He immediately climbed back up the rope in an emergency ascent while the crew made an emergency departure. The object kept rising and eventually pulled the missile back down into the ocean. The missile was never recovered.

Elizondo described the crew's reaction as panic: "everybody is absolutely Panic at the Disco freaking out."

## The "Fast Mover" Story

The "fast mover program" anecdote that Gerb tells in the same video is not part of Elizondo's account. It belongs to astronomer [[Mark Dantonio]], in a story Gerb dates to 2017: on a Navy submarine ride, sonar picked up a "fast mover" travelling at several hundred knots, and years later Dantonio asked a senior naval figure about the fast mover program and was told it couldn't be discussed. Gerb reads that refusal as confirming the program existed.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions credited the fast-mover story to Elizondo as a follow-up to this encounter. In Gerb's USO video it is Mark Dantonio's separate account, and Elizondo does not mention it.

## Significance

The object's estimated size, comparable to a small island, is among the largest claimed for a USO in witness accounts. The Puerto Rico Trench setting is consistent with other USO accounts from the same area. The USO also physically interacted with the missile, which, in Elizondo's words, "sucks the missile underwater and then disappears", and that goes beyond simple observation. And because the event happened at the same location in two consecutive months, it suggests a resident phenomenon rather than a chance encounter.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the object pulled the missile 22,000 feet below the surface. Elizondo says only that it sucked the missile underwater and disappeared; the depth figure was taken from his description of the trench.

Gerb presents the account without documentary support; it rests on Elizondo's retelling of what an unnamed person told him.
