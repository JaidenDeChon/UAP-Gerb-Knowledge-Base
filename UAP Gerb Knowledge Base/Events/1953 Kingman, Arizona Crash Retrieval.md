The 1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval refers to the alleged recovery of an unidentified craft near [[Kingman, Arizona]] on 21 May 1953. The primary witness is [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], an engineer working under the Air Research and Development Command (ARDC), who claims he was summoned to the site by [[Edward Bushnell Doll]] as part of a team of specialists. Stansel described the recovered object as an oval-shaped craft approximately 30 feet in diameter, constructed of a brushed aluminum-like material with no visible seams or rivets. He reported seeing two small bodies in silver flight suits in a tent erected near the crash site. Stansel came forward publicly under the pseudonym "Fritz Werner" in a 1973 disclosure to UFO researcher [[Raymond Fowler]], and later permitted his real name to be used.

The Kingman event is treated in UAP research as a pivotal data point because it links [[Edward Bushnell Doll]] — a Manhattan Project veteran who subsequently worked at [[TRW]] Systems Group from approximately 1955 to 1977 — to the earliest alleged instance of non-human craft exploitation within the personnel network that later became associated with TRW and ultimately [[Northrop Grumman]]. The alleged chain of custody of recovered materials from Kingman, passing through TRW and into Northrop Grumman via the 2002 acquisition, is a central thread in UAP researcher analysis of the Northrop Grumman legacy program hypothesis. A related claim in the video asserts that [[James Lacatski]] and the AAWSAP program encountered a blocked material transfer from [[Lockheed Martin]] linked to Kingman-era recovered materials.
