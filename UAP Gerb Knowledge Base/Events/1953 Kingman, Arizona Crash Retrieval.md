The 1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval is the alleged recovery of an unidentified oval craft near [[Kingman, Arizona]] on May 21, 1953. It is among the earliest alleged crash retrievals in UAP research to be backed by a notarized sworn affidavit. Its primary witness, [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], has verifiable professional credentials, which set him apart from more contested figures in the disclosure literature. UAP Gerb's "80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering" timeline video argues that the Kingman case rivals both [[Roswell Crash|Roswell]] and the [[1933 Magenta UFO CrashRetrieval|1933 Magenta, Italy crash]] as the most impactful UFO crash retrieval in history. The video compares Kingman to a dye tracer injected into a vein. By following the people and institutions involved in the case, researchers can trace individuals and offices central to the alleged golden-era legacy program apparatus, and ultimately trace custody of the craft wreckage forward into the 21st century.

## The Incident

On the evening of May 20, 1953, [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]] received a phone call from [[Edward Bushnell Doll|Ed Doll]], physicist and project director for the nuclear test series, telling him of a special assignment the next day. Stansel was then an AEC project engineer measuring blast effects at [[Frenchman's Flat, Nevada]] during [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]]. He and 15 other [[Atomic Energy Commission]] specialists reported to [[Indian Springs Air Force Base]], where they surrendered their personal items to military police. A blacked-out bus then drove them for approximately four hours to a site in the desert near Kingman.

At the site, two high-intensity spotlights lit a crashed object ringed by armed guards. The craft was oval and approximately 30 feet in diameter. It was made of a material like brushed aluminum and had no seams, rivets or structural damage, even though it had struck 20 inches deep into the desert sand. An open hatch approximately 3.5 feet tall and 1.5 feet wide was visible on the leading end. A colleague who glimpsed the interior reported two swivel-like seats, an oval cabin and anomalous instrument displays. A single dead occupant lay in a guarded tent near the craft. It was a humanoid approximately 4 feet tall with a dark brown complexion, dressed in a silvery metallic suit and skull cap, with no helmet or face covering.

Drawing on his engineering specialization in blast-effects analysis, Stansel estimated the impact velocity at approximately 1,200 mph. All 16 specialists were sworn to secrecy and told to write their reports longhand only. Stansel gave his account to researcher [[Raymond Fowler]] in 1973 and signed a sworn affidavit on June 7, 1973, at first under the pseudonym "Fritz Werner". His real name was later confirmed and published.

## Corroborating Testimony

[[Bill Uhouse]], a 14-year USMC veteran and mechanical engineer whose service record has been independently verified, claimed separately that a representative from [[Link Aviation]] recruited him in the late 1950s to help build a flying disc simulator modeled on the Kingman craft. Uhouse placed the craft's transport at [[Area 51]] (Groom Lake) and the occupants at [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]]. The simulator allegedly became operational between 1963 and 1964.

In 1997, a pilot approached researcher [[Leonard Stringfield]] at Lunken Airport in Cincinnati, Ohio, and claimed to have been at the crash site. Independently of Stansel, the pilot described the occupants as approximately 4 feet tall, large-eyed and brown-skinned, in silvery metallic suits, which is consistent with Stansel's account.

## Institutional Context and Legacy

[[Edward Bushnell Doll]], the AEC official who coordinated the Stansel assignment, later joined [[TRW]] Systems Group (approximately 1955–1977). UAP legacy program research cites the Kingman event as a founding data point in the chain of non-human craft exploitation. That chain runs from AEC-connected personnel through TRW and ultimately to [[Northrop Grumman]], which acquired TRW in 2002. Researchers have also connected a blocked material transfer from [[Lockheed Martin]] to the [[AAWSAP]] program with Kingman-era recovered materials.

The 80 Years timeline video ties this custody chain specifically to the [[Kona Blue]] prospective special access program. Citing "credible rumors and indirect confirmation" from [[Hal Puthoff]], Gerb alleges that the Kona Blue material transfer or technology transfer agreement from Lockheed Martin consisted at least partly of Kingman wreckage. He further speculates that there may actually have been three separate Kingman-area crashes. If the allegation is accurate, traceable custody of Kingman-derived material would extend as late as the 2010s.

Stansel's supervisor at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], Dr. [[Eric Henry Wang]], is independently alleged to have been one of the main directors of UAP reverse engineering from the time of the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery]] onward.

Three months after the Kingman crash, the USAF issued Regulation 200-2, diverting UFO reports to Air Force Intelligence rather than [[Project Blue Book]].
