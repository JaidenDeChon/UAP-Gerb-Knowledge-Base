On May 6, 1978, at 4:15 PM, thousands of people near El Taire, Bolivia (a mountain by the Bermejo River, on the border between Bolivia's Tarija department and Argentina) saw a cylindrical object crash into a mountainside. The crash set off a sonic boom heard up to 150 miles away and cracked window panes within a 30-mile radius. It became one of the best-documented UFO crash cases involving a US government response: declassified State Department cables confirm that [[Project Moon Dust]] was involved and that US Air Force personnel were sent to investigate the crash site.

## Eyewitness Observations

Local Army Corporal [[Natalio Ruiz]] saw the object fly directly over his head before it crashed. He described it as resembling "a gigantic wine container emitting a trace of white smoke." The cylindrical shape with trailing smoke suggests either an active propulsion system or structural damage during atmospheric flight.

Thousands of civilians in the El Taire area watched the object come down and strike. Their testimony corroborates the sonic boom and the visible shock wave that damaged windows across a 30-mile radius.

## Bolivian Military Response

Bolivian military personnel and three jets were sent at once to investigate the wreckage. After finding the crash site, the military issued this statement:

"Our men have discovered the object and inspected it but have received no further instruction or further action. It is a dull metallic cylinder 12 feet long with a few dents. No one knows what is inside it and we are waiting for the arrival of various technical commissions. A NASA expert is also expected to arrive tomorrow morning."

The military's description of a "dull metallic cylinder 12 feet long with a few dents" corroborates Corporal Ruiz's account of the object before impact. Its request for technical assistance, and the mention of NASA involvement, indicate that Bolivian authorities saw the object as beyond their ability to analyze.

## US Government Response

Secret documents obtained through the Freedom of Information Act show that US Secretary of State [[Cyrus Vance]] wrote that "appropriate government agencies have been contacted regarding this crash." The agencies contacted were confirmed to include [[Project Moon Dust]], the classified Air Force crash retrieval program run under the [[USAF 1127th Field Activities Group]].

Project Moon Dust received film of the object from the Bolivian military and was tasked with monitoring the situation. Two US Air Force officers, [[Colonel Robert Simmons]] and Major [[John Heise]] (whom Gerb calls "Jesse Haaste"), arrived later. According to the State Department documents as researchers have reported them, both were assigned to the military attaché's office at the US Embassy in La Paz and flew to Tarija with a Bolivian Air Force officer in connection with Project Moon Dust.

## Documentary Trail Cutoff

The declassified documents stop without confirming whether US entities retrieved the object. Moon Dust received film and sent personnel, which shows it was actively involved in operations, but what it did next is either still classified or missing from the available FOIA releases.

The gap in the records may point to:

- Retrieval operations that remain classified
- Transfer of the case to deeper compartmentalized programs
- Deliberate destruction or withholding of records during the alleged 1990s SAP reorganization described in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]]

## UAP Caucus Investigation

The [[UAP Caucus]], a bipartisan Congressional group that includes Representatives [[Tim Burchett]], [[Anna Paulina Luna]], [[Eric Burlison]], and [[Representative Moskowitz|Jared Moskowitz]], has brought the Bolivia crash to official Congressional attention as documentary evidence of US government involvement in foreign UFO crash retrieval operations.

## Significance

The 1978 Bolivia UFO crash is one of the few cases in which:

- Declassified government documents confirm official US response to a foreign UFO crash
- Multiple levels of government coordination (State Department, Air Force, intelligence agencies) are documented
- US personnel deployment to a foreign crash site is confirmed through FOIA releases
- The crash involved thousands of civilian and military witnesses, making denial impossible

The case provides concrete evidence that [[Project Moon Dust]] did more than passive monitoring. It was an active crash retrieval operation, able to deploy quickly and coordinated across agencies at the highest levels of government.
