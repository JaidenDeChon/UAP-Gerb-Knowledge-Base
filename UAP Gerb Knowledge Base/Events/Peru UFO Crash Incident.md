The Peru UFO Crash Incident refers to the alleged encounter by US Marine Corps personnel with a crashed non-human craft during Operation Laser Strike, a US-supported counter-narcotics aerial operation in [[Peru]] in 1997. The primary witness is USMC Lance Corporal [[Jonathan Wagant]], who testified about discovering a large egg-shaped craft embedded in rock at a remote crash site. The craft was described as oblong and spherical, roughly the size of a sedan, with a shimmering green and purple "mother of pearl" field on its exterior — absorbing light rather than reflecting it and casting no shadow despite direct sunlight. A "soupy air effect" was noted near the object, described as the air feeling like the space between two magnets, and the craft reportedly displayed a "nesting doll" internal structure.

Upon arrival at the site, Marines were held at gunpoint by men in all-black fatigues believed to be from the 7th Army, preventing them from approaching the craft. Department of Energy personnel wearing MOPP gear — rubber suits and gas masks — were observed conducting what appeared to be CBR (chemical, biological, radiological) risk assessments and securing debris from the crash. The rapid deployment of DOE radiological assessment teams (consistent with [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)]] capabilities) and the use of armed personnel to exclude US military witnesses is cited by UAP researchers as evidence of a standing crash retrieval protocol. The incident is treated as one of the most detailed and credible first-hand crash retrieval accounts in the UAP research record, with the craft's physical descriptions corroborated by subsequent independent witnesses describing other alleged retrieval events.
