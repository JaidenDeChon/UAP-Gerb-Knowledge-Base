The 1952 UFOs Over Washington DC were a series of UFO sightings and radar detections over the nation's capital on two consecutive weekends in July 1952: July 19–20 and July 26–27. They are among the most thoroughly documented and highest-profile mass UFO sightings in American history. Several independent radar systems tracked the objects, air traffic controllers and pilots saw them, and the sightings caused serious concern at the highest levels of the US government, including President [[Harry Truman]]. The incidents led directly to the CIA forming the [[Robertson Panel]], whose mandate to suppress public interest in UFOs shaped US policy toward the phenomenon for decades afterward.

## First Wave (July 19–20, 1952)

At 11:40 p.m. on Saturday, July 19, 1952, [[Edward Nugent]], an air traffic controller at Washington National Airport, spotted seven objects on his radar scope. They moved erratically, in ways that did not match any known aircraft. Nugent recognized the anomaly at once and alerted his shift supervisor, Harry Barnes. Other controllers in the radar-equipped control tower visually confirmed bright hovering lights, which departed at high speed when they were observed to approach. More radar scopes at the airport tracked multiple objects across every section of the screen, with tracks passing over the White House and the US Capitol.

Jets were scrambled from Newcastle Air Force Base in Delaware. They found no objects and returned to base when their fuel ran out. President Truman personally contacted his Air Force aides for an explanation of the intrusion into restricted airspace over the American capital.

## Second Wave (July 26–27, 1952)

The sightings returned on July 26, 1952. A pilot and a stewardess on a National Airlines flight inbound to Washington DC described erratic lights moving above their aircraft. Within minutes, radar centers at Washington National Airport and [[Andrews Air Force Base]] were both tracking the objects, so each confirmed the other independently. Jets were scrambled again and again reported nothing visible at their positions.

The events made front-page headlines across the country. General Roger Ramey and General John Samford then held a press conference, the largest the Air Force had held since World War II. They acknowledged the sightings but attributed them to radar anomalies caused by temperature inversions in the atmosphere. Many air traffic controllers rejected this explanation at the time.

## Presidential Response and CIA Interest

President Truman's direct involvement, which included personal calls to Air Force aides about objects tracked over the White House, indicates that the incidents were treated as a matter of national security. Separately, Arthur Lovkin, a former aide to Eisenhower, reportedly described overhearing Eisenhower discuss the 1952 incidents at Camp David some time after the events. This suggests the subject was still a concern at the most senior levels, including the president.

The CIA was alarmed enough to convene the [[Robertson Panel]] in January 1953. This scientific advisory committee was charged with assessing what public interest in UFOs meant for national security. The panel eventually recommended actively suppressing public fascination with UFOs through media management and debunking programs, and that recommendation created the institutional framework for the UFO stigma that lasted for decades.
