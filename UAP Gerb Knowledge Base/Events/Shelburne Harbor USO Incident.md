The Shelburne Harbor USO Incident was a 1960 event in which Royal Canadian Navy (RCN) divers participating in a joint US-Canadian minesweeping exercise off Shelburne Harbor, Nova Scotia, reported observing two disc-shaped unidentified craft resting on the seabed, along with apparent occupants attempting to repair one of the objects. The divers allegedly recorded footage of the encounter. The case became widely known after [[Rear Admiral Timothy Gallaudet]] included it in his 2024 white paper *Beneath the Surface*, where Gallaudet cites it as a significant historical USO event.

## Incident Details

During a US-Canadian joint minesweeping exercise off Shelburne Harbor, on the southwestern coast of Nova Scotia, a team of Royal Canadian Navy divers went down to the seabed. There they came upon two disc-shaped craft resting on the bottom. They reported seeing what appeared to be occupants of the craft, engaged in what looked like repair work on one of the objects. The divers allegedly captured video footage of the encounter.

No formal public investigation or official report has been released regarding this event. The footage, if it exists, has not been made publicly available.

## Gallaudet's Assessment

Rear Admiral Gallaudet acknowledged in his white paper that he at first found the account implausible, because occupants repairing a craft on the seabed was a detail he found hard to accept as credible. He stated that later disclosures by US military whistleblowers prompted him to revise his assessment and include the case in his formal white paper on USO research priorities. He was likely referring to the 2023 testimony of figures including David Grusch.

## Relation to Shag Harbour

Shelburne Harbor is approximately 30 miles northeast of [[Shag Harbour, Nova Scotia]], the site of the better-known 1967 UAP impact into the water. Researchers studying USO patterns in the northwestern Atlantic have noted that a 1960 underwater disc encounter and a 1967 aerial impact event took place within the same 30-mile stretch of coast. Gerb explicitly distinguishes the Shelburne Harbor case from the Shag Harbour incident, noting they are separate events at different locations.

## Significance

The Shelburne Harbor case is significant mainly because of who cited it. A retired US Navy rear admiral with a scientific background in oceanography included it in a formal published white paper as a historical USO precedent. Gallaudet's institutional credibility, and his open admission that he was skeptical at first and later reassessed the case, give the account unusual weight compared with cases cited only by civilian researchers.

If the alleged footage of the encounter survived and was preserved in either Canadian or US military archives, it would be some of the earliest physical documentation of a USO encounter with apparent occupants.
