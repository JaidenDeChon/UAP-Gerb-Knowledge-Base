The Hunter Warrior Advanced Warfighting Experiment was a Marine Corps field experiment held in March 1997 at the [[Marine Corps Air Ground Combat Center 29 Palms]] in Southern California. The UAP Gerb video gives the dates as 2–14 March, while contemporary Marine Corps accounts put the force-on-force phase at roughly 1–15 March. The newly renamed [[Marine Corps Warfighting Laboratory]] organized it. It was the largest experiment of its kind at the time, and it was designed to test ways of increasing the area of influence and combat effectiveness of Marine units with experimental equipment and new warfighting tactics and techniques.

## Objectives

Hunter Warrior had three main objectives:

1. Tactical operations on the dispersed, non-contiguous battlefield: how small units perform against a numerically superior force on a battlefield with no defined front, flank, or rear areas.
2. C4I (Command, Control, Communications, Computers, and Intelligence): building and testing a shared digital communications network so that information could be shared across multiple battlefield areas, as a way of exploring the "single battle concept."
3. Enhancement of fire support and targeting: making all indirect fire more effective and efficient, so that it could dominate a broad, dispersed battlefield.

## Phases

The experiment ran in three phases:
- Phase One: reconnaissance, surveillance, shaping, and deception operations by both the experimental special-purpose marine air-ground task force (SPMAGTF-X) and the opposing aggressor force.
- Phase Two: targeting for the first engagements by air and by long-range indirect fire missions.
- Phase Three: a major night movement by the operating forces and more targeting for the task force, ending in a mobile raid on the opposing forces.

## Naval Involvement

Hunter Warrior was mainly a Marine Corps exercise, but the US Navy had an integral role in it that was less publicly acknowledged. In 1995, Vice Admiral [[Robert J. Spane]] (rendered "Robert Spain" in the video), then Commander of Naval Air Force Pacific, proposed a new mission in which carrier tactical aviation would support troops ashore. He made the proposal in a report titled "An Operational Examination of Sea-Based Aerial Fire Support for Engaged Troops". At the behest of Admiral [[Jay L. Johnson]], Chief of Naval Operations, General [[Charles Krulak]], Commandant of the Marine Corps, asked the Commander of the [[Naval Aviation Warfare Development Center (NAWDC)|Naval Strike Air Warfare Center]] in Fallon, Nevada to assemble air crews from Fallon and from [[Naval Air Station Lemoore (NAS Lemoore)|Naval Air Station Lemoore]], California. Those crews were to explore the "Hunter Air" concept during Hunter Warrior. This naval component was described as an adjunct "bolted on late in the planning stage."

The [[Center for Naval Analyses]], a Navy [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)|FFRDC]] administered by the CNA Corporation, published summary findings together with the Marine Corps Combat Development Center.

## UAP Significance

Hunter Warrior matters to UAP research because of the encounter reported by [[Rodrik Castle]], a sergeant with [[VMA-513|VMA-513 Flying Nightmares]]. In his account, he and five other Marines witnessed an enormous, silent, black triangular craft near [[Emerson Dry Lake]] during the experiment, accompanied by approximately 30 armed, unmarked operators dressed in all black. The incident took place in FAA restricted airspace that the video identifies as R2501C.

UAP Gerb theorizes that the Navy's last-minute addition to Hunter Warrior provided cover for clandestine operations involving alien reproduction vehicles and special mission units. These units, the theory goes, wished to operate alongside "blue" (friendly) assets while keeping their activity secret, training and running practice operations out of sight of the conventional military participants. The theory proposes that NAWDC and NAS Lemoore brought black-ops crash retrieval or tactical teams into the war game exercises so they could practice operations, recoveries, and missions alongside blue assets without being detected. Under this theory, Castle's accidental encounter was a failure of that directive.

The direction in which the craft left supports the theory. It departed to the northwest, toward [[R2508 Complex|R2508 airspace]], [[Edwards Air Force Base]], and the [[Antelope Valley]], a corridor associated with numerous other alleged ARV operations and triangle sightings.
