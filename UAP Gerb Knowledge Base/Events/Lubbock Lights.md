The Lubbock Lights were a series of UFO sightings in Lubbock, Texas, in 1951. The best-known witnesses were four engineering and STEM professors, who saw several flights of 20 to 30 lights moving at extraordinary speed in a perfect semicircle. UAP research often cites the case as a textbook example of [[Project Grudge]]'s approach to UFO investigation. [[US Air Force]] investigators officially put the sightings down to a flock of migrating birds, an explanation the witnesses did not accept and that no corroborating evidence ever supported.

## Witness Credibility

The main witnesses were four professors with technical backgrounds in engineering and STEM fields, which made their testimony particularly credible. The lights' precise formation, a perfect semicircle, and their reported high speed left the observers unconvinced by conventional explanations.

## Official Explanation

[[Project Grudge]], the Air Force's official UFO investigation program at the time, attributed the Lubbock Lights to birds whose undersides reflected city lights as they flew overhead. Grudge gave this explanation even though the witnesses rejected it and no independent evidence supported the bird theory. The case became a standard example of Grudge's operating directive, which was to explain sightings away to "alleviate public anxiety" rather than investigate them objectively.
