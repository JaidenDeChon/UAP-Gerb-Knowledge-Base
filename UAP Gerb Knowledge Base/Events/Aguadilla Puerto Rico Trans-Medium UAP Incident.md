The Aguadilla Puerto Rico Trans-Medium UAP Incident is a 2013 event in which a US Customs and Border Protection aircraft equipped with a forward-looking infrared (FLIR) thermal imaging system filmed an unidentified object entering and exiting the Atlantic Ocean near Rafael Hernández Airport in Aguadilla, Puerto Rico. The Scientific Coalition for UAP Studies (SCU) later analyzed the footage and concluded that no known aircraft, naval vessel, or human-made technology has the performance characteristics the object showed. The event is among the most rigorously analyzed unidentified submerged object (USO) cases on record, and [[Rear Admiral Timothy Gallaudet]] cites it in his 2024 white paper as a leading example of documented trans-medium UAP activity.

## The Footage and Object Behavior

In April 2013, a CBP aircraft operating over the Aguadilla area filmed the unidentified object for approximately three minutes. The footage recorded the following behavior:

- Atmospheric flight speeds between approximately 40 and 120 mph
- Entry into the Atlantic Ocean without significant deceleration
- Continued travel underwater at a maximum recorded speed of approximately 95 mph
- Re-emergence from the ocean and resumption of aerial flight
- At one point, apparent splitting into two separate objects before re-entry into the water

## SCU Analysis

The Scientific Coalition for UAP Studies carried out a detailed technical analysis of the footage, using a methodology described as comparable to peer-reviewed scientific standards. It concluded: "No known aircraft, naval vessel, projectile, or human-made technology possesses these characteristics or capabilities." The analysis considered and ruled out conventional explanations, including military aircraft, drones, birds, and weather phenomena.

## Significance

The Aguadilla footage has a distinctive place among USO evidence for several reasons. A US government sensor system, operated by trained federal personnel, captured it. The footage is high resolution and long enough for meaningful analysis. The behavior it documents, a transition from aerial flight to underwater travel and back without deceleration, is unambiguous trans-medium performance. The methodology the SCU applied to it also approximates the analytical rigor normally reserved for academic research.

Rear Admiral Gallaudet cited the Aguadilla incident in his 2024 white paper *Beneath the Surface* as documentary evidence for his argument that USO study should be made a national research priority. The object was close to the [[Puerto Rico Trench]], the Atlantic Ocean's deepest point at approximately 28,000 feet, and this proximity has been noted as a potentially relevant geographic factor.
