[[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)|Air Force Materiel Command]] promotional materials cite the creation of the parachute, the computer mouse, and GPS together as examples of the command's historical role as the primary engine of Air Force technological innovation. Gerb quotes the line from one promotional video as part of a broader argument. AFMC, which commands a budget portfolio of $82.9 billion in fiscal year 2025 alone, is in his view the single most significant institutional target for investigating alleged UAP [[Legacy Programs|legacy program]] activity within the Air Force.

## Context in UAP Research

The AFMC promotional video states: *"We've been turning imagination into reality for years. We were there for the creation of the parachute, the computer mouse, and GPS."* Gerb sets this beside AFMC's acknowledged budget, its control over the [[412th Test Wing|Edwards 412th Test Wing]] at [[Edwards Air Force Base]], and its oversight of the [[Major Range and Test Facility Bases (MRTFBs)]] infrastructure. He argues that the command responsible for these well-known civilian and military technologies would logically also be the custodial home for reverse-engineered non-human technologies.

The same pattern appears elsewhere in UAP research. Legitimate, publicly acknowledged technological breakthroughs are attributed to the same research infrastructure that is alleged to house clandestine programs, including technologies that figures such as [[Philip J. Corso]] and [[Ben Rich]] have alleged were seeded from Roswell and other crash retrievals. On this reading, the parachute, computer mouse, and GPS are the publicly visible outputs of a command whose classified portfolio, Gerb argues, extends well beyond acknowledged programs.
