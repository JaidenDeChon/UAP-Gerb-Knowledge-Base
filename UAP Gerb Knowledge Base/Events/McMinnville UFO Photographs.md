On May 11, 1950, Paul and Evelyn Trent photographed a slow-moving, metallic, disc-shaped object near their farmhouse in McMinnville, Oregon. Their two pictures are among the most famous and most extensively analyzed UFO photographs ever taken. They show a disc-shaped craft with a prominent dome or protrusion on its upper surface, hanging in daylight against a clear sky.

## Physical Description and Comparison to Other Evidence

The craft in the McMinnville photographs has a distinctive protrusion on top of the disc, described as dome-like or resembling "half of a ping-pong ball". This closely matches the feature [[Robert Jacobs]] described in his testimony about the 1964 Big Sur warhead shootdown footage. The same dome is visible in the [[Flyby Footage]], so three separate incidents from 1950 to 2008 show a consistent shape.

The object is fairly small in the frame, and there is ongoing debate over whether it is a large craft far away or a small object close to the camera (the "hoax via suspended model" hypothesis). Photographic analysis has not definitively settled the question, and the photos have withstood decades of scrutiny.

## Historical Context and Analysis

The McMinnville photographs were taken early in the modern UFO era, just three years after the Kenneth Arnold sighting that set off widespread "flying saucer" reports. The Condon Committee (University of Colorado UFO Project) analyzed the images in the late 1960s and concluded they showed "an extraordinary flying object, silvery, metallic, disc-shaped, tens of meters in diameter, and evidently artificial". The Condon Report was generally skeptical, and this was one of the few cases it treated as potentially genuine.

Later analyses have produced mixed results. Some investigators claim evidence of hoaxing, such as suspension wires and inconsistent lighting, while others maintain the photos show an authentic unidentified object. The photographs remain iconic images of the "classic flying saucer" shape.
