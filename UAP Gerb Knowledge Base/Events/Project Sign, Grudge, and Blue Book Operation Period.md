The **Project Sign, Grudge, and Blue Book Operation Period** spans 1948 to 1969 and covers the three successive official U.S. government UFO investigation programs. Together these programs make up the publicly visible history of American UAP research. According to Gerb's analysis and the admissions of scientific adviser [[J. Allen Hynek]], they were also the main structure through which the [[UFO Stigma]] was deliberately constructed and institutionalized. Gerb argues that covert programs, including [[Project Moon Dust]], ran in parallel and carried on the classified UAP collection and analysis that the public programs were designed to conceal.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions stated as fact that covert programs including Moon Dust ran in parallel throughout the whole period. That is Gerb's argument, and Moon Dust is only documented from 1961.

## Project Sign (1948–1949)

On 23 September 1947 Lieutenant General [[Nathan Twining]] of Air Materiel Command wrote to the Commanding General, Army Air Forces, for the attention of Brig. Gen. George Schulgen, in a [[Twining Memo|letter]] that said "the phenomenon reported is something real and not visionary or fictitious" and recommended a study. Under the authority of a 30 December 1947 directive, Air Materiel Command's Technical Intelligence Division then set up [[Project Sign]] on 22 January 1948. Sign's final written report concluded that some UFO reports presented actual objects of undeterminable origin, and future director [[Edward J. Ruppelt]] stated that Sign endorsed the interplanetary explanation for some unexplained sightings. Twining's letter recommended that the study's data go for comment to the Army, the Navy, the [[Atomic Energy Commission]], the JRDB, the Air Force Scientific Advisory Group, NACA, and the RAND and NEPA projects; Gerb reads that list as institutional involvement well beyond the Air Force chain of command.

According to Ruppelt, in 1948 Sign sent up a Top Secret [[Project Sign Estimate of the Situation (1948)|Estimate of the Situation]] concluding that UFOs were interplanetary, and General [[Hoyt Vandenberg]] rejected it because it "lacked proof". No copy of the Estimate has been made public. Gerb tells this as Vandenberg shutting Sign down in 1949 for lack of proof. By Ruppelt's account, Sign was renamed Grudge by an order of 11 February 1949. Sign's positive conclusion, that some UFOs were real and unexplained, was institutionally unacceptable.

## Project Grudge (1949–1952)

[[Project Grudge]] was commissioned the same year Sign was terminated, taking over in February 1949. Its explicit task was to ease public anxiety by explaining UFO sightings away as balloons, aircraft, optical illusions, planets, or mass hallucinations. Hynek later stated Grudge was "less science and more of a public relations campaign." Grudge formally ended in December 1949 but carried on at a minimal level until late 1951, when it was revived under [[Edward J. Ruppelt]] and then replaced by Blue Book in March 1952. Gerb adds that Grudge records continued to be updated until 1999.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said Grudge ran only in 1949 and stated as fact that its records were updated until 1999. Grudge continued at a reduced level until it was revived in late 1951 and renamed Blue Book in March 1952 (Wikipedia, "Project Grudge"; The Black Vault); the 1999 detail is Gerb's.

The Lubbock Lights case shows how Grudge worked. Four engineering professors witnessed multiple flights of 20–30 lights in perfect semicircle formation. Grudge officially attributed the sightings to migrating birds, a conclusion the witnesses rejected.

## Project Blue Book (1952–1969)

[[Project Blue Book]] analyzed 12,618 UFO reports between 1952 and 1969. After extensive investigation, 701 of them were classified as unexplained. Hynek was scientific adviser throughout and later admitted that Blue Book "was not a scientific project" and that the Air Force "did everything they could to keep it down."

The [[Robertson Panel]] (January 1953) and the [[Condon Committee]] (1966–1968) were major institutional tools for discrediting UFO inquiry. Physicist [[Edward Condon]]'s final report recommended ending all UFO investigation, and his summary omitted the finding that 25–30% of examined cases could not be explained. Blue Book was officially terminated in December 1969, which completed the stigmatization of serious UFO research.

## Parallel Classified Programs

Gerb argues that classified programs operated alongside the official investigations throughout this period. According to Gerb, Ruppelt suggested on 24 April 1952 that programs parallel to the official investigations were "conducting a more complete investigation," and Hynek reached the same conclusion. Gerb suggests [[Project Moon Dust]], active from at least 1961 under the [[USAF 1127th Field Activities Group]], could have been one such program, collecting UFO material while Blue Book publicly dismissed the phenomena.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions stated as fact that Ruppelt disclosed parallel programs in 1952, that Hynek independently confirmed it, and that Moon Dust was one of them. These come from Gerb's Moon Dust video, where he gives the date as 24 April 1952 and says Moon Dust "could have been" such a program.

When Senators [[Jeff Bingaman]] and [[Pete Domenici]] inquired about Moon Dust in 1992, the Air Force initially denied that it existed. The denial is consistent with the system of suppression built during the Sign–Grudge–Blue Book period.
