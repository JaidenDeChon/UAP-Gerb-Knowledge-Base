The **Project Sign, Grudge, and Blue Book Operation Period** spans 1948 to 1969 and covers the three successive official U.S. government UFO investigation programs. Together these programs make up the publicly visible history of American UAP research. According to Gerb's analysis and the admissions of scientific adviser [[J. Allen Hynek]], they were also the main structure through which the [[UFO Stigma]] was deliberately constructed and institutionalized. Covert programs including [[Project Moon Dust]] operated in parallel throughout this period, continuing the classified UAP collection and analysis that the public programs were designed to conceal.

## Project Sign (1948–1949)

[[Project Sign]] was set up on 22 January 1948 by the Air Materiel Command's Technical Intelligence Division, following Lieutenant General [[Nathan Twining]]'s [[Twining Memo|letter of 23 September 1947]] to the Commanding General, Army Air Forces, for the attention of Brig. Gen. George Schulgen, which said that "the phenomenon reported is something real and not visionary or fictitious" and recommended a study. Sign's final written report concluded that some UFO reports presented actual objects of undeterminable origin, and future director [[Edward J. Ruppelt]] stated that Sign endorsed the interplanetary explanation for some unexplained sightings. Twining's letter recommended that the study's data go for comment to the Army, the Navy, the [[Atomic Energy Commission]], the JRDB, the Air Force Scientific Advisory Group, NACA, and the RAND and NEPA projects; Gerb reads that list as institutional involvement well beyond the Air Force chain of command.

According to Ruppelt, in 1948 Sign sent up a Top Secret [[Project Sign Estimate of the Situation (1948)|Estimate of the Situation]] concluding that UFOs were interplanetary, and General [[Hoyt Vandenberg]] rejected it because it "lacked proof". No copy of the Estimate has been made public. Gerb tells this as Vandenberg shutting Sign down in 1949 for lack of proof. By Ruppelt's account, Sign was renamed Grudge by an order of 11 February 1949. Sign's positive conclusion, that some UFOs were real and unexplained, was institutionally unacceptable.

## Project Grudge (1949)

[[Project Grudge]] was commissioned the same year Sign was terminated. Its explicit task was to ease public anxiety by explaining UFO sightings away as balloons, aircraft, optical illusions, planets, or mass hallucinations. Hynek later stated Grudge was "less science and more of a public relations campaign." Grudge records continued to be updated until 1999, even though the program officially ended in 1949.

The Lubbock Lights case shows how Grudge worked. Four engineering professors witnessed multiple flights of 20–30 lights in perfect semicircle formation. Grudge officially attributed the sightings to migrating birds, a conclusion the witnesses rejected.

## Project Blue Book (1952–1969)

[[Project Blue Book]] analyzed 12,618 UFO reports between 1952 and 1969. After extensive investigation, 701 of them were classified as unexplained. Hynek was scientific adviser throughout and later admitted that Blue Book "was not a scientific project" and that the Air Force "did everything they could to keep it down."

The [[Robertson Panel]] (January 1953) and the [[Condon Committee]] (1966–1968) were major institutional tools for discrediting UFO inquiry. Physicist [[Edward Condon]]'s final report recommended ending all UFO investigation, and his summary omitted the finding that 25–30% of examined cases could not be explained. Blue Book was officially terminated in December 1969, which completed the stigmatization of serious UFO research.

## Parallel Classified Programs

Classified programs operated alongside the official investigations throughout this entire period. Ruppelt himself disclosed in 1952 that programs parallel to official investigations were "conducting a more complete investigation." Hynek independently confirmed this. [[Project Moon Dust]], active from at least 1961 under the [[USAF 1127th Field Activities Group]], was one such program. It carried out actual UFO crash retrieval and collection operations while Blue Book publicly dismissed the phenomena. When Senators [[Jeff Bingaman]] and [[Pete Domenici]] inquired about Moon Dust in 1992, the Air Force initially denied that it existed. The denial is consistent with the system of suppression built during the Sign–Grudge–Blue Book period.
