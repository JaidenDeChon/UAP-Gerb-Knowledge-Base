On April 25, 1961, [[Project Moon Dust]] recorded observations of three cigar-shaped UFOs flying in a close Delta formation above Karachi, Pakistan. An internal memo documents the sighting, which is one of the earliest confirmed cases of Moon Dust actively investigating UFO reports. It shows that as early as 1961, Project Moon Dust was monitoring and investigating UFOs as well as collecting terrestrial space debris.

## Observed Characteristics

The internal memo describes three characteristics of the objects:

- Shape: cigar-shaped (cylindrical with tapered ends)
- Formation: a close Delta formation (a triangle, with the objects near one another)
- Location: above Karachi, Pakistan

Cigar-shaped or cylindrical objects were reported consistently in UFO sightings throughout the 20th century. The shape sets these objects apart from conventional aircraft, which typically have wings, and from satellites, which have irregular shapes with solar panel arrays.

## Delta Formation Flight

The objects' flight in a close Delta formation indicates:

- coordinated movement, suggesting intelligent control
- flying close together, which requires precise navigation
- formation discipline that does not fit atmospheric or astronomical phenomena
- a formation possibly used for reconnaissance or surveillance

Formation flight is a hallmark of controlled craft, and it rules out explanations such as meteors, atmospheric phenomena or astronomical misidentifications.

## Project Moon Dust Documentation

Because Project Moon Dust documented this sighting in an internal memo, it is confirmed that by 1961 Moon Dust had:

- established protocols for monitoring and reporting UFOs
- authority to investigate and document unidentified aerial phenomena
- information collection networks able to capture sightings in foreign nations
- institutional recognition that UFO investigation was part of its mission

The memo predates Moon Dust's better-known crash retrieval operations in Nepal (1968) and Bolivia (1978). Over its operational lifespan, the program moved from monitoring sightings to active crash retrieval.

## 1961 Operational Context

The Karachi sighting happened in the same period as the AFOSR-16 Draft Policy proposal, which formally suggested giving the [[USAF 1127th Field Activities Group]] collection responsibility for investigating unidentified flying objects. The 1961 date suggests Moon Dust may have been operating informally, or under different authority, before Air Force policy documents formally codified it.

## Significance

The 1961 Karachi Pakistan sighting establishes that Moon Dust was investigating UFOs as early as 1961. It is documentary evidence that the program's mission included active UFO monitoring and that Moon Dust had international intelligence collection capabilities. It also shows that the program tracked formation flights of anomalous craft as well as crashed objects.

The Karachi incident helps establish that [[Project Moon Dust]] was created to investigate UFOs as well as to recover Soviet or US space debris, and that UFO investigation was a core mission from its earliest documented operations.

## Related Operations

The Karachi sighting fits a broader pattern of Moon Dust UFO activities:

- 1968, Nepal: investigation of a circular disc crash
- 1976, Canada: a request for photos of metal fragments
- 1978, New Zealand: investigation of spherical or pressure-vessel objects
- 1978, Bolivia: a cylindrical object crash, with US personnel deployed

Across these cases, Moon Dust moved from passive observation (Karachi 1961) to active crash retrieval (Bolivia 1978).
