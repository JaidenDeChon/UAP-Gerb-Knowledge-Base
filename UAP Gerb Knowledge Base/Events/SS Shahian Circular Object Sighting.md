The SS Shahian Circular Object Sighting occurred on January 5, 1880, when Commander Ari Harris of the SS Shahian observed a milky white circular object in the sky off the Malabar Coast of India, on a clear night with no moon. The object resembled a nebula and emitted wave-like pulses of light. Harris stopped the vessel to attempt to understand the anomalous object. The case belongs to the [[Maritime Light Wheel]] sightings, and it is one of those in which the phenomenon was clearly in the air, above the ocean surface rather than beneath it.

## Incident Description

Commander Harris and the ship's third officer were on the bridge at approximately 10:00 p.m. The night was clear, the water calm and the stars visible, with no moon. Harris observed:

- A milky white circle in the sky
- Described as resembling "the nebula sometimes seen in the heavens"
- Emitting waves of light
- Approaching the vessel

Harris stopped the ship to watch the object and try to work out what it was. That experienced officers would stop a vessel at sea to examine something unexplained in the sky shows how deep an impression the object made on them.

## Relationship to Maritime Light Wheel Phenomenon

The Shahian case is one of two 1880 encounters, with the [[SS Patna Light Wheel Sighting]], that document the [[Maritime Light Wheel]] phenomenon in different places: the Patna in the Persian Gulf and the Shahian off the western coast of the Indian subcontinent. The Shahian object was in the sky and resembled a celestial nebula. In that respect it parallels the 1910 [[Dutch Steamship Valentin Hovering Object Sighting]] in the South China Sea, where a similar rotating horizontal wheel was seen hovering above the water. Together these aerial cases establish that the Maritime Light Wheel phenomenon was observed both submerged and airborne during the same historical period.
