The **1965 Kecksburg crash** is an alleged UFO crash and military recovery in the woods near [[Kecksburg, Pennsylvania]], a village in Westmoreland County, on the evening of 9 December 1965. Thousands of people across six US states and Canada watched a fireball cross the sky, and around 4:45 p.m. something came down outside the village. Local witnesses describe a seamless bronze object shaped like an acorn or a bell, 10 to 12 feet long, with a raised band of hieroglyph-like markings. They also describe soldiers, airmen in blue berets and men in civilian clothes who sealed the woods within the hour and took the object away on an Army flatbed by the early morning of 10 December. [[Project Blue Book]] recorded the event as a meteor. It is one of the most thoroughly witnessed crash-retrieval cases, investigated for decades by [[Stan Gordon]] and [[Leonard Stringfield]].

## The fireball

The object was seen from Michigan, Indiana, Ohio, West Virginia, Pennsylvania, New York and Canada, leaving a smoke trail visible for about 20 minutes. Pieces appeared to fall off over Lapeer, Michigan and Elyria, Ohio. Researchers calculated a course change of about 25 degrees over Elyria, an altitude of 40 to 60 miles over Michigan, and a speed of roughly 17 miles a minute (about 1,020 mph), far slower than a meteor. Near Kecksburg it reportedly turned more than once before descending into the woods at about 30 degrees and cutting a 25-foot trench.

## Witnesses at the site

[[Bill Bully Bush|Bill "Bully" Bush]] reached the object first and saw a metallic acorn embedded in the ground, giving off blue sparks and a smell of rotten eggs. Volunteer fireman [[Jim Romansky]] arrived with a search team expecting a crashed aircraft. He instead found a seamless bronze acorn or bell, 10 to 12 feet long and 8 to 10 feet across, with a flat bottom and a raised "bumper" carrying markings of broken and straight lines, dots, rectangles and circles, which he said were neither Russian nor Polish. Within about 15 minutes, men in trench coats ordered the firemen out and uniformed troops marched in. WHJB news director [[John Murphy]] was told by State Police fire marshal [[Carl Mets]] to get his information from the Army. Musician [[Jerry Betters]] saw the tarp-covered object leave on a flatbed at a military roadblock before soldiers turned their guns on him. Other witnesses describe M1 rifles, blue berets and men in white "moon suits".

## Official explanations

Project Blue Book sent a three-man team from the 662nd Radar Squadron and concluded, under Major [[Hector Quintanilla]], that the object was a meteor and that no material was found. It nevertheless received fragments seen falling from the fireball, which it identified as chaff. Sceptics, notably [[James Oberg]], proposed the failed Soviet Venus probe [[Cosmos 96]]. A 1965 NASA release and records obtained by Stan Gordon put that probe's re-entry over Canada about 13 hours earlier. At [[Leslie Kean]]'s May 2000 conference, NASA orbital-debris scientist [[Nicholas Johnson]] said Cosmos 96 could not have landed in Pennsylvania and that no man-made object came down there that day.

## The recovery and afterwards

Witnesses allege the object was taken first to [[Lockbourne Air Force Base, Columbus, Ohio|Lockbourne Air Force Base]] and then to [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. There, according to the trucker "[[Myron]]" and his cousin JS, it was kept in a brick warehouse inside a Navy-ordered, lead-lined brick enclosure while technicians tried in vain to cut into it. Myron later said he also saw a small, three-fingered body on a workbench. Penn State president [[Eric A. Walker]] admitted going to the crash site with two off-duty military men and would not say whether it was a UFO. President [[Lyndon B. Johnson]] spent 10 December in meetings with the Joint Chiefs and senior defense and science officials.

Murphy's radio documentary on the crash, *[[Object in the Woods Radio Documentary|Object in the Woods]]*, was heavily cut before broadcast, and witnesses withdrew their accounts for fear of the Army. Leslie Kean and the Coalition for Freedom of Information won a 2007 settlement requiring NASA to search for its Kecksburg records. NASA then reported that it had sent its files to the National Archives, which had listed them as lost since 1987. Gordon has also connected the case to [[Project Moondust]], the Air Force program for recovering foreign space objects.

## The Blue Berets

Witnesses' descriptions of airmen in blue fatigues and blue berets tie the case to the [[Blue Berets]], the rapid-reaction crash-retrieval unit Leonard Stringfield wrote about. The same uniform was worn by the Strategic Air Command Elite Guard, which [[Curtis LeMay]] created in 1956 and which was active until 1966.
