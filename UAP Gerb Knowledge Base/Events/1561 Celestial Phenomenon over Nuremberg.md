The 1561 Celestial Phenomenon over Nuremberg is considered one of the earliest recorded mass UFO sightings. In 1561, a news broadsheet printed in the Holy Roman Empire described a mass sighting of celestial phenomena over the city of Nuremberg at sunrise. A broadsheet was an illustrated public notice, similar to a newspaper. Hundreds of witnesses reportedly saw the sky fill with unidentified objects that seemed to fight one another above the Sun, and some of the objects then crashed to the ground.

## The Sighting

The broadsheet described hundreds of globes, cylinders, rods, crosses, small spheres, two large crescents, and a black spear, all visible in the sky at the same time. The objects were seen flying across the Sun, which gave witnesses the impression of a large aerial battle. Afterward, a black triangular object was reportedly seen close to the ground, and some of the objects in the sky were described as crashing. The broadsheet included a detailed woodcut showing the described objects above the city.

## Skeptical Explanations

Skeptics have offered two main explanations for the event:

- Mass hysteria or collective consciousness: [[Carl Jung]] and others have proposed that mass sightings of this type come from shared psychological processes rather than from physical events outside the witnesses. On this view, the Nuremberg sighting is one of the earliest documented cases of group hysteria producing a shared visual story.
- Sun dog (parhelion): Some researchers suggest the event was a meteorological optical effect, in which ice crystals in the atmosphere create several bright spots around the Sun. Critics note that sun dogs stay still, and that they do not explain the variety of reported shapes, the movement, or the black triangular object described near the ground.

Neither explanation is regarded as fully satisfactory, given how detailed and consistent the witness accounts in the broadsheet are.
