The Defense Support Program (DSP) is a US Air Force satellite system originally designed to detect ballistic missile launches through infrared sensors. DSP satellites operate in geosynchronous orbit and have been a cornerstone of America's early warning infrastructure since the early 1970s. The satellites were developed with significant involvement from TRW (later part of [[Northrop Grumman]]).

## UAP Detection

Beyond its stated missile-detection mission, DSP satellites have reportedly tracked anomalous objects entering or leaving Earth's atmosphere at extreme speeds — objects designated as "[[Fast Walkers]]" by [[NORAD]] and US Northern Command. In communications found during the 2016 Hillary Clinton campaign leaks, [[US SOUTHCOM|USG]] contractor [[Bob Fish]] told Clinton campaign manager [[John Podesta]]: "One of the government programs that collects hard data on unidentified flying objects is the USAF DSP satellite program."

Former Army Criminal Investigation Command agent [[Joseph Stafula]] relayed a case in which a DSP satellite allegedly detected a UAP traveling at 22,000 mph toward Earth, passing within 1.5 miles of the satellite before abruptly changing course.

## Role in the Peru UAP Crash Reconstruction

DSP satellites, managed by the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)]], play a central role in the theoretical reconstruction of the 1997 [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]]. UAP Gerb theorizes that NRO-managed DSP satellites monitoring the egg-shaped craft's crash in Peru provided the intelligence that allowed a [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|DOE NEST]] rapid recovery team to mobilize and reach the site within approximately 8–9 hours.

This theory is supported by a documented 1996–1997 incident in which [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|NEST]] used DSP satellite intelligence from US Space Command to track debris from the Russian Mars 96 probe over South America — establishing a provable pattern of DSP/NEST coordination for recovery operations in the same geographic region and time period as the Peru crash.
