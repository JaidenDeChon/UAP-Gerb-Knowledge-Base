Operation Desert Shield was the US military's defensive operation in response to Iraq's invasion of Kuwait. It began on August 2, 1990, with the rapid deployment of US and coalition forces to Saudi Arabia, both to defend against possible Iraqi aggression and to prepare for possible offensive operations. On January 17, 1991, coalition forces began offensive operations to liberate Kuwait, and Desert Shield became [[Operation Desert Storm]].

## Bob Fish and DSP Personnel Contact

In 1990, during the preparation phase for Operation Desert Shield, [[Bob Fish]] was introduced to [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellite personnel at a secure facility in [[El Segundo, California]]. Fish was then a USG contractor who managed highly classified government communications systems.

Fish had lunch with DSP operators in the facility's cafeteria. Entering it required Top Secret/Sensitive Compartmented Information (TS/SCI) clearance, so everyone present was working on the most sensitive national security programs. Fish first learned about DSP's detection of [[Fast Walkers]] in these conversations, which took place while US space and intelligence assets were being prepared for the Gulf War.

## Fast Walker Discussion Context

The pace and urgency of the Desert Shield preparations likely meant that classified program personnel were working closely together and discussing capabilities. Fish described one DSP operator as "really excited" about detecting a Fast Walker, which suggests the detection may have happened during this period of heightened military activity and satellite surveillance.

The timing matters because Desert Shield was a major test of US space-based surveillance and early warning systems. DSP satellites had a critical role in monitoring Iraqi military activities, Scud missile launches and regional threats. In the same period, DSP personnel were also detecting and discussing anomalous objects entering Earth's atmosphere from deep space. This shows that DSP operations did two things at once: strategic military surveillance and, inadvertently, UAP detection.

## Significance

Operation Desert Shield sets the time and the military situation for one of the most detailed insider accounts of DSP Fast Walker detection. Bob Fish met DSP personnel while a major military operation was being prepared, outside routine peacetime operations. This suggests that multi-program integration and facility sharing during crisis operations can inadvertently give people in separate compartmented programs chances to share information.
