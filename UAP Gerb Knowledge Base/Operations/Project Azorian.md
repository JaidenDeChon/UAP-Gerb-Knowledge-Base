Project Azorian (also called Project Jennifer) was the CIA's covert operation to recover the Soviet ballistic-missile submarine K-129, which sank in the Pacific northwest of Hawaii in 1968. The CIA built a purpose-designed ship for it, the Hughes Glomar Explorer, launched in 1972 under a cover story of deep-sea mining. In 1974 the ship raised part of the submarine from about 1,560 miles northwest of Hawaii. In UAP research the Glomar Explorer is alleged to have been used for further clandestine recoveries, including a downed UFO in 1973.

## The Glomar Explorer

Gerb puts the ship's cost at about $1.68 billion in modern terms. He treats it as an asset of the [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] (NURO), a CIA–Navy office formed in 1969, partly in response to the K-129 sinking. The [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]] and the Navy ran it together. Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]], who later became NURO's director, said that before his time NURO "had been engaged in the efforts to pick up a submarine off the ocean floor with the Glomar Explorer". He said he was first exposed to the program in Hawaii, as assistant chief of staff for intelligence.

From 1978, Ocean Minerals Company, whose prime contractor was Lockheed Missiles and Space Company (now [[Lockheed Martin]]), leased the ship for ostensible deep-sea mining. Gerb cites a Medium article on the Glomar's links to General Motors, Lockheed Martin and UAP to say that Lockheed used the ship for nearly two decades. He suggests the mining work may have been cover for continued NURO operations.

## Alleged 1973 UFO recovery

According to a case in [[Leonard Stringfield]]'s *UFO Crash/Retrieval Status Report III* (case A7), a witness identified as RK heard this story from a sailor in San Diego. In June 1973 a Navy destroyer tracked a teardrop-shaped UFO that made three close passes and one overflight. A lucky hit from the destroyer brought it down into about 350 feet of water between Hawaii and the mainland. The Glomar Explorer recovered it, and it was shipped to Hawaii, then stateside, then Chicago. In September 1973 RK saw a matching 30-foot, silvery-blue, windowless craft on a wooden platform in a Quonset hut at Great Lakes Naval Base. The account was published in the 1980s, before the Glomar's NURO links were widely known. Gerb notes that it matches the chain of events alleged in 2024 by [[Christopher Sharp]]'s sources: recovery at sea, then custody at an [[Office of Naval Research]] location.
