Project Moon Dust (sometimes written "Moondust", and also called Operation Moon Dust) was a classified United States Air Force program whose official task was to locate, recover, and deliver descended foreign space vehicles. Documents obtained through Freedom of Information Act requests show that the program also investigated and collected unidentified flying objects, with field operations running from the early 1960s through at least the mid-1990s. Moon Dust worked under the authority of the [[USAF 1127th Field Activities Group]] and was responsible for collecting Soviet, American, and redacted categories of space vehicles. The Air Force at first denied the program's UFO work, then acknowledged it after US Senators challenged the denial with documents. Files on the 1965 [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|Kecksburg crash]] were associated with this program, and officers from Moon Dust (see [[Moondust Officer]]) were ostensibly the source of classified UAP documents sent to researcher [[Tim Cooper]] through mailbox drops.

## Establishment and Authority

Moon Dust is first mentioned officially in a 1961 US Air Force document from the Air Force's intelligence directorate, AFCIN-1E. The document, a draft policy memo dated 3 November 1961 (which Gerb reads as "AFC 1e draft policy"), proposed giving the [[USAF 1127th Field Activities Group]] collection responsibility for investigating reliably reported unidentified flying objects within the United States. The memo lists Moon Dust as a project that had historically held this duty, with authority to "locate, recover, and deliver descended foreign space vehicles."

A 1967 review for the Assistant Chief of Staff for the Intelligence Community, describing the functions of the 1127th USAF Field Activities Group, confirms that Moon Dust was operational. That year, Moon Dust captured 49 fallen Soviet space objects and 17 US space objects. A large redacted portion of the memo follows, indicating further classified collection categories.

## UFO Investigation and Collection Operations

### 1961 Karachi, Pakistan Sighting

As early as April 25, 1961, Moon Dust recorded observations of three cigar-shaped UFOs flying in close Delta formation above Karachi, Pakistan. The record shows the program was already monitoring UFOs, in addition to collecting space debris that had fallen to Earth. See [[1961 Karachi Pakistan UFO Sighting]].

### 1968 Nepal Circular Disc

In 1968, the Defense Intelligence Department monitored a crashed circular metal disc discovered in Nepal. Four months after the discovery, a State Department cable titled "Moon Dust" went to the [[Defense Intelligence Agency|DIA]], the 1127th, and [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. It described a diplomatic visit to Kathmandu, Nepal as a "fishing expedition for info on space objects." Later cables show that the Royal Nepalese Army showed US officials photos of these space objects. A possible cover story was put together identifying the object as a crashed satellite, even though [[NORAD]] and [[NASA]] data show a 100% successful satellite launch rate in 1968. The records end without confirming whether the US recovered the object. See [[1968 Nepal Circular Disc Crash]].

### 1976 Canada Metal Fragment Request

A 1976 memo obtained through FOIA shows that Moon Dust asked Canadian officials for photos of space-related metals from a non-US satellite. According to the request, these materials would be "of particular interest" to the USAF Foreign Technology Division at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] and to the CIA's Office of Weapons Intelligence.

### 1978 New Zealand Space Fragments

In 1978, Moon Dust investigated space fragments found in New Zealand, described as "spherical or like a pressure vessel." Commander [[David Fravor]] used the same comparison for the [[2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter (Tic Tac)|Tic Tac UAP]] he encountered in 2004, calling it "similar to a propane tank or pressure vessel without any inlets or outlets."

### 1978 Bolivia UFO Crash

On May 6, 1978, at 4:15 PM, thousands of people near El Taire, Bolivia saw a cylindrical object crash into a mountainside. The crash caused a sonic boom heard up to 150 miles away and cracked window panes within a radius of 30 miles. Local Army Corporal [[Natalio Ruiz]] saw the UFO fly above his head and said it looked like "a gigantic wine container emitting a trace of white smoke."

Bolivian military forces and three jets were sent to the wreckage. Local military and police issued the following statement: "Our men have discovered the object and inspected it but have received no further instruction or further action. It is a dull metallic cylinder 12 feet long with a few dents. No one knows what is inside it and we are waiting for the arrival of various technical commissions. A NASA expert is also expected to arrive tomorrow morning."

Two US Air Force officers, [[Colonel Robert Simmons]] and Major [[John Heise]] (rendered "Jesse Haaste" in the video), arrived afterwards. Secret documents obtained through FOIA show that US Secretary of State [[Cyrus Vance]] wrote that "appropriate government agencies have been contacted regarding this crash." The agency in question was Moon Dust, which received film of the object from the Bolivian military and was assigned to monitor the situation. The documents end without saying whether US entities recovered the craft. See [[1978 Bolivia UFO Crash]].

### Peru UFO Intercept Documentation

A [[DOD Joint Chiefs]] briefing document associated with Project Moon Dust corroborated the account of [[Peruvian Air Force]] Lieutenant [[Oscar Santa Maria Huertas]] about his April 11, 1980 encounter at [[La Joya Airbase, Arequipa, Peru|La Joya Airbase]]. According to the briefing, Santa María fired 64 30mm shells at an unidentified silvery orb without damaging it and chased it for 84 kilometers. The same briefing also covered the [[1968 Nepal Circular Disc Crash]], which indicates that Moon Dust documented international UAP incidents as well as retrieving material.

## Connection to Wright-Patterson Air Force Base

Several Moon Dust cables and memos were addressed to [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], in particular to its Foreign Technology Division. Wright-Patterson has long been identified as a central storage site for UFO crash retrievals, including alleged materials from the [[Roswell Crash|Roswell]] incident. Senator [[Barry Goldwater]] famously asked Wright-Patterson officials to see "the room where it was stored" and was told never to ask again. The steady routing of Moon Dust materials to Wright-Patterson supports the base's role as a collection point for anomalous aerospace objects.

## Parallel Classified Programs

Captain [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], director of [[Project Sign]], [[Project Grudge]], and [[Project Blue Book]], disclosed on April 24, 1952 that programs running parallel to the official USG UFO investigations were "conducting a more complete investigation." Dr. [[J. Allen Hynek]], the scientific advisor to Project Blue Book, independently confirmed that other classified programs were studying UFOs alongside Blue Book. Project Moon Dust was one of these parallel programs. It operated at a higher classification level while the public programs managed perception and information control.

## Air Force Denial and Institutional Cover-Up

In 1992, Senators [[Jeff Bingaman]] and [[Pete Domenici]] asked the Air Force about Project Moon Dust. The Air Force replied that "there is no Project Moon Dust. These missions have never existed." The Senators challenged the denial with documents, and the Air Force had to revise its statement and acknowledge that Moon Dust existed and what it did with regard to UFOs.

In 1994, Senator Domenici asked the Air Force for 11 documents about Moon Dust. The Air Force claimed that the project no longer existed and that its files, including any classified reports that may have existed, had been destroyed. This claim is demonstrably false, since Moon Dust documents have since been obtained through FOIA requests.

## Alleged SAP Reorganization and Compartmentalization

The Air Force's denials and its claims of document destruction came during a critical period in UAP program history. According to the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] stated that in the early 1990s, Special Access Programs for crash retrieval and reverse engineering were reorganized and hidden within deeper compartments (SAP-X structures), which made them effectively inaccessible even to senior intelligence officials. The reorganization happened at precisely the time the Senators requested Moon Dust documents, in the mid-1990s.

The timing suggests the Air Force may have destroyed Moon Dust files and denied the program existed as part of a wider effort to compartmentalize UAP crash retrieval operations after the SAP reorganization. It also fits the "Dark Ages" period from 1969 ([[Project Blue Book]] closure) to 2008 (AATIP establishment), when official USG UFO investigation appeared to stop in public while continuing in classified channels.

## Related Programs

Moon Dust is cited with the following programs as part of a wider classified system for collecting UFO material:

- [[Operation Bluefly]]: crash retrieval and rapid response operations
- [[Operation Zodiac]]: a related unacknowledged program
- [[CIA Office of Global Access]]: alleged to coordinate modern crash retrievals. Journalist [[Christopher Sharp]] has accused the office, which was established in 2003, of conducting foreign UAP crash recovery operations using similar logistics and coordination structures.

The Moon Dust documents are rare official confirmation that the US Air Force kept active UFO crash retrieval and investigation capabilities well beyond the 1969 closure of [[Project Blue Book]].
