Project Magnet was the Canadian government's first official investigation into UFOs, run by the Department of Transport under senior radio engineer [[Wilbert B. Smith]] from its approval in December 1950 until its official termination in mid-1954. Its stated aim was to study whether the Earth's magnetic field could be exploited as a source of propulsion, the principle Smith suspected lay behind the flight of "flying saucers." It is remembered in UFO research chiefly because of what prompted it: Smith's 1950 memo relaying a Washington source's claim that the subject was the most highly classified in the United States government, a source later identified as the physicist [[Robert Sarbacher]].

## Origins

In 1950, Smith wrote to the Controller of Telecommunications in the Department of Transport asking for Canada to begin an official study of UFOs, specifically of magnetism as a form of propulsion. The memo passed on four claims from his Washington contact: that the matter was classified even higher than the hydrogen bomb; that flying saucers exist; that their modus operandi was unknown but a small group headed by [[Vannevar Bush]] was making a concentrated effort to understand it; and that US authorities considered the entire matter to be of tremendous significance. Smith's handwritten notes of the underlying conversation with Sarbacher were later found in his estate by historian [[Arthur Bray]].

According to UAP Gerb, the memo "directly led" to the formation of two Canadian programs: Project Magnet, which studied the possibility of reverse-engineering UFO magnetic propulsion concepts, and its companion [[Project Second Story]], which was to recommend a course of action on UFOs to the Canadian government. Project Magnet was approved in December 1950, with the backing of the Department of Transport's air services leadership.

## Operations

Smith directed the project using Department of Transport resources. In 1953 it moved into borrowed Department facilities at Shirley's Bay, on the Ottawa River, where Smith set up instruments intended to register the passage of anomalous objects.

The Department officially ended the project in mid-1954, citing adverse publicity and a view that Smith's work had drifted outside its mandate. Smith continued the research informally, without government funding, until his death in 1962.

## Legacy

The UAP Gerb video on Sarbacher treats Project Magnet as evidence that Canada was "close on the heels" of US UFO research from the start, and draws a line from it to 2023, when Canadian member of Parliament Larry Maguire wrote to the Minister of National Defence about research and development on recovered UAP material in connection with retrieval operations by the Five Eyes nations.
