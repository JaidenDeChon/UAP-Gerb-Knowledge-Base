The Manhattan Project was the United States' secret World War II program, run with the United Kingdom and Canada, to develop the first atomic bombs. Directed militarily by General [[Leslie Groves]] from September 1942, with [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] as scientific director of its weapons laboratory at [[Los Alamos National Laboratory|Los Alamos]], it produced the Trinity test of 16 July 1945 and the bombs dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki. It was administered through the Manhattan Engineer District (MED), an Army Corps of Engineers organization, and was succeeded by the civilian [[Atomic Energy Commission]], created by the Atomic Energy Act of 1946, which took over on 1 January 1947. In UFO research, and above all in the work of UAP Gerb, the project matters less for the bomb than for its security apparatus, which [[David Grusch]] says was "overlaid" onto the UFO crash retrieval effort. UAP Gerb calls the result the [[Manhattan Project 2.0]].

## Scale and organization

According to UAP Gerb, the project cost nearly $24 billion in today's money and employed nearly 130,000 people across widely separated sites, including Los Alamos, [[Oak Ridge, Tennessee|Oak Ridge]] and Hanford, most of whom did not know what they were building. Its administrative shell, the Manhattan Engineer District, was named after the Army Corps of Engineers' ordinary geographic districts, a deliberately mundane name that hid the program while it handled acquisition, contracting, construction and personnel security. Groves reported through an unusually short chain of command, directly to the Secretary of War and Army Chief of Staff [[George C. Marshall]], which let him bypass normal acquisition channels.

Political oversight rested with a small **Top Policy Group**: President Franklin D. Roosevelt (later [[Harry Truman]]), Vice President Henry Wallace, Secretary of War Henry Stimson, Marshall, [[Vannevar Bush]] of the Office of Scientific Research and Development, and Bush's deputy James Conant. Truman, who as a senator had been refused information about the project's unexplained budget lines, was not briefed as vice president and learned of it only on taking office in 1945.

## Security apparatus

The project made "need-to-know" famous. Groves introduced tiered compartments in which staff were read into only what their tasks required, a practice Oppenheimer complained hindered science. Wartime nuclear information was controlled first by voluntary, then mandatory, self-censorship under Bush's OSRD and the MED, and Groves treated fission secrets as "born secret." The Atomic Energy Act of 1946 turned that practice into law as [[Restricted Data|restricted data]]. The project also hid work in plain sight: [[Enrico Fermi]]'s Chicago Pile-1, the first artificial reactor (1942), was built beneath the stands of Stagg Field at the University of Chicago.

In 1943 the MED created Special Engineer Detachments (SEDs), Army units of scientifically trained soldiers who worked beside civilian scientists at Los Alamos, Sandia Base and Oak Ridge. At Marshall's suggestion it also ran the [[Alsos Mission|Alsos Missions]], an intelligence operation outside normal military channels that, with the T-Force securing units, seized German atomic scientists, materials and facilities, some in Soviet-controlled territory. The Soviet Union nevertheless penetrated the project through Klaus Fuchs.

## Alleged role as a template for UFO secrecy

UAP Gerb argues that five of the project's security pillars (compartmentalization, organizational architecture, physical security, classification and information control, and a political shield) were copied onto the UFO crash retrieval effort between 1947 and 1954. In his account, the MED model survives in Air Force structures such as the [[Rapid Capabilities Office (RCO)|Rapid Capabilities Office]]; custody of recovered craft went to the project's successor laboratories under the Atomic Energy Commission; the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]] extended restricted data to cover recovered material; and the Top Policy Group became a UFO control group inside the [[National Security Council]]. He names Vannevar Bush and George C. Marshall as the figures who carried the project's security model across, and treats Alsos and T-Force as the blueprint for UFO crash retrieval teams. Several project veterans are alleged to have worked on early crash retrievals, including [[Edward Bushnell Doll]], a physicist on the project's [[Project Alberta]], whom an eyewitness accused of coordinating the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|1953 Kingman retrieval]].
