Project Sand Dollar was a highly classified United States Navy program tasked with the discovery, monitoring, and retrieval of militarily sensitive hardware and other items with national security importance from the seafloor. First disclosed in fragmentary form by [[John P. Craraven]] in his 2002 memoir *The Silent War: The Cold War Battle Beneath the Sea*, Sand Dollar is notable for several reasons: its extraordinary compartmentalization (buried within a program hidden within the Polaris subprogram), the global scope of its inventory, and its temporal relationship to a series of Navy deep-submergence capability developments that UAP researchers argue were built specifically to service it.

## Discovery and Briefing

In 1965, [[John P. Craraven]] — chief scientist of the Navy Special Projects Office and founder of the [[Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP)]] — was given a high-level intelligence briefing by a naval intelligence officer. He was informed of the existence of an "extremely sensitive program" named Sand Dollar and was shown a classified inventory itemizing objects of interest distributed across seafloors worldwide. According to Craraven:

> "The salvageable items of interest were spread around the globe. There were plenty of interesting objects on ocean floors worldwide, not just in the Bermuda Triangle. And there was an equal distribution of these objects between those that were lying on the continental shelf and those in deep oceans."

The inventory showed a distribution of objects across both continental shelf and deep ocean locations globally, with a skew toward the continental shelf because of shallower water and greater susceptibility to storm disturbance. Craraven noted that much of the continental shelf hardware had already been salvaged or scattered.

## Compartmentalization

Sand Dollar was buried within the structure of a separate secret program, which was itself hidden within the Polaris subprogram — a pattern of nested compartmentalization consistent with that observed in other classified naval programs, including the [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] (NURO) structure. Craraven wrote that he was tasked to manage the program using the assets of the DSSP "in such a manner that my family and friends and closest professional colleagues would have no idea that I was involved in such a project." As of Craraven's 2002 writing, Sand Dollar remained classified.

## Relationship to Deep Submergence Systems Project

UAP researchers argue that the DSSP — launched in 1964, one year before Craraven received the Sand Dollar briefing — was in part engineered to satisfy requirements identified by Sand Dollar. The DSSP produced the DSRV-1 (Mystic) and DSRV-2 (Avalon) deep submergence rescue vehicles as well as clandestine submarines such as the NR-1. The DSSP also proposed six DSRV units and a family of Deep Submergence Search Vehicles (DSSVs) with 20,000-foot operational depths; UAP Gerb theorizes that at least some of the unacknowledged DSSV-type vessels were built and deployed for Sand Dollar operations.

## UAP Research Interpretation

UAP Gerb argues that Sand Dollar may represent the Navy's first formalized UFO legacy program — a program specifically designed to identify, track, and recover non-human craft from the ocean floor under the cover of generic "militarily sensitive hardware" language. This interpretation is supported by:

- The global distribution of objects in the Sand Dollar inventory, with no regional restriction
- The radioactive signatures of some objects (consistent with the description of the [[1991 North Atlantic USO Retrieval]] case, where the initial detection was a radioactive signal)
- The timing of Sand Dollar's activation in relation to the alleged 1962 Bluegill Triple Prime nuclear test UFO recovery, which disabled a nearby craft that fell into the ocean
- Craraven's own framing of the program as tracking objects of unknown classification on the seafloor worldwide
