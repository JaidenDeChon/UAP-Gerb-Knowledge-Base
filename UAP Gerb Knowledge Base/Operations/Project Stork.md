Project Stork was a classified UFO investigation program that the [[Air Force Technical Intelligence Center]] (ATIC) contracted to [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] beginning in 1952. It ran in parallel to the publicly acknowledged [[Project Blue Book]], and its purpose was independent, non-public analysis of official UFO sighting reports. Blue Book was mainly a public-facing operation for managing information. Project Stork was a separate analytical track, run by a contractor, with access to all of the Air Force's UFO case files.

## Origins and Purpose

In 1952, ATIC commissioned Battelle to set up a machine-indexing system for official UFO sighting reports under the codename Project Stork. A memorandum dated January 9, 1953, written by Battelle's Dr. [[Howard C. Cross]] and classified Secret, proposed that Project Stork "assist the Air Force in reassuring the public that everything is well under control with respect to the UFO problem." The memo mentioned Captain [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], who was then directing [[Project Blue Book]], which indicates that the public investigation program and the parallel Battelle contract were coordinated.

## Robertson Panel Concealment

Dr. [[J. Allen Hynek]] raised the existence of Project Stork when he attempted to testify at the January 1953 [[Robertson Panel]], a scientific advisory committee on UFOs convened by the CIA. His attempt to bring up the parallel program there shows that people inside the official investigation structure knew about Project Stork.

## Special Report 14

On May 5, 1955, Battelle and ATIC jointly published Special Report 14, a comprehensive analysis of 3,200 UFO sightings. The report concluded that UFOs were not aircraft "beyond human scientific knowledge" because no physical matter had ever been recovered from any UFO sighting. The Computer UFO Network (CUFON) later recovered seven Project Stork status reports and made them available to researchers.

UAP researchers argue that Special Report 14's conclusion directly contradicts Battelle's own classified 1949 research on [[Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium Alloy)]]. That shape-memory material was analyzed for Wright-Patterson Air Force Base years before the alloy was officially discovered in public, and it is alleged to have come from the 1947 Roswell crash. On this basis, the publication of Special Report 14 is characterized as deliberate disinformation rather than a genuine analytical conclusion.

## Significance

Project Stork is one of the earliest documented examples of the U.S. government using private contractors to investigate UFOs outside the normal chain of congressional oversight. The program was classified, carried out by a contractor, and run parallel to but separate from official programs. That structure anticipated the model later cited by [[David Grusch]] and others as the basis for ongoing UAP legacy programs. Project Stork combined genuine analysis (the nitinol contract work) with public reassurance (Special Report 14), an example of the two-track approach to managing UFO information that UAP researchers argue has persisted to the present day.
