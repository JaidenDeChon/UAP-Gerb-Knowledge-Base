Project Alberta was the operational delivery section of the [[Manhattan Project]]. It was responsible for the design, procurement, assembly and delivery of the atomic bombs used against Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. Based mainly on Tinian Island in the Pacific, it linked the scientific and engineering work done at [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]] to the combat use of nuclear weapons. In research on UAP legacy programs, Project Alberta matters mainly because [[Edward Bushnell Doll]] held a critical role in it. Doll was a Manhattan Project veteran who later allegedly coordinated the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]], so his career forms a biographical chain from early nuclear weapons administration to early UAP exploitation activities.

## Role in the Manhattan Project

Project Alberta was organized under Project Y (Los Alamos) and operated within the wider [[Manhattan Project|Manhattan Engineer District (MED)]], commanded by [[Leslie Groves]]. At Tinian, Alberta personnel did the final assembly of the Little Boy (uranium gun-type) and Fat Man (plutonium implosion-type) weapons, loaded them into B-29 Superfortresses, and coordinated with the combat units of the 509th Composite Group, which were tasked with delivering them. The project's personnel included military and civilian scientific staff who held the highest levels of compartmented knowledge of the program.

## Edward Bushnell Doll's Connection

[[Edward Bushnell Doll]] was a Manhattan Project veteran who later joined [[TRW]] Systems Group, staying from about 1955 to 1977. UAP Gerb research identifies him as having played a critical role within Project Alberta. After the war, Doll moved into the overlapping defense and scientific infrastructure that administered the nuclear weapons program and, researchers allege, early UAP exploitation activities as well.

Doll is also identified as the man who coordinated the recovery of a crashed unknown object near [[Kingman, Arizona]] on May 21, 1953. The recovery was carried out by a task force overseen by the Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP) during the [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]] nuclear tests. The witness [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], a field engineer, stated that Doll summoned him to the Kingman crash site. He gave his account to researcher [[Raymond Fowler]] in 1973 under the pseudonym "Fritz Werner". Researchers treat this line from Project Alberta through Doll to the Kingman crash as their central biographical evidence that Manhattan Project personnel and administrative structures were recruited directly into early UAP crash exploitation.

## Significance in the Legacy Program Context

UAP Gerb's "Manhattan Project 2.0" investigation argues that the atomic bomb program's security architecture was deliberately transplanted onto the UFO exploitation program. That architecture included compartmentalization, need-to-know rules, the FFRDC/GOCO institutional model, and direct reporting chains that bypassed normal acquisition channels. On this account, Project Alberta veterans such as Doll, who had worked inside that system, were among the most natural people to staff early crash retrieval and exploitation operations because of their clearance levels, their relevant technical expertise and their established loyalty within a proven secrecy framework. The AFSWP was established on January 29, 1947, and designed specifically to bridge atomic and conventional military capabilities. It was the organizational vehicle that kept Project Alberta-era personnel available for UAP-related tasks in the late 1940s and early 1950s.
