Project Second Story was an official Canadian government UFO investigation program set up in the early 1950s alongside [[Project Magnet]]. Project Magnet studied UAP propulsion systems and whether magnetic field propulsion could be reverse-engineered. Project Second Story's task was to analyze what the UFO phenomenon meant for policy, security and strategy, and to recommend what the government should do.

## Establishment and Authority

Project Second Story was created after [[Wilbert B. Smith]] sent a memo in 1950 to the Controller of Telecommunications at Canada's Department of Transport. In it, Smith passed on what "discreet enquiries through the Canadian Embassy staff in Washington" had told him: the subject of flying saucers was the most highly classified in the US government, rating higher than the hydrogen bomb, and a small group headed by [[Vannevar Bush]] was making a concentrated effort to learn how they worked. The memo names no source. Gerb identifies it as [[Robert Sarbacher]], a US DoD consultant, from Smith's notes of his September 1950 meeting with Sarbacher and Sarbacher's own confirmation in 1983.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the memo passed on what Sarbacher had confirmed and that Bush's group was investigating recovered craft. The memo ([[Wilbert Smith Memo]]) credits its claims to enquiries through Canadian Embassy staff, names no one, and says only that the group was trying to learn the saucers' modus operandi; the link to Sarbacher comes from Smith's notes.

Smith's memo led directly to Canada authorizing two parallel UAP programs:

1. [[Project Magnet]], a Department of Transport study, directed by Smith, of whether the Earth's magnetic field could be used for propulsion, the principle Smith suspected lay behind the flying saucers.
2. Project Second Story, an investigation of strategy and policy meant to recommend how the government should respond to the UFO phenomenon.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions called Project Magnet "a scientific investigation into UAP propulsion". Its stated aim, as Smith's memo and the Project Magnet page give it, was to study drawing on the Earth's magnetic field for propulsion.

## Objectives

Project Second Story sought to:

- Assess the national security implications of UAP activity over Canadian airspace.
- Evaluate the reliability and significance of UFO reports.
- Coordinate with US authorities on UAP-related intelligence and research.
- Recommend policy actions to the Canadian government regarding public disclosure, military response, and scientific research priorities.

The program knew about the US government's highly classified UAP programs, as Smith's meeting with Sarbacher documents. This put Canada in a unique position, running an open scientific investigation (Project Magnet) and a classified strategic analysis (Project Second Story) at the same time.

## Relationship to Project Magnet

The two projects complemented each other but had separate jobs. Project Magnet was a scientific and engineering effort to understand, and possibly replicate, UAP propulsion systems. Project Second Story was an intelligence and policy program that looked at what the phenomenon meant for Canadian national security and international relations.

Both programs showed that Canada formally accepted that the UFO phenomenon deserved serious government attention. That view was shaped by insider disclosures from credentialed US sources such as Sarbacher.

## Historical Context

Project Second Story ran during a period of intense UAP activity over North America. Events of the time included:

- The 1952 [[1952 UFOs Over Washington DC|Washington DC UFO Incident]], in which multiple unidentified objects appeared over the US capital on radar and were observed visually.
- Continued sightings over sensitive military installations, including atomic research facilities and air defense radar networks.
- Rising Cold War tensions, which raised the possibility that UFOs might be Soviet reconnaissance technology.

Because Canada shared intelligence with the United States through the Five Eyes relationship, Canadian officials had access to some US UAP data. The full extent of the US crash retrieval and reverse engineering programs that Sarbacher described still stayed compartmentalized, even from close allies.

## Public Records and Closure

Less is known about Project Second Story's findings and operations than about Project Magnet, which produced some public documentation of its activities. Because the program dealt with security and policy, its reports were likely classified, and they have not been fully declassified or released to the public.

Project Magnet and Project Second Story were both closed in the mid-1950s, though UFO sightings in Canada continued. Why they ended is not fully clear. The likely reasons were a combination of limited scientific progress, political pressures and shifting government priorities.

## Significance

Project Second Story is one of the earliest examples of a national government setting up a formal, policy-level program to assess the UAP phenomenon in terms of national security and international relations. It was created as a direct result of insider testimony from [[Robert Sarbacher]]. His confirmation to [[Wilbert B. Smith]] that UFOs were real and highly classified prompted Canada to take the subject seriously at the highest levels of government.

The program also shows that Canada, through Smith's direct contact with US officials, knew of American crash retrieval and reverse engineering efforts as early as 1950. That made Canada one of the few allied nations with insight into the true scope of US UAP programs during the early Cold War.
