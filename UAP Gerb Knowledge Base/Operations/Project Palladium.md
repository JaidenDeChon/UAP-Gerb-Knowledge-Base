Project Palladium was a classified CIA program active in the 1960s that used naval vessels, submarines, radar reflectors, and electronic jamming techniques to project false radar signatures of UFOs into Soviet air defense radar systems. Its purpose was to map Soviet air defense responses to perceived UAP intrusions, testing detection thresholds and reaction protocols. The program was led by Samuel Eugene "Gene" Poteat, a CIA scientific intelligence officer under the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology who was also technical director of the Navy's Special Programs Office (see [[Navy Special Program]]). Project Palladium was not publicly disclosed until Poteat wrote about it in a 1998 CIA journal article, so its appearance in [[MJ-12 Documents|Majestic-12 documents]] rated moderate-to-high confidence is of particular interest.

## Background

Palladium used clandestine naval assets (submarines and surface vessels) along with airborne electronic countermeasure equipment and balloon decoys to generate realistic UAP signatures in Soviet radar systems. It was designed to serve two purposes at once: to map the precise parameters of Soviet air defenses and to provide a "true picture of how Soviet air defenses perceived UFOs." To do both, the false signatures had to look genuine enough to test Soviet radar operators, which required detailed knowledge of what genuine UAP radar returns looked like.

Palladium did not receive its code name until April 1962, when Dr. Albert Wheelon took over as head of the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology (DS&T), although the operational concept existed before then. According to Poteat, only six people knew about Palladium in the 1960s.

## Majestic-12 Document Connections

Researchers have identified references to Palladium in the [[MJ-12 Documents|Majestic-12 documents]]. The "TUR Memo" is a Majestic-12 document dated 28 June 1961 that Ryan and Robert Wood rated moderate-to-high confidence. It purports to show President Kennedy writing to CIA Director Allen Dulles in a national security memorandum with the subject line "Review of MJ12 Intelligence Operations as They Relate to Cold War Psychological Warfare Plans." It is argued that the "psychological warfare plans" it mentions refer directly to Project Palladium operations.

Palladium is also described in an "Operation Review" document purportedly written by Allen Dulles on 5 November 1961. It describes the CIA creating UAP signatures with airborne electronic countermeasure equipment and balloon decoys in order to map Soviet air defenses.

These references matter because Palladium was not publicly disclosed until 1998. If the Majestic-12 documents that mention it were fabricated after 1984, when Jaime Shandera received the original MJ-12 documents, the forger would have needed classified access to a program known to only six people in the 1960s. This is circumstantial evidence that whoever produced those specific MJ-12 documents had genuine classified access.

## Connection to Naval UFO Legacy Programs

Gene Poteat ran Palladium while he was technical director of the Navy Special Program Office, which links the program to the wider set of naval UFO legacy programs. The Navy Special Program Office is an umbrella classification structure for clandestine naval undersea operations, and Gerb argues that it takes in the [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] (NURO). Poteat worked in the CIA DS&T, the NRO and the Navy Special Program Office at the same time, the same cross-agency pattern seen among senior figures in the alleged UFO legacy program hierarchy.
