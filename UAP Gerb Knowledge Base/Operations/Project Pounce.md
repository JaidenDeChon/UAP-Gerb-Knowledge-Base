**Project Pounce** was a 1952 US Air Force proposal to form a dedicated flight of stripped-down F-94C interceptors, carrying special nose cameras, to chase and photograph UFOs. The commander of the air-defense division at Kirtland Air Force Base near Albuquerque, New Mexico, drew it up after UFO sightings over the area. It appears in the evidence reviewed by the January 1953 [[Robertson Panel]] as an "outline of investigation of UFOs proposed by Kirtland Air Force Base." In later decades the name resurfaced in the claims of [[Steve Wilson|Colonel Steve Wilson]] as an alleged crash-retrieval unit.

## The 1952 Proposal

According to [[Edward J. Ruppelt]]'s 1956 book *The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects*, the plan called for an operational unit of six aircraft, two of them always on alert, stripped of combat gear for peak performance and based where UFOs were most often reported. The Air Force took the plan seriously but shelved it because it could not spare the interceptors during the Korean War.

## Wilson's Account

In testimony gathered by [[Richard Boylan]] in 1996-1997, Wilson claimed that after serving at [[Papoose Lake|Area S4]] in the early 1970s he headed Project Pounce. In his account it was an elite Air Force and "National Reconnaissance Organization" special forces unit that retrieved downed UFOs and supplied cover stories, such as crashed experimental aircraft or mining work. He said it included a team called Delta, an arm of the NRO, and that Delta had a truck-mounted electromagnetic pulse cannon called "the Equalizer", built by LTV, as well as [[XH-75D|XH-75D]] anti-gravity helicopters from [[Teledyne Ryan]].

Gerb notes that the 1952 proposal was exactly the kind of imagery-intelligence mission later handled by the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]], which was founded nine years afterward. He suggests that Pounce may have been re-established under the NRO and its Corona satellites, or may have served as a siloed umbrella for retrieval operations. He also acknowledges that it is unclear how an interceptor-camera plan would have become a crash-retrieval task force.
