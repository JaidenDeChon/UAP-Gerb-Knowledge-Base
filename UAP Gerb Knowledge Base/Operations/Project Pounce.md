**Project Pounce** was a 1952 US Air Force proposal to form a dedicated flight of stripped-down F-94C interceptors, carrying special nose cameras, to chase and photograph UFOs. Drawn up by the commander of the air-defense division at Kirtland Air Force Base near Albuquerque, New Mexico, after UFO sightings over the area, it appears in the evidence reviewed by the January 1953 [[Robertson Panel]] as an "outline of investigation of UFOs proposed by Kirtland Air Force Base." In later decades the name resurfaced in the claims of [[Steve Wilson|Colonel Steve Wilson]] as an alleged crash-retrieval unit.

## The 1952 Proposal

According to [[Edward J. Ruppelt]]'s 1956 book *The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects*, the plan called for an operational unit of six aircraft, two of them always on alert, stripped of combat gear for peak performance and based where UFOs were most often reported. It was taken seriously but shelved because the Air Force could not spare the interceptors during the Korean War.

## Wilson's Account

In testimony gathered by [[Richard Boylan]] in 1996-1997, Wilson claimed that after serving at [[Papoose Lake|Area S4]] in the early 1970s he headed Project Pounce, described as an elite Air Force and "National Reconnaissance Organization" special forces unit that retrieved downed UFOs and supplied cover stories such as crashed experimental aircraft or mining work. He said it included a team called Delta, an arm of the NRO, equipped with a truck-mounted electromagnetic pulse cannon called "the Equalizer" built by LTV and with [[XH-75D|XH-75D]] anti-gravity helicopters from [[Teledyne Ryan]].

UAP Gerb notes that the 1952 proposal was an imagery-intelligence mission of exactly the kind later handled by the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]], founded nine years afterward. He suggests Pounce may have been re-established under the NRO with its Corona satellites, or served as a siloed umbrella for retrieval operations, while acknowledging it is unclear how an interceptor-camera plan would have become a crash-retrieval task force.
