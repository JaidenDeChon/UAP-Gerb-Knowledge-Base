Kona Blue was a proposed waived Unacknowledged Special Access Program (USAP) that would have acted as a Department of Homeland Security Science and Technology "catcher's mitt" for recovered non-human craft materials held by [[Lockheed Martin]] since the 1950s. Senator [[Harry Reid]] championed the program as part of the [[AAWSAP]] initiative to bring alleged UAP crash retrieval materials into a government-controlled research framework with Congressional oversight. Kona Blue is documented evidence of an attempt to formally transfer UAP materials from a defense contractor to a DIA-administered program. CIA and DNI officials ultimately stonewalled that attempt between 2008 and 2011.

## Program Structure and Intent

Kona Blue was designed as a PAP (prospective special access program). It would have set up a secure Special Access Program structure to:
- Receive recovered UAP craft materials and possibly complete craft from [[Lockheed Martin]]
- Operate under [[DHS (Department of Homeland Security)|Department of Homeland Security]] Science and Technology oversight to avoid traditional DoD/IC gatekeeping structures
- Give Senator Harry Reid and Congressional intelligence committees visibility into UAP material custody
- Enable [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]] (BASS) to conduct research and analysis on the transferred materials under [[AAWSAP]] contract
- Keep waived USAP security protocols to protect sources, methods, and the materials themselves

The program's name "Kona Blue" appears to follow the standard DoD/IC cryptonym convention for compartmented programs.

## Material Transfer Attempts ( 2008-2011)

[[James T. Ryder]], Vice President of [[Lockheed Martin]] Space Systems Corporation and head of the Advanced Technology Center, reportedly made at least two separate attempts to transfer recovered UAP materials to the AAWSAP program via Kona Blue:

**First Attempt (~2008-2009):**
According to [[Eric Davis]] and AARO historical documents, the first transfer attempt came shortly after AAWSAP's contract was awarded to BASS in September 2008. It was reportedly blocked by [[James Clapper]] or other senior DoD/IC officials.

**Second Attempt (2011):**
A more formal attempt followed in 2011, using the Kona Blue PAP structure. Documents that Representative Tim Burchett entered into the record of the November 2024 House Oversight UAP hearing give these details:
- Ryder proposed a "UAP Material Divestment Plan" to AAWSAP leadership
- The materials were described as residing in "a specific facility known to me" (the facility's location was provided to the Inspector General)
- The materials originated from "crash retrieval materials from the 1950s and other historical operations"
- Some sources claim the materials included craft hull fragments or the complete [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|1953 Kingman, Arizona crash]] vehicle
- [[Tara O'Toole]], DHS Under Secretary for Science and Technology, was approached to establish the PAP. According to AARO's declassified history, she was briefed in May 2011 and established Kona Blue as a prospective SAP in July 2011, and the program was terminated in February 2012
- [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]] built a 5,000 square foot underground vault specifically to receive and secure the materials. It was 15-20 feet deep, with 3-foot thick walls, ceilings and floors, multiple tunnels, security doors and a steel vault door
- The vault was designed to be operational by late 2009 or early 2010

## Stonewalling by CIA and DNI

Several senior intelligence officials worked together to kill the 2011 Kona Blue transfer:

**[[Glenn Gaffney]]**, CIA Deputy Director for Science and Technology:
- Met directly with Lockheed Martin executives, including a senior VP and an executive VP
- Was "very antagonistic" about the company releasing materials to DIA/BASS
- Wielded decisive authority to block the transfer from CIA's position
- Was publicly identified by [[David Grusch]] and [[Christopher Sharp]] as a gatekeeper of UAP legacy programs

**[[Robert Cardillo]]**, Deputy Director of National Intelligence for Intelligence Integration:
- Allegedly exercised veto authority over the transfer from the DNI position
- Later joined the board of [[Peraton]] in 2019. Peraton is the corporation that may ultimately have received Lockheed's divested materials through a separate corporate transaction

**Possible [[Lockheed Martin]] Internal Opposition:**
[[Mary K. Sturtevant]] was Lockheed VP for Intelligence, Joint, and Science and Technology Programs during this period. Given her extensive CIA background and prior work in the joint CIA DS&T/Directorate of Operations office, she may have coordinated with Gaffney from inside Lockheed.

## Official Justification for Rejection

According to declassified AARO documents on Kona Blue, DHS and the other agencies involved ultimately shut down the proposal, citing "skepticism regarding the authenticity of the purported materials". That official explanation contradicts testimony and documents suggesting that high-level stakeholders blocked the transfer to keep control of siloed programs, not because they doubted the materials were authentic.

## AARO Declassification and Tim Phillips Statements

On July 30, 2025, former AARO Deputy Director Tim Phillips made controversial public statements about Kona Blue. He claimed it was established "as the home for all this paranormal UFO crap" and alleged that multiple whistleblowers had cited Kona Blue to justify their claims to Congress. Phillips gave no examples or evidence for these assertions.

[[AARO]] released a declassified Kona Blue slide deck that confirmed the program's structure and DHS involvement but framed it as a proposal ultimately rejected over doubts about the materials' authenticity. UAP researchers dispute that account; they see the declassified materials as confirming the program's legitimacy rather than its supposed paranormal focus.

## Significance for UAP Disclosure

Kona Blue is the best-documented instance of an attempt to formally transfer UAP material from a defense contractor to a government research program with Congressional oversight. The program's failure illustrates:
- Active resistance by CIA and DNI gatekeepers to Congressional visibility of UAP materials
- The existence of Lockheed Martin custody of recovered materials dating to the 1950s (acknowledged in DoD-cleared documents)
- Senator Harry Reid's genuine efforts to bring UAP programs into legitimate government oversight structures
- The "special access program within special access programs" structure that keeps UAP materials siloed from even senior government officials

The blocking of the program by [[Glenn Gaffney]] and [[Robert Cardillo]] is cited as evidence that elements of the IC actively work to prevent oversight and maintain the legacy program status quo.

## Theorized Link to the 1953 Kingman Crash

The UAP Gerb video *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* puts forward a specific theory connecting the Kona Blue materials to the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|1953 Kingman, Arizona crash]]. The video says it has "heard credible rumors and indirect confirmation from Hal Puthoff" that the materials in the Kona Blue technology transfer agreement included some of the Kingman wreckage. That raises the possibility that there may have been as many as three separate Kingman crashes. When Gerb raised this theory directly with [[Hal Puthoff]], Puthoff reportedly gave "a bit of a cheeky look and refused to comment further". The video treats this non-denial as a reason to be more confident that the Kona Blue materials came from the 1953 Kingman retrieval, which would potentially trace custody of that wreckage from 1953 through to Lockheed Martin's divestment attempt in the 2010s.

## Eric Davis's Account of the Materials-Only Program

The same video relays [[Eric Davis]]'s detailed description, given in a podcast appearance with Thirdeye Drops and Michael Phillip, of the specific compartmented program under [[James T. Ryder]] that was connected to Kona Blue. According to Davis, this locked program dealt strictly with materials and metallurgy. He said it did not deal with power/propulsion systems, airframes, biologics, or anything beyond material sampling and analysis. Davis said he has long wondered why no successful derivation or [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicle]] has ever come out of recovered UAP materials. He offered disinformation, partial information and institutional hubris as possible explanations without settling on one. The video also notes that [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], the primary Kingman witness, estimated that the craft hit the ground at over 1,000 mph yet suffered little to no damage or deformation. In the video's assessment, that fits a material set that would have been exceedingly difficult for Lockheed engineers to analyze.

## Possible Alternate Disposition: Veritas Capital Transaction

In his paper "Sub Rosa," UAP researcher Rob Jones presents a theory that after Kona Blue was blocked, Lockheed may have successfully divested the materials through a complex corporate transaction. In 2010, Veritas Capital bought Lockheed Martin's Enterprise Integration Group (EIG) for $815 million in cash. EIG was a unit deeply involved in highly classified systems engineering for US intelligence agencies. Jones theorizes that this deal may have included the transfer of UAP materials to what eventually became [[Peraton]], after multiple mergers and rebrandings under Veritas Capital. Robert Cardillo joined Peraton's advisory board in 2019, which potentially supports this theory.
