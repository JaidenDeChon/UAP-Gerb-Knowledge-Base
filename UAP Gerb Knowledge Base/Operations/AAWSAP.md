The Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP) was a Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) program that ran from 2008 to 2012. It was funded at approximately $22 million and championed by then-Senate Majority Leader [[Harry Reid]]. The DIA awarded AAWSAP through a sole-source contract dated September 22, 2008, to Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS), a subsidiary of Robert Bigelow's Bigelow Aerospace. James T. Lacatski was the program's director on the DIA side. AAWSAP funded 38 Defense Intelligence Reference Documents on topics including UAP flight characteristics, advanced propulsion concepts, and related aerospace science. Personnel and reporting connected to AAWSAP became closely associated with the Pentagon's separate but overlapping [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]] effort. A December 2017 *New York Times* article reporting that both programs existed is widely credited with cementing this conflation in public understanding.

The video argues that [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|ATIP]], by contrast, was actually an informal, unfunded working group. According to the video, AAWSAP was the intended recipient of the [[Kona Blue]] material-transfer proposal from [[Lockheed Martin]]. Through that proposal, Lockheed Vice President [[James T. Ryder]] reportedly sought to divest recovered UAP materials into a DHS-administered Special Access Program structure around 2008–2011.
