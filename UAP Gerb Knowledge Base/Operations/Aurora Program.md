The **Aurora Program** is an alleged black-budget US aerospace program, named after a line item in mid-1980s Defense Department budget documents, that UFO researchers and several witnesses describe as the umbrella for a family of secret high-performance craft. According to former Air Force Master Sergeant [[Edgar Fouche|Edgar Fouché]], its most exotic product was the [[TR-3B]], a triangular [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicle]] code-named Astra. The US government has never acknowledged an aircraft program by this name.

## The budget line

In *Alien Reproduction Vehicle - TR-3B and the Flying Triangles*, UAP Gerb points to DOD procurement documents listing an "Aurora" project that the Pentagon planned to fund at $80 million in fiscal year 1986 and $2.3 billion in fiscal year 1987. The host notes that this 25-fold increase was, at the time, double the cost of the B-2 Spirit bomber program, and treats it as a possible clue to the family of craft Fouché described.

## Craft attributed to the program

- **TR-3B (Astra).** Fouché's "tactical reconnaissance" triangle, allegedly lifted by a [[Magnetic Field Disruptor (MFD)|magnetic field disruptor]] reverse-engineered from non-human technology at [[Sandia National Laboratories]] and [[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory]], managed by the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]], [[CIA]] and [[NSA]], and built by [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Boeing]], [[Northrop Grumman]], [[Teledyne Ryan]] and others. Fouché said three operational models were flying by 1994.
- **TR-3A.** A more conventional craft in the same "TR" line, variously described as a delta-shaped fighter with a cockpit using pulse detonation engines and [[Electrogravitics|electrogravitics]], or as a "pumpkin seed". *Air Force Monthly* speculated that a TR-3A was the aircraft in the 1994 [[Boscombe Down Incident]].
- **Pulsar.** The unmanned pulse-detonation craft that [[Brad Sorenson]] said he saw alongside the saucer-shaped ARVs at the 1988 [[Norton Air Force Base Air Show]].
- **SR-33A.** A craft introduced by [[Michael Wolf]], said to combine conventional fuel with anti-gravity field propulsion in service of a secret space program; UAP Gerb suggests Aurora "may have included" it.

Fouché also said the TR line ran parallel to the "strategic reconnaissance" SR line of the SR-71, and that SR-74 and SR-75 concepts were in development while he worked in defense.

## Other accounts

Author [[Nick Cook]] said that while leaving Lockheed's Palmdale facility with Skunk Works head [[Jack Gordon]] in the late 1990s, he saw a lineage chart of Skunk Works aircraft that ran past the Darkstar drone to an entry labelled "Astra", which Gordon brushed off when asked. The account is cited as indirect evidence that the Astra name belonged to a real Lockheed program.

## Funding

Fouché's sources said that up to 35 percent of [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)|Strategic Defense Initiative]] funding was diverted to Air Force black programs from 1982, and that the first flight-ready operational TR-3B was built with SDI and black-budget money in the early 1990s.
