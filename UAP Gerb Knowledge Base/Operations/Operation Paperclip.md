Operation Paperclip was the secret US government program, run from 1945 by the Joint Intelligence Objectives Agency, that brought more than 1,600 German scientists, engineers and technicians to the United States after World War II to work on American military and aerospace projects, often with their Nazi records concealed. Its best-known recruit was rocket engineer [[Wernher von Braun]]. In UFO research, Paperclip comes up again and again as the route by which Nazi expertise, and allegedly Nazi-held crash material, reached US legacy programs.

## Rocketry and the Space Program

Paperclip's best-known results came in rocketry. From 1946, von Braun and colleagues from Peenemünde tested captured V-2 rockets at [[White Sands Missile Range|White Sands Proving Ground]], where radar operators such as [[Lloyd Eugene Camp]] reported unidentified objects around the missiles. [[Arthur Trudeau]], later head of the [[U.S. Army Foreign Technology Division]], organised efforts to put Paperclip scientists to work on Army R&D, allegedly including the exploitation of recovered technology. Austrian inventor [[Victor Schauberger]], known for his flying-disc concepts, was allegedly recruited and is alleged to have been a close associate of [[Eric Henry Wang]].

## Underground Construction

In *Deep Underground Military Bases (D.U.M.Bs.) - UFO Legacy Programs*, Gerb traces American [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)|deep underground military bases]] back to Nazi underground factories through Paperclip. Two declassified 1947 Paperclip documents record an [[Air Materiel Command]] requirement for four German technicians to consult on a planned underground plant program. One of those named was [[Xaver Dorsch]], director of the Organisation Todt. Gerb also argues that SS engineer [[Hans Kammler]], who ran Nazi underground tunnels and secret weapons projects and allegedly held a disc taken from Italy in 1943, was secretly taken into US custody, and calls Dorsch and Kammler possible "key architects of modern-day US UFO and DUMB legacy programs". Maglev pioneer [[Hermann Kemper]] may also have been contracted by the US, Gerb suggests, though it is unknown whether Paperclip recruited him.

## Majestic 12 Documents

Researcher Ryan S. Wood cites a Joint Intelligence Objectives Agency Paperclip memo bearing the notation "CIA/MJ-12" as corroboration that the [[Majestic 12]] designation appeared in official records.
