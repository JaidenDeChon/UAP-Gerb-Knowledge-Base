Project Sign was the first official United States Government program to systematically investigate unidentified flying objects, operating from 1948 to 1949 under the [[US Air Force]]. It was created following Lieutenant General [[Nathan Twining]]'s assessment that flying discs were "real and not visionary or fictitious," and represented the earliest formal acknowledgment at the highest levels of the Air Force that UFO reports warranted serious investigation. Sign investigated military and civilian UFO reports during the late 1940s, including the famous [[Gorman Dogfight]] of October 1, 1948. The program was shut down in 1949 by General [[Hoyt Vandenberg]] despite — or because of — its preliminary conclusions supporting the reality of unexplained aerial objects.

## Establishment

Project Sign was initialized after General Twining's 1947 memo asserting that flying discs were real, physical phenomena. The memo was addressed to the Commanding General of the Army Air Forces, to the attention of Brigadier General George Schulgen of Air Force intelligence. (The name is garbled in the channel's videos, which render it as "George Shugen" and "George Lugan.") The project was initially named "Project Saucer" before receiving its official designation.

Sign was established with a high-level institutional mandate and oversight. All Project Sign reports were required to be sent to three major organizations: the [[Army and Navy Research and Development Board]], the [[USAF Scientific Advisory Group]] (the Air Force's civilian scientific advisory body, which was renamed the USAF Scientific Advisory Board in the late 1940s; the two videos use the two names interchangeably), and the [[Atomic Energy Commission]]. This distribution requirement reflects the multi-agency concern about UFOs at the highest levels of government and military leadership, and the connection to nuclear-adjacent institutions that would continue throughout subsequent programs.

## Gorman Dogfight Investigation

Project Sign conducted a thorough investigation of the [[Gorman Dogfight]] incident involving Second Lieutenant [[George F. Gorman]]'s 27-minute aerial pursuit of an unidentified luminous object over [[Fargo, North Dakota, USA|Fargo, North Dakota]] on October 1, 1948. Investigators interviewed Gorman and other witnesses, including air traffic controller [[L.D. Jensen]].

As part of the investigation, Project Sign tested Gorman's P-51 Mustang for radiation. The aircraft was measurably more radioactive than other fighters, initially leading investigators to conclude the craft had flown close to an "atomic powered object." However, further analysis attributed this to reduced atmospheric shielding at the 14,000-foot altitude Gorman reached during the pursuit.

Project Sign initially rolled out conventional explanations such as weather balloons and jets but found them inconsistent with the evidence. Ultimately, the program concluded that Gorman had either chased a lit weather balloon or the planet Jupiter, and that the object's fantastic maneuvers were an illusion based on his frame of reference — a conclusion that contradicted sworn testimony from both Gorman and Jensen.

## Findings

Project Sign's written report concluded that some UFO reports present actual objects but that their origins are undeterminable. Captain [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], who later directed Sign's successors ([[Project Grudge]] and [[Project Blue Book]]), stated that Sign had internally endorsed the interplanetary hypothesis as the best explanation for some of its unexplained cases. Ruppelt also wrote that the Gorman dogfight was one of three classic UFO incidents in 1948 that "proved to Air Force intelligence specialists that UFOs were real."

## Termination

General [[Hoyt Vandenberg]] shut down Project Sign in 1949, officially citing insufficient proof for the extraterrestrial interpretation. Critically, the program that replaced it — [[Project Grudge]] — was commissioned the same year Sign was terminated. Grudge was explicitly tasked not with investigation but with explaining UFO sightings away to "alleviate public anxiety." The replacement of Sign with Grudge represents the first major documented shift in the US government's posture toward UFOs: from investigation toward suppression.

## Legacy

Project Sign's findings, brief as they were, established an institutional record that some UFO reports represented genuine unknowns. Ruppelt's assessment of the 1948 cases reflects the institutional stance during Sign's operational period, before the Air Force adopted more dismissive positions under subsequent programs. That finding was administratively overruled, and subsequent programs operated under a different mandate. UAP researcher [[David Grusch]] has cited programs like Sign, Grudge, and [[Project Blue Book]] as part of the "sophisticated disinformation campaigns" he referenced in his congressional testimony.
