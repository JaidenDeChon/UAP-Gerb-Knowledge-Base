The Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP) was a US Navy program begun in 1964, after the 1963 loss of the submarine USS Thresher, to develop the Navy's deep-ocean engineering capabilities for search, rescue, salvage and recovery at greatly increased depths. It was led by Dr. [[John P. Craven]], chief scientist of the Navy's Special Projects Office. Its best-known products were the two [[Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicle (DSRV)|Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicles]], *Mystic* and *Avalon*. In UAP research it is argued to have been the engineering arm of the compartmented seafloor-recovery program [[Project Sanddollar|Sand Dollar]], and to have provided the Navy's means of retrieving craft from the deep ocean.

## Program

The DSSP was founded out of the Navy's Special Projects Office. It aimed to build six DSRVs, plus Deep Submergence Search Vehicles (DSSVs) designed for seafloor exploration and object recovery, with clandestine capability and an operational depth of 20,000 feet. Officially only *Mystic* (DSRV-1) and *Avalon* (DSRV-2) were built, by Lockheed Missiles and Space Company. The project also led to deep clandestine craft such as the nuclear-powered NR-1.

Craven wrote in *The Silent War* that the rescue vehicles were built with intelligence missions in mind: "a DSRV designed, constructed, and deployed for every conceivable rescue mission would also be available for the intelligence 'mission impossible' that were sure to occur".

## Alleged link to Sand Dollar and UFO retrieval

In 1965, a year after the DSSP began, Craven was briefed on Sand Dollar, a program nested inside another secret program within the Polaris program. Its task was recovering militarily sensitive hardware and other items of national-security importance from the world's seafloors. UAP Gerb argues that the DSSP answered Sand Dollar's requirements, and that its submersibles were carried to targets by submarines linked to the [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]], such as the Halibut and Seawolf. He asks whether Sand Dollar was the Navy's first UFO legacy program. He notes that the DSSP began two years after the alleged 1962 [[Bluegill Triple Prime UFO Recovery]].

Gerb also suggests that the craft in the [[1991 North Atlantic USO Retrieval]], raised from nearly 1.5 miles down, was recovered by an unacknowledged DSSV-type vehicle rather than by *Mystic* or *Avalon*, whose rated depth was only just over a mile.
