Operation Menu was a covert, year-long Strategic Air Command B-52 bombing campaign against North Vietnamese Army supply routes, base camps and staging areas inside eastern Cambodia. It began on March 18, 1969, and continued through May 1970. The operation stayed classified until President Bill Clinton declassified it in 2000, more than 30 years after it began. It matters to UAP research because of an anonymous whistleblower's account of reconnaissance missions along the Vietnam-Cambodia border in late 1968, the immediate setting of the UAP crash encounter he reports. He says his LRRP unit's mission was to mark targets for "B-52 bombing the following year," and Gerb identifies this detail as independent corroboration of the credibility of his testimony.

## Background

By the late 1960s, North Vietnam was using Cambodian territory, nominally neutral, as a major transit and staging area for forces and supplies moving into South Vietnam along the Ho Chi Minh Trail. The United States knew about these sanctuaries, but political constraints kept it from publicly acknowledging operations inside Cambodia. Operation Menu was its covert military solution. It was a series of B-52 bombing raids on specific base area complexes inside Cambodia, each given a codename from a food course: BREAKFAST, LUNCH, SNACK, DINNER, DESSERT and SUPPER.

The operation was run in extreme secrecy. Flight crews filed false mission reports that named targets in South Vietnam, and ground radar operators who knew the true targets were told to falsify their logs. Congress was not told about the operation.

These areas held the supply routes and staging areas that Operation Menu was specifically designed to target. Gerb presents this match in location and type of mission as one of several independently verifiable elements of the whistleblower's account.
