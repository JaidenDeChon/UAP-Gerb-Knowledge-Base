**Project Corona** (also known as **Discoverer**) was a classified reconnaissance satellite program run by the CIA and the U.S. Air Force from 1959 to 1972. Its satellites took high-resolution photographs of the Soviet Union, China and other Cold War adversaries. The program matters to UAP research because its film capsules were routinely dropped over the United States and caught in mid-air or collected after landing. For that reason some skeptics proposed Corona as an explanation for certain UFO reports, including the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]].

## Mission Profile

Corona satellites were launched on Thor rockets from Vandenberg Air Force Base in California and placed in low Earth orbit. Their cameras recorded high-resolution images on photographic film. At the end of each mission the film was loaded into a reentry capsule, which was brought out of orbit and came down over the Pacific Ocean or, on some missions, over the continental United States. The capsules were recovered in one of two ways:

1. Mid-air retrieval: C-119 aircraft, and later C-130s, fitted with trapeze recovery gear caught the capsule's parachute in the air.
2. Ocean or ground recovery: if the mid-air catch failed, the capsule splashed down in the ocean or, in rare cases, landed on U.S. soil, where ground teams collected it.

The program was highly successful. It supplied important intelligence at the height of the Cold War and laid the foundation for later U.S. satellite reconnaissance.

## The Kecksburg Connection

Because Corona capsules, small and metallic, came back to Earth over the United States on a regular basis, some skeptics suggested that the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]] might have been a recovery capsule that went off course. [[Nicholas Johnson]], NASA's Chief Scientist for Orbital Debris, investigated this theory and ruled it out definitively.

### Why Project Corona Was Eliminated

In May 2000, journalist [[Leslie Kean]] presented findings from her correspondence with Nicholas Johnson at a conference titled *"Combating Media Ridicule and Searching for Evidence on the 1965 Kecksburg Crash Case."* Johnson had examined whether any Corona recovery capsule could have come down over Pennsylvania on December 9, 1965, at about 4:47 PM EST. His conclusion was unequivocal:

> "No man-made object came down over Pennsylvania at that time."

Johnson said he had access to comprehensive databases of every U.S. space launch, piece of orbital debris and reentry event. From them, he said, he could determine with certainty that no Corona capsule, and no other classified U.S. space object, landed in Pennsylvania on December 9, 1965.

### Design Inconsistencies

Even if a Corona capsule had been nearby, its design does not match what witnesses described at Kecksburg in any respect:

- Shape: Corona reentry capsules were conical or bucket-shaped, built for atmospheric reentry. Witnesses described the Kecksburg object as a bell-shaped or acorn-shaped craft, wide at the base and tapering to a rounded top, which is a very different outline.
- Size: Corona capsules were fairly small, about 3 feet in diameter and 5 feet tall. The Kecksburg object was reported to be 10–12 feet in diameter.
- Markings: Corona capsules carried standard U.S. military markings and serial numbers. The Kecksburg object had symbols resembling hieroglyphics in a band around its base, which does not fit U.S. aerospace design at all.
- Material and construction: Corona capsules had heat-shielded surfaces, visible panels and standard aerospace fasteners. The Kecksburg object was described as having no visible seams, rivets or welds, and a smooth metallic surface the color of bronze or copper.

### Operational Security

Corona was highly classified during the 1960s, and the U.S. government would not have let a public circus build up around a downed capsule. Had the Kecksburg object been a Corona reentry vehicle, the military would have dealt with it swiftly and discreetly, and that would have been the end of it. That response would not have included the elaborate cover story, the intimidation of witnesses and the decades of denial that mark the Kecksburg case.

## Declassification

Project Corona was declassified in 1995, which let researchers compare its mission records with UFO sighting reports. No Corona mission matches the Kecksburg event, which further confirms Nicholas Johnson's analysis.

## Conclusion

Project Corona was considered and eliminated as an explanation for the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]] for three reasons:

1. There was no Corona reentry on December 9, 1965, as NASA orbital debris expert [[Nicholas Johnson]] confirmed.
2. The shape, size, markings and construction of the Kecksburg object do not match those of Corona capsules.
3. The declassified mission records show no Corona mission that fits the time or place of the Kecksburg event.

With Corona eliminated, and with [[Cosmos 96]] and [[Project Blue Book]]'s meteor explanation both debunked, the Kecksburg object remains an unidentified anomalous craft.
