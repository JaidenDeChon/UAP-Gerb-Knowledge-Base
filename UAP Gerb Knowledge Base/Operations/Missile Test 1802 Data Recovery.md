Missile Test 1802 Data Recovery was a US military operation to recover data from a missile test on June 25, 1960, near [[Ascension Island]] in the South Atlantic. During the recovery, several witnesses saw a [[Unidentified Submerged Object (USO)|USO]] rise from beneath the ocean surface near the object they were retrieving. The operation is the source event for the [[Ascension Island USO Sighting]], one of the most anomalous maritime cases in the [[Project Blue Book]] record. Blue Book classified the sighting as a flare, even though its investigation confirmed that no flare was released at the location.

## Operation Description

A range vessel code-named Whiskey was assigned to recover a floating data cassette from missile test 1802. Strobes lit the cassette, and crews watched it from the vessel and from a recovery aircraft overhead. A small boat went out to pick it up.

When the boat was 300 to 500 yards from the cassette, the crew saw a steady white or yellowish light appear about 100 yards beyond it, apparently from beneath the ocean surface. The light sat at or very close to the surface, and no waves broke over it. The witnesses included:

- An RCA photographer and test observer
- Divers from the missile recovery crew
- The pilot and co-pilot of the recovery aircraft

The light lasted approximately ten seconds.

## UAP Connection Pattern

Gerb explicitly compares this operation, a recovery of a missile test data cassette with several technical witnesses at close range, to an account told by [[Luis Elizondo]]. In that account, a Navy helicopter crew recovering a cruise missile encountered a massive, dark, circular [[Unidentified Submerged Object (USO)]] that rose from the depths and in the end pulled the recovered missile back into the ocean. The similarity is taken to suggest that missile recovery operations may specifically attract or interact with USO phenomena, possibly because of electromagnetic emissions from the test hardware or the operational value of recovered missile technology.
