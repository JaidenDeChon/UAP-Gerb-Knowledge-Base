## Overview

This video is Gerb's breakdown of the 31-minute talk that retired US Army Colonel [[Karl Nell]] gave to the [[SOL Foundation Symposium]] in November 2023, at the Nolan laboratory of the Stanford School of Medicine. The full presentation only became viewable in February 2024, after one of its slides had partially leaked. Gerb stresses that it isn't a reaction video: he picked out the parts that matter and connected them to the channel's wider research.

Nell's talk has four parts, and the video follows them in order. First comes a close reading of the [[Schumer Amendment (NDAA)|Schumer Amendment]]: its background, its definitions (UAP against "temporarily non-attributed objects", non-human intelligence, legacy programs, prosaic attribution and technology of unknown origin), and its eminent-domain clause for UAP material held by private corporations. Next is the distinction between a *controlled* disclosure run on a campaign plan and a *catastrophic* one in which everything is dumped at once, followed by the reasons for and against non-disclosure. At the centre is Nell's "Way Forward" [[UAP Campaign Plan - Way Forward|UAP campaign plan]], a six-phase roadmap (Phase 0 to Phase 5) run across four lines of effort, with target dates from January 2024 to October 2034 and beyond.

Gerb then goes past the talk. Two backup slides that [[Matthew Pines]] shared, which weren't shown in the recorded presentation, propose replacing the [[Kardashev Scale]] with a [[Universal Civilization Development Datum]] and set out a proposed taxonomy of UAP origin hypotheses. The video closes on Nell himself. Gerb asks whether Nell's Iraq-era role as a foreign-materials program representative could be the source of his status as one of the 40 firsthand witnesses [[David Grusch]] interviewed. [[Leslie Kean]] named him as one of them the day after Grusch's 2023 hearing.

## Who Karl Nell Is

The video opens with the 5 June 2023 article in *The Debrief* by Leslie Kean and Ralph Blumenthal. That article introduced David Grusch and, in Gerb's words, established that the US government holds craft of non-human origin and runs crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering efforts. Nell appears in it as a retired Army colonel who worked with Grusch on the [[UAP Task Force]]. He has one quote, and Gerb calls it astounding. In it, Nell says Grusch is fundamentally correct that a terrestrial arms race has run sub rosa for 80 years, focused on reverse-engineering technologies of unknown origin. He also says it is indisputable that at least some of those technologies derive from non-human intelligence.

Gerb's closing section adds Nell's credentials, taken from his LinkedIn biography through a Twitter thread by the account UAP News. Nell was:

- a technical intelligence operations officer for the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]]
- the first person from the DIA's directorate of intelligence production and analysis deployed to plan Operation Iraqi Freedom with other agencies
- the foreign materials program's command representative to US CENTCOM's land component command (CFLCC) in Kuwait
- the CJ2 of the one-star Combined Joint Captured Material Exploitation Center in Iraq

## The SOL Foundation

Gerb describes the [[Sol Foundation]] as a group of scientists, academics, military personnel and policy makers. It works to give UAP academic legitimacy and prepare for a post-disclosure world. Its figures include former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense [[Chris Mellon]], David Grusch, Stanford pathology professor [[Gary Nolan]], physicist [[Kevin Knuth]] and Nell. Gerb plans follow-up videos on the symposium talks by Grusch, Mellon, Knuth, Nolan and [[Jacques Vallee]].

## The Schumer Amendment

Nell says that ten years ago the Schumer Amendment would probably have been seen as disclosure in itself. He traces its background through two events:

- Kean's *New York Times* article of 16 December 2017, which put the FLIR, Gimbal and GoFast videos in the public domain. Nell thanks Chris Mellon and [[Luis Elizondo|Lue Elizondo]] for their tireless work getting the videos released.
- The congressional testimony of David Grusch, [[Ryan Graves]] and [[David Fravor]], which again lowered the threshold for treating the topic as respectable.

Two of the bill's sponsors sit in the Gang of Eight: the party leaders of both chambers, plus the chairs and ranking minority members of the Senate and House intelligence committees. Under Title 50 of the US Code these members are entitled to hear about all special access and secret programs. Nell stresses that they still hadn't been told about UAP.

Nell argues that changing the vocabulary starts to strip away the stigma: UFO becomes UAP, USO becomes USP, "flying saucer" becomes UAP, and "ETs" become NHI. He also shows the six characteristics of UAP the legislation now references. Gerb usually cites the [[Five UAP Characteristics (AATIP)|five]] listed by Elizondo. He notes the new sixth item, "physical or invasive biological effects", and suspects Gary Nolan brought it to the table. He links it to Havana syndrome and to the fear witnesses report feeling near a craft.

### Terms of reference

For Nell, the Amendment's definitions show that the NDAA was looking specifically for anything non-human:

- **Non-human intelligence** is cited at least 18 times: "any sentient intelligent non-human life form regardless of nature or ultimate origin" that may be presumed responsible for UAP, or that the federal government has become aware of.
- **[[Legacy Programs|Legacy program]]** means every federal, state, local, commercial, academic or private-sector effort to collect, exploit or reverse-engineer technologies of unknown origin or biological evidence of NHI.
- **Prosaic attribution** means having a human origin, foreign or domestic. It appears in both the UAP and TNO definitions: in Nell's shorthand, "UAP is non-human, TNO human".
- **Technology of unknown origin** is any material or meta-material, ejecta, crash debris, mechanism, machinery, equipment, assembly, engineering model or process, or damaged or intact aerospace or undersea craft associated with UAP, or incorporating science and technology that lacks prosaic attribution or any known means of human manufacture. Nell says this definition is what the eminent-domain clause turns on.

### Archives, eminent domain and the gutting

The executive summary routes all UAP data to the National Archives. Nell voices what everyone is thinking: nobody necessarily trusts that this will happen. That is why he values the review board's oversight, which he contrasts with the 1992 JFK records process. Materials, biological evidence of NHI and technologies of unknown origin fall under the Amendment's eminent-domain clause, the federal government's power, as Gerb puts it, to take whatever it wants. Nell sets out arguments for and against using it. Gerb notes that some of the arguments against concern property rights, which points to private corporations holding UAP material.

Gerb reports that the Schumer–Rounds amendment was then "watered down and gutted" by opponents of disclosure. He hopes the full bill returns, perhaps in the 2025 NDAA.

## Controlled or Catastrophic Disclosure

Nell draws the key distinction. A **controlled disclosure** follows a campaign plan. A **catastrophic disclosure** opens the floodgates: all the information is dumped at once, possibly by an adversary or by NHI themselves, to cause social disruption or for other ends. The rest of the talk is an argument for the first.

## Reasons For and Against Non-Disclosure

Nell's slide gives six reasons for non-disclosure, and Gerb finds all six logical:

- national-security implications
- lack of a credible plan
- social disruption
- covert agreements
- cover for misdeeds
- organizational inertia, or distraction from other priorities

Under national security, Gerb singles out "new physics and technologies" with unprecedented consequences for human society. The slide's other column holds the arguments for disclosure. Nell concludes that these issues are better solved in the academic sector and are largely not government issues, except for the cold war and the arms race.

## The Way Forward: Nell's Campaign Plan

The slide that partially leaked in November 2023 is titled "Way Forward: UAP Campaign Plan Lines of Effort". Gerb calls it "absurdly perplexing". Its end state is a post-disclosure world with proper oversight restored, catastrophic disclosure avoided and scientific understanding advanced. It gets there through five major phases (plus a Phase 0 already behind it) run across four lines of effort: the public sector, philosophical investigation, scientific research and the private sector.

- **Phase 0**, shaping the narrative, was done successfully by [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]], the DoD videos, the 2017 *New York Times* article and the UAP Task Force. Gerb points out that [[AARO]] isn't listed and imagines [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] "seething".
- **Phase 1**, government acceptance, targeted 1 January 2024. Gerb judges it all but complete. It ends with the Schumer legislation: UAP is now largely a bipartisan issue, and "reactive hypothesis generation" is appearing, such as a newly published paper arguing that some UAP are plasma-based pre-life.
- **Phase 2** aims to correlate signatures by 1 January 2026 and to harness academia, as the SOL Foundation does. The slide marks it still at risk, which Gerb puts down to the lingering stigma around serious academic research.
- **Phase 3**, public acceptance, targets 1 October 2030. Nell sees it as an inflection point, possibly humanity's disclosure point. Gerb calls public acceptance "a tall ask".
- **Phase 4** would fully answer the five W's (who, what, when, where and why) by 1 October 2034. The slide marks it off target.
- **Phase 5** has no fixed timeline. It seeks an interactive approach with UAP in a strategic end state, which Gerb reads as possibly integrating NHI and their technology with Earth's civilization.

## Key Claims

- In the June 2023 Debrief article, Nell said Grusch is fundamentally correct that a terrestrial arms race has run sub rosa for 80 years to reverse-engineer technologies of unknown origin, and that it is indisputable that some of them derive from non-human intelligence.
- Leslie Kean named Nell, who worked with Grusch on the UAP Task Force, as one of the 40 firsthand witnesses Grusch interviewed.
- Per Nell, the Schumer Amendment uses "non-human intelligence" at least 18 times. It defines UAP as objects lacking prosaic attribution, meaning they have no known human origin, which Nell reads as non-human. TNOs are objects later found to be human-made.
- Two of the Amendment's sponsors sit in the Gang of Eight, which is entitled to hear about all special access programs, yet had not been briefed on UAP, according to Nell.
- The Amendment's eminent-domain clause would let the government seize technologies of unknown origin and NHI biological evidence, including from private corporations. The provisions were "watered down and gutted" before passage, per the video.
- Nell distinguishes a controlled disclosure run on a campaign plan from a catastrophic one, in which an adversary or NHI dump all the information at once.
- His six reasons for non-disclosure are national security, lack of a credible plan, social disruption, covert agreements with NHI, cover for misdeeds and organizational inertia. Against them he sets the risk that the USA may be losing a UAP arms race.
- Nell's campaign plan targets government acceptance by January 2024, signature correlation by January 2026, public acceptance, possibly humanity's disclosure point, by October 2030, and answers to the five W's by October 2034, before an open-ended strategic end state.
- A backup slide would replace the Kardashev scale with a Universal Civilization Development Datum that forecasts NHI motives from what a civilization is, knows and who it is. It allows that numerous civilizations may be interacting with Earth at once.
- Gerb asks whether Nell's Iraq-era role as the foreign materials program's command representative could have brought him to a downed UAP, the basis of his firsthand testimony.
