## Overview

This video is a full investigation of the [[National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)|National Air and Space Intelligence Center]] (NASIC), the US Air Force's intelligence center at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], which Gerb calls possibly "the most impactful singular organization in all of UFO legacy program history" outside the [[National Security Council]]. Most accounts name "the Air Force" as a major player in covert UFO work; Gerb's aim is to say which part of it. NASIC was founded in 2003, but its line runs back to 1917 through a series of predecessors that each touched the UFO subject: the T-2 intelligence department, the Technical Intelligence Division that ran [[Project Sign]] and [[Project Grudge]], and the [[Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC)|Air Technical Intelligence Center]] and [[Air Force Foreign Technology Division|Foreign Technology Division]] that ran [[Project Blue Book]]. Under every name, Gerb argues, its core mission stayed the same: acquiring and exploiting foreign materiel, the reverse engineering of adversary technology.

The trigger for the video is a run of recent statements by [[David Grusch]] that point at NASIC. Grusch has spoken of "the night job of a certain analyst at NASIC" who worked for the legacy program without telling his boss, whom Gerb identifies as Grusch's late friend [[Matthew Sullivan]]; of foreign adversary estimates of the US retrieval program that NASIC analysed; and of an "off-books" foreign material acquisition and exploitation program hidden in plain sight. Gerb sets these beside the [[Foreign Material Program (FMP)|Foreign Materiel Program]], in which NASIC is the Air Force's exploitation center, and argues that part of the [[UFO Legacy Program Framework|UFO legacy programs]] hides inside it. His central and most specific claim is that NASIC's Geospatial Intelligence Analysis Squadron (GSI), supported since 2015 by an embedded team from the [[NGA|National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency]], is "one of the crown jewels" of the legacy programs: the unit that supplies the imagery and signature intelligence crash retrieval teams need, and a likely contributor to [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)|Immaculate Constellation]].

The second half turns to decades of claims of recovered craft and bodies at Wright-Patterson, mostly from the files of [[Leonard Stringfield]], and ties them to NASIC's forebears: a 1947 White Sands disc report, a man who said he saw nine bodies in the 1960s and was told of a master UFO computer file, a Navy pilot Gerb identifies as Rear Admiral [[Paul Stafford Johnson Jr.]], and TRW's alleged records. A section on the paired histories of Air Force intelligence and engineering at Wright-Patterson leads to Gerb's answer to the [[Blue Room]] that Senator [[Barry Goldwater]] was refused: a trail of Freedom of Information Act replies that ends with the Air Force naming NASIC as the Blue Room's office of record. Gerb recorded much of the video while seriously ill, and dedicates it to Sullivan's memory.

## What NASIC Is

NASIC sits in Building 856 at Wright-Patterson, the Air Force's main hub for research, development and acquisition, and a base Gerb calls practically synonymous with UFOs: witnesses to the [[Roswell Crash]] said wreckage and bodies went to what was then [[Wright Field]], and the base was home to Projects Sign, Grudge and Blue Book. A clip Gerb plays has [[Leonard Stringfield]] reporting an intelligence source's claim of "30 alien bodies underground at Wright-Patterson Field alone".

The center is the Air Force's service intelligence center and an operational wing of its intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance enterprise. Its product is scientific and technical intelligence (S&TI), engineering-level assessments of foreign aircraft, missiles, sensors and space systems, drawn from measurement and signature intelligence (MASINT), geospatial intelligence (GEOINT), signals intelligence (SIGINT) and technical intelligence (TECHINT), the last meaning hands-on exploitation of captured or procured hardware. It owns no aircraft or satellites of its own: in Gerb's one-sentence version, "NASIC drives what intelligence gets collected and owns the deep analysis." In-house, it keeps signature-processing specialists and reference signature databases, engineers and scientists who model foreign systems, and dedicated TECHINT through the Foreign Materiel Program. In 2022 the [[US Space Force]]'s National Space Intelligence Center took over much of the space mission. Gerb warns that he will use the word "materiel" constantly, in its military sense of equipment and supplies.

## A Century of Names

NASIC's line, Gerb says, is "an exhaustingly lengthy series of names and mission changes", but one mission runs through it. It began in 1917 as the Foreign Data Section of the Army Signal Corps' engineering department at McCook Field in Dayton, set up because German and European aircraft so outclassed American ones that engineers began collecting foreign publications and examining foreign aircraft. "NASIC's very bones," Gerb says, "are firmly rooted within technology capture and reverse engineering." ## The Foreign Materiel Program

Gerb's rule of thumb is that "the US government isn't that smart": legacy activity tends to hide exactly where one would expect it, behind ordinary designations rather than some special classification system. The DoD has had an openly named [[Foreign Material Program (FMP)|Foreign Materiel Program]] since 1961, with two halves: foreign materiel acquisition (FMA, getting an item) and exploitation (FME, using it).
