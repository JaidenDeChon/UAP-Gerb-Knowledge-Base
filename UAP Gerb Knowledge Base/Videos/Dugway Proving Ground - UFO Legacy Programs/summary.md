## Overview

This video is UAP Gerb's investigation of [[Dugway Proving Ground]], the Army's chemical and biological defense test site in the Utah desert, as an alleged centre of [[Legacy Programs|UFO legacy programs]]: the programs said to retrieve and exploit technologies of unknown origin. UFO researchers often call Dugway "Area 52", and the host says it rose to prominence in the late 1990s as the successor to [[Area 51]]'s UFO programs once the Nevada site had been heavily exposed. It is also accused of hosting a [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)|deep underground military base]] (DUMB). The host asks which individuals, military units and corporations take part, and whether the programs run both above and below ground.

The investigation has three strands. The first is Dugway's structure. It holds two [[Major Range and Test Facility Bases (MRTFBs)|major range and test facility bases]]: the Army's [[West Desert Test Center]] and the south range of the Air Force's [[Utah Test and Training Range]], which it shares with [[Hill Air Force Base]]. The second is the lore that tied Dugway to UFOs long before any witness came forward. It includes an alleged 1953 crash near Garrison, Utah, a 1997 *Popular Mechanics* cover story on "the new Area 51", and insider claims passed to [[Edgar Fouche]] and [[Steven Greer]]. The third and largest is the testimony of [[MS (Dugway Witness)|MS]], a contractor who says that in 2009 he walked into an unmarked building at [[Avery Technical Center]] and saw a hovering saucer being studied under armed guard. The host has interviewed MS himself. He uses a 2012 environmental assessment of Avery to try to pin down the building, and the claims of a senior DOD chemist, GP, to describe a vast underground facility beneath the range.

From this the host builds a thesis: a joint Army and Air Force legacy program run between the Army Test and Evaluation Command's West Desert Test Center and Hill's [[388th Range Squadron]], supported by contractors such as [[Battelle Memorial Institute]], that operates on the surface at Avery and beneath [[Granite Peak, Dugway|Granite Peak]] in a facility tied into a national underground rail network. He closes by naming Battelle his primary suspect, pointing to leaders who moved between Battelle and the West Desert Test Center.

## The Installation

Dugway lies 85 miles south-west of [[Salt Lake City, Utah|Salt Lake City]] in [[Tooele County, Utah|Tooele County]], ringed by mountains, salt flats and desert. The host describes it as free of urban encroachment, light pollution and stray acoustic and electronic noise, and guarded by a force that will stop approaching civilians with armed personnel and black helicopters. It was founded in 1942, after Pearl Harbor, to test chemical and biological defense systems, and it has since supported Special Forces mission rehearsals, classified testing and urgent-need equipment testing in the war on terror. A 2021 Army report counts 2,120 employees and residents: 784 mainly Army and Air Force contractors, 622 mission and installation contracting staff (on-site security among them) and 486 military or defense agency civilians. Its partners include the Army, the Navy, [[SOCOM]], the 388th Range Squadron, the Departments of Justice, Homeland Security and the Interior, [[NASA]], and three universities. One of them, Utah State University, runs the Space Dynamics Laboratory, a University Affiliated Research Center (UARC) for the Missile Defense Agency. The host believes UARCs and [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)|FFRDCs]] act as project managers and subject-matter experts for joint corporate and government UAP programs, and know far more about them than most defense contractors.

MRTFBs are the DOD's "core set" of test and evaluation infrastructure. They cover more than 10 million acres, employ more than 25,000 people and receive more than $4 billion a year in acknowledged funding. Of the 23 acknowledged bases, eight belong to the Army (including Yuma and [[White Sands Missile Range|White Sands]]), six to the Navy (including Pax River and [[China Lake Naval Air Weapons Station|China Lake]]), seven to the Air Force (including the [[412th Test Wing|Edwards 412th Test Wing]], the [[Nevada Test and Training Range (NTR)|Nevada Test and Training Range]] and Vandenberg), and two to defense agencies. Many of these names recur in the legacy-program literature. The host's earlier witness Ed described a joint reverse-engineering program between two Air Force MRTFBs, Edwards and the Nevada range. Dugway gives the host a variation on that pattern: two MRTFBs from two different services on one range.

The West Desert Test Center is run by the [[US Army Test and Evaluation Command (ATEC)|Army Test and Evaluation Command]], and the host finds its place in the chain of command telling. The Office of Management and Budget and the Office of the Secretary of Defense require Army test and evaluation to be independent of the Army's ordinary research, development, test and evaluation structure. The stated aim is unbiased evaluation, warfighter safety and credibility with decision-makers. The host's concern is that the same independence could leave ATEC's programs, and so the West Desert Test Center's, with little oversight.

## Area 52: The Lore

The host admits that, apart from MS, little ties Dugway to legacy programs compared with sites like Edwards, Norton, China Lake or White Sands. What exists is a trail of crash lore, press reports and insider claims, several of which predate any public talk of Dugway.

**The Garrison crash.** Less than a month after the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|Kingman crash]] and about 200 miles to its north, a craft allegedly came down near Garrison, Utah. Researcher Steve Jones investigated the [[1953 Garrison, Utah Crash Retrieval|Garrison case]], and [[Ryan S. Wood]]'s *Majic Eyes Only* rates it a moderate-confidence case. In 1993 a group called the Phoenix Project showed Jones aerial photos dated June 1953 of a triangular feature in a valley beside the Desert Range Experimental Station (DRES), a Forest Service station south-west of the West Desert Test Center. A DRES log book from 1979 to 1980 recorded Air Force and Army personnel from Fort Carson, USAF command centre staff and defense contractors. A local man, Bart Wright, said that as a child he saw a blue light streak across the sky and crash into the valley, shaking the ground. A geologist Jones hired in 1999 found no buried anomaly but did not think the triangle was natural. The host finds the case interesting but calls it a dead end.

**The Flux Liner over Provo.** Among the witnesses [[Mark McCandlish]] met while researching the [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|Flux Liner]] that [[Brad Sorenson]] saw at Norton in 1988 was USAF Captain Harvey Williams, who photographed a similar disc in 1967 from a C-47 at 12,000 feet over Provo, Utah. McCandlish and Sorenson said the Flux Liner flew by a synthetic vision system of cameras in acrylic bubbles. The host reasons that a 1967 model would have needed bigger cameras. Provo lies about 100 miles from Dugway and under 75 from Hill, which shares Michael Army Airfield and the Utah range with Dugway. If Williams did photograph an early Flux Liner, the host argues, it points to an ARV testing program between two MRTFBs, the Utah range and Edwards, much like the claims of Ed and Fouche, who said [[TR-3B]] pilots came from the Edwards test pilot school.

**"The New Area 51".** *Popular Mechanics*' June 1997 cover story, by Jim Wilson, said the Air Force had left Area 51 for a more private site because of overexposure and a need for a different proving ground for ultra-high-performance aircraft. Working from a 1997 agreement between Air Force General Howell Estes and NASA administrator Daniel Goldin to share "redundant assets", and a 1996 NASA announcement that [[Michael Army Airfield]] would serve several X-33 missions, the magazine settled on Utah: Michael Army Airfield on Dugway, paired with the Green River launch complex about 200 miles away. It was warned that it would be shot down if it overflew the airfield, and was refused satellite imagery of Green River. The host notes that the article mocks crash-retrieval believers, yet one of its photographs shows a sticky note reading "call Admiral Inman at Science Applications International". He reads this as a nod to [[Bobby Ray Inman]], the former NSA director and [[SAIC]] board member who told [[Bob Echler]] about recovered craft. :wiki-cue{t=1087 video="rePrVmR6a68" video-title="Dugway Proving Ground - UFO Legacy Programs"}

**Insiders.** Several insiders made similar claims:

- **Fouche.** In his 1998 International UFO Congress presentation, Edgar Fouche said a source told him the Air Force began a contingency plan in 1992 to move most exotic platforms out of Area 51, to Utah, Colorado, Alaska, Greenland, Diego Garcia and S4.
- **Goodall.** [[James Goodall]] described at least eight black programs flying out of Groom Lake in 1990, including a silent triangle.
- **AH.** Witness AH, said to be a former Boeing employee with friends across the intelligence agencies, Area 51 and industry, was taped in 2000 for Greer's 2001 [[Disclosure Project|disclosure]] briefing document. He said 35 to 40% of Area 51, craft included, had moved to an underground Utah base south-west of Salt Lake City that could be reached only by air. The host thinks AH is a primary source for Greer.
- **Greer.** In a 2016 presentation Greer called Dugway "the most critical one" among black-program sites: classified airspace, little to see above ground, everything underground.
- **Jones.** In a March 1998 email to Greer, Steve Jones said Groom Lake work had moved to an area of Dugway called "Dog", west of the main gate. Since the 1980s he had also logged untarped truckloads left between Hill and Wendover.
- **Kadok.** Earliest of all, a 1996 email to Greer from AJ (Tony) Kadok, then his webmaster, relayed an insider who checked badges and ran tremor sensors and ground radar. The insider said the programs were not only at Area 51: investigators should look south-west of Tooele County and west of Salt Lake City, where there were nine exclusion zones, four of them with underground locations. The same source mentioned EMP weapons that could fry vehicle electronics.

The host stresses that the Kadok exchange predates the *Popular Mechanics* article, Fouche's disclosures and AH's testimony. :wiki-cue{t=1653 video="rePrVmR6a68" video-title="Dugway Proving Ground - UFO Legacy Programs"}

## Witness MS

At his June 2023 whistleblower symposium at the National Press Club, Greer told the story of a witness he called MS. According to Greer, MS was a C Martin contractor at Dugway who saw an extraterrestrial vehicle being studied by scientists in 2009, knew the chief scientist, and was shown by him the location of a DUMB in the "Avery sector", with an elevator large enough to take Air Force One. :wiki-cue{t=1826 video="rePrVmR6a68" video-title="Dugway Proving Ground - UFO Legacy Programs"} When Greer's DPI archive was released, the host found MS's files under witness ID 10655, and lapses in the documents let him track MS down. He interviewed MS in June 2024 and uses clips from that conversation here. He had covered MS once before, in a video he later deleted to protect other witnesses. He confirms that elements of the [[Senate Select Committee on Intelligence]] asked MS to testify: MS showed him screenshots of the outreach. MS declined because, as a railroad worker, he could not take a week or more off, even though the committee offered to pay for his travel.

MS served in the Navy from 1990 to 2000, including in the auxiliary division of the USS Theodore Roosevelt. From 2009 to 2013 he worked at Dugway as a maintenance painter and sign technician for [[C Martin Company|C Martin]], a DOD prime subcontractor for facility and range operations and maintenance, with an office near Dugway's administration centre. He held a level five proximity badge that opened many facilities, but no security clearance, and rode to work from Kville, Utah in a van in which everyone else held top-secret clearances. MS says the government controls about 48% of Dugway and private contractors 52%, which is why so little is disclosed.

### The meter run

About four months into the job in 2009, MS's apprentice called in sick, and MS took over his electrical meter readings, which the government uses to bill contractors on site. The route took him to about 15 facilities he had never seen, and to Avery Technical Center, where he entered a building staff called the "ghost building" because it had no number and no insignia.

The host first suspected the Material Test Facility or the Defensive Test Chamber just south of Avery, across the road from [[Ditto Technical Center]], because both are high-profile Chemical and Biological Defense Program test areas and MS called the room a kind of clean room. But those buildings are well known and numbered. Avery, by contrast, barely appears in searches, is left out of many Army and ATEC documents, and is missing from Dugway's visitor guides, though Ditto is listed. His main source is an unclassified July 2012 [[Environmental Assessment Report on Avery Technical Center|environmental assessment]] of the Air Force's exclusive use of Army land at Dugway. Stressing that everything he discusses is in the public domain, he summarizes what it says about Avery:

- It is a 40-acre area bordering Michael Army Airfield, and both are a focus of the 388th Range Squadron from Hill.
- It served partly for the squadron's air testing and training.
- Historically it hosted radiological studies and research into handling radioactive material, including the [[Atomic Energy Commission]]'s radiobiological warfare laboratories.
- It has an internal rail system for moving radioactive material, which the host calls "truly massive" for the underground question.

Aerial views label none of Avery's buildings, unlike Ditto's. The assessment's map shows the 102 Avery tunnel entrance, 1012 photo operations and maintenance, 1038 general storage and 1016 classified storage. The host bets MS's encounter took place at building 1010, test operations.

### The ghost building

MS opened the door to an M16 in his chest, and believes his level five badge is all that saved him. Inside, in what he took for a small clean room, "white coats" were walking round a craft, analysing it, while several armed guards stood by. He describes time slowing, as in a car crash, while he took everything in. :wiki-cue{t=2371 video="rePrVmR6a68" video-title="Dugway Proving Ground - UFO Legacy Programs"}

The craft, as the host relays MS's account:

- **The disc.** A saucer about 10 feet tall and 20 feet in circumference, hovering 10 to 12 inches off the floor under its own power, with no noise.
- **The finish.** No seams, fasteners or door. It looked cast in one piece, like brushed metal, "flawless", in a way MS says we could not replicate today.
- **The component.** The bottom section had been removed, so MS thought it was being dismantled or prepared to move. It exposed a conical or triangular component that seemed to float almost independently and generate power. The host compares it to the extraterrestrial reactor [[Bob Lazar]] drew for the "sport model" saucer.
- **The display.** Beside the craft stood a flat-screen display showing what looked to MS like coordinates on a mapping system.

The host identifies the six technicians as probably ATEC and Battelle scientists. He asks the same question of the guards and weighs two possibilities:

Chenega's subcontractor Vance Federal Security Services and [[Wackenhut Services]], where Bobby Ray Inman once sat on the board, were subcontractors at Army installations. ### Interrogation and GP

The guards took MS to the Garrison building at nearby Ditto Technical Center. Two men in black suits, whom MS took for FBI or CIA and the host suggests may have been the NSA's alleged [[Men in Black]], questioned him for six hours. He kept saying he had no idea what he had seen and was only doing his job, and was eventually let go. He kept working at Dugway under his confidentiality agreement. :wiki-cue{t=2780 video="rePrVmR6a68" video-title="Dugway Proving Ground - UFO Legacy Programs"}

In the months and years afterwards, MS asked around, and by his own account got a man the host calls GP to talk about his situation "by accident". GP was a DOD chemist by degree who had been promoted into Dugway's UAP legacy work, above ground and underground, and may still be there. He said little about himself. He told MS the craft was extraterrestrial and moved by gravity displacement. He said the display was a mapping system on which two reference points could be set to "generate a wormhole and drop through", which is how such craft travel between stars so quickly. MS lost touch with GP when he left C Martin in 2014, and the host is trying to reach him. He finds a chemist unusual in legacy-program stories, which usually feature engineers, physicists and military officers, but natural at the nation's premier chemical and biological test site. GP might have worked on the craft's propulsion chemistry, on biologics or on metallurgy.

## The Underground Facility

Both GP and a current railroad co-worker of MS told him of a massive underground facility beneath Dugway. The co-worker says he helped dig its tunnels, and the host and MS are arranging to meet him. :wiki-cue{t=2995 video="rePrVmR6a68" video-title="Dugway Proving Ground - UFO Legacy Programs"} The host sets this against the open record on DUMBs. Such bases were often planned as underground ICBM sites, command bunkers, corporate facilities and continuity-of-government installations, and some plans connected them by tunnel. Studies by [[TRW]] explored such networks, dug by [[Tunnel Boring Machine (TBM)|tunnel boring machines]] and [[Nuclear Subterrene|nuclear subterrenes]]. The National Research Council held a 1981–82 workshop on deep underground defense facilities, and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], one of its key members, lists western tunnels tens of thousands of feet long, tens of feet wide and under hundreds or thousands of feet of rock.

According to GP:

- **Size.** The facility lies at an undisclosed depth and spans 1,300 square miles, as much as the proving ground itself.
- **Rail.** A light rail system connects it to "every base in the nation". The tunnels are wide enough to carry a 747 or the president's plane "anywhere they need to without putting it in the air". The host takes the trains to be [[Maglev Underground Transportation|maglev]], which can reach 375 mph, and notes that a 1986 [[Los Alamos National Laboratory|Los Alamos]] study proposed maglev-served tunnels under the Moon.
- **Entrance and supply.** The surface entrance is only an elevator for personnel, in a tiny building on the West Desert Test Center near German Village. Supplies, food and logistics arrive through the tunnels from other underground sites.
- **Granite Peak.** Much of the facility lies under Granite Peak, just west of the entrance and the Avery and Ditto areas.

The host did not reveal the entrance when he first covered MS. He does now because Greer has published it in the DPI archive. It lies close to Avery and Michael Army Airfield, between properties listed as Air Force lease rentals, and Google Earth imagery suggests it was built between 1993 and 2006, though it may be older. The 388th Range Squadron runs several sites on and just north of Granite Peak, including mini-mute threat emitter stations used to train pilots to recognize enemy radar. The host wonders whether the Granite Peak South station connects to the facility under the mountain. :wiki-cue{t=3222 video="rePrVmR6a68" video-title="Dugway Proving Ground - UFO Legacy Programs"}

> [!warning] Public domain only, and do not go there
> Twice in the video the host stresses that everything he says about the underground facility is public domain. He was criticized for naming a possible underground site when he covered [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane]] and [[Randy Anderson]], and he names this location only because Greer has already published it. He urges viewers never to look for it: Dugway is protected land and airspace, and if the site is what he believes, it is a military asset.

## Battelle and the West Desert Test Center

The host closes on Dugway's contractors. MS said more than half of Dugway's operations are contract work, and according to the GAO about 77% of the West Desert Test Center's acknowledged work in fiscal year 2013 was for the Chemical and Biological Defense Program. That leads the host to Battelle. He has covered it before, for [[Project Stork]], its parallel UAP study alongside Project Blue Book, and for its links to [[Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium Alloy)|nickel-titanium alloys]], which may have been the shape-memory alloy recovered at [[Roswell Crash|Roswell]]. At Fort Detrick, Maryland, Battelle runs a biodefense institute and manages the National Biodefense Analysis and Countermeasures Center, an FFRDC for the Department of Homeland Security's Under Secretary for Science and Technology. Fort Detrick has lately entered UFO lore as a possible store for non-human biologics, though the host sets aside a Reddit whistleblower's claims about it as needing more research.

A Chemical and Biological Defense Program partners chart lists Dugway and Battelle among a select few program locations. The West Desert Test Center and Fort Detrick's interagency biodefense campus are two of the program's four primary R&D and test sites. Battelle has also run the Chemical and Biological Defense Information Analysis Center at Dugway. Researcher Anthony Bragalia has pointed to documents showing that Battelle's design-build division engineered sealed test structures at Dugway meeting containment standards "never achieved before", and that its services include clean rooms. That is the kind of room MS described. Then the host turns to three careers:

::wiki-panel{title="Ryan W. Harris" tone="accent"}
[[Ryan W Harris|Harris]] is a chemical engineer and former Army chemical officer. In 2000 he became a Battelle project manager at Dugway, overseeing testing and training programs. In 2004 he moved to a Dugway project manager post, and in 2012 he rose to director of the West Desert Test Center, in charge of all its chemical, biological and acquisition test programs.

[[Greg Frank|Frank]], of the Army Chemical Corps, was a chemist in Dugway's Chemical Laboratory Division in 1984–85 and a test officer on vehicle and chemical munition programs. On leaving service in 1985 he joined Battelle as a project manager. By 2008 he was executive vice president of Battelle Science and Technology International, over more than 5,000 staff.

::wiki-panel{title="Anders Weborg (as named in the video)"}
He was formerly director of the West Desert Test Center's special programs division, running daily test operations, and now works for the Department of Energy. Before Dugway he was a Battelle test director for chemical and biological defense equipment.

"Interesting," the host says, "how a Battelle project manager becomes director of an MRTFB where witnesses have claimed a UAP legacy program employing DOD chemists takes place." Jacobs Solutions, which does range testing and lab services at the West Desert Test Center, is another suspect for future work. But because Dugway is so focused on chemical and biological weapons, Battelle remains his primary suspect. :wiki-cue{t=3670 video="rePrVmR6a68" video-title="Dugway Proving Ground - UFO Legacy Programs"}

## Closing Remarks

The host made the video because Dugway is constantly linked to UFOs with almost no evidence behind the claim. His example is a Quest TV documentary in which Nick Pope and [[Richard Dolan]] call it the new Area 51 without presenting any testimony. In his view, MS's account makes Dugway a better case than rumour. It points to a joint program between two services' MRTFBs, where witness Ed's program joined two Air Force ones. He tried and failed to find links between the 388th and the Edwards 412th. Even so, he believes there is enough here for a policymaker to investigate MS's claims properly, and he hopes to interview MS again, ideally in person, along with GP and the co-worker who says he dug the tunnels.

Several further threads appear only in the closing remarks:

- **Weinbrenner.** Colonel Weinbrenner was one of two Air Force men who, in the 1970s, showed producers Allan Sandler and Robert Emenegger film of a Holloman Air Force Base landing, a story later told by [[James Fox]]. On his deathbed he reportedly told friends and family that Utah is where the alien bodies are stored. With Dugway's ties to Fort Detrick and its secretive chemical and biological work, the host wonders whether it studies biologics as well as craft.
- **The host's friend.** A friend who served in the Army in the early 2010s says he travelled across state lines between underground facilities on a train so fast that it felt like the takeoff acceleration he fears in planes. He entered through a small building that looked like a janitorial or water-treatment shed and led to an elevator, much like the Dugway entrance. The host says he has spoken to several such people.
- **Continuity of government.** Underground sites are real: in the 1990s and 2000s [[AT&T]] kept underground server banks and ICBM storage. GP's line about moving the president's plane reminds the host of continuity-of-government installations, and of [[John B. Alexander]]'s claim that [[Majestic 12]] is real and works like a continuity-of-government body.
- **Missing funds.** Witness Ed, who served at the Pentagon in Air Force [[RDT&E (Research, Development, Test and Evaluation)|RDT&E]], says $34 to $40 billion went missing through non-authorized transfers of authority funds in fiscal years 2013 and 2014. The host suggests DOGE examine Air Force and Army RDT&E. :wiki-cue{t=4545 video="rePrVmR6a68" video-title="Dugway Proving Ground - UFO Legacy Programs"}

## Key Claims

- Dugway Proving Ground, often called "Area 52", allegedly became the successor to Area 51's UFO programs in the late 1990s after the Nevada site was heavily exposed. AH put the share of Area 51 moved to Utah at 35 to 40%.
- Insiders pointed researchers to the Dugway area before any public exposure. A 1996 source described nine exclusion zones south-west of Tooele County, four of them with underground locations.
- A craft allegedly crashed and was buried near Garrison, Utah in June 1953, beside the Desert Range Experimental Station. The host considers the case a dead end.
- A 1967 photograph of a disc over Provo may show an early Flux Liner ARV, suggesting ARV testing between the Utah range and Edwards.
- Witness MS, a C Martin contractor, says that in 2009 he entered an unmarked "ghost building" at Avery Technical Center and saw a seamless, brushed-metal saucer about 10 feet tall hovering silently, partly dismantled and studied by technicians under armed guard.
- MS was interrogated for six hours at the Garrison building in Ditto Technical Center by two men in black suits. The Senate Select Committee on Intelligence later asked him to testify.
- The guards near the craft were probably industrial security specialists rather than contract guards such as Chenega or Wackenhut, according to the host.
- GP, a senior DOD chemist in Dugway's legacy work, said the craft was extraterrestrial, propelled by gravity displacement, and navigated by setting reference points to open a wormhole.
- According to GP, a 1,300-square-mile underground facility lies beneath Dugway, mainly under Granite Peak, and is reached by a personnel elevator near German Village. It is linked by rail to other underground bases nationwide.
- The host theorizes a joint Army and Air Force MRTFB legacy program between the West Desert Test Center and the 388th Range Squadron, using Avery's internal rail system and the south range of the Utah Test and Training Range.
- Battelle Memorial Institute is the host's primary contractor suspect. Several West Desert Test Center leaders, including its director Ryan W. Harris, previously worked for Battelle.
- Colonel Weinbrenner reportedly said on his deathbed that Utah is where alien bodies are stored.
