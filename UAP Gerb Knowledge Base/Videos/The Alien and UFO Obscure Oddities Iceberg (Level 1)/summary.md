## Overview

"The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 1)" opens UAP Gerb's six-part series on the "Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg", a chart posted to Reddit by the user Blake_Sama. An iceberg ranks the parts of a topic from the best known, above the waterline, to the most obscure, at the bottom. Gerb calls this top layer "the Observer layer" and works through its twenty entries in turn: famous cases, scientific frameworks, alien archetypes, belief systems and pieces of UFO folklore. He did not make the iceberg and has no say over its entries, so he promises to say plainly when an entry is "bogus or absolutely absurd" and when he strongly agrees with one. The series continues in [[Videos/The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 2)/summary|Level 2]].

Gerb keeps that promise. He treats the 1950 [[McMinnville UFO Photographs]] as a genuine distant craft on the strength of [[Condon Committee]] astronomer [[William H. Hartman]]'s analysis. He calls the [[Pentagon UFO Videos]] "classics" and says he trusts the pilots who recorded them over debunkers such as [[Mick West]]. He will not rule out an anomalous craft over Phoenix in 1997, given the hundreds of witnesses who included Arizona's governor, [[Fife Symington]]. Against that, he thinks [[Crop Circles|crop circles]] are almost certainly human-made, favours the prosaic reading of the [[Nazca Desert, Peru|Nazca Lines]] over [[Erich von Däniken]]'s landing strips, suspects the [[Battle of Los Angeles]] has an ordinary explanation, calls the suit-wearing [[Men in Black]] "a total hoax", and refuses to spend any time on "Little Green Men".

The layer's one sustained argument comes at its end. Gerb retells the [[1952 UFOs Over Washington DC|1952 radar sightings over Washington]], which alarmed President [[Harry Truman]] and drew the [[CIA]]'s interest, and argues that the CIA's response, the [[Robertson Panel]] and its brief to "strip" UFOs of their "special status", is the direct origin of the decades-long stigma around the subject. The same thread runs through his asides elsewhere in the episode: the Condon Committee, he says, was "basically told to explain UFOs away as swamp gas and weather balloons", and the phrase "Little Green Men" survives mainly as a way for officials to wave sightings off.

## Famous cases

### The McMinnville photographs (1950)

Gerb calls the [[McMinnville UFO Photographs]] some of the most famous UFO photographs ever taken. On 11 May 1950, walking back to her farmhouse near [[McMinnville, Oregon]], [[Evelyn Trent]] saw a slow-moving, metallic, disc-shaped object heading toward her from the north-east. She called her husband, [[Paul Trent]], who saw it too, fetched his camera and took two photographs.

Because the photographs became so well known, the negatives were lent in 1967 to [[William H. Hartman]], an astronomer on the [[Condon Committee]], the government-funded UFO study run from the University of Colorado Boulder. Gerb wants to make a video on the committee itself, which he says was "basically told to explain UFOs away as swamp gas and weather balloons". Hartman nevertheless found the images impressive. The underside of the craft looked brighter than the underside of an oil tank also in the pictures, which he put down to atmospheric extinction, the effect that makes distant mountains look washed out and blue. That placed the object farther from the camera than the tank, not hanging from a string close by, as debunkers claim. Hartman wrote that this was one of the few UFO reports in which every factor investigated, "geometric, psychological, and physical", was consistent with "an extraordinary flying object, silvery, metallic, disc-shaped, tens of meters in diameter, and evidently artificial".

### Maury Island and the Men in Black (1947)

The [[Men in Black]] of UFO folklore are suit-clad government agents said to question, threaten, harass and even kill people who have seen UFOs. Gerb traces the legend to 27 June 1947 and the [[Maury Island Incident]]. [[Harold Dahl]] was working on Puget Sound, off the eastern shore of [[Maury Island, Washington]], when he saw six donut-shaped craft hovering about half a mile above his boat. Debris fell from them, and a piece struck and killed his dog. Dahl took photographs and some footage. The next day a man in a black suit took him to a local diner, recounted in great detail what Dahl had seen, and told him: "What I have said to you is proof to you that I know a great deal more about this experience of yours than you will want to believe." Dahl was told not to speak of the incident, or bad things would happen.

Gerb's own view is that the suit-wearing Men in Black are "a total hoax" that distract from something real: people in the US government and high-ranking officials working to stop the spread of genuine sightings and footage. Those people, he stresses, are more often "men in military fatigues", as in the [[Roswell Crash|Roswell incident]] with [[Jesse Marcel]] and the girl whose fireman father saw the crashed craft, and with Lieutenant [[Robert Jacobs]] and his footage of a UFO disabling a nuclear warhead.

### Betty and Barney Hill (1961)

The [[Betty and Barney Hill Abduction Incident|Betty and Barney Hill incident]] is one of the most famous alien abduction stories in US history. At about 10:30 p.m. on 19 September 1961, [[Betty Hill|Betty]] and [[Barney Hill]] were driving home from a holiday at Niagara Falls and Montreal, just south of [[Lancaster, New Hampshire]], when Betty noticed a bright point of light below the Moon and Jupiter. It moved erratically and grew brighter. Through binoculars she saw an odd-shaped craft with flashing multicoloured lights cross the face of the Moon. Barney took it for an airliner until it turned toward them and descended. It came down toward their car, and Barney stopped in the middle of the highway.

The silent craft, which reminded Barney of a large pancake, hovered 80 to 100 feet above them. Barney said he saw 8 to 11 humanoid figures peering out of its windows; all but one withdrew, and that one told him telepathically, "Stay where you are and keep looking." Red lights telescoped out of the craft's sides and a long structure lowered from its underside. Barney later told the [[National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena]] that the beings were "somehow not human".

The couple reached home with incomplete, fragmented memories. About ten days later Betty had vivid dreams on five successive nights, in which small humanoids, 5 feet to 5 feet 4 inches tall, escorted her with Barney walking behind, and later returned her to her car. Their 178-mile drive should have taken about four hours but had taken seven, so the Hills sought hypnosis to explain the three missing hours.

In his first session, which Gerb calls extremely disturbing, Barney recalled fleeing the craft, then feeling an irresistible urge to pull off the road into the woods, where six figures stood on a dirt road. One stared into his eyes: "All I see are these eyes... they're just up close to me, pressing against my eyes."

Betty's sessions were "remarkably similar" to Barney's. She recalled being aboard the craft, the technology she saw, and a star map in what she guessed was its control room. Some read the map as showing the Sun and the beings' home in the Zeta Reticuli system (see the claims below).

Gerb says there is "a lot more to study" in the case than a segment of a few minutes can cover.

### The Phoenix Lights (1997)

The [[Phoenix Lights]] were a mass sighting of lights in a boomerang or V formation over [[Phoenix, Arizona]], on 13 March 1997, one of the largest and most perplexing in US history. The official explanation for what hundreds of people saw was flares and formations of A-10 Thunderbolt II aircraft. But Arizona's governor, [[Fife Symington]], saw the event himself and asked for an official inquiry, which was refused. "I'm a pilot and I know just about every machine that flies," he said. "It was bigger than anything I've ever seen." In a CNN editorial (Gerb dates it to 2017; CNN published it in November 2007) he described a dramatically large, silent, delta-shaped craft and rejected the flare explanation.

### The Battle of Los Angeles (1942)

On the night of 24 February 1942, weeks after the attack on Pearl Harbor, radar signatures over [[Los Angeles, California]] sent the local military into a scramble and set off hours of anti-aircraft fire: "the air erupted like a volcano", one witness said. Five civilians died, three in car accidents and two of heart attacks. The barrage was later put down to war nerves or a meteorological balloon. On 26 February the *Los Angeles Times* published a photograph of searchlights converging on what looks like a saucer, but the photo may have been heavily altered. The [[Battle of Los Angeles]] is one of the cases that drew Gerb to UFOs as a boy; he now thinks it probably has a prosaic explanation, but keeps "a lot of nostalgia" for it.

### Washington, 1952, and the origin of the stigma

Gerb ends the layer with the case he considers the most consequential. On Saturday 19 July 1952, at 11:40 p.m., air traffic controller [[Edward Nugent]] spotted seven objects on his radar at Washington National Airport, moving erratically compared with any normal aircraft. Two controllers in the radar-equipped tower looked out and saw a bright light hover and then leave at great speed; soon objects filled every section of the scopes and moved over the White House and the Capitol. The sightings came back a week later, and their effect, Gerb argues, outlasted everything else on this layer. He promises a separate video on it.

## Theories and frameworks

### Extraterrestrial life and alien invasion

The layer opens with its simplest entry: extraterrestrial life is any life, carbon-based or otherwise (silicon, say), that did not originate on Earth, from a single-celled organism to a civilization that can cross the stars in hours or days. On alien invasion, Gerb admits it is impossible to attribute human motives to a non-human intelligence, but finds it "rather idiotic" that a species able to cross the cosmos and warp spacetime would be warmongering as humans are. He thinks humans would be more interesting to study from a distance: "a hairless primate" that has just learned to harness nuclear technology and stands at the foot of exploring its solar system, or of destroying itself. That, he suggests, may be something like the [[Great Filter]] the [[Fermi Paradox]] talks about.

### Panspermia

[[Panspermia]] holds that life exists throughout the universe in microbial or cellular form and is spread by space dust, meteors, asteroids, comets and planetoids. Gerb's example is Mars: if it once had life, an impact could have thrown microbe-laden Martian soil into space and carried "the seeds of life" to Earth. He names three types:

- **Lithopanspermia**: life carried from one solar system to another.
- **Ballistic panspermia**: life carried from one planet to another in the same system.
- **Directed panspermia**: life sent deliberately, as humans might one day send it to other planets.

### The Kardashev scale

The [[Kardashev Scale]], proposed by the Soviet astronomer [[Nikolai Kardashev]] in 1964, measures a civilization's technological advancement by the energy it can harness. Gerb calls it "a fascinating method".

- **Type I, planetary**: harnesses all the energy available on its planet, controlling natural forces such as the weather.
- **Type II, stellar**: harnesses all of its star's output, through hypothetical means such as a [[Dyson Sphere]], and occupies other planets and moons in its system.
- **Type III, galactic**: controls energy on the scale of its galaxy, travelling from star to star and colonizing it.
- **Types IV and V**: extensions to a galactic cluster and to an entire universe, which may not be possible even in theory.

Humanity rates about 0.7.

### Grays

The [[Grays]] are the most famous depiction of extraterrestrials ever. Gerb says the archetype can possibly be traced to 1917, when the occultist [[Aleister Crowley]] claimed to have contacted a being named [[Lam]], but that it really became popular with the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]], and that the Hills' humanoids match it too. Grays are so common in abduction claims that, by the figures Gerb gives, they make up about 50 per cent of claimed abductions or sightings in Australia, 73 per cent in the US, 48 per cent in continental Europe and 12 per cent in the UK. He runs through three theories of what they are: biological drones used by other civilizations, which would fit their emotionless, hive-minded manner; future humans, evolved to rely less on muscle, spend more time indoors and grow large eyes from looking at screens ("I like it", though he is unsure); or simply humanoids that evolved on a planet with conditions like Earth's.

### Nordic aliens

The [[Nordic Aliens]] are said to belong to a [[Galactic Federation]] and to travel in craft from spherical to cigar-shaped. Gerb points to [[Travis Walton]], whose abduction was dramatized in *Fire in the Sky*: aboard the craft Walton describes several kinds of being, the common Gray among them, and a Nordic-like race of tall humanoids. Gerb does not know whether the Nordics are real, and says he prefers not to focus on abduction stories or alien races: "there is something in our sky that's of non-human intelligence", he says, and there is a lot to work through before deciding what these things are, or whether they are extraterrestrial at all.

### Reptilians

[[David Icke]]'s [[Reptilian Theory]] holds that a shape-shifting reptilian race is slowly taking control of Earth by assuming human form and gaining political and social power. Icke names the Rothschilds, the Bush family and the British royal family among the reptilians. Gerb isn't sure what their conspiracy is supposed to achieve, but notes the theory's popularity: people search videos of political leaders for "lizard eyes", and Denver International Airport, near his home, has leaned into the joke with posters of reptiles.

### Little Green Men

Gerb declines to spend time on "Little Green Men", which he calls essentially green Grays. The phrase, he says, is mostly used by politicians and in Pentagon and military statements to shrug off and mock UAP sightings ("we found no evidence of little green men").

## Belief systems and folklore

### Crop circles

[[Crop Circles]] are large designs flattened into fields, usually wheat, whose symmetry and complexity lead many to credit them to paranormal or extraterrestrial causes. In 1991 two pranksters, [[Doug Bower]] and [[Dave Chorley]], took credit for the first wave, made with planks, rope and baseball caps. Gerb thinks they are almost certainly human-made and moves on.

### The Nazca Lines

The [[Nazca Desert, Peru|Nazca Lines]] are geoglyphs in the soil of the Nazca Desert in southern Peru, most of them made between 500 BC and 500 AD by removing stones and pebbles to expose differently coloured ground. From the ground they look like lines; from the air they resolve into animals. In *Chariots of the Gods* (1968), [[Erich von Däniken]] proposed that extraterrestrials made them as landing sites. Gerb favours the religious explanation, but admits the [[Ancient Astronaut Theory|ancient astronaut theory]] is fun to think about: "that's why Ancient Aliens has like 30 seasons".

### Raelism

[[Raelism]] is a UFO religion founded in France in the 1970s by [[Claude Vorilhon]]. It teaches that an extraterrestrial species, the Elohim, created humanity with advanced technology; that Buddha, Jesus and Muhammad were mistaken for gods though they were Elohim-human hybrids, among 40 such prophets; and that since the bombing of Hiroshima in 1945 humanity has lived in an Age of Apocalypse, bound to destroy itself unless it learns to use its technology peacefully. The Raelians have sought to build an embassy for the Elohim with a landing pad for their spacecraft. Gerb finds it harmless, not as dangerous as "some other UFO death cult", and "a fun little read".

## Government evidence

### The Pentagon UFO videos

The [[Pentagon UFO Videos]] are three military recordings, labelled FLIR, Go Fast and Gimbal, that were classified until their release through the efforts of [[Luis Elizondo]] and [[Chris Mellon]] and publication by *The New York Times* with [[Leslie Kean]] and [[Ralph Blumenthal]]. Gerb focuses on Gimbal, recorded in 2015 off the coast of Florida by an F/A-18's targeting pod: the pilot said the object moved with no visible means of propulsion and held still in severe winds. Part of the video has not been declassified, as the pilots and Mellon confirm; in it, the craft is said to rendezvous with other craft and form a V. "I would give anything to be able to see that footage," Gerb says.

### Area 51

[[Area 51]] is the common name for a highly classified US Air Force installation near Groom Lake in the Nevada desert, beside former nuclear test ranges. Long associated with UFOs and experimental aircraft, it went unacknowledged until 2013, when the CIA acknowledged it in response to a Freedom of Information Act request. It is known for aircraft tests such as the U-2's and for reverse-engineering adversaries' aircraft such as MiGs; many believe it also houses the reverse engineering and test flights of UFOs. Saving the subject for a video of its own, Gerb leaves one item: a project patch supplied by [[Eric Taber]], who submitted a memorandum for record to [[AARO]]. Taber says the patch belonged to his great-uncle Sam, who worked at the base as a contractor and head of security for an engineering group, the radar cross-section training team, and who told him the base housed an egg-shaped UFO found intact in the Nevada desert. Gerb notes that Taber has put this in a legal document to AARO.

### The Hoia Baciu forest

The [[Hoia Baciu Forest, Romania|Hoia Baciu forest]] in Romania is said to be the most haunted forest in the world, with many strange phenomena including UFO sightings. Gerb had never heard of it, and adds only that UFO sightings and photographs were apparently reported there in the 1960s.

## Key Claims

- William H. Hartman of the Condon Committee concluded that the McMinnville photographs showed "an extraordinary flying object, silvery, metallic, disc-shaped, tens of meters in diameter, and evidently artificial".
- Atmospheric extinction in the McMinnville photographs placed the object farther from the camera than a nearby oil tank, which Gerb presents as ruling out a small model on a string.
- The Condon Committee was, in Gerb's words, "basically told to explain UFOs away as swamp gas and weather balloons".
- Harold Dahl's encounter near Maury Island on 27 June 1947, and the suited man who warned him the next day, are the origin of the Men in Black legend; Gerb considers the suited figures a hoax and the real suppression the work of officials and soldiers.
- Betty and Barney Hill lost about three hours on 19 September 1961; under hypnosis both described being taken by humanoid beings, and Barney told NICAP the beings were "somehow not human".
- Researchers put the odds of Betty Hill's star map being a random configuration of stars at "at least 1,000 to one against", while Carl Sagan offered a rebuttal in *Cosmos*.
- Arizona Governor Fife Symington saw the Phoenix Lights, was refused an official inquiry, and rejected the Air Force's explanation of flares and A-10 formations.
- Part of the Gimbal footage, in which the object is said to join other craft in a V formation, remains classified, according to the pilots and Chris Mellon.
- Eric Taber told AARO in a memorandum for record that his great-uncle learned Area 51 housed an intact egg-shaped UFO found in the Nevada desert.
- The 1952 Washington radar sightings prompted President Truman to call his Air Force aides and led the CIA to form the Robertson Panel, whose brief to strip UFOs of their "special status" Gerb identifies as the origin of the decades-long UFO stigma.
- By the figures Gerb gives, Grays account for about 50 per cent of claimed abductions or sightings in Australia, 73 per cent in the US, 48 per cent in continental Europe and 12 per cent in the UK.
- Earth rates about 0.7 on the Kardashev scale.
- Gerb dismisses crop circles, the suited Men in Black and "Little Green Men" as distractions from substantive UAP evidence.
