## Overview

This video is the second in UAP Gerb's "Redacted List" series, which mines the redacted entries in the witness list that [[Steven Greer]] and the [[Disclosure Project]] published in 2024 as part of their document archive. The list holds more than 700 names, including [[Michael Herrera]], [[Robert Wood]], [[Richard Doty]], [[Jonathan Weygandt]] and [[Bob Lazar]], plus 22 pages of redacted ones. The first volume produced the Dugway witness [[MS (Dugway Witness)|MS]] and a more shadowy witness, "TB". This one covers witness 11063, whom Gerb calls [[Ed (Witness 11063)|Ed]]: a career Air Force officer who, according to Greer's files, tested reverse-engineered technology as a test director of an electronic warfare group at [[Edwards Air Force Base]].

Gerb says he has worked out Ed's identity and tried many times to reach him, but will not reveal it, for Ed's operational security and his family's safety. He uses only public sources, and says he has confirmed from them that Ed served at [[Nellis Air Force Base]], Edwards and the [[Pentagon]]. His evidence is two documents Greer uploaded in late summer 2024: an internal Disclosure Project email summarising Ed's background and motives, and two pages of notes from Greer's 25 November 2023 phone call with him. Gerb walks through the notes line by line, following Ed from Nellis and a briefing at [[Area 51]]'s S4 site, through [[Hanscom Air Force Base, Massachusetts|Hanscom]], to the [[Air Force Test Pilot School]] and the [[412th Test Wing]] at Edwards, an aborted Air Force plan to "go public", and a final posting at the Pentagon where he noted large transfers of funds into "non-blue" accounts.

His thesis is that Ed's testimony points to a joint reverse-engineering program for craft of non-human origin, run between Edwards and Nellis, Area 51 and S4 through the [[Nevada Test and Training Range (NTR)|Nevada Test and Training Range]], out of the 412th Test Wing, with [[Lockheed Martin]] and [[Boeing]] as contractors. He concludes with high confidence that Ed's work centred on building and testing [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicles]]. He then asks whether Edwards has other stories to support that, and finds them in [[Gordon Cooper]]'s 1957 film of a landed disc, Air Force propulsion research, [[Edgar Fouche|Edgar Fouché]]'s [[TR-3B]] claims, and three cases from [[Leonard Stringfield]]'s files. He ends with an alleged 1991 NRO document naming a "Blackjack Team", which Greer's sources split into a Nellis half and an Edwards half.

## Witness 11063

Gerb first noticed Ed when Greer released his witness list. The entry for 11063 reads, in part, "career Air Force Nellis, Edwards, test director of electronics warfare group, tested reverse engineer technology". Unlike witnesses MS and TB, Ed had no files in the archive at first. The first lead came in late summer 2024, when the archive added intelligence briefing modules. Module 3, "Covert Government Retrieval and Handling ET Vehicles", adapts a March 2024 oral presentation by Greer. It discusses more than 130 downings and retrievals of non-human objects involving [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works]] and Boeing, carried out with electrogravitic technology and EMP weapons. It also mentions witness MS and a former senior Naval Research Laboratory scientist who described large, silent triangular ARVs over remote parts of [[Dugway Proving Ground]].

Towards its end, Module 3 describes a lieutenant colonel who managed pilots at Edwards and trained those who flew man-made UFOs. Greer introduced him to the Senate intelligence and armed services committees, and he was waiting to hear from members of the House Oversight Committee, including Representatives [[Anna Paulina Luna]] and [[Tim Burchett]]. Having left the Air Force in 2009, he was briefed on operations at Edwards, Nellis and Area 51, and believes that because they were illegal he is free to discuss them in an open hearing under oath. Around the time the module appeared, Greer uploaded two documents under witness 11063.

### Free energy and Kirkpatrick

The email's line on free energy leads Gerb to a topic Greer has raised since the late 1990s: energy and propulsion systems derived from off-world technology and, Greer says, suppressed. He summarises the 2001 Disclosure Project briefing document's claim that insiders could prove in open hearings that the US holds classified [[Zero Point Energy|zero-point]] energy and anti-gravity systems, said to tap the quantum vacuum and able to replace all current energy and transport. He recommends [[Nick Cook]]'s *The Hunt for Zero Point*, [[Jesse Michaels]]'s video on the aerospace search for anti-gravity, and Greer's 2023 documentary *The Lost Century*. Another redacted entry, 10747, is described as a former chairman and vice president of a well-known corporation, which Gerb believes is General Electric, with "huge details" on the suppression of free energy.

On Kirkpatrick, Gerb calls the charge a serious one that many UFO investigators will agree with. He cites AARO's historical report and its 2022 contracts with a firm he says was built to plug whistleblower leaks. He adds a US Space Command FOIA release, posted on Twitter, showing a January 2023 meeting that Kirkpatrick scheduled with SPACECOM and senior joint command staff on "UAP response and recovery and material transfer".

## Ed's Career, Station by Station

### Nellis and the range (1999–2002)

Ed worked with Lockheed as a range technical contractor at Nellis in the early 2000s, alongside smaller specialised contractors. Gerb found no public record of Boeing in his work history. He points to a Lockheed Martin case study in which the company updated the intelligence collection system of the USAF Warfare Center, reconfiguring a two-phase security classification system used by the [[NGA|National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency]] for the Combined Air and Space Operations Center at Nellis. He notes that [[David Grusch]] was detailed from the NGA to the UAP Task Force, and that journalist [[Christopher Sharp|Chris Sharp]] has reported NGA involvement in undersea crash retrievals. Gerb speculates that such systems could be used to monitor UAP and their retrieval.

Greer's notes say Ed was briefed at Area 51's S4, where "a major said they had caught/captured lots of aircraft", by way of a 30-minute video of essentially invisible "inspection craft". Gerb takes these to be either crash retrievals or ARVs shown for inspection. He compares them with the "Flux Liner" ARVs that [[Brad Sorenson]] saw with panels removed at the 1988 exhibit described by [[Mark McCandlish]], shown at [[Norton Air Force Base]], about 80 miles from Edwards. Other witnesses he has covered describe ARVs with cloaking. He notes that the Groom Lake facility is administered by a detachment from Edwards, the base Ed would later move to.

At Nellis, Ed worked at [[Building 200]] under the first commander of the 98th Range Wing, whose name is redacted. The wing, now the Nevada Test and Training Range, began operations in 2001. It is the largest contiguous air and ground space for peacetime military operations in the free world, with 2.9 million acres of land and 5,000 square miles of restricted airspace. It also supports Department of Energy testing and serves as the single point of contact for range customers. Groom Lake sits on its South Range. Gerb could not identify the first commander, so he examines [[Christopher Haave|Colonel Christopher Haave]], commander from 2005 to 2007. Haave later directed global trade controls at Boeing, managed relationships with the Office of the Secretary of Defense for Raytheon, and advised Vice President [[Dick Cheney]] on national security affairs.

### Hanscom (2002–2005)

Greer writes that Ed worked with [[MIT]] on foreign military assets at Hanscom but saw only terrestrial craft. Gerb is still interested in MIT. He has written before about university affiliated research centers in UAP programs, most recently [[Eric A. Walker]] of Penn State's Applied Research Laboratory, who admitted involvement in the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|Kecksburg]] crash. MIT has its own Army-sponsored center, the Institute for Soldier Nanotechnologies. The only Hanscom link to UAP he found is an anonymous 14 March 1975 letter to Stringfield. It reports a "Colonel Sims" naming Hanscom and MIT among the sites performing UFO operations, with Edwards, [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base|Wright-Patterson]], Carswell, Los Alamos and Sandia.

### Edwards (2006–2010)

The Edwards test pilot school trains pilots, navigators and engineers on experimental platforms of [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)|Air Force Materiel Command]]'s 412th Test Wing. It began at Wright-Patterson during the Second World War. Ed was allegedly formally read into several "advanced aircraft programs" at Edwards. Gerb reads the word "formally" as meaning that at Nellis Ed received only fragments, while as a test director he received a full briefing. He sees this as consistent with claims that UAP programs are as compartmented as the Manhattan Project, or more so.

Gerb treats the Edwards period as the most important part of the testimony. The electronic warfare group belongs to the 412th Test Wing, the same wing that houses the test pilot school. If Ed is right, the 412th tests reverse-engineered craft of non-human origin and works with the Nevada range. He calls this one of the biggest takeaways of the whole project.

### The go-public plan and the two-star general

According to Greer, in 2008 Ed was part of an Air Force plan to go public, as director of a redacted program. A two-star general killed it in 2009–2010, and Ed moved to the Pentagon. Gerb found no confirming evidence of the plan, but he examined Air Force major generals of the period. Besides the four compared below, he names C. Donald Alston, H. Brent Baker Sr. and Randy A. Kee.

What he weighs for each candidate is his rank when the plan died, and so whether he could have had the decision-making power to kill it, and the posts that link him to the story. Only his "prime suspect", Thomas K. Andersen, was already a major general then. Andersen commanded the Curtis E. LeMay Center for Doctrine Development and Education, which produces Air Force doctrine and input for joint and multinational doctrine, after Air Staff, Joint Staff, combatant command and NATO posts and a spell as chief of staff and director of intelligence at US Strategic Command. Casey D. Blake later oversaw all Air Force contracting for weapon systems, logistics and operational support as Deputy Assistant Secretary for Contracting. Christopher P. Azzano went on to command the [[Air Force Test Center]], a $31 billion enterprise of more than 19,000 people across Edwards, Eglin and Arnold.

### Wright-Patterson and Hangar 6

According to Ed, Wright-Patterson has a close connection with Edwards, and he made an educated guess that [[Hangar 6]] at Wright-Patterson, held by logistics, is significant. Internal base documents say the hangar was first used by the Signal Corps to install radios and antennas and after the war became a facility for retrofitting and modifying aircraft. Gerb notes that the Wright Field fitness center is in Hangar 22, not Hangar 6. He found almost no internal images or reports on Hangar 6 and makes it a research priority, looking especially for infrastructure able to hold living or deceased biologics, as Ed has claimed.

### The Pentagon (2010–2015)

Greer and Ed said little about his Pentagon work, which was within a research and development panel chain. One line survives: "$34 billion then $40 billion to non-Air Force authorization, non-blue TOA", TOA meaning transfer of authority. Gerb sees it as support for decades of whistleblower claims that UAP programs are funded through off-book unacknowledged special access programs. He links it to [[Catherine Austin Fitts]]'s 11 December 2017 paper on the black budget. It discussed $21 trillion in unauthorized spending at the Department of Defense and HUD between 1998 and 2015, and DoD announced its first department-wide audit soon afterwards. Fitts believes some of the money funded illegal UAP programs.

## Edwards Before Ed

With Area 51 and S4 long associated with Bob Lazar, Gerb asks whether other stories about Edwards support or undercut Ed's claims.

- **1957, Gordon Cooper.** The Mercury astronaut said that while he was a project manager at Edwards, cameramen filming a precision landing facility watched a shiny silver saucer about 30 feet across hover, lower three landing gear and settle on the dry lake bed. They filmed it from within 20 yards before it climbed away at high speed. In a clip, Cooper says he was told by ever more senior officers to send the film to Washington by courier without running it; he held it up to a window and saw good close-up shots.
- **1972, Project Outgrowth.** A document from the Air Force Rocket Propulsion Laboratory at Edwards, *Advanced Propulsion Concepts: [[Project Outgrowth]]*, discusses an anti-gravity program.
- **1988, electrostatic propulsion.** A technical report for the Air Force's astronautics laboratory at Edwards explored the [[Biefeld-Brown Effect|Biefeld–Brown effect]]. Around the same time [[SAIC]], which Gerb has tied to the study of off-world technology, studied similar electric propulsion for Edwards.
- **1990, Aviation Week.** An article reported 45 sightings of aircraft accelerating from near-hover to supersonic speed over Edwards, George Air Force Base and [[China Lake Naval Air Weapons Station|China Lake]]; one was triangular.
- **1998, Edgar Fouché.** The Air Force master sergeant said that at a defense advanced research facility under the Jet Propulsion Laboratory at Edwards in 1979, a full anti-gravity propulsion system was developed for a secret space fleet. He also said graduates of the Edwards test pilot school flew the TR-3B.

Edwards also appears many times in Leonard Stringfield's *Crash Retrieval Status Reports*. Gerb takes three cases from them.

## Gerb's Conclusions

Gerb restates his thesis: Ed's testimony points to a joint reverse-engineering program for craft of non-human intelligence between Edwards and Nellis, Area 51 and S4, stemming from the 412th Test Wing's test pilot school and electronic warfare group. He compares it with the [[Off-World Technologies Division]], which he says dealt with single pieces of technology and near-weapons. In Ed's account, by contrast, the Air Force, with Lockheed Martin, Boeing and possibly others, actually pilots reverse-engineered craft. He does not know whether those craft are TR-3B triangles, as Fouché described, or Flux Liner saucers like those allegedly housed at Norton in 1988, and suggests it may be both.

Because Edwards is a busy base, he reasons that such a program must be confined to very few people in a small area, and wonders whether it connects to underground facilities in the region, such as the [[Helendale]] site Greer has posted about. In material he cut from the video, he tied recent sightings near [[Air Force Plant 42]] in Palmdale, about 30 miles from Edwards, to the base; Norton is about 80 miles away. He calls the whole complex deeply entwined with UFO sightings and reverse-engineering testimony.

## Key Claims

- Witness 11063, "Ed", served in the Air Force from 1999 to 2015 at Nellis, Hanscom, Edwards and the Pentagon, and says he tested reverse-engineered non-human technology.
- At Nellis, Ed was allegedly briefed at Area 51's S4, where a major said they had captured many craft, and he says he saw man-made craft on the Nevada Test and Training Range.
- From 2006 Ed allegedly headed an electronic warfare group in the Edwards 412th Test Wing, acting as intermediary between engineers and test pilots when they had a man-made craft to test; he says test pilot school graduates are trained for such programs.
- According to Greer's notes, a 2008 Air Force plan to go public was killed in 2009–2010 by a two-star general.
- At the Pentagon Ed noted "$34 billion then $40 billion" transferred to non-blue authority, which Gerb reads as black-budget funding for UAP programs.
- Ed has threatened legal action against Lockheed and Boeing, and would testify to AARO only without Sean Kirkpatrick.
- Gerb concludes that a joint reverse-engineering program for non-human craft, centred on ARVs, runs between Edwards and Nellis, Area 51 and S4 out of the 412th Test Wing.
- Stringfield's files describe a craft and occupants held at Edwards (Project Heaven), a 1971 crash near the base, and a recovered craft shipped from Edwards for test flights at Area 51 (Project Red Light).
- An alleged 1991 NRO document lists a "Blackjack Team", which Greer's sources pair with a "Blackjack Control" across Nellis and Edwards.
