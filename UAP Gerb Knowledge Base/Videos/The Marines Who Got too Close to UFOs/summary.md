## Overview

This short, documentary-style video sets two accounts side by side: United States Marines who say they came too close to unidentified craft while serving abroad, and were detained and silenced for it. Gerb opens by noting that the public has heard plenty from Navy and Air Force pilots such as [[David Fravor|Commander David Fravor]] and [[Ryan Graves]], but very little from servicemen on the ground. The first account is that of Lance Corporal [[Jonathan Weygandt]], who says that in the spring of 1997, during the counter-narcotics [[Operation Laser Strike]] in Peru, he found a crashed egg-shaped craft wedged in a cliff face and was arrested by men in black fatigues and "DOE" rain jackets (the [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]]). The second is that of Rifleman [[Michael Herrera]], who says that in October 2009, on a relief mission after the [[2009 Sumatra Earthquake|Sumatra earthquake]], his team saw unmarked US forces loading a huge octagonal craft in the Indonesian jungle and were held at gunpoint (the [[2009 Indonesia UFO Encounter (Herrera)|2009 Indonesia encounter]]).

Gerb's emphasis falls less on what Weygandt saw than on who he saw. He identifies the men at the Peru crash as a [[Department of Energy]] [[DOE Special Response Teams (SRTs)|Special Response Team]], the "nuclear commandos", working within the [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|Nuclear Emergency Search Team]], and ties the department back to the [[Atomic Energy Commission]], which he says has long been used to classify crash material and which he reads the 2024 UAP [[Schumer Amendment (NDAA)|NDAA language]] as naming for the same purpose. That link, with Weygandt's demeanour, is why Gerb believes him.

On Herrera he is "torn". Herrera testified under oath, but if his account is true the craft he saw was an [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicle]] flown by shadow US military factions, the theory [[Steven Greer]] promotes. Gerb doubts that anyone has successfully reverse-engineered non-human technology, citing the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], [[Ross Coulthart]], [[David Grusch]] and [[Bob Lazar]], and allows one exception: the black triangles associated with the [[TR-3B]]. Both men first reached the public through Greer and his [[Disclosure Project]], and both stayed silent for years before speaking again in 2023.

## Jonathan Weygandt: Peru, 1997

In February 1997, USMC Lance Corporal [[Jonathan Weygandt]] was transferred to [[Pucallpa|Pucallpa Airbase]] in Peru to serve in [[Operation Laser Strike]], a [[US SOUTHCOM|US Southern Command]] counter-narcotics mission involving up to 20 agencies, among them the [[United States Marine Corps|Marine Corps]], the [[CIA]] and the [[US Air Force|Air Force]]. He served as a Stinger Avenger gunner on perimeter security with a [[Marine Air Control Group 28|MACG-28]] team under Master Sergeant Chris Diggins and Major Bob Cole. Gerb stresses that the base could detect anything unusual in several ways at once: TPS-43 radar, relocatable over-the-horizon radar and CIA Cessna Citation spotter aircraft. Weygandt says he overheard Air Force personnel there discussing the tracking of UFOs with strange flight characteristics. (Gerb follows the usual assumption that the base was Pucallpa; in his later [[Video - 1997 Peru UFO Crash Retrieval - the Story of Jonathan Weygandt|full study of the case]] he argues for Iquitos.)

On a late-night guard detail in March or April 1997, Weygandt, [[Sergeant Montil|Staff Sergeant Montil]], [[Sergeant Allen]] and [[Sergeant Atkins]] were ordered to secure the crash site of a "potentially friendly" downed aircraft. Records shown in the video note a shootdown on 25 March by an A-37 that sent its target down in a large fireball. By early morning the platoon had driven near the area and crossed the rest of the bush on foot; the site was easy to find, Weygandt says, because of "a huge gash in the land". Most of the Marines stayed at that site, which Gerb notes is all they ever saw. Weygandt, Atkins and Allen followed the object.

What Weygandt found frightened him:

- **Shape:** an egg, with its narrow end jutting out of a cliff face.
- **Surface:** metallic, but moving like a liquid and constantly changing, "gasoline dripping into water" (the [[Mother of Pearl Effect|mother-of-pearl effect]] Gerb returns to at the end).
- **Vents:** large openings like fish gills.
- **Sound:** a dull hum that filled the Marines with fear and unease.
- **Damage:** a large gash in its side, which Weygandt guessed was caused by a [[MIM-23 Hawk Missile|Hawk missile]].

Atkins and Allen ordered him away from the craft. As the three left, a group of men approached from two landed US Army CH-47 helicopters. What followed, as this video tells it, is a chain of hand-offs that kept Weygandt in custody for more than three weeks.

### Who wore the DOE jackets

"Forget for a second what Jonathan saw," Gerb says, and think about who he saw. Weygandt recalls "the big D ... Delta Oscar Echo" on the backs of the rain jackets. Gerb's identification is the Department of Energy's shadow [[DOE Special Response Teams (SRTs)|Special Response Team]], known as the "nuclear commandos", a highly trained special operations group with arrest authority when protecting DOE national laboratories and assets, and staffed in 1997, he says, by contractors such as [[EG&G]].

He then goes a level deeper. In his account the SRT is part of [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|NEST]], the Nuclear Emergency Search Team, which combines special forces, scientists, technicians and engineers with the US Army's 52nd EOD for a variety of emergency responses. NEST's technical information is protected like that of [[Special Access Programs (SAPs)|special access programs]]; its main intelligence comes from the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|National Reconnaissance Office]] and the CIA, with support from DOE contractors including EG&G, [[Raytheon]] and [[Lockheed Martin]]. Finally, the Department of Energy and the [[Nuclear Regulatory Commission]] were formerly the [[Atomic Energy Commission]], which Gerb says has been named again and again in the classification of crashed UFO material. He reads the 2024 UAP NDAA language sponsored by Chuck Schumer (the [[Schumer Amendment (NDAA)|Schumer Amendment]]) as saying the AEC's authority is used to misclassify UAP records as "transclassified foreign nuclear" material, exempt from disclosure. A DOE team at a crash site, in short, is exactly what Gerb would expect, and it is the main reason he finds Weygandt credible.

## Michael Herrera: Indonesia, 2009

The second Marine also came forward through Greer's work. In summer 2023, just after [[David Grusch]] went public, [[Michael Herrera]] made his first public appearance on Greer's panel at the [[National Press Club]]. Gerb calls his story one of the strangest first-hand encounters he has ever heard, and "extremely disturbing if true".

In 2009, aged 20, Rifleman Herrera was sent to [[Indonesia]] on a humanitarian mission after the [[2009 Sumatra Earthquake|Sumatra earthquake and tsunami]]; Gerb dates the mission to October 2009. Herrera and five other Marines were hiking a 900-foot ridge to take position for a supply drop when they spotted something in the jungle, moving, rotating and pulsing between light matte grey and an "absurdly dark" matte black. In his words, the huge object "like an octagonal pyramid" was emitting a low hum. He described a scaly exterior that looked man-made but like no known craft. Unmarked US forces were loading it with supplies. Like the men Weygandt saw, they wore black fatigues without insignia.

Because the team carried no radios on the short hike, they could not report what they were seeing, and moved in for a closer look. Near the craft they were ambushed and held at gunpoint by the paramilitary unit, whose members spoke with American accents. Their cameras and gear were confiscated and they were told not to disclose the sighting. After their release, Herrera says, a USAF lieutenant colonel told him: "You're not allowed to talk about what happened. You will go to prison or you will die."

Herrera also says unmarked trucks were preparing to load weapons onto the craft, and that one truck hauled a small storage crate with what looked like temperature, humidity and air-control systems on its exterior. From that brief look he believes it may have carried biologics, possibly even humans or extraterrestrial beings.

New UFO whistleblower protections emboldened Herrera to break his 14-year silence. In April 2023 he testified under oath about the encounter, behind closed doors (to Congress, as Gerb puts it here; the channel's later Herrera videos describe that April 2023 testimony as given to AARO), while fearing reprisals. Gerb notes that Herrera is a native of, and lives in, [[Denver, Colorado|Denver]], and says he hopes to contact him; the channel later covered him at length, in [[Video - Michael Herrera - UFO Whistleblower (ft. Joeyisnotmyname)|a video with Joeyisnotmyname]] and [[Video - Michael Herrera - Insights into UAP Encounter and Black Program Insiders|an interview with Herrera himself]].

## Analysis: an ARV in the jungle?

Gerb's closing thoughts split along the two cases. He believes Weygandt: the story, set against his body language, is credible, above all the DOE jackets and the "nuclear commandos", which Gerb reads as NEST, or some DOE-affiliated special access program, recovering a crash. The craft itself fascinates him, especially the gills, the "water over oil" mother-of-pearl surface and the low hum that frightened the Marines, a psychological effect of fear or calm that he says recurs across many cases.

Herrera's account raises a harder question. If it is true, the men in the jungle were flying an [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicle]] of their own, which would mean reverse-engineering programs like those described in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] have put craft of non-human origin into human hands. Gerb notes the long-rumoured claim that [[Lockheed Martin]]'s special project "Astra" reverse-engineered a triangular craft, the [[TR-3B]], but Herrera's craft was massive and octagonal.

## Key Claims

- In February 1997 Lance Corporal Jonathan Weygandt was sent to Pucallpa Airbase, Peru, for Operation Laser Strike, where he says Air Force personnel discussed tracking UFOs with strange flight characteristics.
- In March or April 1997 Weygandt says he found an egg-shaped craft embedded in a cliff face, with a liquid-looking shifting surface, gill-like vents, a fear-inducing hum and a large gash, possibly from a Hawk missile; records shown in the video note an A-37 shootdown on 25 March 1997.
- Weygandt says men in black fatigues, hazmat suits and "DOE" rain jackets arrested him, interrogated him for 15 hours aboard a CH-47, had a lieutenant colonel make him sign papers, and held him with Air Force personnel for three weeks.
- Gerb identifies the men as a DOE Special Response Team ("nuclear commandos") within NEST, and argues that the Department of Energy's descent from the Atomic Energy Commission, which he says the 2024 UAP NDAA language names as a route for misclassifying UAP records, makes Weygandt's account credible.
- In October 2009, on a relief mission after the Sumatra earthquake, Rifleman Michael Herrera says he and five Marines saw unmarked US forces loading a huge octagonal, humming craft, were held at gunpoint, and were later told by a USAF lieutenant colonel that talking would mean prison or death.
- Herrera says a crate with environmental controls was being loaded, which he believes may have held biologics, humans or extraterrestrials.
- Herrera broke a 14-year silence, testifying under oath in April 2023 and appearing publicly on Steven Greer's National Press Club panel that summer.
- Gerb believes Weygandt but is torn on Herrera: an ARV flown by shadow US military factions would mean reverse engineering has succeeded, which he doubts, citing the Wilson-Davis Memo, Ross Coulthart, David Grusch and Bob Lazar.
