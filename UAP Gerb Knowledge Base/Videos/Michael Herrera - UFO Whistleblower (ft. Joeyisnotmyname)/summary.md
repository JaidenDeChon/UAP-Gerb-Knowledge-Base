## Overview

This video revisits the case of [[Michael Herrera]], the former Marine who says that in October 2009, during earthquake relief in [[Indonesia]], his team came upon a 300-foot octagonal craft guarded by armed men with American accents and no insignia. Gerb had covered Herrera earlier in the year in *[[Videos/The Marines Who Got too Close to UFOs/summary|The Marines Who Got too Close to UFOs]]* and admits he was dismissive of him there, while finding [[Jonathan Weygandt]] credible. What changed his mind was the Reddit researcher [[Joey Is Not My Name]], who had been investigating Herrera for months, spoke to him weekly and, Gerb says, has since shown him evidence privately. Joey joins Gerb on the show for the first time.

Gerb is careful about what the evidence covers. He retells Herrera's encounter "as told by Michael", because nothing proves the sighting itself. The military setting around it can be checked, though, and Joey's first line of work was to check it: the ship, the unit, the helicopters and the landing zones all matched the public record. Joey's second claim is stronger. He says objective data, including flight records, proves that Herrera was flown to a secure facility to meet an anonymous insider who had reached him through [[Steven Greer]]. Gerb, who has seen a censored version of that evidence, says he is highly confident the meeting took place. Neither man vouches for what the insider told Herrera.

The rest of the video weighs the insider's allegations about a secret "black group", the doubts they raise (including a book the insider is writing, *[[Sentinels of Ether]]*), the Marines who say Herrera is lying, and the [[AARO Historical Report Volume 1]], which Gerb says misreported Herrera's testimony in two ways. It ends by asking viewers to judge Herrera separately from Greer.

## The 2009 Encounter

Gerb retells the story as Herrera tells it. In 2009 Herrera was a 20-year-old SAW gunner with the Second Battalion, Fifth Marines, stationed on the [[USS Denver]], which Gerb says was the only ship the US 7th Fleet routed to the west coast of Indonesia after the [[2009 Sumatra Earthquake]]. The unit was told it would provide security for deliveries of food and medical supplies. Humanitarian missions do not usually carry weapons, but Gerb notes the first Marines in reportedly did, M16A4s, because of the heavy presence of terrorist training in the area. :wiki-cue{t=251 video="6DyTfIV87Ck" video-title="Michael Herrera: UFO Whistleblower (ft. Joeyisnotmyname)"}

The team flew by CH-53 to a hasty landing zone near Padang and climbed a ridge of about 300 m for reconnaissance. Herrera says they had no radio, which he himself calls highly abnormal and which critics have made one of the biggest points of contention. From the top, something "stuck out like a sore thumb" against the jungle: a craft rotating just above the tree line, shifting between light matte grey and dark matte black. The Marines went down the slope to investigate, and Herrera photographed the craft with a Panasonic camera he kept in his spent-magazine pouch. From about 150 m away he estimated it at 300 ft across. It was octagonal, rotated clockwise, had Vantablack-like exterior panels and a pyramid-shaped top, hummed like a guitar amp and floated 15 to 20 ft above the ground. :wiki-cue{t=320 video="6DyTfIV87Ck" video-title="Michael Herrera: UFO Whistleblower (ft. Joeyisnotmyname)"}

Around it stood what Herrera calls a "rogue military force": men with American dialects and gear like the Marines' own, only better, with no rank or insignia. Eight of them aimed their weapons at the Marines, clicked off their safeties and threatened to kill them. They searched the Marines, took their weapons, and went straight to the military IDs in their left breast pockets, which they photographed. Herrera glanced back at the craft as four F-350s carrying weapons cases brought containers fitted with an oxygen or humidity-control unit onto a platform beneath it. He later came to learn the containers held people. The platform rose, joined the craft, and the two shot off westward at once, with no rotor wash, exhaust or sonic boom, and without disturbing the vegetation. :wiki-cue{t=403 video="6DyTfIV87Ck" video-title="Michael Herrera: UFO Whistleblower (ft. Joeyisnotmyname)"}

Herrera thought they were about to be killed. Instead the operators reloaded the Marines' equipment onto them and walked them back up the slope, and what followed, as Herrera tells it, was a steady effort to keep him quiet:

## Checking the Mission

Joey says he was drawn in by the reaction to Herrera's story. People dismissed him for what Joey considered flimsy reasons, because his team had no radios or because he misnamed a rifle, while taking seriously the idea of a 300-foot UFO. Joey saw an opening: a humanitarian operation leaves a trail of photojournalism, press coverage, military reports and assessments. If Herrera had lied about any detail of the mission, his whole account could be thrown out. Joey looked for conflicts and found the opposite. :wiki-cue{t=627 video="6DyTfIV87Ck" video-title="Michael Herrera: UFO Whistleblower (ft. Joeyisnotmyname)"}

## The Insider Meeting

Herrera then began telling Joey about an insider, which is where Gerb says followers of the case, himself included, rightly grew more skeptical. The insider first noticed Herrera at his first public appearance, on Steven Greer's [[National Press Club]] panel. According to Herrera, the man later told Greer he knew exactly what the containers were for. Herrera had guessed that their cylinders were for oxygen or vacuum sealing, and had suspected drug smuggling. The insider said the containers carried people. Joey found it "pretty outrageous" that a man with such an unusual sighting would now be sought out by an insider too. Herrera, for his part, was scared, since the last people connected with this had held guns on him. He decided he had to take the chance if the story was to go any further.

Joey says he swung back and forth throughout. At times he let himself believe it all and wanted to tell Herrera not to go, or he would be killed. At other times it looked like a setup, with Herrera passing on "sensitive" information he actually wanted leaked. What confused him most was being trusted with the meeting's time, date and place for no clear reason, until he realized Herrera was using him as a [[Dead Man Switch|dead man switch]]: if anything happened to him, Joey was to release everything. The day before the meeting, Joey called Herrera and recorded the call without telling him, so that he could prove the contact if Herrera went missing. The video plays the recording. Gerb says what struck him most was the fear in Herrera's voice. Joey later told Herrera about the recording. :wiki-cue{t=1232 video="6DyTfIV87Ck" video-title="Michael Herrera: UFO Whistleblower (ft. Joeyisnotmyname)"}

Using the details Herrera gave, Joey says he verified the trip by tracking flight records, along with other evidence he cannot share. His point is that none of it depends on trusting Herrera: it is objective data. When Herrera came back, Joey did not tell him he had tracked the trip, and used the chance to test Herrera's account with specific questions about timing. Gerb acknowledges criticism of Joey for keeping the evidence private, but says that if the version Joey showed him is genuine, he can say "with a high degree of certainty" that the meeting took place. Both men draw a line there: the evidence says nothing about whether what the insider showed or told Herrera is true. They add that a larger investigation is going on behind the scenes, which publishing the evidence could compromise. :wiki-cue{t=1403 video="6DyTfIV87Ck" video-title="Michael Herrera: UFO Whistleblower (ft. Joeyisnotmyname)"}

## What the Insider Told Herrera

Herrera will not say what he was shown at the facility, citing an NDA. Oddly, the insider allowed him to share what he was told. Gerb says he and Joey present these points reluctantly, since neither can back them up. The insider describes himself as an advocate for disclosure who has been in the program for decades. As relayed in a Reddit post by Joey, which Gerb shows on screen, the insider alleges:

- **Two groups study UAP within the US government.** The "blue group" is programs with proper congressional oversight, such as [[AARO]], [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]] and [[Project Blue Book]]. The "black group" is an organization founded in the 1940s to reverse-engineer non-human technology. Gerb says its lack of oversight and accountability, and its endless reach, mirror almost exactly what the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] describes.
- **Its craft are "boring tech".** The black group has reverse-engineered non-human craft to an unknown degree and uses them in operations, but the insider says other technologies are far more exciting and "utilitarian for humanity".
- **Weapons and lures.** [[EMP-like Weaponry|EMP-like weapons]] can target, disrupt and disable UAP. [[Crash Retrieval|Crash retrieval]] programs lure UAP to places where they are disabled, and recover the craft and bodies.
- **"P3" recruits.** The program recruits people with "psionic abilities", called [[Psionic Abilities (P3)|P3]], often from developing countries, to interface with and control non-human technology. They are bargained into joining in exchange for their families' safety, probably because they are isolated permanently or for long periods. The insider says this is the operation Herrera stumbled on in 2009. :wiki-cue{t=1680 video="6DyTfIV87Ck" video-title="Michael Herrera: UFO Whistleblower (ft. Joeyisnotmyname)"}

Joey adds that other people have confirmed the insider's existence to him personally. Knowing what the facility officially is, he says, it amazes him that anyone could bring in a random civilian without the security checks and surveillance he would expect, which suggests the insider has considerable authority. Still, he knows the insider exists and that Herrera was taken there, but not whether anything the insider says is true. :wiki-cue{t=1774 video="6DyTfIV87Ck" video-title="Michael Herrera: UFO Whistleblower (ft. Joeyisnotmyname)"}

### Dissenter or psyop?

Why would the insider tell Herrera all this? Gerb sets out two readings and finds both "somewhat logical and plausible". A book the insider says he is writing adds to the doubt. A few weeks after the meeting, he told Herrera it was being written with a ghostwriter and mixed truth and fiction, like [[Tom DeLonge]]'s *Sekret Machines*. Herrera had hoped to be given evidence at the meeting, and this book was presented as corroboration. But *[[Sentinels of Ether]]* concerns a 2004 event in Mexico, in which a black-program paramilitary group allegedly wiped out a JSOC team while moving people in shipping containers to a hidden mountain base. The two chapters the insider provided were under review by the [[DOPSR (Defense Office of Prepublication and Security Review)|Defense Office of Prepublication and Security Review]], the office Gerb notes [[David Grusch]] was then going through. The insider called this playing [[4D Chess on DOPSR|"4D chess" on DOPSR]]: deliberately including deep-black material to see whether the reviewers catch it, so that "any response will provide us with information".

## Key Claims

- Herrera claims that in October 2009, while providing security for Sumatra earthquake relief from the USS Denver, his team found a craft about 300 ft across, octagonal and rotating, and was held at gunpoint by eight operators with American dialects and gear but no rank or insignia.
- According to Herrera, containers that he later learned held people were loaded onto a platform beneath the craft, which then left westward with no rotor wash, exhaust or sonic boom.
- Herrera says his camera's SD card and battery were confiscated, and that a man in Air Force blues on Okinawa warned him of prison or death before he signed an NDA.
- Joey says the public record matches Herrera's account of the ship, the unit, the helicopters and the hasty landing zones, and stresses that this does not prove the sighting.
- Joey says objective data, including flight records, proves that Herrera was flown to a secure facility in 2023 to meet an insider who contacted him through Steven Greer. Gerb, who has seen a censored version, says he is highly confident the meeting happened.
- The insider alleges a "black group" founded in the 1940s that reverse-engineers non-human craft, uses EMP-like weapons to bring down UAP for retrieval, and recruits people with psionic abilities ("P3") from developing countries. Gerb and Joey present these claims as unverified.
- Gerb considers that the insider may be running a disinformation operation like the one against Paul Bennewitz, and counts the *Sentinels of Ether* manuscript and its "4D chess" with DOPSR among his reasons for suspicion.
- Most of the platoon members Joey has spoken to say Herrera is lying, but Joey says those he pressed were not on the ground with him. Team leader Nathan's photograph of Marines flying in without rifles is disputed by Herrera.
- Gerb says the AARO Historical Report Volume 1 misstated Herrera's testimony twice, since he never described US Special Forces or an extraterrestrial craft, and he leans towards calling it deliberate.
