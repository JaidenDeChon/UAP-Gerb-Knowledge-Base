## Overview

This video presents UAP Gerb's investigation into the [[MITRE Corporation]], a nonprofit [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)|FFRDC]] operator founded in 1958 and spun off from [[MIT Lincoln Laboratory]], which the presenter calls the "apex" science and technology institution within the alleged UFO legacy program architecture. Its central thesis rests on roughly two years of research and testimony from several MITRE engineers and program managers, with a knowledge cutoff around 2019. According to the video, MITRE has served since its founding as R&D lead and program manager for two distinct, Navy-sponsored waived unacknowledged special access programs. The first is a decades-old reverse-engineering effort tracing to material recovered at the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]]. The second monitors an enormous unidentified submerged object in the Atlantic nicknamed the "[[Tic Tac Factory]]." The video opens with a primer on the FFRDC model itself: government-owned, contractor-operated institutions trusted with more sensitive material than ordinary defense contractors, yet outside standard federal oversight and FOIA obligations.

The presenter traces MITRE's founding to Project SAGE, the United States' first computer-based national air defense system, but argues that its creation about a decade after Roswell was no coincidence. On this account, MITRE's founding scientists, many from Naval special projects (including first technical director [[Robert R. Everett]]), were tasked from 1958 with reverse-engineering exploitable technology from Roswell wreckage held at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. The program is alleged to be administered by the US Navy, through the [[Office of Naval Intelligence]] and [[Naval Air Systems Command]], rather than by the Air Force that formally chartered MITRE, with [[Naval Air Station Patuxent River]] as its operational nexus. The Tic Tac Factory is described as an object stationed near [[Bahamas|the Bahamas]] that deploys and recovers smaller transmedium craft. The presenter finds partial corroboration in an anonymous 2023 4chan testimony and in Commander [[David Fravor]]'s 2004 USS Nimitz encounter.

A major secondary thread concerns MITRE's internal security architecture, which a source described to the presenter as the literal origin of the "onion" model of nested secrecy used across the [[Legacy Program Onion Model|legacy program]] apparatus. The video details an alleged "hidden" MITRE Special Security Office staffed with nonofficial-cover personnel, a 2011 clampdown on historical program briefings that coincided with the arrival of CIA partners, and an alleged 2017 loss of MITRE's control over the reverse-engineering program linked to [[James Clapper]]'s tenure as Director of National Intelligence. It closes by listing former senior intelligence and acquisition officials who later held senior MITRE posts, among them [[Donald M. Kerr]], [[Susan Gordon]], [[Dawn Meyerriecks]], [[Paul Kaminski]], [[John Deutsch|John M. Deutsch]], [[Linton Wells II]] and [[William LaPlante]]. It then ties the investigation to the legislative interrogative that Representative [[Eric Burlison]] sent MITRE in May 2026, which the video credits to Burlison and whistleblower [[David Grusch]].

## FFRDCs as the "Missing Link" in Legacy Programs

The presenter opens by recapping his "legacy program pyramid." At the top sits a now-nebulous control group: a committee of 27 formed in 1994, last ruled "with an iron fist" by [[Dick Cheney]] and then by James Clapper. Below it are program administrators, such as the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]], the Office of Naval Intelligence, the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]] and the Air Force Test Center. At the bottom are prime contractors, whose personnel mostly work in a cover capacity. The Department of Energy's statutory special access authority runs through every layer. FFRDCs are the layer he says is usually missing from the discussion. See [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)]] for full detail.

FFRDCs are independent nonprofits that take about 99% of their funding from the federal government while being protected like private corporations. They cannot compete for contracts, and they receive access to government data, staff and facilities that goes far beyond anything an ordinary defense contractor gets. Of the more than 70 established since the Second World War, 41 or 42 exist today under 12 federal sponsors, and together they received over $31 billion in R&D contracts in fiscal year 2024. They come in three kinds:

- research and development laboratories, such as Los Alamos, Oak Ridge and Sandia
- study and analysis centers, such as the [[RAND Corporation]] and the Center for Naval Analyses
- systems engineering and integration centers, such as the [[Aerospace Corporation]] and MITRE's National Security Engineering Center (NSEC)

Quoting MITRE's own primer, the presenter argues the FFRDC is the filter through which information passes from government to private industry, flowing down the pyramid but almost never up.

> [!example] The host's worked example
> Suppose the Office of Naval Intelligence and the Office of Naval Research use the Navy's Supervisor of Salvage and Diving to raise a lenticular disc from the seafloor, judge its propulsion, cloaking or sensors exploitable, and create a waived unacknowledged SAP, but lack the in-house infrastructure to exploit it. Handing the craft to Lockheed or Northrop would be insecure and would cost the government custody. Instead, the presenter says, custody goes to DOE national-laboratory FFRDCs protected by [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954|1954 Atomic Energy Act]] authority. MITRE's NSEC, RAND or the Aerospace Corporation leads derivation, and contractors come in through carve-out contracts to study specific metallurgy, sensors and systems, most of them unaware of what they are working on.

Because FFRDCs are only semi-governmental, they are not subject to FOIA, and their funding footprints are easy to hide. The presenter cites congressional concern about FFRDC "mission creep," meaning sensitive work that drifts beyond an FFRDC's defined scope, as a pattern visible at MITRE from the very start. He credits [[Vannevar Bush]] as the grandfather of the whole model. He also alleges that classified presidential executive orders from 1948 made Atomic Energy Commission laboratories the custodians of recovered non-human vehicles, so that FFRDCs have "held the keys" since Roswell.

## MITRE's Founding and Modern Structure

MITRE's lineage runs through Bush: his MIT Radiation Laboratory (1940–1945) reopened in 1951 as MIT Lincoln Laboratory, where the [[Semi-Automatic Ground Environment (SAGE)|SAGE]] air defense system began as Project Lincoln. SAGE was conceived in 1951, deployed in 1963 and served as the backbone of [[NORAD]] into the 1980s, at a cost the video says exceeded the Manhattan Project's. The system was too complex for Lincoln Lab alone, so in 1958 the Air Force and Lincoln Lab spun off a new group, MITRE, modelled on RAND: no capital stock, no profit motive, no hardware to sell. The name is simply a play on "MIT." Sixth Secretary of Defense [[Neil McElroy]] asked its founders to build in "flexibility" for future Department of Defense requirements, which the presenter reads as room for its hidden mandate. The host believes SAGE and MITRE detected many anomalous signatures over US airspace. He points to 1970s Strategic Arms Limitation Talks understandings under which the US and Soviet Union would alert each other to UFOs over the Moscow-Washington hotline.

From the start MITRE sat in the middle of a bitter roles-and-missions fight, with the Army, Navy and Air Force each afraid of losing control of their own air defense systems. Because it was a trusted, neutral party, MITRE could still take on separate compartmented work for each service. The presenter calls this inherited distrust the root of the siloing that surrounds MITRE's legacy work today.

## MITRE's Security Architecture: the Hidden SSO

For top-secret compartmented work MITRE keeps an ordinary, acknowledged Special Security Office (SSO) on its McLean campus. According to the video, legacy work does not go through it. It runs instead through a "hidden SSO" in MITRE satellite offices near Crystal City, a set-up the presenter compares to the Air Force's [[Hidden Wing]]. Numbered "S" sites, which he says number in the hundreds and likely just under 500, carry the work, about 90% of them under managed need-to-know cover stories such as foreign technology acquisition. Several umbrella sites sit inside major Navy test infrastructure such as Pax River.

The hidden SSO's personnel and program security officers are alleged to operate as nonofficial cover officers inside the company, their true roles known only to MITRE's CEO and the SSO director. The presenter says he has seen badges for MITRE legacy engineers that listed their cover positions. The office is exempt from audits: faced with one, its members were told to say "we are lollipop manufacturers." Funding was skimmed from a range of acknowledged MITRE and Navy projects. The office handles physical, industrial and counterintelligence security, reads in newcomers and briefs MITRE, Navy and government seniors. When investigators, inspectors general or Congress come asking, it leads them in circles. A source called it "the model for all legacy security personnel" and the origin of the [[Legacy Program Onion Model|onion model]]. MITRE even hides unclassified material behind a "handle via special access channels only" caveat.

Compartmentation reaches inside the programs themselves, recalling [[John P. Craven]]'s "seventh veil" analogy. One engineer might puzzle over an anomalous maritime signature that keeps spoofing radar data, never told that it is a USO, while the colleague beside him is fully read into its intelligence. Likewise a MITRE security officer may not know the program began with a disc recovered in the 1940s, while his superior and his Navy counterpart do.

## Two Waived Unacknowledged Programs

The presenter says he knows of two waived unacknowledged special access programs, the most restricted category of SAP, in which MITRE is program manager and R&D expert for its Navy sponsor, with lesser Army involvement. The US Navy is the mission partner in both. MITRE's role, he says, covers UFO technology (not craft, power or propulsion) and USO monitoring. ## Program One: Roswell-Derived Reverse Engineering

The story, as the presenter tells it: one or several discs with non-human biologics aboard crashed in July 1947, setting off the "Manhattan Project 2.0." The wreckage went to Wright-Patterson, headquarters of Air Materiel Command and, from 1951, of the [[Air Research and Development Command (ARDC)|Air Research and Development Command]], which took over Air Force R&D. Both commands are ancestors of today's [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)|Air Force Materiel Command]]. Around the crash's tenth anniversary, assessments found certain technologies on the craft exploitable, and MITRE was established about 11 years after Roswell. The presenter reckons ARDC, which he calls "practically inseparable" from the young MITRE at [[Hanscom Air Force Base, Massachusetts|Hanscom Air Force Base]], guided that founding. MITRE's "true operational directive hidden under the SAGE national effort," he alleges, was to test and mature war-altering systems from Roswell.

He draws a parallel with Lieutenant Colonel [[Philip J. Corso]]. Corso's own manuscript, *Dawn of a New Age* (which the host distinguishes from the ghost-written *The Day After Roswell*), says the Army's foreign technology division under General [[Arthur Trudeau]] seeded Roswell concepts into industry. Trudeau took over Army research and development in spring 1958, months before MITRE was created. In the host's reading the Army took an aggressive route, while the Air Force and Navy took a more conservative one through the new MITRE. He links this to David Grusch's description of a reverse-engineering program that "inspired other innovations" without copying the technology outright. According to the host, no Air Force personnel he knows of were read into this program; it was specifically Navy.

## Program Two: The Tic Tac Factory

The second program is a decades-old MITRE-Navy effort to monitor, track and task for intercept an enormous USO in the Atlantic near the Bahamas. Inside the program it goes by terms such as "anomalous signatures," "unconventional technologies" and "sensor anomalies." It is described as about the size of ten city blocks and appears on sensors as an opaque blob. Engineers are unsure whether that is its real form or the product of signature management or cloaking. It deploys and collects Tic Tac-shaped craft, along with cylinders, discs and "oddly blobby" shapes, that leave the water and fly before sometimes returning. The presenter believes, without direct knowledge, that some are intercepted and recovered. It keeps a permanent or semi-permanent presence but travels, to what extent he could not learn. Engineers on the project described it as more or less harmless.

The Bahamas banks are shallow, but just off the shelf the Atlantic drops to 4,000 metres or more. The nearby Tongue of the Ocean trench, about 2,000 metres deep, hosts the Navy's [[Atlantic Undersea Test and Evaluation Center]] (AUTEC), which the host reckons has first-hand involvement. He places the object just east of the Bahamas in the Blake Bahama Basin. He suggests Representative [[Tim Burchett]]'s talk of deep underwater USO bases was right on the money, and plays a clip describing 200-mile-an-hour, football-field-sized sonar contacts. He also plays Representative Burlison recalling a whistleblower from a "nautical" source, met with [[Anna Paulina Luna]], who described a structure in the deep ocean that reminded Burlison of *The Abyss*. He cites David Fravor's 2004 [[2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter (Tic Tac)|Nimitz encounter]], in which a Tic Tac moved above churning white water, and Senior Chief Wiggins' account of a Tic Tac-shaped object rising from the ocean to join three others, though both happened off San Diego. Perhaps, he suggests, there is more than one such station. See [[Tic Tac Factory]] and [[Unidentified Submerged Object (USO)]].

## The 2011 Clampdown and the 2017 Loss of Control

The presenter has long spoken of an early-2010s effort to restrict historical briefings inside one organization, and here names it as MITRE. In 2011, top-down orders to the hidden SSO restricted newcomers to what they needed for their tasks. They heard nothing of the program's history, origins, material chain of custody or breakthroughs, so that incumbents from then on might not know their programs involved USOs or recovered UFO technology. He calls it catastrophic for institutional knowledge, which is already threatened by ageing personnel and by the absence of any central records repository. He adds that [[Ross Coulthart]] knows of similar restrictions at other institutions in the same years. MITRE, he alleges, has periodically destroyed legacy records by asking the Fairfax County, Virginia waste management depot to clear out for a few hours while its personnel shredded or burned documents. 2011 and 2017 were major years for this.

His explanation is a turf war. From 1958 to 2011 the reverse-engineering project was strictly DoD, Navy and MITRE. In 2011 intelligence-community partners, specifically the CIA, were introduced, and the host's educated guess is that DoD and MITRE leadership did not trust them. Several sources told him that by 2017 MITRE had lost control of the program, or its leadership had shifted entirely. He ties the 2011–2017 window to James Clapper, who he says took over as the fractured programs' "mob boss" after Cheney left office in 2009. As DNI from 2010, Clapper allegedly used that office to fold strictly DoD programs into the intelligence community and his National Security Council crash-retrieval portfolio, possibly including the 2017 [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)]] USAP. Stephanie O'Sullivan's term as Clapper's principal deputy, 2011 to 2017, matches the window exactly. The presenter sees this factional tug-of-war as a symptom of programs "long past the legacy golden age" and at risk of losing a *sub rosa* cold war to peer adversaries.
