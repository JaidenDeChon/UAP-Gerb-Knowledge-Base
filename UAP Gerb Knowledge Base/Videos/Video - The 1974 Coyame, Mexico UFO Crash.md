## Summary
## Summary

The video examines the 1974 Coyame, Chihuahua, Mexico UFO crash, in which US Air Defense radar allegedly tracked an unknown object traveling at 2,200 knots and 75,000 feet altitude on August 25, 1974, before it disappeared over northern Mexico after apparently colliding mid-air with a civilian aircraft departing El Paso for Mexico City. According to the primary source document — the "Denb Report," an anonymously distributed document that surfaced on early internet message boards in 1992 — a CIA-led recovery team staged at Fort Bliss covertly entered Mexican airspace, found the entire Mexican military recovery convoy dead, and retrieved a 16-foot 5-inch silver disc weighing approximately 1,500 lbs, subsequently transporting it to Atlanta, Georgia, with an unconfirmed transfer to Wright Patterson Air Force Base. The video evaluates supporting corroboration from researchers including Leonard Stringfield, who reported hearing of the case in the late 1970s–80s, and Ryan S. Wood, whose book *Magic Eyes Only* rates the case as medium to high authenticity, while also noting unresolved questions about the origins of the Denb Report, the identity of the mysterious "DENB team," and the unexplained deaths of the Mexican recovery personnel. The Coyame incident is contextualized within broader UAP crash retrieval discourse, including its listing by the UAP Caucus among cases to bring before Congress.
