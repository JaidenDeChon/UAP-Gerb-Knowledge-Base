## Overview

This video opens Gerb's "UFOs in the Private Sector" series, which looks at the defense contractors that figures such as [[David Grusch]], [[Luis Elizondo|Lue Elizondo]], [[Chris Mellon]], [[Eric Davis]] and former Senator [[Harry Reid]] have said have a direct hand in UFO crash retrieval and reverse engineering. Its subject is [[Battelle Memorial Institute]], a private nonprofit applied-sciences company in Columbus, Ohio, with a long record of government work: uranium fabrication research for the [[Manhattan Project]], the photocopier and the UPC barcode, Apollo 11 propellant and electronics work, and chemical and biological defense research. Today it manages or helps manage Department of Energy laboratories, among them [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]], the National Renewable Energy Laboratory and [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]]. Gerb's charge is that Battelle has a "dark secret": a long history of studying UFOs, of helping the US government and the Air Force cover them up, and of analysing UFO material itself.

The video's central evidence is a contradiction in Battelle's own record. In 1955 Battelle and the Air Force's intelligence center published Special Report 14, which concluded that no physical matter had ever been recovered from a UFO. Yet six years earlier, [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] had contracted Battelle to analyse [[Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium Alloy)|nitinol]], a nickel-titanium shape-memory alloy not officially discovered until 1961. Gerb infers that the alloy came from the 1947 [[Roswell Crash|Roswell crash]], whose debris [[Jesse Marcel]] described as a light metal that kept its shape after deformation and which was supposedly shipped to Wright-Patterson. A co-author of the 1949 reports, chemical engineer [[EJ Center]], allegedly told his daughter's boyfriend in 1958 that he had studied parts of a flying saucer. That account was published in 1994, sixteen years before the 1949 reports were declassified in 2010, and Gerb calls the connection "just obscene".

The last strand brings Battelle into the present. [[Ronald S. Moultrie]], the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security to whom [[AARO]] and [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] reported, oversaw AARO's $1.9 million support contract with [[Sand Corp]] and joined Kirkpatrick in a December 2022 press conference denying any crash recoveries. He has also, Gerb says, sat on Battelle's advisory board since 2016, a seat he tried to scrub from his record. Gerb's conclusion is that Battelle has played both sides: studying UFO material while helping to quell public interest in it.

## Battelle Memorial Institute

Gerb introduces Battelle by its own mission statement, to "deliver scientific discovery and applied research that makes the world a better place to live", and by its record of US government work, which includes classified special access programs. Beyond its Manhattan Project uranium research and its consumer inventions, he stresses Battelle's present role in the Department of Energy laboratory system: it manages the National Biodefense Analysis and Countermeasures Center and runs or co-runs Los Alamos, the National Renewable Energy Laboratory and Oak Ridge. Because the channel's earlier videos tie Los Alamos and the [[Department of Energy]] to UAP work, Gerb says the ties are visible before the UFO record is even opened.

## Project Stork and Special Report 14

The earliest link Gerb traces is [[Project Stork]]. In 1952 the Director of Central Intelligence received a memorandum titled "Flying Saucers", describing an arrangement between the Air Force's [[Air Force Technical Intelligence Center|Air Technical Intelligence Center]] (ATIC) and Battelle for the contractor to build a machine-indexing system for official UFO sighting reports. On 9 January 1953 Battelle's Dr. [[Howard C. Cross]] proposed that Stork "assist the Air Force in reassuring the public that everything is well under control with respect to the UFO problem." The memo was classified Secret and named Captain [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], who directed [[Project Sign|Sign]], [[Project Grudge|Grudge]] and [[Project Blue Book|Blue Book]].

That same year the [[Robertson Panel]] recommended that national security agencies strip UFOs of their "special status" and tell the public there was no legitimate evidence for them. Around the same time, Gerb says, Battelle had been commissioned under the Stork codename to analyse all Air Force UFO cases separately, away from Blue Book and the official panels. [[J. Allen Hynek]] mentioned the project during his attempted testimony to the panel. With the help of the Computer UFO Network (CUFON), Gerb has found all seven Stork status reports, which he leaves for another video.

On 5 May 1955 Battelle and ATIC published Special Report 14, an analysis of 3,200 UFO sightings. It concluded that UFOs are not craft beyond human scientific knowledge, because no physical matter had ever been recovered from any sighting. Gerb calls this "a massive contradiction" with what Battelle had been doing six years earlier.

## The Nitinol Contract (1949)

In 1949 Wright-Patterson Air Force Base contracted Battelle to research titanium alloys. The first and second progress reports, covering 1 September to 21 October 1949 under a contract whose number Gerb reads as 33-38-3736, concentrate on nitinol, a nickel-titanium alloy officially discovered only in 1961, by Dr. William J. Buehler. Gerb describes its properties. It is superelastic, snapping back to its original shape under high stress with no deformation. It has shape memory. It is elastocaloric, heating under mechanical stress and cooling as the stress is removed. It is also very hard to make: the composition must be tightly controlled, titanium is highly reactive, and even in the 21st century the alloy needs vacuum arc or vacuum induction remelting. Yet Battelle, he notes, had it in 1949.

He sets that against Roswell. Major Jesse Marcel, taken to the debris field by rancher [[Mac Brazel]], described an exceedingly light metal that kept its shape after deformation, and was later told by [[General Ramey|General Roger Ramey]] to pose with balsa wood and foil to support the weather-balloon story. The debris was supposedly shipped to Wright-Patterson. "To summarize," Gerb says, he is directly inferring that the Roswell crash led to the discovery of nitinol, and that this is why Wright-Patterson and the Air Force had Battelle analyse it in 1949.

The research, Gerb notes, was an R&D project under the [[Atomic Energy Commission]], and the channel's recurring argument is that UFO records and materials have been deliberately misclassified under the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]] as nuclear material. Battelle's report is labelled neither classified, unclassified nor declassified, but "restricted", which Gerb describes as a private-sector standard in which access is strictly need-to-know. David Grusch has said that private industry is used for UFO studies partly because its own legal classification standards cannot be breached even by people with special access program clearances like his. The nitinol studies were not declassified until 2010.

## EJ Center's Account

The second progress report contains an inconspicuous section, "Analytical Methods for Titanium Based Alloys", co-written by EJ Center, a senior research chemical engineer at Battelle from 1939 to 1957. The section deals with titanium purity, which nitinol requires because of titanium's reactivity.

Gerb then turns to the summer of 1994 and the *Ohio UFO Notebook* of Dr. [[Irena Scott]], who sat on the board of the [[MUFON|Mutual UFO Network]]. Its piece "The Ohio UFO Crash Connection and Other Stories" describes an informant who came to MUFON in 1992. He had attended North High School in Columbus in the late 1950s and in 1958 had dated Center's daughter. One night at her home, he said, Elroy John Center told them that while at Battelle he had been "responsible for a project which required him to study Parts retrieved from a flying saucer." The passage sits under the heading "Decoding the I-Beam", a reference to the I-beam Marcel found in the Roswell wreckage, alongside memory metal, with markings resembling hieroglyphs.

For Gerb the dates settle it. MUFON reported on Center and his flying-saucer work in 1994, but Center's 1949 nitinol work was not declassified until 2010. Unless Center really was working on crash material for Battelle, most likely the Roswell wreckage, and "blabbed about it" after he left, the connection would have been impossible to make. He closes the video by returning to it as a kernel of credibility in "this already baffling case".

## Vallée, Fox and the Joe Rogan Experience

As "the cherry on top", Gerb points to episode 1574 of the Joe Rogan Experience, recorded on 4 December 2020 with computer scientist [[Jacques Vallee|Jacques Vallée]] and [[James Fox]], director of *The Phenomenon* and *Moment of Contact*, a film about the [[Varginha, Brazil|Varginha]] case. Vallée, known for years of work connecting the private sector to official investigations, said one of the entities that helps control the study of UFOs within the intelligence agencies is "a private contractor", but nervously declined to say who holds UFO materials. Fox named the group indirectly by its involvement with the 1953 Robertson Panel, which Gerb takes to be Battelle. The discussion of metal recovered from UFOs begins about an hour and 42 minutes into the episode; Gerb does not play the clips, citing copyright.

## Battelle, AARO and Ronald Moultrie

The last connection runs to [[AARO]] and [[Sean Kirkpatrick]], whom Gerb calls "our modern-day disinformation agent". Kirkpatrick and AARO reported to [[Ronald S. Moultrie]], the [[Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USDINS)|Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security]], whom Gerb considers almost certainly one of the "hostile witnesses" on David Grusch's list. In 2022 AARO and the Department of Defense awarded [[Sand Corp]] $1.9 million for AARO support services; Sand Corp, Gerb says, specializes in preventing leaks and stopping whistleblowers, and the contract was overseen by Moultrie. On 16 December 2022 Moultrie and Kirkpatrick held a press conference saying they had seen no evidence of crashed UFOs from another planet or dimension, and that there had been no crash recoveries.

Moultrie, Gerb then reveals, has been a Battelle board member since 2016, and has tried to scrub the fact from his record, including removing it from his LinkedIn page. The Project On Government Oversight (POGO) had stated on its website that Moultrie was a Battelle advisory board member and kept screenshots, which Gerb shows on screen. "Even up to the modern day," he says, Battelle "has its hooks sunk in" the US government and is controlling the UAP narrative.

## What Gerb Left Out

Gerb ends by saying Battelle's hands are "insanely dirty" in UFOs, as are [[Lockheed Martin]]'s and the [[Carlyle Group]]'s, and promises videos on both. He also explains an omission. About eight months earlier, in 2023, an online leaker claimed to be a Battelle biologist working on "EBOs", extraterrestrial biological organisms, and set out a taxonomy of recovered beings. Gerb left the claims out because the leaker might be lying and he could not assemble a body of evidence or a well-researched thesis for them, though he may return to them.

## Key Claims

- Battelle Memorial Institute has been involved in official UFO investigation since at least 1952, through [[Project Stork]], an ATIC contract that ran parallel to [[Project Blue Book]] and away from the official panels.
- A Secret memo of 9 January 1953 by Battelle's [[Howard C. Cross]] proposed that Project Stork help the Air Force reassure the public that the UFO problem was "well under control".
- Battelle and ATIC's 1955 Special Report 14 concluded that no physical matter had ever been recovered from a UFO, which Gerb says contradicts Battelle's own 1949 work.
- In 1949 Battelle analysed [[Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium Alloy)|nitinol]] for Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, twelve years before the alloy's official discovery in 1961. Gerb infers that the material came from the 1947 Roswell crash, whose debris Jesse Marcel described as a light metal that kept its shape and which was supposedly shipped to Wright-Patterson.
- The 1949 research was an [[Atomic Energy Commission]] project labelled "restricted", a private-sector standard that Gerb, citing David Grusch, says even special access program holders cannot breach. It was not declassified until 2010.
- [[EJ Center]], a co-author of the 1949 reports, allegedly told his daughter's boyfriend in 1958 that he had studied "Parts retrieved from a flying saucer". The account reached MUFON in 1992 and was published by [[Irena Scott]] in 1994, sixteen years before the reports were declassified.
- [[Jacques Vallee|Jacques Vallée]] and [[James Fox]] pointed toward Battelle as the private contractor holding UFO materials on Joe Rogan Experience episode 1574 (4 December 2020).
- [[Ronald S. Moultrie]], who oversaw AARO, its $1.9 million [[Sand Corp]] contract and the December 2022 denial of crash recoveries, has sat on Battelle's advisory board since 2016 and tried to remove the association from his public record.
