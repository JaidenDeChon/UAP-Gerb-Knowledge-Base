## Overview

This short, self-described "off the cuff" video is Gerb's answer to an op-ed by [[Sean Kirkpatrick]], the first director of the Pentagon's [[AARO|All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office]] (AARO), published in *Scientific American* on 19 January 2024 under the title "Here's What I Learned as the U.S. Government's UFO Hunter". Gerb goes through the piece argument by argument. He concludes that Kirkpatrick is worse than "willfully ignorant": he is "a modern-day disinformation agent" who actively pushes disinformation about unidentified anomalous phenomena. The video opens on a clip of an intelligence official telling an audience that, as an intelligence officer, he would expect them to expect him to lie to them.

Gerb answers from the public record and from the people Kirkpatrick dismissed. The op-ed calls the whole reverse-engineering story a 2008 conspiracy spun around [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]]. Gerb points instead to a paper trail running back to [[Jesse Marcel]] and the [[Roswell Crash]], [[Project Moon Dust]] and the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], and to the language of the FY2024 National Defense Authorization Act. Against the op-ed's claim that no whistleblower ever came to AARO, he cites public statements from [[David Grusch]], [[Chris Mellon]] and [[Luis Elizondo]]. The op-ed portrays a rigorous, science-driven office; Gerb answers with a Department of Defense Inspector General finding that the DoD "has no overarching UAP policy".

The video then turns from the op-ed to Kirkpatrick's record. Kirkpatrick explained the "cube within a sphere" objects reported by pilots as Chinese ball drones, which Gerb likens to the [[Robertson Panel]]'s swamp gas and weather balloons. Gerb also looks at how Kirkpatrick left AARO, including [[Ross Coulthart]]'s reporting on a new LLC and a registration with [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]], and at a public reception where a Pentagon spokesperson, [[Susan Gough]], reportedly never left his side. Gerb calls the "most damning piece of evidence" a 2022 contract for AARO support services awarded to [[Sand Corp]], a company he says specialises in stopping leaks and whistleblowers.

## AARO and its director

Gerb introduces AARO as the Department of Defense's official UFO investigation program, set up in 2022 and tasked with investigating UFOs, USOs (unidentified submerged objects) and "transmedium" unknowns. It succeeded the [[UAP Task Force]], where David Grusch had been the liaison from the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]] and the [[NGA]]. Kirkpatrick, Gerb says, was "shrouded in controversy" for his entire tenure, especially once Grusch went public with his allegations. The office reports to [[Ronald S. ## The Scientific American op-ed

Gerb reads the op-ed as a set of rhetorical moves as much as a set of arguments. In the op-ed, and whenever he speaks, Kirkpatrick talks of "aliens" or "extraterrestrials" and never of "non-human intelligence", the term written into the Congressional Record and the FY2024 NDAA through the [[Schumer Amendment (NDAA)|Schumer amendment]]. (Gerb singles out a 2022 round table with Moultrie in which the two dismissed any extraterrestrial presence on Earth.) Gerb calls this "legalese", a way to sidestep the wording Congress actually uses. He also sees an emotional appeal carried over from a LinkedIn post Kirkpatrick wrote in 2023, after Grusch went public. In that post Kirkpatrick cast himself and AARO's staff as hard-working scientists doing diligent work while whistleblowers "run around with unsubstantiated claims", which cast Grusch and a small group as "crazed conspiracists".

## Sand Corp and the offices around AARO

Gerb calls a contract the most damning evidence that Kirkpatrick and AARO are "a disinformation front". In 2022 the [[Department of Defense]] awarded [[Sand Corp]] $1.9 million for "AARO Support Services". He concedes that contracts go to private industry all the time. But Sand Corp, he says (twice, for emphasis), specialises in preventing leaks and stopping whistleblowers, which is the reverse of what an office inviting whistleblowers would need. The office of Moultrie, the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security, was involved in securing the contract.

## The George Mason reception

Gerb's last item is a LinkedIn post by one Anthony Miller about a reception after what Miller calls Kirkpatrick's last public event as AARO director, at George Mason University in Arlington, Virginia. (Gerb introduces the meeting as taking place in 2022; Miller's post places it "last fall".) Miller writes that Kirkpatrick mingled with the crowd shadowed by Pentagon spokesperson [[Susan Gough]], who never left his side for a full hour, even when someone tried to talk to him one to one. Miller calls it "eerily reminiscent of a Soviet era minder" and says Kirkpatrick paused and looked at her for a nod or a headshake before answering some questions. The answers, Miller says, were "rife with double speak, patronizing dismissal, word play and semantics". Gerb sums it up: "That is the science being discussed here."

## Key Claims

- Sean Kirkpatrick's *Scientific American* op-ed of 19 January 2024 is, in Gerb's view, disinformation, and Kirkpatrick "a modern-day disinformation agent" rather than a sceptical scientist.
- Kirkpatrick speaks of "aliens" and "extraterrestrials" rather than "non-human intelligence", the term used in the Congressional Record and the FY2024 NDAA, which Gerb reads as a deliberate way to sidestep Congress's language.
- The op-ed's framing of the reverse-engineering story as a 2008 AATIP-era conspiracy ignores a paper trail reaching back to Jesse Marcel and Roswell, Project Moon Dust and the Wilson-Davis memo, and cannot explain the NDAA's repeated references to non-human intelligence and legacy programs or the Atomic Energy Act's role in misclassified UAP records.
- Kirkpatrick's claim that no whistleblower came to AARO is contradicted by David Grusch, who says he was never answered, by Chris Mellon, who says Elizondo, Davis and Puthoff each briefed Kirkpatrick for hours in a classified setting without feedback, and by Luis Elizondo.
- The DoD Office of the Inspector General found that the DoD has no overarching UAP policy, against Kirkpatrick's portrait of an office "unwaveringly committed" to clarity.
- By April 2023 AARO had analysed around 650 cases with no published reports, and it had no web page or contact route until the end of 2023.
- Explaining "cube within a sphere" objects as Chinese ball drones repeats the Robertson Panel's debunking method, and would itself describe a serious airspace violation.
- Kirkpatrick's departure is contested: Ross Coulthart reported a new LLC, Nonlinear Solutions, and a registration with Battelle-run Oak Ridge National Laboratory as of 16 October 2023.
- A $1.9 million 2022 DoD contract for AARO support services went to Sand Corp, which Gerb says specialises in stopping leaks and whistleblowers, with Ronald Moultrie's office involved.
- According to attendee Anthony Miller, Pentagon spokesperson Susan Gough shadowed Kirkpatrick throughout his last public event as director, and he looked to her before answering questions.
