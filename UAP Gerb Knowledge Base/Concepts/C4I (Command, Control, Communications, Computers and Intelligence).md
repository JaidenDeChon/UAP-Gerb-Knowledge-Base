**C4I** (Command, Control, Communications, Computers and Intelligence) is the integrated set of systems and processes that lets military commanders coordinate forces across a battlefield through shared digital networks, real-time intelligence feeds and unified command structures. In the UAP context, C4I comes up as one of the three primary objectives of the 1997 [[Hunter Warrior Advanced Warfighting Experiment]], a large Marine Corps exercise at [[Marine Corps Air Ground Combat Center 29 Palms]]. During that exercise, [[Rodrik Castle]] and his team encountered a massive triangular craft near [[Emerson Dry Lake]].

## Hunter Warrior and the Single Battle Concept

The [[Marine Corps Warfighting Laboratory]] (MCWL) ran Hunter Warrior from about March 2–14, 1997, as an experiment in dispersed, non-contiguous battlefield tactics. C4I was central to its design. The exercise tested whether Marine special-purpose air-ground task forces (SPMAGTFs) could operate effectively across a fragmented, wide-area battlespace by sharing targeting and intelligence data over digital C4I networks, an approach called the "single battle concept."

Gerb theorizes that the C4I infrastructure deployed during Hunter Warrior provided communications and coordination cover for clandestine [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|ARV]] testing in restricted airspace R2501C, about 90 miles southeast of [[Edwards Air Force Base]]. In this account, that infrastructure included naval adjunct operations coordinated through the [[Naval Aviation Warfare Development Center (NAWDC)]] at Fallon, Nevada and [[Naval Air Station Lemoore (NAS Lemoore)]]. Castle's encounter with armed, unmarked operators beside the triangular craft took place in the same operational area during the exercise.

## Significance in UAP Legacy Program Research

UAP researchers use Hunter Warrior as an example of how acknowledged military exercises can provide cover for clandestine legacy program operations. In their argument, the C4I networks and restricted airspace set up for a legitimate warfighting experiment would at the same time allow covert ARV test flights in the same operational environment, and the acknowledged exercise would offer a plausible explanation for unusual radar contacts, personnel deployments, and communications activity.
