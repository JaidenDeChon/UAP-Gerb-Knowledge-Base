A seamless craft hull is the reported outer surface of recovered non-human craft. It shows no visible seams, fasteners or joins, and it resists ordinary attempts to cut, drill or otherwise penetrate it. The idea comes up in several UAP whistleblower accounts and is considered a distinguishing feature of craft alleged to be of non-human origin.

## RB's Account

The most detailed account of a seamless craft hull in the UAP Gerb knowledge base comes from "[[RB]]," an anonymous Marine whistleblower who claimed he guarded a 40-foot disc-shaped craft at an undisclosed military base in December 1963. RB described the craft as "totally seamless except for a hatch-like seam on the lower part of the craft which was so well fitted you could not get a razor blade in the crack." He compared the construction to ancient stonework at Machu Picchu, where joints are so precise that paper cannot be inserted between them.

A technical crew tried and failed to breach the hull:
- Drilling near the hatch seam produced no penetration
- Cutting torches heated the metal but left the surface "clean and undamaged" once cooled
- A high-powered laser powered by two semi-sized trailer generator vans deflected completely off the hull, damaging the ceiling of the building instead

The hull's resistance to every known method of cutting and penetration, high-energy laser beams included, is cited as one of the main signs that the material was not made on Earth.

## Broader Context

Unusual hull materials are described in several crash retrieval accounts. Testimony compiled by researcher [[Leonard Stringfield]] and others often reports that recovered craft materials could not be cut, drilled or chemically analyzed. This suggests that alleged crash retrieval programs consistently failed to breach or reverse-engineer recovered craft by conventional means.
