The Aurora Craft refers to the family of classified aerospace vehicles allegedly developed under the [[Aurora Program]], a rumored umbrella program funded through the [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)]] and black budget appropriations. The program was inadvertently revealed in a 1985 Pentagon budget document as a line item with planned spending of $80 million in fiscal year 1986 and $2.3 billion in fiscal year 1987 — a 25-fold increase that at the time was double the cost of the B-2 Spirit bomber program.

According to [[Edgar Fouché]] and other sources, the Aurora program encompassed multiple craft including:

- **[[TR-3B]]** — The reverse-engineered triangular reconnaissance vehicle with [[Magnetic Field Disruptor (MFD)]] propulsion
- **SR-75 Penetrator** — A high-altitude Mach 5+ spy plane
- **Lockheed Pulsar** — As observed by [[Brad Sorenson]] at the 1988 Air Force Plant 42 exhibit; described as a large, all-black, flattened football-shaped unmanned craft approximately 100–110 feet in length, covered in tiles not unlike the Space Shuttle. It featured a synthetic vision system using infrared seeker heads and a dual propulsion system: internal fuselage engines for initial altitude, then external pulse detonation engines for acceleration to Mach 6–8. US officials stated the Aurora could "take out every city over a million population in the Soviet Union within one hour and twenty minutes." Sorenson confirmed this craft matched McCandlish's subsequent sketches
- **SR-33A** — Introduced by Dr. [[Michael Wolf]], an alleged UAP consultant to the National Security Council; similar to the TR-3B and Pulsar, the SR-33A allegedly utilized conventional fuel and anti-gravity field propulsion systems under a secret space program

The first flight-ready operational model of the TR-3B, part of the Aurora family, was achieved in the early 1990s, built through SDI and black budget funding.
