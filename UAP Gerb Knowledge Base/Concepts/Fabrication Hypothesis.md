The **fabrication hypothesis** is the theory, attributed by the [[Infographic Show Whistleblower]] to the crash retrieval program he served, that crashed craft and their biological crews are manufactured, possibly by an automated facility somewhere in the solar system, rather than built and flown by the intelligence behind them.

## The Program's Theory

The witness says the program concluded that "this was all being fabricated for us," and tasked [[NASA]] with looking for "a big 3D printer" in the solar system: "some facility or computer just making these craft and these things and sending them to Earth." The search looked for heat, since "it's hard to hide heat in space," and he says there "was some excitement for a while," though he does not know whether anything was found.

The theory rests on what the program saw of the crews. Recovered beings behaved like biological androids: they reacted only to basic stimuli, had crude digestive systems, breathed Earth's air suspiciously easily, and died within days (see [[Live Biologics Protocols]]). "Whatever's making these things just creates a biological crew when it needs one." The related [[Craft Donation Theory]] holds that many such craft are left for humans to recover.

## Context Cited by UAP Gerb

UAP Gerb links the hypothesis to [[John Von Neumann]]'s universal constructor, the 1940s idea of self-replicating machines that could let a civilization slower than light colonize a galaxy. He notes that [[Robert Sarbacher]] listed Von Neumann among the scientists involved in UFO recovery. He also cites a FOIA-released letter of 13 December 1977 to NASA's chief of ground operations safety, describing "what could be a piece of a UFO" examined at Los Alamos for over two months, and a NASA memo on a late-1970s "UFO HEAP" hard-evidence analysis program. [[Gary Nolan]]'s view that most occupants are "biological robots" sent as intermediaries is cited as a parallel.
