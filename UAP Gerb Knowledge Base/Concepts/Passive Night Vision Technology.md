Passive night vision devices, or image intensifiers, amplify the faint light already present at night, from the moon and stars, instead of lighting the scene with an infrared lamp as earlier "active" devices did. Retired Lieutenant Colonel [[Philip J. Corso]] claimed the US Army's first-generation passive devices owed something to the bodies recovered from the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]]. It is the first technology on his list of breakthroughs said to have come out of the [[U.S. Army Foreign Technology Division|Army's Foreign Technology Division]].

## Corso's claim

Gerb works from Corso's manuscript *Dawn of a New Age* and not from the book *The Day After Roswell*. According to the manuscript, the autopsy reports in the [[Roswell File]] described a transparent film over the eyeballs of the dead beings, and that detail led Lieutenant General [[Arthur Trudeau]] to order work on passive night vision. Corso said the night vision laboratory at Fort Belvoir, Virginia, built the first-generation devices with help from 48 US companies, each with a different degree of knowledge of the project.

Gerb notes that the Army did build passive systems in the early 1960s. In his timeline video he says the manuscript does not claim that people simply invented night vision from Roswell wreckage. In his reading, Corso said the wreckage helped inspire, advance or integrate technologies that already existed in military R&D. In his MITRE video Gerb lists night vision with fiber optics and brain-wave guidance as the programs Corso cared about, which reached industry through partners such as [[Bell Labs]] and [[Sperry Rand]]. His Off-World Technologies Division video opens with the same claim of Corso's, that night vision and fiber optics were reverse engineered from the Roswell crash.

## Objections

[[John B. Alexander]] has accepted some of Corso's testimony but disputed this claim in particular. He says the genealogy of infrared and night vision technology can be fully accounted for. He told of speaking to Dr. Lou Cameron, director of the Army's night vision laboratory at Fort Belvoir, who flatly denied that a non-human eye lens contributed to any breakthrough. Gerb sets this objection beside Corso's claim in his Corso video and says he cannot judge the technology claims himself.

## Background

The Army formed its Night Vision Laboratory at Fort Belvoir in 1965 under Dr. Robert S. Wiseman, who led its in-house development of image intensifiers from 1965 to 1968. First-generation passive "starlight" scopes such as the AN/PVS-1 and AN/PVS-2 were used by US troops in Vietnam.
