The Hazard Support System is a detection and analysis system built with data from [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites. Official DSP documentation mentions it as a project involving researchers at [[Aerospace Corporation]]. The 2014 DSP fact sheet states that "researchers at the Aerospace Corporation have used DSP to develop portions of a hazard support system that will aid Public Safety in the future."

## Bob Fish Analysis

In a 2015 email to [[John Podesta]], later made public by [[WikiLeaks]], [[Bob Fish]] cited this line from the 2014 DSP fact sheet as evidence that the DSP program still collects [[Fast Walkers|Fast Walker]] data, 24 years after his first encounter with DSP personnel in the late 1980s. In Fish's reading, the "hazard support system" involves continued analysis of anomalous objects detected by DSP satellites.

## Dual-Use Interpretation

The term "hazard support system" has dual-use implications, because it can be read in two ways.

### Public Safety Interpretation
Taken at face value, a hazard support system using DSP data could cover:
- Meteor and space debris tracking
- Near-Earth object monitoring
- Satellite collision warning
- Re-entry prediction for space hardware

These are legitimate public safety functions, and DSP's infrared sensors would suit them well.

### UAP Detection Interpretation
Other facts point to a second reading:
- DSP has been detecting Fast Walkers since 1972
- Researchers including [[Jacques Vallee]], [[Kit Green]], [[Hal Puthoff]], [[Eric Davis]], and Kristen B. Zimmerman have repeatedly identified Aerospace Corporation as a suspected UAP legacy program contractor
- The 2014 mention comes in a DSP fact sheet that otherwise downplays or leaves out anomalous detections

Seen this way, the "hazard support system" reference may be a public acknowledgment of continued Fast Walker detection and analysis, worded to avoid any explicit reference to UFOs or UAPs.

## Aerospace Corporation Role

The Aerospace Corporation's part in developing "portions of" this system indicates that it has access to DSP detection data and analyzes it. That data includes objects entering and leaving Earth's atmosphere that move too fast to be atmospheric phenomena. As a Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC) with access to Special Access Programs, Aerospace Corporation would have the clearances needed to work with the most sensitive DSP detection records.

According to [[Ross Coulthart]]'s sources, the corporation's campus in [[Long Beach, California]] has "access to the wreckage" from UFO crash retrievals. That would suggest its hazard support work may be connected to broader UAP program activities.

## Classification and Disclosure

The hazard support system is mentioned in an unclassified DSP fact sheet. This suggests that the program itself, or at least portions of it, is not classified, even if the data it analyzes remains highly classified, particularly the Fast Walker detections. Aerospace Corporation could then acknowledge its DSP analysis work without disclosing specific detection events or the full scope of the anomalous objects being tracked.

## Significance

The hazard support system is one of the few officially acknowledged links between DSP satellite operations, Aerospace Corporation's analysis work, and the ongoing monitoring of objects entering and leaving Earth's atmosphere. Because of classification restrictions, it remains unclear whether it is a genuine public safety initiative, a cover designation for Fast Walker analysis, or a dual-purpose system that serves both functions.
