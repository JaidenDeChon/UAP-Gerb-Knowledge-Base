The Very High Speed Transit System (VHST) was a proposal for a continental transportation network of vehicles running through evacuated tubes in deep underground tunnels, set out by engineer [[Robert M. Salter]] in a 1972 [[RAND Corporation]] paper. UAP Gerb describes it as "electromagnetically levitated and propelled cars in an evacuated tunnel". It is one of the best-documented official studies of deep, high-speed underground transit, and researchers into [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)|deep underground military bases]] cite it as a template for the train systems witnesses allege link those bases.

## The Proposal

- **Speed.** Speeds as high as 14,000 mph were examined, with a direct Los Angeles to New York link taking only 21 minutes. The study said long-haul speeds "will certainly be on the order of thousands of miles an hour".
- **Depth.** It recommended building deep underground to remove interference from grade crossings, weather and infrastructure, and observed that tunnelling at great length and depth was not appreciably harder than at shallow depth.
- **Staging points.** Because of the tunnel's expense, the first VHST would probably not be a non-stop link but one stopping at two intermediate staging points.
- **Antecedents.** UAP Gerb traces the concept to Robert Goddard's 1945 vacuum-tube transport system and to physicist Michael Minovitch's work on a deep underground tube shuttle.

## Place in the DUMB Timeline

The VHST belongs to a period in which, per UAP Gerb, the United States made enormous efforts to study high-speed underground tube trains. These included a mid-1960s working group of the International Society for Terrain-Vehicle Systems, a 1967 Commerce Department report that assumed "extensive tunneling will be involved", and the high-speed ground transportation initiative, whose participants included [[TRW]], General Dynamics, MIT, Southwest Research Institute, General Electric, NASA, the Army, the Navy and [[Battelle Memorial Institute]]. TRW's 1970 study described tube systems as deep as 3,000 feet with trains at about 450 mph.

UAP Gerb reads the staging points as the hub-and-spoke pattern of alleged DUMB networks. He notes that the VHST route map and [[Bill Hamilton]]'s 1990 network map share a direct route across northern Arizona and New Mexico, a split in Southern California towards San Diego and Sacramento before continuing north to Portland and Washington, and connections to Denver.
