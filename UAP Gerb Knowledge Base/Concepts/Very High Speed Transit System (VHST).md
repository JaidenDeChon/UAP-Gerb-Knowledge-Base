The Very High Speed Transit System (VHST) was a proposal for a continental transportation network of vehicles running through evacuated tubes in deep underground tunnels, set out by engineer [[Robert M. Salter]] in a 1972 [[RAND Corporation]] paper. Gerb describes it as "electromagnetically levitated and propelled cars in an evacuated tunnel". It is one of the best-documented official studies of deep, high-speed underground transit. Researchers into [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)|deep underground military bases]] cite it as a template for the train systems that, witnesses allege, link those bases.

## The Proposal

The study examined speeds as high as 14,000 mph, at which a direct link from Los Angeles to New York would take only 21 minutes. It said long-haul speeds "will certainly be on the order of thousands of miles an hour".

It recommended building deep underground, away from grade crossings, weather and other infrastructure. It also observed that tunnelling at great length and depth was not appreciably harder than tunnelling at shallow depth.

Because the tunnel would be expensive, the first VHST would probably stop at two intermediate staging points rather than run non-stop.

Gerb traces the concept to Robert Goddard's 1945 vacuum-tube transport system and to physicist Michael Minovitch's work on a deep underground tube shuttle.

## Place in the DUMB Timeline

The VHST came out of a period in which, according to Gerb, the United States made enormous efforts to study high-speed underground tube trains. These included a mid-1960s working group of the International Society for Terrain-Vehicle Systems, a 1967 Commerce Department report that assumed "extensive tunneling will be involved", and the high-speed ground transportation initiative, whose participants included [[TRW]], General Dynamics, MIT, Southwest Research Institute, General Electric, NASA, the Army, the Navy and [[Battelle Memorial Institute]]. TRW's 1970 study described tube systems as deep as 3,000 feet, with trains running at about 450 mph.

Gerb sees the staging points as the hub-and-spoke pattern of the alleged DUMB networks. He notes that the VHST route map and [[Bill Hamilton]]'s 1990 network map have several features in common: a direct route across northern Arizona and New Mexico, a split in Southern California towards San Diego and Sacramento before the line continues north to Portland and Washington, and connections to Denver.
