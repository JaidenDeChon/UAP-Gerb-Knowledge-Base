The Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI), colloquially known as "Star Wars," was a missile defense program proposed by President Ronald Reagan in 1983. It was intended to protect the United States mainland from ballistic nuclear missile attack with a combination of ground-based and space-based defensive technologies. In UAP research, SDI is alleged to have had a second purpose besides its public missile defense mission: multiple sources claim that significant portions of SDI funding were diverted to classified aerospace programs involving reverse-engineered non-human technology.

## Alleged UAP Connection

[[Edgar Fouche|Edgar Fouché]] claimed that his sources estimated up to 35% of SDI's annual funding was siphoned off to USAF black programs beginning in 1982. This money allegedly supported the Defense Advanced Research Center (DARC) at [[Area 51]] and paid for the development of the [[TR-3B]] and other craft under the [[Aurora Program]]. The Reagan administration purportedly placed extreme importance on building [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicles]].

[[Brad Sorenson]]'s 1988 testimony corroborates the link between SDI and ARV development. At the [[Norton Air Force Base]] exhibit where Sorenson saw the Flux Liner ARV, program officials told attendees they were asking Congress for additional funding to build more reverse-engineered craft. This was during the same period in which SDI was receiving massive appropriations.

Stealth aircraft researcher John Joseph also claims that [[Boeing]] was the primary contractor for Project Brilliant, an SDI program meant to establish an electromagnetic shield around the United States using a boomerang-shaped craft that could loiter over cities.

Gerb's timeline video "80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering" dates the beginning of SDI to March 1983 and repeats the belief that SDI funds were back-channeled to legacy programs. The video also theorizes that the space-based systems were conceived with a secondary, undisclosed function: to target and down UFOs. Gerb further speculates that the idea of using space-based weapons against UFOs was first conceived as far back as the Eisenhower administration, decades before SDI was unveiled to the public in 1983.
