The **Nuclear Security Enterprise** (NSE) is the integrated network of U.S. government laboratories, production facilities, testing sites and contractor organizations that maintains and modernizes the nation's nuclear weapons stockpile. It is overseen by the [[National Nuclear Security Administration]] (NNSA), a semi-autonomous agency within the [[Department of Energy]].

The NSE includes three main weapons laboratories: [[Sandia National Laboratories]], [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]] and [[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory]]. It also takes in production and component facilities, among them the [[Y-12 Complex]] in [[Oak Ridge, Tennessee]], the Pantex Plant in Texas, the Kansas City National Security Campus and the Savannah River Site in South Carolina. Each of these institutions is run either as a [[Federally Funded Research and Development Centers (FFRDCs)|Federally Funded Research and Development Center]] (FFRDC) or as a government-owned, contractor-operated (GOCO) facility. This hybrid legal status, with government funding and private contractor management, sets NSE institutions apart from standard federal agencies and gives them oversight boundaries of their own.

## Alleged Role in UAP Legacy Programs

In UAP research, the Nuclear Security Enterprise is identified as a probable home for UAP legacy programs, especially those that store, analyze or reverse engineer recovered non-human materials. The argument rests on several features of how the NSE is structured:

1. The [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]] allows the DOE to classify materials and activities as "Restricted Data," a category outside the standard classification authority set by Executive Order and exempt from FOIA. This gives the NSE legal protections that Department of Defense and intelligence community programs do not have.
2. NSE facilities have hardened vaults, radiological containment and compartmentalized secure spaces, which could hold and study anomalous materials of unknown composition or origin.
3. The [[Office of Intelligence and Counterintelligence]] (OICI) is embedded across NSE sites. This gives the NSE an in-house intelligence capability that can manage access and compartmentalization without referring matters to outside agencies.
4. Because major defense contractors manage the FFRDCs, there is some institutional buffer between the government chain of command and the day-to-day operations of specific programs.

[[Sandia National Laboratories]] and [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]] are the NSE facilities cited most often in allegations about UAP legacy programs.
