"Legacy Programs" is a term used in UAP research for long-running, clandestine government and contractor programs alleged to involve the retrieval, storage, reverse engineering and exploitation of recovered non-human intelligence craft and related materials. The term "legacy" refers to programs that began decades ago, potentially as early as the late 1940s after alleged crash retrievals, and that have been sustained across successive administrations. Compartmentalized funding, continuity of personnel and institutional structures designed to insulate them from standard oversight have kept them going. The programs are described as having rigid chains of command, strict need-to-know compartmentalization and self-sustaining bureaucratic mechanisms, which let them persist through political transitions and publicly acknowledged reorganizations of the intelligence community.

In the alleged legacy program structure, private defense contractors work alongside government entities, and the contractors' role has become increasingly dominant over time. [[Northrop Grumman]], [[TRW]], [[Lockheed Martin]] and associated organizations are the names most often cited as alleged custodians of legacy programs. Funding is alleged to flow through several routes, including [[Independent Research and Development (IRAD)]] overcharging, misappropriation of funds from acknowledged programs, back-channeled [[Special Access Programs (SAPs)]] and National Reconnaissance Office carryover appropriations. In his congressional testimony, [[David Grusch]] characterized legacy programs as operating entirely outside lawful Congressional oversight and described them as an illegal unacknowledged special access program structure.

The [[Department of Energy]] (DOE) and its subordinate institutions form a distinct tier of the alleged legacy program structure. Under the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]], the DOE holds a classification authority unique to it, which creates a "Restricted Data" category beyond the reach of presidential executive orders and FOIA. This is argued to leave DOE-hosted programs structurally more insulated from oversight than equivalent programs at the CIA or DoD. The [[Office of Intelligence and Counterintelligence]] (OICI) is specifically alleged to run UAP legacy program operations at DOE [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)|FFRDCs]], including [[Sandia National Laboratories]] and [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]].
