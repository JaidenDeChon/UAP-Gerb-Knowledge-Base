Manhattan Project 2.0 is Gerb's working term for the early, centralized phase of the UFO legacy program portfolio: a highly compartmentalized, top-secret national effort that took the security architecture of the United States' atomic bomb program and applied it directly to operations to retrieve, store and exploit recovered non-human technical vehicles and their occasional biological occupants. The term comes from [[David Grusch]]'s direct statements that the US government "took the Manhattan Project secrecy and overlaid it on this issue because that secrecy worked well for atomic bomb developments." The Manhattan Project 2.0 is understood to have been galvanized by the July 1947 Roswell, New Mexico crashes, although some US government awareness of non-human craft may predate 1947. The most notable example is the 1933 crash in Magenta, Italy.

## Core Characteristics

The Manhattan Project 2.0 directly inherited five foundational security pillars from the atomic program:

**Compartmentalization:** Personnel were read into only what was absolutely necessary for their specific task, whatever their clearance level. Scientific teams could not interface with other teams studying the same recovered vehicle. The number of read-in personnel was kept to an absolute minimum: scientists doubled as accountants and security personnel did janitorial work, so the read-in population would not grow.

**Organizational architecture:** Legacy activities were hidden inside deliberately mundane organizational covers: Air Force major commands, the Manhattan Engineer District organizational model and, later, cover offices like the SAF/AA "outside activities". Chains of command were extremely short and reported only to senior officials with a need to know.

**Physical security:** Programs operated hidden in plain sight, inside ordinary corporate parks, scientific facilities and even civilian infrastructure, with overt security kept only at specific national laboratory sites. The DOE NNSA national labs ([[Sandia National Laboratories]], [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]], [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]]) are the primary physical security layer for recovered vehicles and biological materials.

**Classification and information control:** UFO materials were classified through creative applications of the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]], including the "special nuclear material" (Section 51) and "transclassified foreign nuclear information" (TFNI, Section 142) designations. These statutory classifications are born classified, are exempt from normal declassification review and lie beyond the reach of presidential executive orders.

**Political shield/cover:** A UFO control group copying the Manhattan Project's Top Policy Group was set up within the National Security Council, first informally under Truman and then institutionally under Eisenhower's NSC 5412 Committee ("Special Group"). The group was kept as small as possible, left minimal paper trails and worked through informal, unrecorded NSC meetings to avoid documentation.

## Key Architects

[[Vannevar Bush]] and [[George C. Marshall]] are the two people most credited with building the Manhattan Project 2.0 security architecture. Both held senior roles in the Manhattan Project, which put them in a position to carry its security practices over to the UFO issue from 1947 onward. [[Leslie Groves]] receives an honorable mention as the author of the conceptual blueprint for the entire security apparatus. [[Harry Truman]] and [[Dwight D. Eisenhower]] are identified as the political architects who legally established and institutionalized the Manhattan Project 2.0 through classified presidential executive orders and executive actions.

## Centralized Control Group

During its golden age, roughly 1947 to the early 1980s, the Manhattan Project 2.0 had a unified control group and strong centralized leadership from presidents, cabinet-level officials and select senior statesmen. According to Grusch, the program's primary directive was to retrieve and exploit crashed, landed or downed non-human technical vehicles; attempt to understand the modus operandi of the non-human presence; store and study craft occupants; and use counterintelligence, disinformation and general spycraft to protect against insider and outsider threats.

The control group was housed in turn within:
- The National Security Council (Truman administration, 1947)
- The NSC 5412 "Special Group" (Eisenhower administration, 1954 onward)
- Nixon's 303 Committee (evolution of the 5412 framework)
- The SAPOC Senior Review Group quasi-government and industry panel (post-1994)

## The Great Schism

The Manhattan Project 2.0 began to fragment in the early 1980s under several converging pressures. Reagan's 1982 Executive Order 12356 ended the informal era of top-secret codewords known as the "wild wild west"; the 1983 Yellow Fruit audit and the related DoD SAP reforms nearly exposed legacy activities; and a series of close-call audits implicated the NRO and the Air Force. To protect themselves from exposure, legacy programs were forced to sever limbs and hide under different rocks, scattering into siloed cover offices, nested SAP compartments and contractor-held structures.

The schism was cemented in 1994 under Deputy Secretary of Defense [[Bill Perry]], whose SAPOC reorganization permanently moved legacy oversight to a quasi-government and industry panel of approximately two dozen people. [[Dick Cheney]] (Vice President 2001–2009) is identified as the last person able to wield effective centralized authority over what remained of the portfolio. [[James Clapper]] (DNI 2010–2017) is the nearest successor figure, but his authority was substantially diminished. As of approximately 2026, no one holds effective centralized leadership over the complete legacy program portfolio.

## Legacy Program Architecture Today

Gerb describes the modern legacy structure, far removed from the Manhattan Project 2.0, as a scattered set of fiefdoms: siloed programs within elements of the DoD, the intelligence community and the defense industrial base that operate without centralized coordination or shared objectives. Around 2005–2006, Northrop Grumman allegedly used independent research and development (IRAD) funds to set up contractor-led breakaway UFO exploitation projects that became unaccountable to their original government handlers, which further accelerated the fragmentation. The result is significant brain drain and redundancy, and a program portfolio in which even senior graybeards in one silo may not know about redundant programs in adjacent silos.
