TREAT (Tactical Reconnaissance Engineering Assessment Team) is an alleged clandestine US government unit that [[Edgar Fouche|Edgar Fouché]] described as what some would call the "Men in Black." Fouché learned of TREAT from his close source Gerald, a former [[NSA]] investigator and TREAT team member. Gerald worked under cover for the [[Department of Energy]] and the NSA, and the NSA controlled all his movements.

## Public Mentions

Gerb says he could find only one publicly available mention of TREAT besides Fouché's. In a letter to [[Steven Greer]] dated August 31, 1997, an unknown sender accused Liechtenstein's Prince Hans Adam II, whom Gerb calls the Crown Prince, of funding TREAT. As Gerb reads it, the letter states that "Adam's funding should be focused on his support for TREAT and its GRD, who is wed to a former US Army General, ex-head of Army Intelligence and Security Command" (the captions garble the word given here as "GRD"; it may be "Dr"). Gerb identifies the general as Major General [[Albert Stubblebine]], who commanded the US Army Intelligence and Security Command ([[Army INSCOM|INSCOM]]) from 1981 to 1984, and he takes the letter's TREAT to be Fouché's team. Stubblebine allegedly intimidated Greer into dropping his investigation of UFO black projects and once personally offered him $2 billion to end his disclosure activities.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions presented the 1997 letter as a mention of Fouché's TREAT. That reading is Gerb's. A different TREAT, the "Treatment and Research of Experienced Anomalous Trauma" abduction conferences founded in 1989 by the psychiatrist Rima Laibow, who later married Stubblebine and whose conferences Prince Hans-Adam is reported to have funded, fits the letter's wording at least as well (*Baltimore Sun*, 15 April 1992; ISGP; Wikipedia).

## Possible INSCOM Connection

On Gerb's reading of the letter, TREAT may be "the real life Men In Black housed within US Army INSCOM": an intelligence and counterintelligence body in the Army, focused on UFO matters. Its work may have included monitoring personnel at classified facilities and possibly field operations related to UAP encounters or crash retrievals. In a later video he calls Stubblebine "the alleged founder of the nsa's Men in Black treat".

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions stated TREAT's link to Stubblebine and INSCOM as an established fact. That link rests only on Gerb's reading of the 1997 letter, which may instead refer to Rima Laibow's abduction-trauma conferences of the same name.
