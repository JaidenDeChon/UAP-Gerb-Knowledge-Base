A content-only Special Access Program is a budgetary and administrative designation for a [[Special Access Programs (SAPs)|Special Access Program]] (SAP) that holds only critical program information (CPI), as opposed to a full program with its own funding line, major resource allocation, or independent budgetary authority. It is not a protection level like acknowledged, unacknowledged, or waived. A content-only SAP is nominally unfunded, and its costs are absorbed into ordinary Department of Defense, armed-service, or intelligence-community budgets. For that reason it carries no reporting requirements under either 10 U.S. Code § 119, which governs DoD SAPs, or [[Covert Action Program (50 U.S. Code § 3093)|50 U.S. Code § 3093]], which governs covert action programs. Official DoD documentation refers to the designation. Air Force Instruction 16-701 describes it as "a descriptive term used to describe a SAP or any sub element that contains information only and either has no funding associated with it or its funding is managed as part of the Air Force corporate budget process," and DoD Instruction 5205.11 describes a content-only SAP as one that "protects discrete critical program information."

## Alleged UAP Program Application

In *Special Access Required Vol.2*, Gerb identifies the content-only designation as the central mechanism that, in his view, allows legacy UFO programs to operate entirely outside statutory congressional oversight. The argument draws on statements [[David Grusch]] made in his Judicial Watch interview. It holds that legacy UFO activities originated as covert action programs under the National Security Council's 5412 Committee and its successors, and so were inherently subject to Gang of Eight/Gang of Four reporting requirements under 50 U.S. Code § 3093. Gerb argues that two steps together let legacy programs waive all statutory carve-out and reporting obligations at once. First, the program is designated non-covert action, which turns it into a White House-controlled SAP in a legal gray area between Title 10 and Title 50. Second, it is structured as content-only. Under this structure, the actual retrieval, exploitation, and storage activity is only "attributable to" the content-only SAP and is not conducted inside it. The true operational work and its funding are dispersed across other programs, umbrella compartments, and budget lines. Gerb links this structure to alleged "haircuts" taken across unrelated programs, misuse of independent research and development (IRAD) funds, and self-funding cutout companies. He also ties the theory directly to Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]]'s 1997 discovery, described in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], of a records group containing "zero budget info."
