A Tunnel Boring Machine (TBM) is a large, cylindrical excavation machine that bores circular tunnels through rock and soil. TBMs range from small utility-scale machines to massive civil engineering units that can bore tunnels more than 35 feet in diameter. In the theory of DUMB (deep underground military base) construction, they are the main conventional technology cited to explain how deep underground military tunnels and connecting passages could be built.

## How TBMs Work

The front of a TBM is a rotating cutting head fitted with metal attachments made of super-hard alloys, which grind and gouge through rock as the head turns. A conveyor assembly carries the excavated rock, called "muck", to the rear of the machine, where it can be removed by truck or rail. Hydraulics force the machine forward as the cutting head advances, so it bores one continuous circular tunnel.

## Performance Characteristics

[[Richard Sauder]] cites the following figures from informant testimony and industry records:
- A TBM operated by approximately 10 men can bore up to 10 miles per year under favorable rock conditions.
- The Pacific Gas and Electric Company used a Robins Company TBM to bore a tunnel 22,000 feet long and 24 feet in diameter, at a rate of 5–6 miles per year.
- Under more favorable conditions, rates of 10 miles per year or more are possible.

At these rates, a TBM could connect facilities such as China Lake Naval Air Weapons Station and Edwards Air Force Base, which are about 55 miles apart, in as little as five and a half years.

## Spoil Disposal and Concealment

A major problem with using a TBM in secret is getting rid of the excavated rock without leaving visible disturbance on the surface. Even the Nazis recognized this problem in their WWII underground construction. After the war, Allied examinations of Nazi facilities noted that excavated rock "had been carefully scattered into hills miles away," so the surface showed no evidence of major construction. The Bureau of Reclamation hypothesis partly addresses the problem. It proposes that clandestine secondary tunnels branch off legitimate water infrastructure tunnels, which would let the muck be mixed with the legitimate tunnels' waste.

## Defense Industry Use

Several major defense contractors and research organizations cited in research on UAP legacy programs have carried out above-board research into TBMs and tunneling:

- Bechtel Corporation, in a 1974 report titled "Research Program Plan for Meeting Tomorrow's Needs in Tunneling and Excavation", identified underground defense facilities, including ICBM silos and command posts, as a large source of demand for tunneling.
- Battelle Memorial Institute took part in the US National Committee on Tunneling Technology (USNCTT).
- Sandia National Laboratories also took part in the USNCTT.
- TRW took part in the High-Speed Ground Transportation Initiative studies.
