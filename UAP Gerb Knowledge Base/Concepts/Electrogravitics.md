Electrogravitics is a theoretical propulsion concept in which electrical or gravitational fields are manipulated to reduce mass or produce propulsive force. It goes back to work by [[T. Townsend Brown]] in the 1930s. Brown proposed that high-voltage electrical fields could interact with gravitational fields to produce thrust. In UAP research, electrogravitics is alleged to be the operating principle behind several classified aircraft, including the [[B-2 Spirit]] stealth bomber and the [[TR-3A]], and possibly behind parts of the [[TR-3B]] propulsion system.

## Alleged B-2 Application

UFO researchers including [[Richard Boylan]] and retired USAF Colonel [[Donald Ware]] have reported that three-star generals disclosed that the [[Northrop Grumman]] B-2 stealth bomber carries onboard electrogravitic systems that reduce the internal mass of its wings and fuselage. These systems allegedly contribute directly to the aircraft's price of over $1 billion per unit. A March 9, 1992, article by Northrop employees in *Aviation Week & Space Technology* discussed how the B-2 electrically charges the leading edge of its wing to reduce its radar cross-section, and negatively charges its exhaust gases to reduce its infrared signature. This technology is consistent with the electrogravitic principles Brown proposed decades earlier.

## Relationship to Other Propulsion Concepts

Electrogravitics is distinct from the [[Magnetic Field Disruptor (MFD)]] technology that [[Edgar Fouché]] described for the TR-3B. MFD creates a magnetic vortex field that disrupts gravitational effects on mass within its ring (inertial mass reduction). Electrogravitics instead manipulates the coupling between electrical and gravitational fields, either to reduce mass or to propel a craft directly. Both concepts fall under the broader category of anti-gravity or gravity-modification technologies allegedly derived from non-human intelligence craft.
