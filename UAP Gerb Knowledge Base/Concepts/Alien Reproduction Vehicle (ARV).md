An Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV) is an alleged human-built aircraft or spacecraft that copies technology reverse engineered from recovered craft built by a non-human intelligence. The term was first used for the 1988 exhibit at [[Norton Air Force Base]] that [[Brad Sorenson]] witnessed, where the DOD and private industry displayed their attempts to reproduce anti-gravity flying saucers. On November 13, 2024, the term "ARV" entered the Congressional hearing record through the [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)]] report, which used it for triangular craft that INDOPACOM intelligence assets had captured.

## Morphologies

UAP researchers and alleged insiders describe at least two main ARV shapes.

The first is the disc or saucer, called the Flux Liner. This is the original ARV that Brad Sorenson saw at Norton AFB in 1988 and that [[Mark McCandlish]] illustrated. It is a smooth, seamless disc with a central column ringed by concentric capacitor plates and a crew compartment above them. It allegedly runs on [[Electrogravitics]], magnetohydrodynamics or zero-point energy systems. The pilot steers it with a red sphere or trackball mounted on the armrest.

The second is the triangle, the TR-3B and its variants: a pitch-black equilateral craft with a bright white light at each corner and a red or amber light in the center of the underside. The [[TR-3B]] is the best documented of these and allegedly uses a [[Magnetic Field Disruptor (MFD)]] to cut its mass by 89%. Other alleged triangle variants include the [[XF-131 Super Sentinel]], TR-3A, and TR-3E. According to AARO historical reports, triangular ARVs make up 3–4% of all reported UAPs.

## Immaculate Constellation Report

The IMCON report, whose historical data went back to 2009, described at least two encounters with triangular ARVs that INDOPACOM collected. In the first, a large equilateral triangle hovered and slowly rotated 500–1,000 meters above the ocean. It had three bright lights at each corner and a horizontal bar of sweeping lights, and collected intelligence confirmed it was a "reproduction craft." In the second, an equilateral ARV triangle the size of a fighter jet hovered less than 200 meters above a vessel. It showed at least two visible lights, plus one obstructed light on the underside.

## Historical Context

Historical UFO reports show that sightings of triangular craft were rare before 1970 and after 2000 but rose sharply during the 1980s–1990s. [[Project Blue Book]] cataloged only four anomalous triangle sightings in the 1950s. The controversial [[Majestic 12]] document titled Psalm 101, allegedly dated 1954, lists triangular craft alongside saucers, spheres and cigars. It describes an isosceles triangle roughly 300 feet long on its longest side that could reach high speeds and make abrupt maneuvers. Gerb proposes that legacy UFO programs began building triangular ARVs sometime in the 1970s, modeled on recovered non-human triangular craft, and then refined and tested them over military bases from the 1980s through the 2000s.

## Known Sightings and Cases

Triangular craft of the ARV type have been reported worldwide, and the descriptions are consistent: a dark gray or black hull, white lights at the three corners, a red or amber light in the center, structural features on the underside, and almost no sound. Notable cases include the [[Belgian UFO Wave]] (1989–1990), the [[Hudson Valley UFO Flap]] (1980s–1990s), the St. Clair, Illinois sighting (2000), and the [[Boscombe Down Incident]] (1994).
