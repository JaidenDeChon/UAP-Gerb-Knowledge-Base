The TR-3B (Tactical Reconnaissance-3B) is an alleged classified triangular stealth reconnaissance platform described by Air Force Master Sergeant [[Edgar Fouche]] as "the most exotic and classified aerospace vehicle ever built." According to Fouché's 1998 disclosures, the TR-3B is a pitch-black, silent triangular craft capable of Mach 9 flight with indefinite loiter time, employing a gravity-warping propulsion system reverse engineered from nonhuman technology.

## Technical Specifications

According to Fouché, the TR-3B utilizes the [[Magnetic Field Disruptor (MFD)]], a circular plasma field accelerator ring surrounding a rotatable crew compartment. The MFD uses mercury-based plasma pressurized at 250,000 atmospheres at a temperature of 150 degrees Kelvin, accelerated to 60,000 RPM to create a superconductive plasma. This generates a magnetic vortex field that disrupts and neutralizes the effects of gravity on mass within proximity by 89%. Fouché emphasized this is not anti-gravity propulsion per se but rather inertial mass reduction — the vehicle becomes extremely light, enabling extreme performance.

The craft employs three multi-mode thrusters mounted at each corner of the triangular platform. The multi-mode propulsion system operates in the atmosphere with thrust provided by a nuclear reactor, in the upper atmosphere with hydrogen propulsion, and in orbit with combined hydrogen-oxygen propulsion. The TR-3B also features radar, ECM (electronic countermeasures), and ECCM (electronic counter-countermeasures) systems.

## Development and Operations

Fouché claimed TR-3B prototype flights began in the early 1970s with successful implementation of gravity-warping systems. By 1994, three operational prototypes were flying — triangles approximately 250 feet across — along with an enormous 600-foot operational model. Engineering was allegedly performed by [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Boeing]], [[Northrop Grumman]], Teledyne Ryan, and others. The program was managed by three of the US "Big Six" intelligence agencies: the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]], [[NSA]], and [[CIA]]. The MFD propulsion was reverse engineered at [[Sandia National Laboratories]] and [[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory]], both [[FFRDC (Federally Funded Research and Development Centers)|Department of Energy FFRDCs]].

Military pilots were described as elite aviators from the [[Air Force Test Pilot School]] at [[Edwards Air Force Base]] and the Navy's [[NAWDC|Top Gun school]] at NAWDC in Fallon, Nevada. Contractor pilots also operated the craft.

## Connection to Castle Encounter

The craft described by [[Rodrik Castle]] during the 1997 [[Hunter Warrior Advanced Warfighting Experiment]] closely parallels the TR-3B. Castle observed a 200-300 foot silent black equilateral triangle with rounded corners, embedded spherical lights at each vertex (similar to Fouché's vectored rocket engines), visible ridges or vents between the lights (comparable to TR-3B's variable vectored intake vents), and a concave dome structure on the underside (analogous to the MFD accelerator ring). The craft departed to the northwest toward [[Edwards Air Force Base]] and [[R2508 Complex|R2508 airspace]] at incomprehensible speed with no visible propulsion — consistent with TR-3B's described capabilities.

## Related Craft Designations

The TR-3B has been referenced under several variant designations including TR-3B Astra (Advanced Stealth Technology Reconnaissance Aircraft), TR-3B Black Manta, and in connection with the [[XF-131 Super Sentinel]]. [[Sal Pais]]'s 2017 patents for inertial mass reduction craft out of Patuxent River Naval Air Station feature a triangular design with a large concave center reminiscent of the TR-3B's MFD.

## "Aurora" and "Astra" Designations

Fouché described the TR-3B under the designation TR-3B Astra (Advanced Stealth Technology Reconnaissance Aircraft), linking it to the broader [[Aurora Program]] — a rumored family of classified aircraft developed under the [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)]]. The "Aurora" name was inadvertently revealed in a 1985 Pentagon budget document listing it as a line item with a $2.3 billion allocation. Fouché claimed the Aurora program encompassed both the TR-3B and the SR-75 Penetrator, a high-altitude Mach 5+ spy plane.

## Navigation and Control

The cockpit of the TR-3B is described as featuring a red trackball sphere used for navigation — a detail independently corroborated by [[Brad Sorenson]]'s description of the [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)]] control system and by [[Tom DeLonge]]'s novel *Locust* (co-authored with [[AJ Hartley]]), in which a triangular craft is navigated via a similar red trackball. DeLonge's adviser [[Eric Schrock]] allegedly provided factual UAP information for the novel series.

## 1992 Relocation

Sources of the master sergeant ([[Edgar Fouché]]) claimed that as of 1992, the Air Force conducted a contingency plan due to growing public attention on [[Area 51]]. This plan relocated the majority of exotic aircraft platforms to facilities in Utah (likely [[Dugway Proving Grounds]]), Colorado, Alaska, Greenland, Diego Garcia, and locations south of Area 51.
