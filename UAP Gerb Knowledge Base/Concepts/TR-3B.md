The TR-3B, also called the TR-3B Astra, is an alleged classified triangular aircraft that former Air Force Master Sergeant [[Edgar Fouche|Edgar Fouché]] described in the late 1990s as the most exotic and classified aerospace vehicle ever built. According to Fouché it is an [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicle]]: a human-built craft whose propulsion was reverse-engineered from recovered non-human technology. No such aircraft has been acknowledged, and the TR-3B has become the best-known name for the large, silent black triangles reported worldwide.

## Fouché's account

Fouché, who served with Detachment 3 of the Air Force Flight Test Center at [[Area 51]], said the TR-3B was code-named Astra (Advanced Stealth Technology Reconnaissance Aircraft) and was the most exotic model in the alleged Aurora Program, within a "tactical reconnaissance" line that also included a TR-3A. He said prototypes flew from the early 1970s and the craft became operational by 1994, when at least three operational models were flying. He described test models about 200 feet across and operational models about 600 feet across, crewed by four, powered by nuclear reactors, and lifted by a [[Magnetic Field Disruptor (MFD)|magnetic field disruptor]], a plasma ring around a rotating crew compartment that reduced the craft's effective mass. Its skin was said to change reflectivity in response to radar.

In Fouché's telling, engineering was performed by [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Boeing]], [[Northrop Grumman]] and [[Teledyne Ryan]], the program was managed by the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]], [[NSA]] and [[CIA]], and the propulsion was reverse-engineered from non-human craft by the Department of Energy laboratories [[Sandia National Laboratories|Sandia]] and [[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory|Lawrence Livermore]]. He said the craft flew from [[Edwards Air Force Base]] with test pilots of the [[412th Test Wing]] and were hangared near [[Papoose Lake]] south of Groom Lake.

## Lockheed Martin and Sandia

UAP Gerb's *Lockheed Martin - UFO Reverse Engineering, Material Exploitation, & Legacy Programs* sets Fouché's dates against Lockheed's corporate history. [[Martin Marietta]] won the contract to manage Sandia in 1993, the TR-3B allegedly went operational in 1994, and Martin Marietta merged with the Lockheed Corporation to form Lockheed Martin in 1995, so that the company Fouché named as a TR-3B contractor also came to run the laboratory he said built its propulsion. Gerb also ties Fouché's Edwards claims to the witness he calls Ed, who says Boeing and Lockheed were the key contractors in an ARV program spanning Edwards, Area 51 and S4, and to the triangle [[Rodrik Castle]] encountered during the 1997 [[Hunter Warrior Advanced Warfighting Experiment]].

## *Sekret Machines*

[[Tom DeLonge]]'s novel *Sekret Machines*, which DeLonge says drew on military and [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works|Skunk Works]] sources, describes a triangular reverse-engineered craft whose manual names it the "Astra TR3B" and whose crews call it "the Locust". Its pilot sits in a seat resembling an F-16's with a red sphere cradled on the left armrest. UAP Gerb notes that the cradled sphere matches the control [[Brad Sorenson]] and [[Mark McCandlish]] described in the Flux Liner ARV, whose crude copies also used out-of-place F-4 Phantom ejection seats.

## Disputes

Fouché's account has been challenged. [[Jeremy Rys]] and [[David Hilton]] accused him of blending the Nazi Bell story with the [[Belgian UFO Wave]] and of using doctored documents, though both later removed their videos; [[Andrew Johnson]] and Dan Benkert defended him. UAP Gerb treats black triangles as most likely human-built attempts to copy non-human technology.
