The Anunnaki (also spelled Annunaki or Anunaki) are the principal deities of the ancient Mesopotamian religious pantheons of the Sumerians, Akkadians, Assyrians, and Babylonians. In mainstream scholarship, they are understood as a group of major gods whose name translates roughly to "those of heavenly descent" or "princely offspring." In fringe and popular culture, the Anunnaki are the central subject of [[Zecharia Sitchin]]'s highly contested extraterrestrial interpretation of ancient Sumerian texts, in which they are reimagined as physical alien beings who genetically engineered modern humanity.

## Academic Understanding

In Mesopotamian religion, the Anunnaki were a pantheon of fifty or more deities, including major figures such as Enlil, Enki, Anu, and Inanna, who governed domains including sky, earth, storms, water, and the underworld. Their myths — preserved in thousands of clay tablet inscriptions excavated from sites including Ur, Nippur, and Nineveh — form the oldest substantial written religious literature in human history and include narratives that parallel later flood myths (notably the Epic of Gilgamesh).

## Sitchin's Extraterrestrial Interpretation

[[Zecharia Sitchin]] claimed in his 1976 book *The 12th Planet* and subsequent works that his translations of Sumerian cuneiform tablets revealed the Anunnaki to be actual physical extraterrestrial beings, originating from an undiscovered planet called [[Nibiru]] on an extremely elongated elliptical orbit around the Sun. Sitchin's narrative holds that:

- The Anunnaki arrived on Earth approximately 450,000–500,000 years ago.
- They established operations on Earth primarily to extract gold.
- Unable to sustain their mining workforce, they genetically engineered *Homo sapiens* by combining their own DNA with that of existing *Homo erectus* populations, creating a slave labor species.
- The Anunnaki's forced departure from Earth — triggered by catastrophic flooding caused by Antarctic glacial collapse — is the basis for global flood mythology, including the Biblical account of Noah.

Academic Assyriologists and Sumerian linguists have rejected Sitchin's translations as fundamentally inaccurate. His interpretation has no basis in peer-reviewed philology and is not recognized as a legitimate alternative reading of the texts.

## Connection to Other Theories

[[David Icke]] has claimed that the Anunnaki and his proposed [[Reptilian]] extraterrestrial controlling class are the same entities — a position Sitchin himself rejected. The Anunnaki mythology also intersects with alternative interpretations of human genetic origins, ancient religion, and the hypothesis that the UAP phenomenon has been present in human experience for millennia.
