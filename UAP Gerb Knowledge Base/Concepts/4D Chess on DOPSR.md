"4D Chess on DOPSR" is the term an anonymous insider, who contacted whistleblower [[Michael Herrera]], uses for a disclosure game aimed at the Defense Office of Pre-publication and Security Review ([[DOPSR (Defense Office of Prepublication and Security Review)|DOPSR]]). The plan is to deliberately put classified or deep black program information into a fictional manuscript, then watch how DOPSR responds during pre-publication review to learn what the government will and will not acknowledge.

## The Strategy

### Manuscript: Sentinels of Ether

The insider is reportedly writing a book titled "Sentinels of Ether" with a ghost writer. The book is described as a mix of truth and fiction, like [[Tom DeLonge]]'s "Secret Machines" series. It includes an event set in Mexico in 2004, in which a paramilitary black program team wipes out a JSOC team during an operation. The insider gave Michael Herrera a two-chapter manuscript that is currently under DOPSR review.

### The Gambit

The insider stated: "Our intention is to put the DOD in a pickle by creating dilemmas. We are strategically taking advantage of the fact **they must respond**. They don't have a choice. No poker face. Any response will provide us with information/intelligence and **inverse intelligence**."

The plan works like this:
1. The manuscript includes details, based on insider knowledge, about deep black programs that should not be publicly acknowledged.
2. By law, DOPSR must review all manuscripts from individuals with security clearances or insider access.
3. DOPSR's response reveals information:
   - If DOPSR flags the content, that confirms the material is classified and the programs exist.
   - If DOPSR allows the content, either the programs don't exist or they are so deep that flagging them for redaction would itself reveal them.
4. Whatever DOPSR does, the pattern of its responses gives the insider inverse intelligence: it shows where the government draws its classification boundaries and what it considers too sensitive to acknowledge.

### Stated Goal

The insider claims to be playing this game because the manuscript contains "deep black projects that shouldn't be acknowledged". Making DOPSR review it shows whether the office will "catch it" or let it through, and according to the insider, both outcomes have intelligence value.

## Concerns and Red Flags

Investigator [[Joey Is Not My Name]] was highly skeptical of the strategy.

Joey noted that the book was first presented as corroborating evidence for Michael Herrera's testimony, and asked, "how is a fictional book supposed to corroborate anything?" The book describes a completely different event, in Mexico in 2004, not Herrera's 2009 encounter in Indonesia.

The elaborate 4D chess strategy also raises the question of whether a genuine disclosure advocate would choose such convoluted methods over direct testimony, documentation, or whistleblowing through established channels.

Mixing truth and fiction is a classic disinformation technique, because it makes genuine classified information impossible to separate from fabrication. The [[Paul Bennewitz]] disinformation campaign conducted by the CIA in 1989 used the same mix.

## DOPSR Process Context

[[DOPSR (Defense Office of Prepublication and Security Review)|DOPSR]] (Defense Office of Pre-publication and Security Review) is the DOD office that reviews all books, articles, speeches, and other material written by current or former DOD personnel, to make sure they disclose no classified information. [[David Grusch]] is currently going through DOPSR review for an op-ed. Review is mandatory for people with clearances, and it can involve:
- Redaction of classified material
- Complete denial of publication
- Lengthy delays (sometimes years)
- Legal penalties for publishing without authorization

## Assessment

The "4D Chess on DOPSR" strategy could be one of three things:
1. Genuine disclosure gaming by an insider with classified knowledge, who is testing boundaries to reveal information indirectly.
2. A disinformation operation: a psychological operation to inject false information into the UAP community while appearing credible.
3. Self-aggrandizement by someone with limited knowledge, who creates elaborate narratives to seem more important.

Nothing has independently verified the content of the Sentinels of Ether manuscript, its DOPSR submission, or the results of the "4D chess" strategy. The concept remains an unverified insider claim.
