Project Stargate was an umbrella name for U.S. government programs that studied remote viewing and related claims of anomalous cognition under controlled conditions, both during and after the Cold War. In different phases, parts of the program were sponsored by the U.S. Army, the DIA and the CIA, with support from contractors.

## Relevance in UAP Discourse

Writers on UAP often cite Project Stargate as a precedent for government interest in intelligence methods that draw on the mind. They also point to it in support of later claims that some classified programs explored ways of linking the mind to technology.

The video on SAIC in this knowledge base mentions Stargate as part of a wider argument: that lines of consciousness research overlapped with the contractor settings later described in accounts of legacy UAP programs.

## SAIC Takeover (1991)

UAP Gerb's *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* dates to 1991 the takeover by [[SAIC|Science Applications International Corporation]] of Stargate and the wider government SCI research initiatives, which covered remote viewing, telekinesis and related research into mental phenomena. The video says it agrees with [[David Grusch]]'s public statements linking UAP legacy programs to SCI research. It also speculates that one possible use of the Stargate-derived programs was to develop methods for interfacing with, or "unlocking," specific recovered non-human technical vehicles.
