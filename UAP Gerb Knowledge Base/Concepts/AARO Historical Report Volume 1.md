The AARO Historical Report Volume 1 is an official report by the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) on the history of U.S. government involvement with unidentified anomalous phenomena from 1945 to October 2023. Many in the UAP community have criticized it for allegedly misrepresenting witness testimony and for reaching conclusions that firsthand accounts contradict, especially in its treatment of [[Michael Herrera]]'s testimony.

## Michael Herrera Misrepresentation

One passage in the report appears to describe Michael Herrera's 2009 Indonesia encounter, although it does not name him directly:

> "An interviewee who is a former U.S. service member said that in 2009, while participating in a humanitarian and security mission in a foreign country, he encountered **U.S. Special Forces** loading containers onto a large **extraterrestrial spacecraft**."

Herrera has adamantly stated that this description is factually wrong on two important points:

1. "U.S. Special Forces": Herrera testified explicitly that the operators wore no identifying markings, no insignia and no unit identifiers. He said they had American dialects and American gear, but they could not be identified as U.S. Special Forces or as any conventional military unit. The report's attribution is a fabrication.

2. "Extraterrestrial spacecraft": From his first public statement, Herrera testified that he believed the craft was human reverse-engineered, not extraterrestrial in origin. He has never claimed to have seen an alien spacecraft.

## SKIF Testimony Context

Herrera testified to [[AARO]] Director [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] in a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility (SKIF) in April 2023. According to Herrera:
- The testimony was not electronically recorded.
- The people present wrote their notes by hand on notepads.
- Without an audio or video recording, the testimony was open to misinterpretation or deliberate distortion.
- Another person who was in the SKIF can corroborate that Herrera never made the statements attributed to him.

Herrera calls the misrepresentation intentional disinformation rather than an innocent error or poor note-taking. He said: "I think this is so bogus and just in a two-sentence summary getting so many facts wrong, in my opinion this sort of misreporting is on purpose and a symptom of the larger sickness that is this report."

## Page 32 Misconception

A separate section on page 32 of the report discusses "a UAP with peculiar characteristics [that] refers to an authentic non-UAP-related SAP." Some in the UAP community have confused this passage with Herrera's account, but the investigator [[Joey Is Not My Name]] pointed out several differences.

The report says: "AARO was able to correlate this account with an authentic USG program because the interviewee was able to provide a relatively precise time and location."

The passage does not fit Herrera for these reasons:
- Herrera narrowed his encounter to within a couple of days, but he did not know the precise time.
- He did not know the exact location of the landing zone in Indonesia.
- The passage says the characteristics "matched closely with the platform's characteristics which was being tested at a military facility".
- Herrera's encounter took place in a remote jungle, not at a military facility.

This suggests that the page 32 passage describes a different witness account entirely, while the passage quoted earlier appears to be AARO's summary of Herrera's testimony.

## Belief-Focused Language

The analyst Joey Is Not My Name observed that when the report discusses witness testimony, it carefully stresses what the witnesses believe rather than whether their accounts are factual:

> "AARO emphasized how accepting they are of the fact that these whistleblowers **believe** what they're telling AARO."

This wording appears designed to avoid legal liability under whistleblower protection statutes, which impose penalties for giving false testimony. By stressing that witnesses "believe" their accounts, instead of saying the accounts are objectively false, AARO avoids creating grounds for prosecution or other legal action against the witnesses. It also avoids giving witnesses grounds to demand evidence that they lied.

## Community Reaction

UAP researchers have called the report an "abomination" and described it as a symptom of institutional resistance to UAP disclosure. The apparent misrepresentation of Herrera's testimony, especially the attribution of statements he explicitly denies making, has been cited as evidence of one of the following:
- Gross incompetence in recording witness testimony
- Deliberate distortion to discredit inconvenient accounts
- An institutional effort to muddy the waters around black program operations
