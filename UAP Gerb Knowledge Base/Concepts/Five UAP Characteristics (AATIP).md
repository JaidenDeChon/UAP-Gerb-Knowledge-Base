The Five Observables, also known as the Five UAP Characteristics, is an analytical framework developed by [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)]] under the direction of [[Luis Elizondo]]. It is used to identify and categorize anomalous aerial phenomena whose capabilities go beyond those of known conventional aircraft, and it has become a basic tool in modern UAP analysis.

## The Five Characteristics

1. **Instantaneous Acceleration.** Sudden changes in velocity far beyond what known aerospace technology can do, such as rapid acceleration from a standstill or extreme deceleration with no visible means of braking. Objects with this characteristic can go from hovering to hypersonic speeds in seconds, or the reverse, with no visible propulsion.

2. **Hypersonic Velocity.** Movement at extreme speeds (usually defined as Mach 5 or greater) without the sonic booms, visible exhaust plumes or other signs of conventional high-speed flight. This suggests propulsion or flight methods that get around the usual aerodynamic limits.

3. **Low Observability.** The ability to avoid detection by radar, infrared sensors or the eye while physically present in the airspace. This may involve radar-absorbent properties, active electronic countermeasures, or physical features that reduce electromagnetic signatures. Some UAP appear to "cloak", becoming invisible to some types of sensor while staying visible to others.

4. **Trans-Medium Travel.** Smooth movement between physical environments (air, water, space, and possibly other media) with no apparent difficulty or loss of control. Objects with this characteristic can enter water at high speed without the expected splash, or pass from underwater to airborne flight with no visible change in propulsion.

5. **Anti-Gravity/No Visible Propulsion.** Flight that suggests control of gravitational forces, or propulsion with no visible exhaust, wings, rotors or other conventional flight surfaces. Such objects may hover silently, make 90-degree turns at speed, or fly steadily in conditions that known aircraft could not handle.

## Application to Metapod Analysis

The Metapod footage filmed near [[El Escorial, Madrid, Spain]] on September 6, 2015 was checked against these five characteristics by Gerb, who judged that the craft showed three of them:

- **Instantaneous acceleration**, seen in the craft's sudden drop in altitude.
- **Anti-gravity/No visible propulsion**: the craft had no wings, rotors, exhaust or other conventional propulsion systems.
- **Low observability**, suggested by its silent operation and unusual flight profile.

The Metapod did not clearly show trans-medium travel, since it was not seen entering or leaving water. Nor did it clearly show hypersonic velocity, though speed estimates from handheld footage are imprecise.

## Significance in UAP Research

The Five Observables give researchers, military personnel and analysts a standard set of descriptive terms for UAP encounters. The framework deals with what can be observed about an object's flight and leaves aside speculation about its origin or propulsion, which makes data collection more rigorous and cases easier to compare.

Elizondo's description of these characteristics has influenced how military pilots are trained to report UAP encounters, and how government bodies such as AARO (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office) categorize and investigate reported phenomena.
