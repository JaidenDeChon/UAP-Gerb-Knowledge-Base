A Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicle (DSRV) is a small, highly advanced military submarine designed for deep-sea operations including crew rescue from disabled submarines, underwater reconnaissance, object recovery, and — according to their designer — classified intelligence missions that could not be accomplished by any other means. The United States Navy built two publicly acknowledged DSRVs: DSRV-1 (Mystic) and DSRV-2 (Avalon), both manufactured by Lockheed Missiles and Space Company in Sunnyvale, California, and operational within the US Navy from approximately 1970–2008. In UAP research, DSRVs are significant because their designer explicitly acknowledged they were built with clandestine intelligence missions in mind, and because a witness claims one was used to recover a non-human triangular craft from nearly 1.5 miles of depth in the North Atlantic in 1991.

## Capabilities

Mystic and Avalon were among the most technologically advanced submersibles in the world at the time of their operation:

- **Operational depth**: Officially just over 5,000 feet (approximately one mile), though UAP Gerb argues evidence from the 1991 retrieval case suggests unacknowledged variants may have been built with greater depth capability
- **Guidance**: More complex than the guidance system used on Apollo 11, allowing the DSRV to hover in strong ocean currents and precisely mate with disabled submarines
- **Mechanical arms**: Systems for grasping and rigging objects for recovery
- **Deployment**: Transportable worldwide within 72 hours via C-5 Galaxy aircraft, flatbed truck, or as a piggyback payload on a mother submarine

The two vessels operated within the Navy from approximately 1970 to 2008. Despite being maintained in constant readiness for submarine rescue, no official rescue missions were ever conducted — no American submarine sank after 1971. Classified DSRV deployment for intelligence missions was confirmed by [[John P. Craraven]], the DSSP's founder, who wrote: "There were many highly classified missions associated with national security that could not be accomplished without a DSRV system."

## Origins in the Deep Submergence Systems Project

Mystic and Avalon were products of the 1964 [[Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP)]], established by [[John P. Craraven]] partly in response to the classified [[Sand Dollar]] program. The original DSSP plan proposed six DSRV units and a separate family of Deep Submergence Search Vehicles (DSSVs) with 20,000-foot operational depth, specifically designed for seafloor exploration and object retrieval. Only Mystic and Avalon were officially acknowledged to have been built. UAP Gerb theorizes that additional DSRVs or DSSVs with greater operational depth may have been secretly built under the DSSP framework to service classified retrieval requirements.

## The 1991 North Atlantic Retrieval

A retired Marine Corps Lieutenant Colonel identified as "Mark" described a 1991 operation in which he was a crew member on a DSRV dispatched from Virginia to approximately 250 nautical miles west of Aberdeen, Scotland — in the vicinity of the [[Rockall Trough]], where ocean depths exceed 8,000 feet. The vessel dove to just under 1.5 miles of depth, beyond the publicly stated operational limit of Mystic and Avalon. The crew located a triangular, charcoal-gray craft approximately 68–70 feet long, partially buried in silt, bearing repeating geometric markings on its hull. Over a series of four dives across three days, the crew photographed the craft, brought aboard a marine archaeologist who estimated it had rested there 30–40 years, and ultimately rigged and recovered the object. The craft ascended two and a half to three times faster than anticipated given its size, indicating anomalously low mass. The underside was completely smooth with no propulsion or mechanical features.

UAP Gerb notes the discrepancy between the vessel's stated 1-mile operational depth and the 1.5-mile depth of the retrieval, arguing this implies the use of an unacknowledged vessel — possibly a DSSV-type unit of the kind proposed but never officially confirmed as built under the DSSP.
