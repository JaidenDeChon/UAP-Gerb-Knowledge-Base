The Biefeld-Brown Effect is an electrostatic phenomenon: when a high enough voltage is applied across an asymmetric capacitor assembly, it produces a net thrust toward the positive electrode. The effect is named for the American inventor [[T. Townsend Brown]] and his mentor, the physicist Paul Alfred Biefeld, who discovered it and first described it in the 1920s. Brown spent decades trying to turn the effect into a practical propulsion system. His experiments with high-voltage capacitor arrays produced measurable lift and directional movement both in air and in a vacuum.

## Relationship to ARV Propulsion

In his December 3, 1990 interview with [[William B. Scott]], [[Brad Sorenson]] said that the [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|ARV Flux Liner]] craft had a large array of capacitor plates as a central part of its structure. That detail led [[Mark McCandlish]] to ask Sorenson at first whether the craft ran on the Biefeld-Brown effect. Sorenson replied that the effect may have had "some sort of application," but that the craft's actual source of propulsion energy was Zero Point Energy drawn from quantum vacuum fluctuations. McCandlish's hypothesis was that the Biefeld-Brown effect and [[Electrogravitics]] were an early human discovery of a physical principle, which the ARV program later developed into a full propulsion system based on manipulating gravity.

## Connection to Edwards Air Force Base

[[Edwards Air Force Base]] lies approximately 30 miles northeast of [[Air Force Plant 42]] in Palmdale, California. According to McCandlish, the base has historically placed "very heavy importance" on alternative propulsion methods, and the Biefeld-Brown effect was one specific area of research interest. UAP researchers see the closeness of Edwards to AFP 42, the alleged site of ARV testing and reverse engineering, as consistent with a sustained research program into electrogravitic propulsion.

UAP Gerb's video *UAP Reverse Engineering at Edwards Air Force Base* adds evidence from documents. A 1988 technical report, submitted under contract with the Air Force's astronautics laboratory at Edwards, explored the Biefeld-Brown effect as a way to convert electrostatic energy into propulsion. Around the same year, [[SAIC]] studied similar electric propulsion mechanisms for the base. Gerb sets these alongside [[Project Outgrowth]], a 1972 Rocket Propulsion Laboratory study of anti-gravity concepts, as part of Edwards's long interest in alternative propulsion.
