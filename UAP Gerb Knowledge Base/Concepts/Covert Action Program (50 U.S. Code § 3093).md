50 U.S. Code § 3093 is the U.S. statute that governs presidential findings and the reporting of covert action to Congress. Covert action means activities of the U.S. government intended to influence political, economic or military conditions abroad without acknowledging the U.S. government's role. The authority for covert action goes back to the 1947 National Security Act. It became a formal National Security Council function under President Eisenhower's 1954 policy on covert action, which set up the NSC's 5412 Committee and its "Special Group" to oversee and approve covert operations. By law, covert action programs must be reported to Congress. They typically go at minimum to the "Gang of Eight" (the majority and minority leaders of the House and Senate, plus the chairs and ranking members of the two chambers' intelligence committees) or, for the most sensitive matters, to the smaller "Gang of Four."

## Alleged Application to UFO Legacy Programs

Gerb's *Special Access Required Vol.2* builds its central argument on statements [[David Grusch]] made in his Judicial Watch interview. Grusch described legacy UFO retrieval and exploitation activities as having originated within the National Security Council's 5412 Committee and its successor bodies. In Grusch's account, that origin makes such activities inherently classified as covert action, and so subject to the reporting requirements of 50 U.S. Code § 3093. Grusch stated, however, that a loophole exists: a program of the covert action type can be designated non-covert action, which turns it into a White House-controlled [[Special Access Programs (SAPs)|Special Access Program]]. Gerb argues that such a program sits in a legal gray area between Title 10 (10 U.S. Code § 119, which governs DoD SAPs) and Title 50 (which governs covert action), and is subject to the formal reporting or carve-out requirements of neither statute. In his view, the non-covert action designation, combined with structuring the resulting program as [[Content-Only Special Access Program|content-only]], is the specific two-part mechanism legacy UFO programs have used to evade both tracks of congressional oversight at once.
