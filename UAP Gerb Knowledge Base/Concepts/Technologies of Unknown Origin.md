"Technologies of unknown origin" (TUO) is the term the UAP Disclosure Act of 2023, the [[Schumer Amendment (NDAA)|Schumer amendment]] to the annual defense bill, used for physical material associated with UAP. Its definition covers "any materials or meta-materials, ejecta, crash debris, mechanisms, machinery, equipment, assemblies or sub-assemblies, engineering models or processes, damaged or intact aerospace vehicles, and damaged or intact ocean-surface and undersea craft associated with unidentified anomalous phenomena or incorporating science and technology that lacks prosaic attribution or known means of human manufacture." Gerb uses the phrase throughout his videos for whatever the [[Legacy Programs|legacy programs]] are said to retrieve, exploit and reverse-engineer.

## In the Schumer amendment

In his video on [[Karl Nell]], Gerb walks through the amendment's definitions with Nell. A legacy program, in the bill's terms, is any federal, state, local, commercial, academic or private-sector effort to collect, exploit or reverse-engineer technologies of unknown origin or biological evidence of non-human intelligence. Nell says the TUO definition is what the bill's eminent-domain clause turns on: it would have let the federal government take such technology and biological evidence, including from private corporations. Gerb notes that some of the arguments against using the clause concern property rights, which to him points to private companies holding UAP material. In his account these provisions were "watered down and gutted" before the bill passed.

Gerb also quotes Nell from the 5 June 2023 article in *The Debrief* by [[Leslie Kean]] and [[Ralph Blumenthal]] that introduced [[David Grusch]]. There Nell said Grusch was fundamentally correct that an arms race to reverse-engineer technologies of unknown origin had run sub rosa for 80 years, and that some of that technology derives from non-human intelligence.

## Earlier accounts

Gerb applies the term to much older claims. [[Philip J. Corso]] said the [[U.S. Army Foreign Technology Division]] under Lieutenant General [[Arthur Trudeau]] brought in Bell Labs, universities, Army laboratories and Paperclip scientists such as [[Wernher von Braun]]'s team to exploit recovered technology, disguising artifacts as ordinary research proposals and letting industry keep the patents. [[Albert Bruce Collins]] told researcher [[Tim Cooper]] in 1990 that by 1949 the intelligence offices of the Army, Air Force and Navy, the [[CIA]], the [[RAND Corporation]] and the [[Vatican]] were all involved in research on such material.

Gerb places TUO work at a number of sites: [[Dugway Proving Ground]], deep underground bases, Navy surface warfare centers such as [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane|Crane]], and the Air Force's test ranges, where he theorizes that [[Air Force Operational Test and Evaluation Center (AFOTEC)|AFOTEC]] teams support test and evaluation of the technology. His [[SAIC]] video has a section under this heading on SAIC's 1988–1990 *Electric Propulsion Study* for the Air Force Astronautics Laboratory and on the Defense Intelligence Reference Documents produced under [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]].
