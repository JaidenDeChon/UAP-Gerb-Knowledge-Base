The Pentagon UFO Videos are three short videos recorded by the targeting systems of US Navy aircraft. The Department of Defense had classified them before investigative journalists obtained and published them in 2017–2018. They are the most significant officially acknowledged footage of unidentified aerial phenomena in American history. [[Luis Elizondo]] and [[Chris Mellon]] helped bring about their release, and the *New York Times* published them in partnership with journalists [[Leslie Kean]] and [[Ralph Blumenthal]]. The release was a turning point in how mainstream media and the government treated the UAP phenomenon.

## The Three Videos

FLIR1 ("Tic Tac") was recorded on November 14, 2004, by the crew of the USS Nimitz Carrier Strike Group off the coast of Southern California. The AN/AAQ-28 LITENING targeting pod on an FA-18F Super Hornet captured a white, oblong object, nicknamed the "Tic Tac", moving erratically at high speed with no visible propulsion. Commander David Fravor and Lieutenant Commander Jim Slaight intercepted the object and saw it by eye. Fravor described it as a white Tic Tac performing flight maneuvers he had never seen in a 40-year career. In the end, the object vanished instantly from their position.

Go Fast was recorded on January 21, 2015, by an FA-18 Super Hornet off the US East Coast. It shows a small object moving rapidly over the ocean surface with no apparent propulsion signature. The object was officially estimated to be moving at extraordinary speed relative to the water below.

Gimbal was recorded on January 21, 2015, off the coast of Florida, during the same fleet engagement that produced the Go Fast footage. The AN/ASQ-228 ATFLIR targeting pod on an FA-18 captured a rounded, oblong object that rotated in place against the direction of the aircraft's motion while holding a stationary position in high winds. The pilot commentary includes "There's a whole fleet of them — look on the SA" and "It's rotating." Pilots who recorded the footage have confirmed that part of the video remains classified; in that part, the object accelerates and joins a formation of other craft in a V-shape. Many UAP researchers consider Gimbal the most significant of the three.

## Release and Context

Luis Elizondo directed the Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program (AATIP) at the Pentagon. He resigned from the Department of Defense in 2017, citing frustration over institutional resistance to UAP investigation. He then worked with [[Chris Mellon]], a former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence, to provide the videos and supporting context to journalist [[Leslie Kean]] and her colleague [[Ralph Blumenthal]] at the *New York Times*. The Times published its article, with the videos, on December 16, 2017, and it immediately drew significant attention from the media and Congress.

The Department of Defense officially confirmed the authenticity of all three videos in April 2020.

## Significance

The videos are significant both as physical evidence and as institutional milestones. Their release effectively ended the Pentagon's decades-long policy of officially denying UAP, and it established that the US military had a classified program dedicated to studying them. The videos were also the first officially authenticated footage of objects whose performance was far beyond that of any known aircraft. They led directly to later legislation, including the UAP reporting requirements in the National Defense Authorization Acts of 2022 and 2023, and to the establishment of AARO.
