A missile chaff decoy is a countermeasure that [[ICBM (Intercontinental Ballistic Missile)|ICBMs]] release in flight to deceive enemy air defense systems. During re-entry, the warhead separates from multiple chaff decoys, which are lightweight objects built to give radar returns similar to those of the actual warhead. Enemy interceptor missiles or air defenses then aim at the decoys instead of the live payload. The chaff and the warhead separate at high altitude, and both continue at high velocity on similar trajectories.

## Role in the Vandenberg Incident

Chaff decoys bear directly on the [[Vandenberg Air Force Base UFO Film Incident]] of September 1964. The ICBM test that day was explicitly designed to test this kind of deception. A dummy warhead with the exact dimensions and weight of a live nuclear warhead was launched together with chaff meant to replicate the warhead's radar signature. The aim was to simulate a real nuclear attack, in which Soviet air defenses would target the chaff while the warhead continued to its target.

According to Lieutenant [[Robert Jacobs]]'s description of the event, the disc-shaped UFO that entered the camera frame appeared to track the dummy warhead specifically and ignore the chaff, which suggests the craft could tell the actual warhead from its decoys. The warhead was traveling at 6,000–8,000 miles per hour at the fringe of space when the craft fired four directed energy beams at it.

## Significance

The chaff decoys were intended to be the most sophisticated defensive countermeasure available against Soviet anti-ballistic missile systems. The unknown craft apparently identified and specifically targeted the warhead while ignoring the surrounding chaff. That implies sensing or identification capabilities significantly beyond the air defense technology of the era.
