The Mother of Pearl Effect is the name for a distinctive visual phenomenon observed on the surface of certain alleged UAP craft. A shimmering, iridescent pattern of color shifts and flows across the object's exterior, much like the rainbow sheen of gasoline on water, an oil slick or a soap bubble.

## Peru Crash Observation

The most detailed description of the effect comes from [[Jonathan Weygandt]]'s testimony about the 1997 [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]]. Weygandt described the crashed [[Egg-shaped UAP|egg-shaped craft]] as purplish-green, with the color changing continuously across its surface. It would shift from green to purple and sometimes looked almost silver or translucent. Weygandt compared it to the rainbow sheen on bubbles of dish detergent, or to the iridescence of gasoline on a wet surface. The effect was seen to move around the craft's exterior, and it appeared to come from some kind of field on the craft instead of being a property of the material itself.

The craft also absorbed direct sunlight instead of reflecting it, and cast no shadow even though it was lit. This suggests that the color-shifting field may be related to the way the craft interacts with electromagnetic radiation.
