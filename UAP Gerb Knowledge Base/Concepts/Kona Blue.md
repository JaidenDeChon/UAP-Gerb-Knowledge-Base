Kona Blue refers to an alleged Program Access Procedure (PAP) and associated technology transfer initiative aimed at moving reportedly recovered non-human materials from a [[Lockheed Martin]] special access program into a government-administered research program, later associated with the AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program) effort. The Kona Blue transfer was reportedly blocked in approximately 2010–2011 by CIA Directorate of Science and Technology Deputy Director [[Glenn Gaffney]], with possible additional involvement from [[Mary K. Sturdivant]], Jim Ryder, and Robert Cardillo. The failed transfer is considered by UAP researchers to be one of the most significant documented paper trails confirming both that Lockheed Martin allegedly held non-human materials and that senior CIA officials acted to prevent those materials from reaching a congressionally visible research program.

The significance of the Kona Blue incident extends beyond the transfer itself. If the PAP was formally initiated, its existence in classified records would constitute documentary evidence of the government's awareness of alleged non-human material holdings at a defense contractor. The blockage is interpreted as an act of program protection — a deliberate effort by CIA DS&T elements to maintain siloed legacy program control rather than permit oversight. UAP researcher Gerb describes the Kona Blue paper trail as among the most important evidentiary artifacts in the UAP disclosure landscape.
