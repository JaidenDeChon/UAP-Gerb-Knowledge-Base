Interplanetary contamination is the transfer of living organisms from one world to another, a central concern of planetary protection in spaceflight. It takes two forms: **forward contamination**, carrying Earth life to another celestial body, and **back contamination**, bringing extraterrestrial organisms to Earth, which could in principle endanger all of humanity.

## In the UFO iceberg

The idea is the closing entry of "The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 2)", where Gerb calls it "a crucial thing to consider when planning space travel". He illustrates back contamination with the film *Alien*, in which a parasitic species infects the crew of the *Nostromo*. He also notes that returning spacecraft and their samples follow rigorous protocols, and that outbound craft are sterilised to prevent forward contamination.

As a real example of the risk, he cites a 2019 lunar crash that left tardigrades on the Moon and led to speculation that they could survive there. Gerb describes the craft as Indian, but the lander that carried tardigrades and crashed on the Moon in April 2019 was the Israeli SpaceIL mission Beresheet. (India's Chandrayaan-2 lander, Vikram, also crashed that year.)
