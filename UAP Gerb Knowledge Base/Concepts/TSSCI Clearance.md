**Top Secret/Sensitive Compartmented Information (TS/SCI)** is a United States security clearance level that grants access to highly classified information beyond a standard Top Secret clearance. The SCI designation means the information is divided into compartments tied to specific programs or intelligence sources, and access is granted only on a strict need-to-know basis. A TS/SCI clearance requires an extensive background investigation and is one of the highest clearance levels granted to US government personnel and contractors.

## Fast Walker Program Access

[[Bob Fish]] held a TS/SCI clearance as a program manager for classified US government advanced communications systems. He described having lunch with [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellite operators in the cafeteria of a highly classified organization in [[El Segundo, California]]. Fish noted that no one could enter the cafeteria without a TS/SCI clearance, and he stressed that this was "not a lightweight group of gossipers."

The TS/SCI requirement for access by DSP personnel indicates that [[Fast Walkers|Fast Walker]] detection data and satellite operations are among the most highly compartmented programs in the US military space enterprise. A standard Top Secret clearance is not enough: personnel need additional compartmented access to work with DSP detection records.

## Compartmentation and Need-to-Know

Because of the SCI component, even people with Top Secret clearance can view Fast Walker data only if they have access to the specific program and a demonstrated need to know. This structure lets the government keep tight control over who can see satellite detection records of anomalous objects, and it limits disclosure even within classified channels.

## Significance for UAP Secrecy

The TS/SCI protection of DSP Fast Walker data explains why FOIA requests from [[John Greenewald]], [[Barry J. Greenwood]], and others have been categorically denied. The information is both classified and compartmented into specific SCI programs, which fall under the most restrictive disclosure protocols available under Executive Order 13526.

According to Fish, cleared DSP personnel openly discussed Fast Walker detections among themselves inside the TS/SCI-protected facility. This suggests the phenomenon is well known and regularly observed within these compartmented programs, even though public disclosure through official channels remains impossible.
