Connective tunnel systems are an alleged network of deep underground tunnels linking separate Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs) and classified facilities across the United States, particularly in the American Southwest and along the East Coast. The tunnels are distinct from the individual DUMB facilities. They would allow personnel, materials, and transport vehicles (potentially including maglev rail systems) to travel between installations without coming to the surface, which would keep travel between facilities secret and secure.

## Evidence Base

Official documents, witness testimony, and circumstantial analysis support the existence of connective tunnel systems.

Official documentation:
- A 1982 workshop report by the US Army, USAF, Navy and DOE set out the need for 400 miles of tunnels, 2,500 to 3,500 feet deep, for various purposes including housing nuclear missiles. The tunnels would have "interconnecting passageways and additional horizontal passageways for storage, living quarters, and other needs." The report described the proposed location as "probably in the desert of the western United States."
- The Rand Corporation's 1972 VHST study proposed a continental underground tube transit system running at extreme speeds. The geography of its map corresponds closely to Bill Hamilton's 1990 map of the DUMB network.
- A FOIA document obtained by [[Richard Sauder]] gave firm estimates of the personnel and cost needed to build and operate a "deeply buried, hundreds-of-mile-long tunnel system": 7,800–8,700 people and about $17 billion over 10 years.

Witness testimony:
- In the 2010s, an unnamed Army officer told Gerb that he had traveled by underground train from a fort in Texas to [[White Sands Missile Range]]. He said the speed and acceleration reminded him of the fear of flying.
- [[Bill Hamilton]]'s informants described maglev train systems connecting Tehachapi, Helendale, McDonald Douglas, Edwards AFB, and China Lake.
- One of Hamilton's sources claimed to travel regularly between California desert facilities and the Nevada Test and Training Range.
- [[Richard Sauder]] received reports of underground rail systems running to Area 51 from Mercury, Nevada, and from the Naval Surface Warfare Center Corona Division.

## Construction Timeline

Gerb proposes this timeline for building the connective tunnel systems:
- After 1961: the primary DUMB facilities are built.
- From 1972–1974 onward: construction of the connective tunnels begins, at the same time as the National Committee on Tunneling Technology is formed and major tunnel feasibility studies are published.
- 1980s–1990s: maglev transport systems are installed in the existing tunnel network, which fits the timeline of the public National Maglev Initiative research.

## Bureau of Reclamation Hypothesis

One proposed method of construction uses [[Bureau of Reclamation]] water conveyance tunnels in the American Southwest as cover for secretly boring secondary tunnels. Sauder's informants have indicated that TBMs working inside legitimate Bureau tunnels could branch off to bore secondary classified passageways. The excavated material would be mixed with the legitimate tunnel's waste, and the secondary tunnel's entry point would be hidden within the primary infrastructure.

## Relationship to DUMB Network

The connective tunnel concept cannot be separated from the broader [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)]] analysis. Individual DUMBs may be significant on their own as storage or testing sites. If connecting tunnels exist, however, they would turn the network from a collection of isolated facilities into an integrated underground complex that could operate as one system.
