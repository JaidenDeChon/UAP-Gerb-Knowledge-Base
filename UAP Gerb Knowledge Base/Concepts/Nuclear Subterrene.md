The nuclear subterrene is an experimental tunneling machine concept developed by [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]]. It uses compact nuclear reactors to melt its way through rock instead of cutting or blasting it mechanically. Liquid lithium circulates from the reactor core to the tunneling face, where it melts the rock at the leading edge. The cooled lithium then flows back along the outside of the machine and cools the surrounding rock as the machine is forced forward. Because the melted rock vitrifies into glass, the process leaves neat, structurally solid tunnels with glass-lined walls that need no additional lining or support.

## Development History

The US Atomic Energy Commission filed patents for nuclear subterrene technology in 1972, and the US Energy Research and Development Administration filed in 1975. All but one of the inventors named on the three patents were Los Alamos researchers. A 1973 Los Alamos study, "Systems and Cost Analysis for a Nuclear Subterrain Tunnel Machine: A Preliminary Study", concluded that nuclear subterrenes were a cost-efficient way to meet US demand for transportation tunnels.

The concept essentially disappeared from public literature after the mid-1970s, which suggests it was either abandoned or reclassified.

## Significance in DUMB Research

DUMB (deep underground military base) research cites the nuclear subterrene as a probable tool for building clandestine underground tunnel systems, for several reasons:

1. A nuclear subterrene melts rock in place and leaves glass-lined tunnel walls. Tunnel boring machines (TBMs) have to remove spoil and cause visible disruption at the surface, but a subterrene leaves no "muck" to remove and little evidence at the surface.
2. The technology was designed specifically for deep underground work, which fits the depths of hundreds to thousands of feet described in DUMB testimony.
3. Los Alamos National Laboratory, a federally funded research and development center (FFRDC) that Gerb deeply implicates in UAP legacy program research, developed both the nuclear subterrene and the tunnel system design studies. The laboratory allegedly has its own extensive underground facilities and connections to non-human technology research.
4. The patents, filed in 1972–1975, line up with Gerb's proposed timeline, in which DUMB tunnel construction began around 1972–1974.

## Relationship to TBMs

In DUMB theory, the nuclear subterrene and conventional [[Tunnel Boring Machine (TBM)|Tunnel Boring Machines]] are complementary ways of building tunnels. TBMs are conventional and well documented, and their use can be partly hidden by routing clandestine tunnels off Bureau of Reclamation water infrastructure tunnels. Nuclear subterrenes, if they work, would be far more covert, because they could bore sealed, glass-lined tunnels with no surface signature.
