A security oath is a legally binding agreement that military or government personnel swear or sign when they gain access to classified programs. It requires them to keep secret what they have witnessed, under penalty of criminal prosecution. Security oaths appear in several UAP whistleblower accounts as a way to enforce long-term silence about crash retrieval programs, recovered craft, and related classified operations.

## RB's Account

The anonymous Marine whistleblower "[[RB]]" said he signed a security oath when he was honorably discharged from the [[United States Marine Corps]] in 1966. The oath covered his 1963 guard assignment involving a recovered disc-shaped craft at an undisclosed military base. The stated penalty for breaking it was ten years in prison and a $110,000 fine. RB cited the oath as one reason he never went public. He disclosed his account only through an intermediary to researcher [[Michael Johnstone]], who then put him in touch with [[Leonard Stringfield]].

Oaths of this kind for witnesses to UAP-related programs come up again and again in whistleblower testimony. The stated penalties are severe, including criminal prosecution under federal law, and they act as a significant institutional deterrent against disclosure. This helps explain why many alleged witnesses to crash retrieval programs have either stayed silent entirely or come forward only anonymously and through intermediaries.
