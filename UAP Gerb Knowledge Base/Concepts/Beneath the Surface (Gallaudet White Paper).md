*Beneath the Surface: We May Learn More About UAP by Looking in the Ocean* is a 2024 white paper by retired US Navy [[Rear Admiral Timothy Gallaudet]], published by the [[Sol Foundation]]. It argues that [[Unidentified Submerged Object (USO)|unidentified submerged objects]] and [[Trans-Medium Vehicle|trans-medium]] UAP deserve to be a national research priority, both as a maritime security problem and as an unprecedented scientific opportunity.

## Argument

Gallaudet calls for action to uncover the "unknown unknowns" of UAP and USOs, citing what he calls the Department of Defense's "concerning non-reaction" to unidentified objects with unexplainable characteristics entering US waters. The executive summary notes that pilots, credible observers and calibrated military instruments have recorded objects crossing the air-sea interface in ways not possible for human-made craft, and that congressional legislation defines UAP as trans-medium vehicles. It argues that the engineering, materials science and physics behind such objects could transform transportation, energy, agriculture, communication, computing, manufacturing and space travel, and that further study "may lead to discoveries that make those of the Scientific Revolution in the 17th and 18th centuries look like baby steps."

The paper stresses the gap between the attention paid to UAP in the sky and in the ocean. It points to AARO's [[AARO Historical Report Volume 1|Historical Report Volume 1]], which contains no reference to any USO case or maritime whistleblower.

## Cases Cited

- The [[2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter (Tic Tac)|2004 Nimitz Tic Tac encounter]], in which F/A-18F pilots saw the object hover over roiling whitewater, possibly indicating a larger object below.
- The [[Aguadilla Puerto Rico Trans-Medium UAP Incident|2013 Aguadilla incident]], filmed by US Customs and Border Protection thermal imaging, in which an object reportedly entered and left the Atlantic without significant deceleration, reached 95 mph underwater and split in two.
- The [[Shelburne Harbor USO Incident|1960 Shelburne Harbor incident]], in which Royal Canadian Navy divers reportedly saw two disc-shaped craft and their occupants on the seabed. Gallaudet writes that he first found the story implausible, until recent US military whistleblower disclosures.

The paper also summarizes the literature's range of USO forms: luminous orbs, silver and grey discs, triangular and cigar-shaped objects, and large lighted craft seen beneath the surface without ever emerging.
