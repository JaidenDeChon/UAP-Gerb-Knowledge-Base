An Unidentified Submerged Object (USO) is an anomalous phenomenon observed operating in or transitioning through bodies of water — oceans, lakes, rivers, or reservoirs — that cannot be identified as any known vessel, submarine, sea life, or natural feature. USOs are the aquatic counterpart to unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) and in many documented cases involve objects observed entering or exiting water, traveling at speeds and depths impossible for any known submarine or underwater vehicle, or being recovered from the seafloor in configurations inconsistent with human manufacture.

In the UAP legacy program research framework, USOs are particularly significant because they imply a recovery and storage challenge requiring specialized naval infrastructure rather than the helicopter or flatbed truck logistics employed in land-based crash retrievals. UAP Gerb argues this operational complexity is a primary reason why the US Navy's UAP programs are described by multiple sources as the most secretive of any US military branch.

## Physical Characteristics

Reported USOs share several recurring characteristics across independent witness accounts:

- Transmedium capability: the ability to transition from air to water or water to air without apparent deceleration or physical disruption
- Speeds and maneuver profiles impossible for any known submarine
- Absence of standard propulsion signatures (no sonar bubble trails, no exhaust, no cavitation noise)
- Operation at depths far exceeding any known crewed submarine
- Radiation or electromagnetic signatures detectable by ship-based sensors

The 1991 North Atlantic case ([[1991 Deep Sea Triangle UFO Recovery]]) involved a craft approximately 68–70 feet long, triangular, with no propulsion features on its underside, that had rested on the seafloor for an estimated 30–40 years. It ascended far faster than predicted when raised by a Navy DSRV crew, indicating anomalously low mass.

## Documented Recovery Operations

The US Navy is alleged to have conducted USO recovery operations across several decades:

- **1962 Bluegill Triple Prime Recovery**: A UFO reportedly disabled by a high-altitude nuclear test and recovered by the USS Safeguard
- **1967 Shag Harbor, Nova Scotia**: A UAP entered Shag Harbor and was tracked underwater by Canadian and US forces; UAP Gerb notes US Navy divers reportedly took over the recovery operation
- **June 1973 Hawaii USO**: A USO allegedly shot down by a Navy destroyer between Hawaii and the mainland, recovered by the Hughes Glomar Explorer and transported to the Great Lakes Naval Base
- **1991 North Atlantic**: A triangular craft recovered by a DSRV crew approximately 250 nautical miles west of Aberdeen, Scotland, at nearly 1.5 miles of depth

## Infrastructure for USO Retrieval

The US Navy's alleged USO retrieval infrastructure includes:

- The [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] (NURO) — a joint CIA–Navy black agency responsible for coordinating undersea intelligence collection and recovery
- The [[Office of Naval Research]] (ONR) — alleged recipient of recovered craft before transfer to defense contractors
- [[Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicle (DSRV)]] units and potentially classified DSSV variants with 20,000-foot operational depth
- The [[Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution]] — named by journalist Christopher Sharp as providing deep submergence vehicles for retrieval support
- [[SAIC]] and [[Lockheed Martin]] — contractors involved in material exploitation at facilities including [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane]]
- [[Project Sand Dollar]] — a classified Navy program maintaining a global inventory of objects on the seafloor warranting recovery
