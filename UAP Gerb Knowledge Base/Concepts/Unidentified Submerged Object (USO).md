An Unidentified Submerged Object (USO) is an anomalous craft or phenomenon seen operating underwater or passing into or out of oceans, lakes, rivers or coastal waters, which cannot be identified as a known natural phenomenon, submarine or other conventional vehicle. USOs are closely related to the broader category of [[Trans-Medium Vehicle|transmedium objects]], craft seen moving between water, the atmosphere and, in some cases, near space without any apparent change in how they fly. USO reports go back through recorded history on several continents and in several navies. They appear in US government files (notably [[Project Blue Book]]), Soviet naval records, Australian government archives and centuries-old maritime logs.

## Characteristics

Documented cases share a set of recurring physical traits that set USOs apart from conventional underwater vehicles and surface phenomena:

- Transmedium transition: entering or leaving the water at speed without disturbing the surface. Reports describe objects submerging "without affecting the water" or rising from depth with no visible wake.
- Extreme speed: travel rates that known submarine technology cannot match, from 25 to 500 mph in cases near the surface.
- Electromagnetic interference: compass readings, ship communications and engines disrupted when the objects come close, consistent with the electromagnetic effects documented in many aerial UAP encounters.
- Shape consistency: the most common shapes in historical reports are cigars or cylinders, eggs or ellipses, glowing spheres, and rotating wheel-like structures.
- Luminosity: objects described as glowing, metallic white or orange-yellow, or as giving off structured patterns of light.
- Intelligent maneuver: changing direction, hovering, reversing heading, and apparently noticing the vessels watching them.

## Regulatory Suppression

Official USO documentation is thin partly because of deliberate regulation, not a lack of reports. Two US directives in particular limited how much of a public USO record could build up:

- [[OPNAV 3820]] (1952/1954) limited the circulation of Navy UFO sightings to the Air Force Technical Intelligence Center, USAF Intelligence and the Director of Naval Intelligence. This sent maritime UAP reports into classified channels instead of the public [[Project Blue Book]] investigation.
- [[JANAP 146C]] (1954) barred military personnel, and civilians who had filed official sighting reports, from discussing those reports in public, under criminal penalties that included fines and imprisonment.

As a result of these directives, only 258 of the 13,000+ sightings in Blue Book's 20-year record involved ships, and only 13 of those met the program's own merit standards. The Soviet Union had a parallel system of suppression. After the 1977 Vulga incident, though, [[Fleet Admiral Nikolai Smirnov]] issued a directive making USO reports mandatory for Soviet ships, which in effect created a Soviet version of OPNAV 3820 after the fact.

## Historical Record

USO reports cover a long period and a wide area. The main documentary sources are:

- Project Blue Book ship sightings (1949–1969): 258 ship cases, 13 of them meeting merit standards, with several describing submerged or transmedium objects. Notable cases include the [[MV Marala North Atlantic Sighting]] (1950, classified "unidentified"), the [[SS City of Alako Hawaiian Islands Sighting]] (1959), the [[Ascension Island USO Sighting]] (1960) and the [[SS Morgantown Victory Sighting]] (1966).
- Soviet naval and scientific records: compiled in *Russia's USO Secrets* by [[Paul Stonehill]] and [[Philip Mantle]] and cited in Jacques Vallée's *UFO Chronicles of the Soviet Union*. Notable cases include the [[Soviet Submarine Repair Ship Vulga Sighting]] (1977) and the [[Kamchatka Lake USO Sighting (1970)]].
- Australian government files: the Royal Australian Air Force began formally logging unusual aerial and maritime sightings in the early 1960s. These include events near Groote Island in 1964 that disrupted instruments, and similar cases through 1967.
- Pre-twentieth-century maritime records: the [[Maritime Light Wheel]] phenomenon covers repeated encounters with rotating, luminous wheel-shaped structures in the Eastern Hemisphere from 1873 until at least 1910. The encounters are preserved in admiralty logs, hydrographic bureau records and scientific publications.

## Significance in UAP Research

UAP researchers and government officials have argued that USOs may be the most significant category of observation within the wider UAP phenomenon. Water covers about 71% of Earth's surface, and the ocean floor is among the least surveilled places on the planet, which makes the ocean an ideal place to operate for craft that do not want to be detected. [[Rear Admiral Timothy Gallaudet]] has argued publicly, including in his 2024 white paper *Beneath the Surface*, that confirmed trans-medium vehicles would be discoveries of greater scientific and strategic importance than anything in recorded history. [[Kevin Knuth]] at the [[Sol Foundation]] has discussed the electromagnetic and propulsion physics implied by craft that can operate both in the atmosphere and in water without changing their flight profile.
