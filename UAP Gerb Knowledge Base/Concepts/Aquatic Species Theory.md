The Aquatic Species Theory is a hypothesis in UAP research that some of the non-human intelligences associated with UAP may be aquatic or amphibious, living in or coming from water. It draws on patterns in crash retrieval testimony, in encounters with underwater UAP (USO), and in physical evidence from alleged recovery operations.

## Evidence from the Peru Crash

Much of the theory's detail comes from [[Jonathan Weygandt]]'s testimony about the 1997 [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]]. Weygandt described a crashed [[Egg-shaped UAP|egg-shaped craft]] that was leaking large quantities of a clear liquid with the viscosity of maple syrup, much of it pouring from a half-open hatch where a non-human occupant's arm was visible. In conversation with Gerb, Weygandt theorized that the craft may have been filled with water, making it a closed aquatic environment, in effect an aquarium, for whatever species piloted it. Gill-like vents along the outside of the craft also suggest a design influenced by aquatic biology.

Weygandt's observations raise the possibility that some UAP, unlike remote-controlled or consciousness-operated craft, may be habitation vessels for beings that need an aquatic environment to survive, with the interior kept as a pressurized fluid environment.

## Connection to USO Phenomena

The theory overlaps with wider research into [[USO - Unidentified Submerged Objects|Unidentified Submerged Objects (USOs)]], which are UAP that operate beneath water, and into [[Air-Water Interface Crossing]] phenomena, in which craft are seen moving smoothly between air and water.
