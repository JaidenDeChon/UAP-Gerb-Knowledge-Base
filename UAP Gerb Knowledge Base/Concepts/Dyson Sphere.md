A Dyson Sphere is a hypothetical megastructure that would completely enclose a star and capture all of its energy output. The theoretical physicist Freeman Dyson proposed it in a 1960 paper in the journal *Science*. Building one is the defining technological ability of a Kardashev Type II Civilization, which by definition uses the full energy of its host star. A Dyson Sphere, or the more commonly discussed Dyson Swarm of separate collector units, would sharply change the light we observe from the enclosed star. That change is a techno-signature that [[Dysonian SETI (CTI)]] searches could detect.

## Concept and Physical Basis

The Sun puts out approximately 3.8 × 10²⁶ watts of energy, and Earth intercepts roughly one billionth of it. A civilization that could collect all of the Sun's output would have approximately a billion times more energy than Earth currently uses. Dyson proposed that an advanced civilization would inevitably want to build such a structure.

A rigid spherical shell around the Sun at Earth's orbital distance (1 AU) would be gravitationally unstable. Two variants are more physically plausible:

- A Dyson Swarm is a dense collection of independent solar power satellites in stable orbits, assembled gradually over centuries.
- A Dyson Bubble is a network of solar sails held in place by radiation pressure.

A complete or partly assembled Dyson structure would absorb the star's energy and re-radiate it as heat. The star would then give off unusually little visible light and unusually strong infrared radiation, which is a detectable anomaly.

## Construction Method

The most plausible way to build a Dyson Sphere uses von Neumann self-replicating probes. These are autonomous robots programmed to mine asteroids, moons and other planetary bodies for material and to make copies of themselves, so the swarm grows gradually without any living workers in space. That avoids the long delays that direct human (or alien) labor in deep space would cause.

## Dysonian SETI

The Dyson Sphere idea is the basis of [[Dysonian SETI (CTI)]], a search method that looks for techno-signatures in a star's light instead of radio transmissions. These include excess infrared light, irregular changes in a star's brightness, and energy absorption with no known cause. The star KIC 8462852 ("Tabby's Star") drew wide attention in 2015 because its light dimmed sharply and irregularly. For a short time this was considered a possible sign of Dyson construction, before natural explanations gained more support.
