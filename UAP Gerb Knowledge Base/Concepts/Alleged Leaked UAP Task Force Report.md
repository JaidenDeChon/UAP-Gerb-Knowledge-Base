The Alleged Leaked UAP Task Force Report is a set of purported pages from a longer, classified version of a [[UAP Task Force]] report prepared for Congress. The pages circulated online in 2021 or 2022, including in a post on 4chan. Two pages are discussed in Gerb's analysis of the [[Flyby Footage]]: a cockpit communications log of a fighter intercept, and a page from a section titled "Behavioral Data Analysis". Gerb argues that the intercept the log describes lines up almost exactly with the Flyby clip.

## Structure

As Gerb reads them, the two pages belong to one document:

- **Appendix F, Section 4B, "DoD 1992 through 2017 High Value Witness Interviews".** This holds the communications log. The log page carries a header Gerb reads as "Appendix F4B D03", from page 508 of the report.
- **Section 5, "Behavioral Data Analysis".** This page describes an analysis of 1,292 UFO cases by joint elements of the [[Office of Naval Intelligence]], the [[NSA]] and [[DHS (Department of Homeland Security)|DHS]], together with SAP-cleared Department of Defense experts. At the foot of the page it cites Appendix F, Section 4B, which ties it to the log.

## The communications log

The log is presented as the cockpit voice recording of an intercept involving three parties: a two-seat fighter with the call sign Club 11, crewed by a pilot and a weapon systems officer; an [[AWACS (Airborne Warning and Control System)|AWACS]] aircraft with the call sign Brigam; and an unidentified controller, BLK, which uses [[NATO Brevity Language|NATO brevity language]]. According to the log, BLK guided Club 11 toward a radar contact that the WSO reported closing at 2,820 knots. Electronic countermeasures activated, and the two contacts merged on BLK's scope with no visual contact. When the crew said they could see nothing, BLK told them to stand by. The crew then saw an object off the starboard wing, "hanging out about 10 to 15 ft from the canopy". BLK ordered them not to change velocity or vector and to "open your blue envelopes and snooze", which Gerb reads as an instruction to confirm weapons were off and follow briefed mission-control procedures.

## The Behavioral Data Analysis page

The Section 5 page uses the term UA/SP, for unidentified aerial and submerged phenomena. Gerb considers this distinction a small point in the document's favour. The page concludes that UAP are either remote or autonomous drones or a form of mechanical life, and that an "organic species" has been deploying UA/SP whose rapidly improving flight performance indicates a "stepped virtual evolution process". It names [[M. Harmon]], [[S. McCaron]] and [[K Shakoya]], credits Shakoya with the finding that gradual changes in UA/SP appearance and behaviour reflect generational changes, and mentions a "Blackout Flower report" and a "Layer Three Behavioral Assessment".

Gerb found almost nothing on the names or the report titles and assumes the names are pseudonyms or have had their first names removed. His one lead is [[Hillary S. McCaron]], a research neuroscientist at the [[US Army Medical Research Institute of Chemical Defense]] according to her LinkedIn page. Because the latest contribution, Shakoya's, is dated 2020, while the UAP Task Force was operating (2017 to 2022), Gerb suggests the document may be an internal Task Force report.

## Significance

If the report is genuine and its appendix log describes the Flyby encounter, the clip would rank as "high value witness testimony" in the government's own records. In Gerb's view that would make it one of the most crucial pieces of UFO footage ever taken.
