A Deep Submergence Search Vehicle (DSSV) is a category of unmanned or crewed deep-ocean submersible proposed under the United States Navy's 1964 [[Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP)]], designed specifically for seafloor exploration, object detection, and object recovery at operational depths up to 20,000 feet. Unlike the [[Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicle (DSRV)]] — designed primarily for crew rescue from disabled submarines — the DSSV was conceived from the outset as a vehicle for finding and recovering objects from the deep ocean floor.

## Origin and Design

The DSSV concept emerged from the DSSP, which aimed to build six DSRV units and a family of DSSV variants. The DSSVs were intended to be capable of:

- Seafloor navigation and object search at depths up to 20,000 feet (approximately 6,100 meters)
- Object recovery, including the rigging and lifting of large, heavy, or anomalously configured objects
- Clandestine operation — the DSSP explicitly described classified intelligence missions as a primary justification for the program

## Non-Acknowledgment

No DSSV units have been publicly acknowledged as having been built. Only DSRV-1 (Mystic) and DSRV-2 (Avalon) were formally confirmed as DSSP products. UAP Gerb argues that at least some DSSV-type units may have been secretly built and operated, citing:

- The 1991 North Atlantic retrieval described by witness Mark, in which a DSRV-type vessel reportedly operated at just under 1.5 miles of depth — exceeding the publicly stated operational ceiling of Mystic and Avalon by approximately 0.5 miles
- The mechanical arm and rigging capabilities described by Mark are more consistent with a purpose-built retrieval vehicle than with the DSRV's primary rescue configuration
- [[John P. Craraven]]'s explicit statement that the DSSP was designed to support "the intelligence 'mission impossible' that were sure to occur," suggesting capabilities beyond what the acknowledged DSRV units provided
