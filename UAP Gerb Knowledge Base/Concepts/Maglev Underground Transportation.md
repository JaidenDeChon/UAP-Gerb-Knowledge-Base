Maglev underground transportation is the alleged use of magnetic levitation (maglev) trains in deep underground tunnels that connect classified military installations, particularly Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs). The idea has appeared consistently in UAP witness testimony since the 1980s. It is corroborated by a substantial body of above-board research into high-speed underground transportation systems, carried out by the government and defense contractors from the 1960s onward.

## Technology Overview

Maglev trains are rail systems whose vehicles are levitated by electromagnetic forces instead of running on conventional wheels. With no contact between wheel and rail, they allow:
- Higher maximum speeds (top recorded speeds exceed 375 mph in conventional maglev systems)
- Superior acceleration and deceleration
- Lower maintenance requirements
- Reduced noise
- Improved gradient handling

Pneumatic tube versions surround the train with a partial vacuum. This greatly reduces aerodynamic drag and allows theoretical speeds of thousands of miles per hour. For long-haul underground routes, the Rand Corporation's 1972 Very High Speed Transit (VHST) study estimated that "speeds as high as 14,000 mph have been examined in studies by the Rand Corporation", which would make a trip from Los Angeles to New York take only 21 minutes.

## Historical Antecedents

DUMB research credits the idea of underground maglev transport to Herman Keur, a Third Reich engineer who began developing the "Rohrban" concept in 1934. His system used magnetic levitation trains in underground vacuum tunnels, which were meant to connect European cities at 600–1,000 mph. Whether Keur was recruited under [[Operation Paperclip]] is unknown. Gerb presents it as a feasible hypothesis, because other Nazi underground construction engineers are documented as having been recruited.

## Above-Board Research Timeline

A large body of acknowledged, above-board research into high-speed underground tube trains was carried out from the 1960s through the 1990s:

- **Mid-1960s**: The International Society for Terrain Vehicle Systems (ISTVS), established with support from the Office of Naval Research, Army Research Office, and Booze Allen Hamilton, coordinated research on high-speed underground tube systems.
- **1967**: A multi-agency panel of the US Department of Commerce recommended accelerated research in its report "R&D for High-Speed Ground Transportation", stating that "regardless of the system adopted, it can be assumed that extensive tunneling will be involved."
- **1970**: TRW published "State-of-the-Art Tube Vehicle System," discussing tube systems and tunnels up to 3,000 feet underground with trains traveling at 450 mph.
- **1972**: RAND Corporation published the VHST study by Robert Salter, proposing a continental deep underground electromagnetic system at speeds up to 14,000 mph.
- **Early 1990s**: The [[National Maglev Initiative]] (NMI), a joint DOE/Army Corps of Engineers program, published system concept definition studies involving Boeing, General Dynamics, Grumman, Battelle, and Honeywell.

## Witness Testimony

- [[Bill Hamilton]]'s informants in the [[Antelope Valley]] during the late 1980s and early 1990s described electric vehicles within DUMBs and maglev train systems connecting Tehachapi, Helendale, McDonald Douglas, Edwards AFB, and China Lake.
- A source called "Gober", cited by Hamilton, described working at Tehachapi, Helendale, and the Nevada Test and Training Range. The account included details about on-site personnel rotating every 14–16 days and about underground transport systems.
- An unnamed Army officer in the 2010s told Gerb he traveled by underground train from a fort in Texas to [[White Sands Missile Range]], and that the speed and acceleration were so extreme they reminded him of his fear of flying.
- [[Richard Sauder]] said he had read reports of underground rail systems connecting Area 51 with Mercury, Nevada, and with the Naval Surface Warfare Center Corona Division.

## Network Architecture

Gerb proposes that the alleged underground transportation network has a hub-and-spoke layout, like a railway system. Central hub installations connect to other major hubs and also branch out to smaller local facilities. RAND's VHST study, which described "intermediate staging points" between long-haul endpoints, supports this model, and it matches the layout implied by Hamilton's 1990 DUMB network map.
