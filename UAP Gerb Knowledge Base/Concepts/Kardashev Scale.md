The Kardashev Scale is a theoretical way of classifying how technologically developed a civilization is, based on the total amount of energy it can harness and use. Soviet astrophysicist [[Nikolai Kardashev]] proposed it in a 1964 paper titled "Transmission of Information by Extraterrestrial Civilizations," and the scale gives a practical way to compare how advanced hypothetical extraterrestrial civilizations might be and to place Earth's own stage of development.

## Classification System

Kardashev originally defined three types of civilization.

A Type I, or planetary, civilization can harness all the energy available from its home planet, including full control over natural phenomena such as weather, earthquakes and volcanic activity. It has used up the surface and atmospheric energy resources of its world.

A Type II, or stellar, civilization can capture all the energy its host star puts out. The theoretical mechanism most often linked to Type II status is a [[Dyson Sphere]], a megastructure that encloses or surrounds a star to intercept all of its radiation. Type II civilizations could occupy other planets and moons in their star system.

A Type III, or galactic, civilization can harness energy on the scale of a galaxy. It colonizes or otherwise exploits every star system in its home galaxy and travels freely among them.

### Extended Scale

Physicist Michio Kaku and others extended the scale with two further types. A Type IV civilization would control the energy of an entire galactic cluster, and a Type V civilization would control the energy of the entire observable universe (a theoretical level).

## Earth's Current Status

Earth currently ranks at about **0.73** on the Kardashev Scale, which makes it a subtotal Type I civilization. Humanity does not yet have full control of its planet's energy, since large-scale mastery of weather, geothermal and tidal energy is still beyond current capability.

## Implications for UAP Research

The Kardashev Scale matters to UAP research because it gives a framework for thinking about what non-human intelligences might be able to do. A Type II or Type III civilization would have technology so far beyond human capability that the phenomena ascribed to UAP, such as transmedium travel, instantaneous acceleration, gravity manipulation and physical invisibility, would be trivial for it. The scale helps explain why researchers consider that the technological gap between humanity and any visiting civilization could be vast.
