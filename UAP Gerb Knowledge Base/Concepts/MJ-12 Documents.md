The MJ-12 Documents, also called the Majestic documents, are a body of alleged classified US government papers about [[Majestic 12]], the purported committee formed in 1947 to recover and study crashed UFOs and their occupants. They were leaked to UFO researchers between 1984 and 1999. [[Ryan S. Wood]] counts about 3,500 pages over 19 years from seven different sources. Most date from the 1940s and 1950s and are concentrated in 1947–1954. They have been contested since the first leak: the FBI declared the first document bogus, and professional sceptics such as Philip Klass tried to debunk them. Researchers including [[Stanton Friedman]], Ryan Wood and [[Robert Wood]] have argued for the authenticity of the core documents.

## Sources of the leaks

- **[[Jaime Shandera]]** received the [[Eisenhower Briefing Document]] on undeveloped film in December 1984.
- **[[Don Berliner]]** received the [[Special Operations Manual (SOM 1-01)]] on undeveloped 35 mm Tri-X film in 1994.
- **[[Tim Cooper]]** received the largest share. It arrived by mailings from Sacramento, California; by drops into his mailbox without postage; by mailings from the Freedom of Information Act office at Fort Meade, Maryland (including the [[Bowen Manuscript]]); and as documents marked "-1" and "-2" in the corner. Cooper's sources included the pseudonymous [[Thomas Cantwheel]] (from 1992), his alleged daughter "Selena", and "Source One", who supplied the Burned Memo in 1999.

## Principal documents

- **[[Eisenhower Briefing Document]]** (dated 18 November 1952): a briefing for President-elect Eisenhower listing the twelve members and describing Roswell and a 1950 crash near El Indio, Texas.
- **[[Special Operations Manual (SOM 1-01)]]** (1954): MJ-12's field manual for crash recovery, with craft and entity descriptions and a table routing recovered material to named facilities.
- **IPU Field Order and IPU Report** (public from 1995): documents of the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] describing the Roswell recovery, including a claim that part of it was shared with "representative John F. Kennedy".
- **Cutler–Twining memo** (1954): a memo from Robert Cutler of the National Security Council referring to an "NSC/MJ-12 Special Studies Project", which Wood describes as original onion-skin paper in a Washington archive.
- **White Hot Report** (1947): the report of the Roswell investigation team, whose mission list includes [[Leslie Groves]], [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]], Theodore von Kármán, James Doolittle and Professor [[Einstein]].
- **[[Bowen Manuscript]]**: a 1960 UFO encyclopedia returned through FOIA in 1999 with annotations attributed to [[Vannevar Bush]].
- **The Burned Memo**: allegedly pulled from a fire, from MJ-1 to MJ-2 through MJ-7, saying that "Lancer" ([[John F. Kennedy]]) had made inquiries "which we cannot allow".
- **The Aquarius document**: brought forward by [[Lee Graham]] and allegedly connected to the Carter administration. Wood places it in his "neutral basket" as "informed fluff" with nothing checkable.

## Authentication debate

Ryan and Robert Wood give every document a weighted authenticity rating based on firsthand witnesses, forensic analysis where possible, and the source. They rate the Eisenhower Briefing Document, SOM 1-01 and the IPU documents highest. Their evidence includes period printing and spelling in SOM 1-01, personnel on Kirtland Air Force Base who match the manual's control initials, and government acknowledgement of the IPU through FOIA. They also cite appointment dates in [[Harry Truman]]'s records and archival documents bearing the MJ-12 designation. Wood says he has "no credible evidence" that any document listed on the Woods' Majestic Documents website is fake. Critics include Jan Aldrich, whose 1996 critique targets SOM 1-01, and [[John B. Alexander]], who says MJ-12 was real but "had absolutely nothing to do with UFOs".

UAP Gerb and Wood note that documents dated after 1954 carry far less operational detail. They theorise that the atomic energy framework of that year moved the subject out of the Air Force officers' hands, which may explain both the gap and the motive to leak.
