AWACS (Airborne Warning and Control System) is the name for military aircraft fitted with long-range radar and command-and-control systems. They give the units in their network aerial surveillance, battle management and tactical command. The best-known AWACS aircraft is the U.S. Air Force E-3 Sentry, a flying command center that can detect and track airborne, maritime and ground targets and direct operations against them.

## Role in UAP Intercepts

AWACS aircraft act as "eyes in the sky" for fighter units. Their radar reaches farther than the sensors on any single fighter, and their crews direct intercepts of unidentified or hostile contacts. In a UAP encounter, an AWACS aircraft would typically be the first to pick up anomalous radar returns, and it would then send fighters to intercept the unknown object and identify it by sight.

## Alleged Leaked Cockpit Communications

A communications log, allegedly a leaked classified military document, surfaced online in 2021-2022 and is purportedly connected to the [[Flyby Footage]]. It describes a UAP intercept directed by an AWACS aircraft using the call sign "Brigam." In the log, the AWACS sends a fighter (call sign "Club 11") to intercept a radar contact ("bogey") traveling at 2,820 knots. The fighter crew switches on electronic countermeasures and gets a missile lock or active radar tracking alert. They eventually see an object off their starboard wing, approximately 10-15 feet from the canopy, which matches what the flyby footage shows.

The log shows how AWACS aircraft run an intercept, giving fighter crews target vectors and instructions. Its detailed use of NATO brevity codes and operational procedures lends it some credibility, though where the document came from has not been verified.

## Tactical Command Functions

AWACS aircraft typically handle these tasks in a UAP encounter:
- Long-range radar detection: finding contacts more than 200 nautical miles away
- Track correlation: telling multiple contacts apart and giving precise targeting data
- Intercept vectoring: steering fighters to the best position for an intercept
- Battle management: coordinating multiple assets and communication channels
- Threat assessment: judging whether an unknown contact may be hostile

If the alleged communications log is authentic, at least one documented UAP encounter followed standard military intercept procedures with AWACS coordination. That would suggest that the phenomenon has been handled with conventional air defense protocols.
