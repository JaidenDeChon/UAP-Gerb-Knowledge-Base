AWACS (Airborne Warning and Control System) refers to specialized military aircraft equipped with long-range radar and command-and-control systems that provide aerial surveillance, battle management, and tactical command services to units within their operational network. The most well-known AWACS platform is the U.S. Air Force E-3 Sentry, which functions as a flying command center capable of detecting, tracking, and directing operations against airborne, maritime, and ground targets.

## Role in UAP Intercepts

AWACS aircraft serve as "eyes in the sky" for fighter units, providing radar coverage beyond the range of individual aircraft sensors and directing intercepts of unidentified or hostile contacts. In the context of UAP encounters, AWACS platforms would typically be the first to detect anomalous radar returns and would direct fighter aircraft to visually identify and intercept the unknown object.

## Alleged Leaked Cockpit Communications

An alleged leaked classified military communications log that surfaced online in 2021-2022, purportedly connected to the [[Flyby Footage]], details a UAP intercept sequence directed by an AWACS platform using the call sign "Brigam." In the log, the AWACS directs a fighter aircraft (call sign "Club 11") to intercept a radar contact ("bogey") traveling at 2,820 knots. The log describes the fighter crew activating electronic countermeasures, experiencing a missile lock or active radar tracking alert, and eventually observing an object on their starboard wing approximately 10-15 feet from the canopy— matching the visual characteristics of the flyby footage.

The log demonstrates the operational command structure in which AWACS platforms manage intercept operations, providing target vectors and instructions to fighter crews. The detailed use of NATO brevity codes and operational procedures in the log lends some credibility to its authenticity, though the document's provenance remains unverified.

## Tactical Command Functions

AWACS platforms typically perform the following functions relevant to UAP encounters:
- **Long-range radar detection**: Identifying contacts at distances exceeding 200 nautical miles
- **Track correlation**: Distinguishing multiple contacts and providing precise targeting data
- **Intercept vectoring**: Directing fighter aircraft to optimal intercept positions
- **Battle management**: Coordinating multiple assets and communication channels
- **Threat assessment**: Evaluating unknown contacts for potential hostility

If the alleged communications log is authentic, it demonstrates that at least one documented UAP encounter involved standard military intercept procedures with AWACS coordination, suggesting the phenomenon has been engaged using conventional air defense protocols.
