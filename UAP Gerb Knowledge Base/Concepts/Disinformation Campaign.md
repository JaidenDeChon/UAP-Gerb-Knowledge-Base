A disinformation campaign is a coordinated effort to spread deliberately false, misleading, or manipulative information to shape public or institutional beliefs. In UAP research, the term disinformation campaign most frequently refers to the series of US government UFO investigation programs — [[Project Sign]], [[Project Grudge]], [[Project Blue Book]], the [[Robertson Panel]], and the [[Condon Committee]] — that appeared to be objective scientific inquiries but served, according to participants and later analysis, as public relations operations to suppress interest in the UFO phenomenon while covert research continued.

## The UFO Disinformation Architecture (1948–1969)

Whistleblower [[David Grusch]] referenced "sophisticated disinformation campaigns" in his Congressional testimony, which UAP researchers interpret as referring directly to the five major Air Force and government programs that collectively created the [[UFO Stigma]].

[[J. Allen Hynek]], who served as scientific adviser to [[Project Blue Book]] for its entire operational period, stated that Grudge was "less science and more of a public relations campaign" and that Blue Book "was not a scientific project." The [[Robertson Panel]]'s chairman [[Howard P. Robertson]] instructed panel members before the first meeting to debunk all UFO reports presented to them, according to panel signatory [[Thoron L. Page]]. [[Robert J. Low]]'s internal Condon Committee memo explicitly outlined a trick: "describe the project so that to the public it would appear a totally objective study but to the scientific community would present the image of a group of non-believers trying their best to be objective but having an almost zero expectation of finding a saucer."

These programs were effective. By 1969, when Project Blue Book was shut down on the basis of the [[Condon Report]], the US government could state publicly that UFOs did not exist and were not being studied — while classified research continued in parallel programs.

## Other UAP Disinformation Contexts

Disinformation has appeared in multiple contexts within UAP history:

- **Soviet misdirection theory**: Some researchers, including Ryan Wood citing Bob Alexander, have proposed that the [[SOM 1-01]] manual was deliberately leaked to Soviet spies to misdirect resources toward studying UAP crash recoveries
- **Individual witness manipulation**: Cases like [[Paul Bennewitz]], who was systematically fed false information by intelligence operatives to discredit him, represent targeted disinformation
- **AARO report inaccuracies**: UAP researchers have accused [[AARO]] of deliberate factual inaccuracies in its report on cases like [[Michael Herrera]]'s testimony, suggesting purposeful disinformation rather than mere error
- **Kecksburg insider concerns**: In the Kecksburg case, alleged insiders warned of deliberate spread of false information within UAP research circles to muddy the waters
