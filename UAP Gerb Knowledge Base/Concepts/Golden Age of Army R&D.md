The "golden age" of Army research and development is retired Lieutenant Colonel [[Philip J. Corso]]'s name for the years from 1958 to 1963, when Lieutenant General [[Arthur Trudeau]] ran Army R&D. Corso set it against the "dark ages" of 1947 to 1958, which he described as a disorganized period in which the Army lost ground on several fronts. Gerb takes the term from Corso's manuscript *Dawn of a New Age* and uses it for the period in which he believes the UFO legacy programs began actively reverse-engineering recovered technology instead of only collecting and studying it.

## Corso's account

By 1958, in Corso's telling, the Army had lost its Project Horizon moon base plan when [[NASA]] was founded, had seen atomic energy handed to the [[Atomic Energy Commission]], and often clashed with the new Advanced Research Projects Agency ([[DARPA|ARPA]]). That year all Army R&D was brought under one office, the Office of the Chief of Army R&D, headed by Trudeau, who controlled its personnel, technical services, laboratories, installations and budget. Even then, Corso said, the Army faced civilian control of space, opponents inside the Department of Defense and hostility from the CIA and State Department. That is why, in his account, anything touching on areas "out of this world" was held by a few people and tightly compartmented.

The golden age is the setting for Corso's main claims. In 1960 Trudeau made him chief of the [[U.S. Army Foreign Technology Division]], and in 1961 gave him the [[Roswell File]]. Corso said the division passed recovered material to industry partners such as [[Bell Labs]] and [[Sperry Rand]], to Army laboratories and to universities, and that this led to advances including [[Passive Night Vision Technology|passive night vision]] and fiber optics.

## Gerb's use of the term

In his Corso video, Gerb checks the account against an Army R&D primer. It confirms that Trudeau's office set up the Army Research Office in 1958 as the Army's single point of contact for civilian scientists, and that the Chief of R&D was given a parallel line of authority over technical services. Gerb infers that the Foreign Technology Division could have run alongside the Army Research Office, out of public and congressional view. The primer also records the Army's early use of university research, in-house laboratory research and [[Independent Research and Development (IRAD)|independent research and development]], which Gerb ties to the later growth of university-affiliated research centers and [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)|FFRDCs]].

Two other videos widen the idea beyond the Army:

- His timeline video, *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering*, calls 1958 to 1963 "the golden age of legacy R&D", when the effort to reverse engineer UFO technology began. It sets Corso's account beside the founding of the [[MITRE Corporation]] in 1958 and of the [[Aerospace Corporation]], spun off from [[TRW]] in 1960, and the founding of the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]] in 1963. Gerb says he does not think this work was confined to the Army.
- His first Northrop Grumman video dates the golden age to 1958–1962. It ties the founding of MITRE, [[BDM International|BDM]] and the Aerospace Corporation to this period, and argues that some of the companies Northrop later bought already had legacy programs rooted in it.

## Background

Trudeau took charge of Army research and development in February 1958 and held the post until he retired from the Army on 30 June 1962.
