An airfoil or triangular craft is a type of unidentified aerial vehicle that several witnesses describe as an isosceles triangle, with its longest side measuring about 300 feet. Disc-shaped and egg/bell-shaped craft appear in a much wider range of UAP accounts, and triangular craft are considered a comparatively rare type. Researchers therefore treat sightings of triangular objects as a possible sign of a more recently recovered or less common non-human vehicle type, or else of classified airframes reverse-engineered from such vehicles.

## Description

The isosceles profile sets the airfoil-type triangular craft apart from the equilateral triangular craft described in accounts such as [[Borland's Triangular Craft Sighting]] at [[Langley Air Force Base]]. The two shapes are treated as possibly distinct vehicle types rather than variants of one design, because their different geometry implies different aerodynamic or propulsion design principles.

At about 300 feet on its longest side, a craft of this size would be much larger than any conventional fixed-wing military aircraft. Witnesses and researchers set these objects apart from conventional military platforms because of that size together with the silent flight typical of UAP accounts, with no audible propulsion and no rotor wash.

## Rarity and Significance

Triangular craft are reported less often than disc or ovoid craft, and this rarity is cited as a reason to treat the triangular form as possibly newer technology. One interpretation in the UAP research community is that if triangular craft are recovered non-human vehicles, fewer of them were recovered, which would explain why sightings are rarer. A competing interpretation holds that triangular craft are human-built, reverse-engineered derivatives, consistent with the TR-3B-type craft discussed in declassified AATIP research themes, and that their rarity reflects limited production numbers under classified programs.

The "Hidden Wing" investigation by UAP Gerb treats the triangular shape, whether in the equilateral form (Borland's Langley sighting) or the isosceles airfoil form, as one of the main vehicle signatures associated with the alleged [[Hidden Wing]] portfolio of Air Force test and evaluation programs at the Western Ranges.
