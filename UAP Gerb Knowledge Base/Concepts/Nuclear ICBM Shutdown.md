Nuclear ICBM shutdown describes reported incidents in which unidentified aerial phenomena made the guidance and control systems of Intercontinental Ballistic Missiles ([[ICBM (Intercontinental Ballistic Missile)|ICBMs]]) fail all at once, leaving the missiles unable to launch. The most formally documented case is the [[Malmstrom Air Force Base UFO Incident]] of March 24, 1967. All 10 Minuteman missiles assigned to a single underground launch control facility at [[Malmstrom Air Force Base]], Montana, failed at the same moment while a pulsating red oval craft hovered over the base's front gate.

## The Malmstrom Case

Lieutenant [[Robert Salas]], the on-duty commander of the launch control facility, received reports of an unusual craft that did not fly like an aircraft: it moved at high speed, changed direction abruptly and made no engine noise. Within minutes came a report of a glowing red oval object, approximately 30–40 feet in diameter, hovering directly over the base's front gate while armed security personnel stood outside. At the same time, the indicators for all 10 Minuteman missiles showed a red fault status, meaning guidance and control failure, with no conventional explanation. The craft then left. The missiles stayed inoperable for the rest of the night but showed no permanent damage.

The incident was classified Secret by the Air Force Office of Security and Intelligence. Four personnel eventually submitted sworn affidavits confirming the event: Salas, missile targeting officer Robert Jamison, Airman First Class Patrick McDonough, and communications officer Dwynne Arneson.

## Significance

All 10 missiles in a single launch control facility failed at once with no mechanical cause, which implies a capability to affect nuclear electronics remotely and selectively. The red fault status, a failure of the guidance and control system, is specifically what prevented launch: the missiles were physically intact but electronically non-functional. Researchers interpret this pattern as evidence of deliberate UAP interference with nuclear deterrent capability rather than incidental electromagnetic effects. No Air Force investigation produced an explanation.

## Related Cases

The 1964 [[Vandenberg Air Force Base UFO Film Incident]] documents an earlier and more visually explicit form of nuclear interference, in which a craft fired directed energy beams at a dummy warhead during an ICBM test flight. Together, these cases form the core of the [[UFO Interference with Nuclear Weapons]] pattern and the broader [[UFO-Nuclear Connection]].
