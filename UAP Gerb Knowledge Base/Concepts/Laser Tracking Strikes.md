"Laser tracking strikes" is the cover story that Major [[Florence J. Mansman]] ordered Lieutenant [[Robert Jacobs]] to use if anyone ever asked him about the beams of light filmed during the September 14, 1964 ICBM test at [[Vandenberg Air Force Base]], an event known as the [[Vandenberg Air Force Base UFO Film Incident]]. Jacobs said the explanation was technically impossible. In 1964, lasers were still at an early experimental stage and had no operational use in missile tracking or targeting.

## Context

Government agents confiscated the film, which showed a disc-shaped craft firing beams at a dummy nuclear warhead. Afterwards, Mansman met with Jacobs and told him to attribute any future question about what was observed to "laser tracking strikes." The term was chosen to sound like a plausible technical explanation to anyone who did not know the state of laser technology in 1964, giving an official, prosaic alternative to UFO interaction.

Jacobs later noted that operational laser tracking did not exist in 1964, and that the premise also contradicted itself. A laser tracking system would not produce what the film documented: four beam impacts, one after another, from four different approach vectors.

## Significance

The laser tracking cover story is one of the clearest documented examples of the US government fabricating a prosaic technical explanation for filmed UFO evidence. Other cover explanations known from UAP cases have blamed UAP sightings on weather balloons, Venus or aircraft, and this one is similar, though its false technical detail is unusually specific. The choice of "laser tracking" as a cover term shows an awareness that the beam footage needed an explanation that would seem plausible to a general audience without technical knowledge of laser engineering.
