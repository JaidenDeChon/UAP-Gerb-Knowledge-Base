NRO Sentient is an intelligence collection and analysis program, driven by artificial intelligence, that the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)]] runs. Development reportedly began in 2010, and the core infrastructure was built by 2016. The program is designed to task multiple satellite collection streams and correlate their data autonomously. It provides near-real-time analysis and lets the NRO process far more overhead intelligence than traditional work by analysts allows. Its capabilities include autonomous satellite retasking, cross-sensor data fusion and predictive collection scheduling.

Sentient matters to UAP research because of its alleged role as the main intelligence resource for the [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)]] USAP, which was established in 2017 specifically to surveil non-human craft and derivative technologies. UAP researchers treat the timing as no coincidence: Sentient's buildout was completed in 2016 and IMCON was established in 2017. The Matthew Brown [[Immaculate Constellation Report|report on IMCON]] does not name Sentient or the NRO; the link is Gerb's. He argues that an AI system able to correlate overhead imagery across the entire NRO constellation on its own would be uniquely suited to tracking UAP events, and to generating the intelligence that feeds both IMCON and alleged crash retrieval tasking.

## Public Record

Sentient's existence became public through Freedom of Information Act releases obtained by John Greenewald of [[The Black Vault]]. The NRO describes it as an ongoing research and development program that combines problem-centric intelligence, end-to-end multi-INT and trusted machine automation to transform the collection tasking, processing, exploitation and dissemination (TCPED) cycle. Former NGA director [[Robert Cardillo]] has said it uses "automated inferencing" to aid collection.

A briefing dated 6 May 2021, [[Recent Sentient Highlights]], which the NRO approved for release on 2 June 2022, reports that Sentient's image processing detected a possible airborne object, "small (<10m)", about 78 km southeast of a redacted location at 0038 Zulu that day. The object "did not match the visual signature of typical aircraft detections" and did "vaguely resemble similar detections of airborne objects by US Navy aircraft and surface vessels". The briefing noted "a rough similarity to the previously-reported 'tic tac' shape" and judged the object "likely not a sensor artifact or focal plane anomaly", while asking for more imagery analysis and calling its confidence in the detection "relatively low". In 2021 the NRO also contributed to the ODNI's report on the UAP Task Force, although it had stated in 2015 that UFOs were outside the scope of its responsibilities and that it held no records.

## UAP Gerb's Thesis

Gerb argues that raw overhead data is encoded into imagery before analysts see it, and that systems such as Sentient likely identify anomalies, such as UFOs or crash sites, and strip them from ordinary analysts' feeds so they go to personnel with higher clearances. He suggests this explains why rank-and-file NRO and NGA analysts have not reported seeing UFOs. He also wonders whether the 2021 Sentient detection is the same tic-tac over US Southern Command that the Immaculate Constellation report describes.
