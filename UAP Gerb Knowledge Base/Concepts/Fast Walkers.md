**Fast Walkers** is a military term used by [[NORAD]], [[US Northern Command]], and [[US Space Force]] for objects that space-based sensors detect entering or leaving Earth's atmosphere at extreme speeds. These objects move too fast against the background imagery to be located within the atmosphere. The term is separate from "UFO" or "UAP," which usually refer to atmospheric phenomena, and it lets military organizations classify and withhold detection data under a different name.

## Technical Definition

According to official US Air Force research papers:

> "Satellite based sensors looking down at the Earth's surface occasionally observe reflected light from an object passing through the image which is moving too fast relative to the background of the image to be located within the atmosphere. These objects are commonly called Fast Walkers."  
> — [[Bradley R. Townsend]], USAF Captain, 2008

Fast Walkers are detected mainly by [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] infrared satellites in geosynchronous orbit, about 22,000 miles above Earth. Ground-based sensors cannot track these objects; only space-based systems can detect them.

## Detection History

Ballistic missile early warning satellites (DSP) have systematically recorded data on Fast Walkers since 1972. Both [[Richard P. Osedacz]]'s 1989 paper and [[Bradley R. Townsend]]'s 2008 study confirm that these objects have been detected and cataloged continuously for decades.

The 1989 Osedacz paper noted that Fast Walker analysis had become "an increasingly important issue due to the increase in geosynchronous satellites which detect the objects while ground-based sensors cannot."

## Course Corrections and Controlled Flight

Many Fast Walker detections show evidence of controlled flight instead of a ballistic trajectory, and this is one of their defining characteristics. [[Bob Fish]] described a DSP detection of an object that entered Earth's atmosphere from deep space and then made a 30-degree course correction turn in mid-flight. The turn showed it was "under some sort of control" and not following a meteor's ballistic path.

Controlled maneuvering of this kind sets Fast Walkers apart from meteors, space debris and satellites, which follow predictable orbital mechanics.

## Notable Detection Cases

### Bob Fish Incident (1980s-1990s)
A DSP team member described to [[Bob Fish]] in [[El Segundo, California]] a Fast Walker that entered from deep space, passed close to the DSP satellite, and made a 30-degree course correction, which Fish took as a sign of controlled flight.

### 1984 Indian Ocean Case
[[Joe Stefula]] relayed an anonymous source's account of a May 5, 1984 DSP detection of an object traveling at 22,000 mph that came within 1.8 miles of the satellite, changed course, and returned to outer space.

### 1976 Tehran UFO Incident
[[Lee Graham]] and [[Ron Regehr]] said a DSP printout recorded an anomalous object during the Tehran encounter, with 238 scans (about 39.7 minutes of tracking).

## Slow Walkers

The military also uses the term "Slow Walkers" for objects entering or leaving the atmosphere at lower speeds. Both terms file objects passing through space under technical military names that are separate from "UFO" or "UAP."

## Classification and Secrecy

Every FOIA request for Fast Walker data has been denied on national security grounds under Executive Order 13526:
- 1979: [[NORAD]] told [[Barry J. Greenwood]] and [[Lawrence Fawcett]] processing their request would cost $155,000
- 2013: [[US Northern Command]] classified it as "critical to National Defense"
- 2023: [[US Space Force]] issued a final denial under FOIA Exception One

These denials show that Fast Walker data is among the most protected information held by US military space commands.

## Significance

The Fast Walker designation is an official military acknowledgment that satellites routinely detect objects that:
1. Move too fast to be atmospheric phenomena
2. Often exhibit controlled flight characteristics (course corrections)
3. Cannot be tracked by ground-based sensors
4. Have been detected continuously since at least 1972
5. Originate from or depart into deep space

Using a specialized term lets the data stay classified and avoids disclosure under standard UFO/UAP FOIA requests.
