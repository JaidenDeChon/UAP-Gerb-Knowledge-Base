A Rapid Reaction Recovery Unit is a hypothesized standing military and scientific task force, maintained by the US government specifically to retrieve and contain downed unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) and associated materials quickly. The idea recurs in decades of UFO crash retrieval testimony. Witnesses consistently describe specialized, organized teams that reach crash sites with remarkable speed and work with operational precision.

## Operational Model

Drawing on several crash retrieval testimonies, Gerb has outlined a theoretical model of how such a unit would operate:

1. Intelligence: [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites managed by the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)]], ground-based radar, or both detect UAP crashes or anomalous signatures.
2. Scientific response: [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|DOE NEST]] flies in a rapid scientific team on its own jets (such as Cessna Citation 2s) for radiological assessment and containment.
3. Armed security: [[JSOC (Joint Special Operations Command)|JSOC]] or regional special forces (such as the [[US Army 7th Special Forces Group]]) provide armed enforcement on site.
4. Logistics: the CIA's [[CIA Office of Global Access|Office of Global Access]] coordinates the operation's logistics.
5. Transport: military aviation units provide helicopter transport, for example CH-47s of the [[1st Battalion 228th Aviation Regiment]] or the [[160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment|160th SOAR]].

## Historical Evidence

The concept appears in many alleged incidents:

- 1953, Kingman, Arizona: a rapid response USAF unit was reportedly first on the scene.
- 1965, Kecksburg, Pennsylvania: USAF "Blue Berets" were allegedly on site within hours, and witnesses reported men in "moon suits" carrying equipment to the crash site.
- 1974, Coyame, Mexico: the CIA mobilized a rapid recovery unit from Fort Bliss, Texas, which arrived in four unmarked helicopters (UH-1 Hueys and CH-53 Super Stallions) with hazmat equipment.
- 1997, Peru: [[Jonathan Weygandt]] described a [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|NEST]] team that deployed within approximately 8–9 hours, arriving in CH-47 Chinooks with approximately 30 personnel in biological containment gear.
- Blue Boys: the researcher [[Leonard Stringfield]] documented these rapid retrieval and transport helicopter units.

The deployment times are consistent across these cases, which suggests existing protocols and possibly pre-positioned assets dedicated to crash retrieval. For the Peru case, Gerb calculated that the NEST team could have deployed from [[Sandia National Laboratories]] to [[Iquitos, Peru]] in approximately 8 hours of total travel time.
