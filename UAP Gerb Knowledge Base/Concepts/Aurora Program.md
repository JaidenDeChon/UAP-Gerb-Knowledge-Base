The Aurora Program is an alleged classified US aerospace program under which multiple advanced reconnaissance and strike aircraft were developed, including the [[TR-3B]] Astra, the TR-3A, and possibly the TR-3E. The program name first appeared in unclassified DOD procurement documents, where the government planned to spend $80 million in fiscal year 1986 and $2.3 billion in fiscal year 1987 — a 25-fold increase that at the time was double the cost of the B-2 Spirit bomber program.

## Known and Alleged Aircraft

According to [[Edgar Fouché]], the Aurora umbrella encompassed several craft in the Tactical Reconnaissance (TR) line:

- **TR-3B Astra** — The gravity-warping reverse-engineered triangle, code-named Astra (Advanced Stealth Technology Reconnaissance Aircraft). The most exotic platform in the program.
- **TR-3A** — A more conventional delta-shaped fighter featuring a cockpit and pulse detonation engines, possibly with [[Electrogravitics]] for internal mass reduction. Often confused with the TR-3B in public discourse. Possibly the craft that crashed at [[Boscombe Down Incident|Boscombe Down]] in 1994.
- **Pulsar** — The pulse detonation unmanned "pumpkin seed" craft observed by [[Brad Sorenson]] at the 1988 Norton AFB exhibit.
- **SR-33A** — Introduced by Dr. Michael Wolf, an alleged UAP consultant to the National Security Council. Allegedly utilized conventional fuel and anti-gravity field propulsion under a secret space program.

Fouché stated that the TR line (Tactical Reconnaissance) was analogous to the SR line (Strategic Reconnaissance, e.g., SR-71), and that concepts for the SR-74 and SR-75 were also in development during his service.

## Funding

[[Edgar Fouché]] claimed the Aurora Program was substantially funded through [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)]] appropriations under the Reagan administration, with up to 35% of SDI money allegedly siphoned to support USAF black programs beginning in 1982. [[Brad Sorenson]] was told at the 1988 Norton AFB exhibit that program officials were requesting additional Congressional funding to build more reverse-engineered craft.
