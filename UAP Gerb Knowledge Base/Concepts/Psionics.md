Psionics is the theoretical or alleged use of the mind or consciousness to operate technology, communicate, or act on physical systems. In UAP research, the term names the hypothesis that non-human intelligences use thought or consciousness, instead of mechanical controls, to fly their craft, communicate, and interact with humans.

## Evidence in UAP Testimony

The idea appears in testimony from more than seven decades:

- **[[Jonathan Weygandt]] (2000):** During the 1997 [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]], Weygandt reported sensing a telepathic presence from the occupants of a crashed [[Egg-shaped UAP|egg-shaped craft]]. He compared the sensation to an AM radio station turned to static at high volume. He felt the beings projecting thoughts that they meant no harm and were asking for help, and he received mental images of what they looked like: tall gray-type entities, 8–10 feet, with elongated oblong heads. These experiences led him to believe the craft was controlled through consciousness. Since the event he has had recurring mental intrusions, including dreams of the entities.

- **[[Philip J. Corso]] (1950s era):** Corso described UAP controlled through consciousness, headband transceivers, or other unusual non-mechanical controls, going back to the 1950s.

- **[[Jake Barber]] and [[Skywatchers]] (2025):** Testimony from Barber and discussion within the Skywatchers community made psionics a major topic in UAP discourse. Both describe interacting with craft and non-human entities through consciousness.

## Significance

Weygandt gave his testimony in 2000, before psionics became a prominent topic in UAP discourse. It is therefore early, independent evidence of consciousness-based interaction with non-human technology, and it carries particular weight because it predates the current wave of psionic claims.
