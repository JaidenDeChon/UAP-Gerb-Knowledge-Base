NATO brevity language (also known as multiservice tactical brevity codes) is a standardized set of voice communication codes that NATO forces and U.S. military branches use to pass tactical information quickly and clearly in combat. Its short, unambiguous terms describe complex tactical situations in a few words, which cuts the time spent on the radio and lowers the chance of miscommunication under stress.

## Application in UAP Encounters

An alleged leaked log of classified military communications surfaced online in 2021-2022 and is purportedly connected to the [[Flyby Footage]]. It describes a UAP intercept and uses NATO brevity language throughout. Terms in the alleged log include:

- **Bogey**: An unidentified radar contact, not yet confirmed as hostile
- **Bogey dope**: Request for bearing, range, and altitude of an unidentified contact
- **Merge**: Two radar contacts that overlap at the current resolution and appear as one
- **Angels**: Altitude in thousands of feet
- **Vector**: Heading and altitude instructions
- **Stand by**: Wait for further instructions
- **Blue envelope**: Probably refers to specific mission control procedures or weapons safety protocols

## Authenticity Indicators

The alleged log uses authentic NATO brevity codes, proper operational sequencing and technically accurate terminology, and this has been cited as evidence that it is genuine. The person who posted the document to 4chan (if the attribution is accurate) was familiar with military radio procedures and pilot lingo, knowledge that would require either direct military experience or extensive study of operational communications protocols.

However, NATO brevity code references are publicly available, so a sufficiently motivated hoaxer could write plausible-sounding communications. The document's authenticity remains unverified.

## Operational Context

If the alleged log is authentic, at least one documented UAP encounter involved:
- Coordination between an [[AWACS (Airborne Warning and Control System)|AWACS]] platform and a fighter aircraft
- Electronic countermeasures activation or missile lock alerts
- Visual confirmation of an object at extremely close range (10-15 feet from the canopy)
- Specific instructions regarding weapons status and mission procedures ("open your blue envelopes and snooze")

The use of standard tactical communications procedures in a UAP encounter suggests that the phenomenon has been met with conventional military intercept protocols, and that such encounters may be categorized and handled within existing air defense frameworks.
