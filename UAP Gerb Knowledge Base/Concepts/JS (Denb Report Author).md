**JS** is the unidentified author, or authoring entity, of the [[Denb Report]], the main source document for the [[1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval]] case. Who JS is, what organization JS belonged to, and what JS intended are all unknown, and this is one of the most puzzling parts of the Coyame case.

## The Document

The Denb Report is dated March 23, 1992, and is addressed "to all Denb team members" from "JS." Its designation, "File UFO 3263," suggests it is one of many UAP cases studied by the [[DENB Team]]. The report gives a minute-by-minute operational account of the alleged 1974 crash retrieval. It covers radar tracking data, communications intercepts, the assembly of a CIA recovery team, helicopter flight paths, a description of the disc, and the destruction of the site with high explosives.

The report closes with this statement:

> "The facts that are known have been gathered from two eyewitness accounts, documentation illegally copied, and a partially destroyed document. This was done in 1978 by a person who is now dead. Only in February of this year [1992] did the notes and documents come into the hands of our group."

## Identity Hypotheses

### Acronym Hypothesis

Researchers [[Noe Torres]] and [[Ruben Uriarte]] propose that "JS" may be an acronym for a department or other entity within the US government. Possible readings include:

- Joint Staff, the military organization that assists the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff
- Joint Services, a designation for military operations or organizations involving more than one service
- some other government or intelligence acronym, given the report's operational detail and the access to classified information it suggests

### Individual Hypothesis

JS may instead be a person's initials. In that case JS would be someone with direct or indirect access to classified crash retrieval documentation who chose to leak it through early internet message boards and anonymous mailings to UFO researchers. Signing with initials rather than a full name or a pseudonym suggests concern for operational security.

### Disinformation Hypothesis

Some researchers consider whether JS was a disinformation agent or operation meant to seed false information into the UFO research community. Several points weaken this idea:

- [[Leonard Stringfield]] knew independently of a US cross-border retrieval in Chihuahua before the report was released.
- The report uses "authoritatively written" military terminology and operational detail.
- The report clearly separates confirmed facts from speculation.

## Relation to the DENB Team

JS appears to be a member or representative of the [[DENB Team]], the mysterious group the report names as its recipients. If the DENB Team was an investigation group inside the intelligence community that worked through early internet message boards during the "UAP Dark Ages" (1969–2007), JS may have been its designated spokesperson, or an authorized leaker charged with the controlled release of compartmented information.

JS refers to the DENB Team as "our group", which suggests JS is either a member of the group or closely affiliated with it.

## Distribution Method

The Denb Report first appeared on an electronic bulletin board in 1992. In early summer 1993 it was mailed anonymously to researchers including [[Elaine Douglas]] and [[Nick Redfern]]. The use of the new internet first and anonymous post afterwards suggests JS (or the DENB Team) knew both digital and traditional ways of passing on information in secret.

The [[MJ-12 Documents]] were spread the same way in the 1980s, mailed anonymously to researchers. The parallel raises the question of whether JS is a similar insider source.

## Operational Security

The Denb Report's operational detail, including specific timelines, radar data, helicopter types, disc measurements and site destruction protocols, suggests JS had access to one of the following:

1. Primary documentation (intelligence reports, after-action reports, communications intercepts)
2. Direct testimony from participants in the operation
3. Secondary documentation compiled from multiple classified sources

The report warns that documentation was "illegally copied" and that the original compiler is "now dead". These caveats suggest JS is protecting sources and methods while admitting that the leak is sensitive and possibly illegal.

## Unanswered Questions

- Is JS an individual or an organizational acronym?
- What is JS's relationship to the DENB Team?
- Did JS have direct access to classified documentation, or did JS compile information from secondary sources?
- Was JS an active intelligence community member in 1992, or a retired or former member?
- Why was the Coyame case selected for public release via internet message boards?
- Are there other "File UFO" cases compiled by JS and the DENB Team that have not been released?
- Is JS still alive, and does JS have additional documentation on the Coyame case or other crash retrievals?

Unless JS comes forward or is identified, the authenticity and sourcing of the Denb Report cannot be firmly established.
