The **Condon Report**, formally titled *Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects*, was a comprehensive UFO investigation commissioned by the United States Air Force. The University of Colorado carried it out from 1966 to 1968 under the direction of physicist Dr. Edward Condon. Many in UAP research circles believe the report was designed to give scientific legitimacy to dismissing and stigmatizing UFO phenomena, even though it documents extraordinary military encounters that remain unexplained.

## Overview

The Condon Report was conducted under contract to the Air Force, with the explicit goal of giving an independent scientific assessment of UFO phenomena. The final report, published in January 1969, concluded that UFO investigations were not scientifically valuable and recommended that the Air Force terminate [[Project Blue Book]]. The Air Force acted on this quickly and officially disbanded Project Blue Book in December 1969. Gerb's video on [[The Origin of the UFO Stigma]] treats the Condon Report as a central instrument in the institutional suppression of serious UFO inquiry.

## Case 5: The 1957 RB-47 Encounter

Case 5 of the Condon Report documents a 1957 encounter involving a USAF RB-47 reconnaissance aircraft of the 55th Strategic Reconnaissance Wing, flying from Forbes Air Force Base in Topeka, Kansas, on a training mission over the Gulf Coast and Texas. Gerb, reading the case, dates it to 19 September 1957 and says it was "also from [[Carswell Air Force Base]]" in Fort Worth. The Condon study worked from the crew's recollections a decade later, and Blue Book records that James McDonald found afterwards date the flight to 17 July 1957. The case stands out because several different sensors corroborated it. Gerb cites it in his discussion of the [[Del Rio, Texas]] crash cases as evidence of a pattern of high-credibility UAP encounters originating from the same airbase.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the RB-47 was launched from Carswell AFB on 19 September 1957. The aircraft flew from Forbes AFB, Kansas, and the flight is now dated 17 July 1957 (The Black Vault, "Project Blue Book: The RB-47 UFO Incident"; Patrick Gross, "RB-47"); the Carswell link and the September date are Gerb's.

The RB-47 carried electronic countermeasures (ECM) and reconnaissance equipment. A large bright fireball, alternating between red and white, pursued it for more than 600 miles. The flight crew (pilot and co-pilot) saw the object, ground radar detected it (Gerb calls it "Carswell radar"), and the RB-47's onboard ECM equipment registered it, recording a strong radar signal coming from the object at 2,800 megacycles.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said Carswell ground radar detected the object. The aircraft was not based at Carswell, and identifying the ground radar as Carswell's is Gerb's.

Pilot Lewis D. Chase described the encounter: "Before any evasive action could be taken, the light crossed in front of the plane moving to the right at a velocity far higher than airplane speeds. The light was seen by pilot and co-pilot and appeared to the pilot to be a glowing body as big as a barn."

After the aircraft landed, intelligence personnel removed all ECM graphic data and radar records. The original CIA report of the incident is publicly available and corroborates the documented details.

## Relevance to Del Rio Cases

Gerb sees the RB-47 encounter as paralleling [[Robert Willingham]]'s 1955 account in both structure and geography. In his telling both originated from Carswell AFB, though the RB-47 in fact flew from Forbes AFB in Kansas; both involved high-speed luminous objects detected by military radar, and in both, intelligence personnel stepped in to suppress or confiscate evidence. UAP researchers interpret the Condon Report's inclusion of this thoroughly documented case, together with its dismissive overall conclusions in spite of such evidence, as a sign of institutional bad faith in UFO investigation.
