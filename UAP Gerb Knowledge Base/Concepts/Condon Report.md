The **Condon Report**, formally titled *Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects*, was a comprehensive UFO investigation commissioned by the United States Air Force. The University of Colorado carried it out from 1966 to 1968 under the direction of physicist Dr. Edward Condon. Many in UAP research circles believe the report was designed to give scientific legitimacy to dismissing and stigmatizing UFO phenomena, even though it documents extraordinary military encounters that remain unexplained.

## Overview

The Condon Report was conducted under contract to the Air Force, with the explicit goal of giving an independent scientific assessment of UFO phenomena. The final report, published in January 1969, concluded that UFO investigations were not scientifically valuable and recommended that the Air Force terminate [[Project Blue Book]]. The Air Force acted on this quickly and officially disbanded Project Blue Book in December 1969. Gerb's video on [[The Origin of the UFO Stigma]] treats the Condon Report as a central instrument in the institutional suppression of serious UFO inquiry.

## Case 5: The 1957 RB-47 Encounter

Case 5 of the Condon Report documents an encounter on September 19, 1957, involving a USAF RB-47 reconnaissance aircraft launched from [[Carswell Air Force Base]] in Fort Worth, Texas. The case stands out because several different sensors corroborated it. Gerb cites it in his discussion of the [[Del Rio, Texas]] crash cases as evidence of a pattern of high-credibility UAP encounters originating from the same airbase.

The RB-47 carried electronic countermeasures (ECM) and reconnaissance equipment. A large bright fireball, alternating between red and white, pursued it for more than 600 miles. The flight crew (pilot and co-pilot) saw the object, Carswell ground radar detected it, and the RB-47's onboard ECM equipment registered it, recording a strong radar signal coming from the object at 2,800 megacycles.

Pilot Lewis D. Chase described the encounter: "Before any evasive action could be taken, the light crossed in front of the plane moving to the right at a velocity far higher than airplane speeds. The light was seen by pilot and co-pilot and appeared to the pilot to be a glowing body as big as a barn."

After the aircraft landed, intelligence personnel removed all ECM graphic data and radar records. The original CIA report of the incident is publicly available and corroborates the documented details.

## Relevance to Del Rio Cases

The RB-47 encounter parallels [[Robert Willingham]]'s 1955 account in both structure and geography. Both originated from Carswell AFB, both involved high-speed luminous objects detected by military radar, and in both, intelligence personnel stepped in to suppress or confiscate evidence. UAP researchers interpret the Condon Report's inclusion of this thoroughly documented case, together with its dismissive overall conclusions in spite of such evidence, as a sign of institutional bad faith in UFO investigation.
