"Administrative terrorism" is a term [[David Grusch]] and UAP Gerb use for a pattern of alleged reprisal against UAP whistleblowers and prospective whistleblowers. The alleged tactics are non-legal: instead of facing criminal prosecution in the courts, the targets have their careers, reputations, and security clearances damaged. The term rests on the observation that most whistleblower protection statutes formally bar retaliation but do nothing to stop softer forms of institutional pressure that are harder to prove.

## Alleged Tactics and Examples

In his Judicial Watch interview, which UAP Gerb's *Special Access Required Vol.2* cites at length, Grusch described "manufactured allegations" made against him on security and personal-conduct grounds after his 2023 congressional testimony, even though he kept his Top Secret/Sensitive Compartmented Information (TS/SCI) clearance throughout. He said that after he left the [[NGA]], government offices tried to revoke the clearance of his former chief of staff, and that his former boss at the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]] had his own clearance revoked. Grusch described this as part of a broader pattern of "potshotting" people connected to him to find out who might have enabled his disclosures. He also said he has personally known people who have been out of work for years, and a senior figure in the SAP community who has spent years in legal appeals after similar reprisal.

UAP Gerb connects the concept to on-camera statements by whistleblower [[Matt Brown]]. Brown described an unnamed program-protection figure at [[Northrop Grumman]] as a "nexus point for legacy security" and part of a "dirty tricks department" that matches prospective whistleblowers with people able to carry out targeted disinformation and reprisal campaigns. The video presents [[DARPA Security and Intelligence Directorate (SID)|DARPA SID]], [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations|AFOSI]]/PJ, and other alleged "program protection" offices as the institutions capable of running such campaigns.
