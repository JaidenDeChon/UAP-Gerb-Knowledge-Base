The Legacy Program Onion Model is Gerb's conceptual framework for understanding the security architecture of the UFO legacy program as a set of concentric protective layers. The innermost core holds the program's fundamental secrets: recovered non-human technical vehicles, biological occupants, exploitation data and internal program documentation. Successive layers of protection surround the core and hide it from everyone except the most deeply cleared program insiders. The model is attributed both to Gerb's independent analysis and to corroboration from a personal source, who described giving "onion briefings" hundreds of times to senior statesmen, general and flag officers, and special programs personnel.

Gerb quotes the source directly: "I was on the legacy program when I found out there were others". The remark indicates that even people working on legacy programs did not know about other programs running in parallel, at the same time, within other silos.

[[David Grusch]] independently used the phrase "rice bowl" for a similar architecture, in which programs exist as scattered, disconnected grains with no central organizing force. This matches the onion model's description of the fragmented legacy structure after 1994.

## Core

The innermost layer holds the center of UFO legacy activities: the physical craft, biological occupants, exploitation data and reverse-engineered insights, internal program documentation, and the identities of senior program personnel.

## Key Layers (Outer to Inner)

**Disinformation and narrative management:** The outermost skin of the onion is made up of organized efforts to shape public perception of the UFO topic, keep up the stigma against discussing UFOs, and discredit credible witnesses. This includes early Cold War debunking programs such as the [[Robertson Panel]] (1953) and the [[Condon Committee|Condon Committee]] (1969), both of which Gerb alleges were pre-mandated to produce dismissive conclusions. It also includes the modern [[AARO]] (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office) under [[Sean Kirkpatrick]], which is accused of misrepresenting whistleblower testimony and promoting the disputed "Yankee Blue" hazing-program narrative. The layer further covers "partial transparency" efforts, meaning controlled releases of information from within the program. One is the [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|ATIP]] effort fronted by [[Luis Elizondo]] and [[To The Stars Academy]], which Gerb's *Special Access Required Vol.2* argues was a National Security Council cover vehicle championed by [[James Clapper]]. Another is the alleged attempt to position Hillary Clinton as a "disclosure president" in 2016.

**Special Access Program (SAP) structure:** [[Special Access Programs (SAPs)]] provide the formal legal and administrative structure in which legacy programs operate beyond standard classification. The structure is tiered into umbrella SAPs, compartmented sub-SAPs and sub-compartments. Waived Unacknowledged SAPs (USAPs) extend this to programs whose very existence may be withheld from congressional oversight, apart from select notifications. *Special Access Required Vol.2* adds a further refinement: the alleged combination of the non-covert action designation under [[Covert Action Program (50 U.S. Code § 3093)|50 U.S. Code § 3093]] with the [[Content-Only Special Access Program|content-only]] budgetary designation. The video argues that this combination allows legacy White House SAPs to waive both Title 10 and Title 50 reporting requirements at the same time.

**Cover programs and offices:** Programs are nested inside organizational covers that absorb accountability. Examples include SAP umbrellas like Polaris hiding Project Sanddollar, and Air Force "outside activities" hidden under SAF/AA, beyond even SAF/AAZ oversight. Other cover programs also let legacy activities go on while hiding their true nature from oversight personnel.

**Program protection agencies:** Internal units within agencies act as antibodies. They identify and neutralize insider threats (whistleblowers, unauthorized disclosures) as well as outsider threats (congressional inquiries, GAO audits, media investigations). *Special Access Required Vol.2* describes four such offices in detail: the [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations|AFOSI]] Office of Special Projects (PJ), [[DARPA Security and Intelligence Directorate (SID)|DARPA's Security and Intelligence Directorate]] (SID), the [[Air Force Life Cycle Management Center (AFLCMC)|AFLCMC]] Information Protection Directorate (IP), and the DOE's [[Office of Intelligence and Counterintelligence]] (OICI). It names them alongside the DOD and component-level SAPCOs themselves. [[David Grusch]]'s experience during his authorized investigation into crash retrieval programs, including alleged clearance revocations against colleagues, is cited as an example of these antibodies being activated. Gerb calls this pattern "[[Administrative Terrorism]]."

**FFRDC/GOCO layer:** Federally funded research and development centers and government-owned, contractor-operated institutions (particularly DOE NNSA national labs and [[MITRE Corporation]]) put distance between government program administrators and program materials, while the government keeps iron control over deliverables and read-on access. This layer decides what information passes down to the defense industrial base.

**Defense Industrial Base:** Prime contractors and subcontractors receive compartmented pieces of work through carve-out contracts and do not see the full program context. Only specific executive-level positions and special program directors are read into the legacy effort at a functional level.

**Funding and financial concealment:** *Special Access Required Vol.2* describes a further, more minor set of secrecy layers that concern how legacy programs are financed outside normal budget visibility. They include alleged misuse of independent research and development (IRAD) funds; budget "haircuts" taken across unrelated programs and diverted through agencies such as the U.S. Forest Service and Bureau of Land Management; and self-funding cutout companies, historically including [[E-Systems]] and the BSI consulting front used in the [[Yellow Fruit]] program.

## MITRE as an Alleged Originating Template

Gerb's investigation into the [[MITRE Corporation]] cites a source who described MITRE's internal security architecture as the literal origin of the onion model, which was later adopted throughout the wider legacy program apparatus. The source called it "the model for all legacy security personnel." MITRE's alleged apparatus includes a "hidden" Special Security Office, parallel to and separate from its acknowledged SSO and staffed with program security officers who reportedly operate under nonofficial cover (NOC). It also includes personnel instructed to describe themselves as "lollipop manufacturers" if questioned during an audit, and program funding allegedly skimmed from acknowledged MITRE and Navy projects instead of being carried on any visible budget line. Gerb connects this compartmentation model to the "seventh veil" analogy attributed to [[John P. Craven]], chief scientist of the Navy Special Projects Office. Under that analogy, no individual, cleared or not, can be certain whether they have reached a program's innermost layer or are only working a cover program in service of another cover program.

## Historical Evolution

In its earliest form (1947–1950s), the onion model had a compact, unified structure with strong centralized control at the core. The Manhattan Project 2.0's control group, hidden within the NSC 5412 Committee, was a powerful and coherent center that could direct all layers. Over the following decades, regulatory pressure, audit near-misses and organizational fragmentation caused the layers to multiply and the core to weaken. By 1994 the control-group core had been replaced by the SAPOC Senior Review Group, a quasi-government and industry panel of approximately two dozen individuals with no centralized executive authority. By 2009 even this vestigial center had lost its most effective leader.
