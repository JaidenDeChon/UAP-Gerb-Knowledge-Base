**Strategic Reconnaissance (SR)** is military intelligence gathering aimed at information critical to national security and strategic planning. It typically relies on high-altitude surveillance platforms, satellite imaging, signals intelligence and other methods to monitor an adversary's capabilities, weapons systems and force deployments. In space-based surveillance, strategic reconnaissance includes monitoring foreign space activity, missile launches and other objects passing through near-Earth space.

## Notation on DSP Fast Walker Printouts

The designation "**possible SR**" appears on [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellite detection printouts that researchers studied while investigating [[Fast Walkers|Fast Walker]] incidents. In the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]], satellite sensor technicians [[Lee Graham]] and [[Ron Regehr]] found a DSP printout showing 238 scans with the notation "**possible SR**."

[[Brad Sparks]], who also analyzed this data, read "possible SR" as possibly meaning "Strategic Reconnaissance," though the exact meaning of this classification notation has never been officially confirmed.

## Interpretation Ambiguity

The "possible SR" notation can be read in three ways:

1. As foreign reconnaissance: did analysts classify the object as possibly a foreign strategic reconnaissance platform, such as a satellite, a high-altitude aircraft or an advanced surveillance system?
   
2. As an unknown needing assessment: did the notation mean the object itself was unidentified and needed strategic reconnaissance analysis to determine its nature and origin?

3. As a label for the DSP mission: did "SR" mean that the DSP satellite detection itself was part of strategic reconnaissance operations, and so describe the mission rather than the object?

The same classification uncertainty surrounds Fast Walker detections in general. These are objects that don't fit standard threat categories, yet they are detected by systems designed to track hostile missiles and satellites.

## Significance of SR Designation

If "SR" does refer to Strategic Reconnaissance, its appearance on Fast Walker printouts suggests military analysts were attempting to categorize these detections within existing threat frameworks. The "possible" qualifier shows they were unsure: the object had characteristics that might match foreign reconnaissance activity, but it couldn't be definitively classified as such.

This fits [[Bob Fish]]'s observation that a DSP-detected object making a 30-degree course correction was "under some sort of control" but analysts couldn't determine "whether it was manned or just robotic." The objects show controlled flight that suggests intelligence-directed operations, which would be consistent with reconnaissance, but their performance characteristics and origins don't match known foreign capabilities.

## Classification and Compartmentation

The "possible SR" notation on classified DSP printouts indicates that these detections were analyzed at high classification levels, with a specific vocabulary for categorizing them. Graham and Regehr were contractor personnel, and their access to these printouts suggests they worked in compartmented programs with TS/SCI-level access to strategic reconnaissance data.
