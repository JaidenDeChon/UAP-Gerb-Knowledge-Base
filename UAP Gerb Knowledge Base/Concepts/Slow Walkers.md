**Slow Walkers** is a military designation, used alongside [[Fast Walkers]], for objects that space-based sensors detect entering or leaving Earth's atmosphere. They move more slowly than Fast Walkers, but still in ways that do not match atmospheric phenomena or known spacecraft. The term is used for the same classification and FOIA evasion purposes as Fast Walkers: it lets military organizations categorize and withhold space-based UFO detection data under a name other than "UFO" or "UAP."

## Terminology and Classification

The Walker callsign family, made up of Fast Walkers and Slow Walkers, covers objects that enter or leave Earth's atmosphere at variable speeds. Slow Walkers are low velocity objects. Fast Walkers are high velocity objects and the primary focus of detection efforts.

[[NORAD]], [[US Northern Command]] and [[US Space Force]] use both designations to classify objects detected by [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites and other space-based sensor systems.

## Detection and Tracking

Like Fast Walkers, Slow Walkers are detected by infrared sensors on DSP satellites in geosynchronous orbit. Atmospheric UAPs operate only within the atmosphere, while Fast and Slow Walkers transit into or out of it, crossing the boundary between space and atmosphere.

As with Fast Walkers, ground-based sensors typically cannot track Slow Walkers. Only space-based detection systems can monitor these objects as they enter or leave Earth's atmospheric boundary.

## FOIA Denials

All FOIA requests for Slow Walker data have been denied, along with the requests for Fast Walker data. [[John Greenewald]] asked specifically for information on both designations and received blanket denials citing Executive Order 13526 and national security concerns.

Because "UFO/UAP" refers to atmospheric objects and "Fast/Slow Walker" to space-transiting ones, agencies can keep the Walker data classified even as some atmospheric UAP information has been disclosed under pressure.

## Significance

That both Fast and Slow Walker designations exist indicates the military tracks a spectrum of anomalous objects moving between space and Earth's atmosphere at varying speeds. This suggests the phenomenon includes objects with diverse flight characteristics and performance envelopes, with slower objects alongside the ultra-high-velocity targets.

Both designations remain equally classified, which suggests that US military space commands treat all space-transiting unidentified objects as highly sensitive, regardless of speed.
