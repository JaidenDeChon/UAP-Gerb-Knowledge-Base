A dead man switch is a security and disclosure strategy in which a person gives sensitive information to a third party, with explicit instructions to make it public if the person goes missing, is killed or otherwise fails to check in. For UAP whistleblowers, it is meant to deter assassination and to make sure critical information survives even if the whistleblower does not.

## Use in Michael Herrera Case

Before meeting an anonymous insider at a secure facility, whistleblower [[Michael Herrera]] used investigator [[Joey Is Not My Name]] as an informal dead man switch. The arrangement worked like this.

### Information Transfer

Herrera gave Joey:
- The date and time of the meeting with the insider
- The location details (though Joey tracked additional details independently)
- Who was arranging the meeting (it was set up through [[Steven Greer]])
- His worries about his own safety

### Joey's Realization

At first Joey wondered why Herrera was "trusting me with information without any real justification." Then he realized:
> "He was going out to meet this guy and I got the feeling that he was telling me this information in case something happened to him."

Joey asked Herrera directly whether this was the reason, and Herrera confirmed it: "Yeah, if something happens to me while I'm out there, I want you to release all this information."

### Recorded Evidence

To have proof of the arrangement, Joey recorded a phone call with Herrera the day before the meeting without Herrera's knowledge. Herrera was told about the recording later and consented to its existence. Joey explained why he made it:
> "I felt like I needed proof in case I did need to — in case something did happen to him and I couldn't get in touch with him and I needed to reach out to someone to prove that yes, I'm in touch with Michael and these are the things he's told me and now I can't get a hold of him."

On the recording, Herrera says he is afraid of the meeting: "I'm nervous, a little apprehensive... if this is going to get carried any further then I need to speak to the horse's mouth, so to speak."

### Outcome

Herrera came back from the meeting safely, so Joey never had to set off the dead man switch. Afterwards, though, Joey used what Herrera had told him before the meeting, together with his own tracking, to verify that Herrera had in fact been taken to the location he claimed. This lends credibility both to Herrera's account and to the existence of the insider contact.

## Tactical Considerations

### Deterrent Effect

A dead man switch gives hostile actors a reason not to harm the whistleblower. Killing the whistleblower achieves nothing, because the information will be released anyway. Through the Streisand Effect, an assassination would also validate the information and would turn the whistleblower into a martyr. And because the information is held by third parties, a hostile actor would have to identify and neutralize every one of them, which may be impossible if their identities are protected.

### Limitations

The person holding the information must be reliable and willing to follow through. Giving sensitive information to someone else also creates another way for it to leak. It is unclear how long the holder should wait before assuming the whistleblower has been harmed. And the switch deters only lethal action; it does not protect against harassment, imprisonment or psychological operations.

### Alternative Strategies

Other whistleblowers have used variations on the idea:
- Encrypted files given to several parties, with the decryption keys released on the owner's death (for example, [[Julian Assange]]'s insurance files)
- Automated services that require regular check-ins and release the information automatically if one is missed
- Information left in legal custody with attorneys, under protected disclosure

## Historical Context in UAP Disclosure

The dead man switch has become more relevant as UAP whistleblowers face:
- Alleged harassment and intimidation
- Career destruction and security clearance revocation
- Threats against family members
- Mysterious deaths of researchers in the 1990s-2000s

Several witnesses to Michael Herrera's own encounter, his fellow Marines, have refused to come forward publicly. One of them texted: "It's not worth the risk. My family and military career far exceed anything you are asking of me."
