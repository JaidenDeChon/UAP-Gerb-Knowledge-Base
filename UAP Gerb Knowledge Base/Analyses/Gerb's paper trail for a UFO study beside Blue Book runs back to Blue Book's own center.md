A recurring argument in Gerb's videos is that [[Project Blue Book]] was the public face and the serious UFO work happened somewhere else. In UFO Whistleblowers Vol.1 he puts it in his viewers' mouths: "gerb we all know Blue Book was a front you talk about it in every single video you make everyone knows that scientific evidence was compartmented outside of Blue Book". Across his videos he backs it with four documents. He never mentions the office all four point to: the [[Air Force Technical Intelligence Center|Air Technical Intelligence Center]] (ATIC) at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], which ran Blue Book. His larger argument may still be right, but these four documents don't support it.

## The finding

On the wording of its own documents, each of the four channels Gerb presents as running apart from Blue Book leads into ATIC, Blue Book's parent center and chain of command. Two go further. Battelle's report came out under Blue Book's own name, and the 1961 draft ties the 1127th's UFO duty to the regulation Blue Book worked under.

This is a narrow claim. Sending something to ATIC does not prove that Blue Book's small staff saw it: ATIC was a large center, and material could be kept from Blue Book's desk inside it or held above it. The finding is that these documents don't show a separate channel, which is different from showing that none existed.

## The evidence

1. **Project Stork.** In the Sarbacher video Gerb says "atic commission battel for project stor a parallel project to Blue Book not known to the public". In [[Videos/UFOs in the Private Sector - Battelle Memorial Institute/summary|the Battelle video]] he says [[Battelle Memorial Institute|Battelle]] was hired "to separately analyze all Air Force UFO cases away from Bluebook" and that "Batel and atic published" Special Report 14 in May 1955. The report itself is titled *Project Blue Book Special Report No. 14* (scan at The Black Vault; Wikipedia). The 1953 Battelle memo he reads also names [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], "the project sign Grudge and Blue Book director". [[Project Stork]]'s codename was secret, so "not known to the public" holds. "Away from Bluebook" doesn't: Stork worked from Blue Book's case files, and its findings came out under Blue Book's name.
2. **AFOIN-X-SG.** In [[Videos/USO - Unidentified Submerged Objects/summary|the USO video]] Gerb says the 24 April 1952 letter went to ATIC from "afoi n-x SG", "addressed to one Colonel Dunn". He reads the [[AFOIN Special Group (AFOIN-X-SG)|special group]] as "one of the US government's real secret UFO investigation groups". Ruppelt's book and NICAP's Blue Book history both name Col. Frank Dunn as ATIC's chief after Harold Watson was removed in late 1951. If the letter's Dunn is that Dunn, the "special group" was writing to Blue Book's commanding officer about five weeks after Blue Book was set up in March 1952.
3. **Regulation 200-2.** Gerb returns to [[USAF Regulation 200-2]] in many videos. In the USO video it "restricted all official UFO Reporting to be sent to atick and not Blue Book". In [[Videos/The Origin of the UFO Stigma/summary|The Origin of the UFO Stigma]] reports were "to be sent to the Air Force intelligence and not Bluebook". In [[Videos/The 1953 Kingman, Arizona UFO Crash/summary|the Kingman video]] they were "sent to USAF intelligence and not Project Blue Book". The regulation does name ATIC, at Wright-Patterson, as a recipient. ATIC was also where Blue Book sat: Ruppelt ran it as a branch of ATIC's analysis division.
4. **The 1961 draft and Moon Dust.** In [[Videos/MOON DUST - The Pentagon's Secret UFO Programs/summary|the Moon Dust video]] Gerb introduces the program by saying "pck additionally said that alongside Blue Book there were other classified programs". He then reads the 1961 draft as giving the [[USAF 1127th Field Activities Group|1127th]] "collection responsibility duties for the usaf's established program for investigation of reliably reported unidentified flying objects within the United States", adding "the memo also lists one project that has historically had this Duty that project is moondust". In the transcriptions of the draft these are separate paragraphs. The UFO paragraph reads: "Headquarters USAF has established a program for investigation of reliably reported unidentified flying objects within the United States. AFR 200-2 delineates 1127th collection responsibilities." [[Project Moon Dust]] gets its own paragraph, "to locate, recover and deliver descended foreign space vehicles". In [[Videos/US Special Forces Confession - I Recovered Crashed UFOs - Fact or Fiction/summary|another video]] Gerb does list them apart, as "project UFO and moondust", but he never names the UFO program. It was the reporting program run under AFR 200-2, which is to say Blue Book's. Blue Fly, the subject of the third paragraph, delivered Moon Dust items to FTD, the name ATIC took on 1 July 1961 and Blue Book's parent until it closed.

## How it fits together

Gerb's argument runs: these units and rules took UFO material away from Blue Book, so the real study was elsewhere. In each document, though, the destination is ATIC (later FTD). The contractor's report carries Blue Book's name. The special group writes to ATIC's chief. The regulation names ATIC. The 1127th's UFO duty is defined by Blue Book's own regulation. Gerb never says ATIC ran Blue Book, and that missing fact is what makes "to ATIC and not Blue Book" sound like a separate track. The pattern points to ATIC, and to the gap between Blue Book and the rest of ATIC, as the place to look for anything hidden; none of these documents points to a body outside ATIC.

## What cuts against it

- **ATIC was more than Blue Book.** Blue Book was one branch of a much larger center, and Ruppelt himself described cases where he was refused access to information. A report could reach ATIC without reaching him.
- **Regulation 200-2 had other addressees.** The 1954 version also sent reports to the Air Defense Command and to the Director of Intelligence at Air Force headquarters, and field investigations went to the 4602nd Air Intelligence Service Squadron. Each of those was a point where a case could be screened before Blue Book saw it. In his Kevin Knuth video, Gerb points to a case "reported under regulation 200-2" that he says "never made it into Blue Book files". That's the kind of gap his argument needs, although a case can be missing from a file for many reasons.
- **"Colonel Dunn" is identified only by surname.** Ruppelt and later historians name Col. Frank Dunn as ATIC's chief from late 1951, but the 1952 letter's own text, which Gerb cites through Timothy Good's book, wasn't checked here.
- **The 1961 paper is a draft,** and its wording is known from transcriptions of the 1979 release, not a scan seen here.
- **Gerb's wider case doesn't rest only on these four.** The claims of a classified side to Blue Book that he takes from witnesses such as [[Steven Lovekin|Lovekin]] and from [[J. Allen Hynek|Hynek]] are untouched by this post.

## Already known?

Partly. Special Report 14's Blue Book title is standard in the literature (Wikipedia, The Black Vault). Notes circulating with the 1979 Moon Dust release already suggest the draft's "UFO" paragraph means Blue Book. NICAP's history names Col. Frank Dunn as ATIC's chief. No source found sets the four together against the "parallel to Blue Book" reading.

## What would settle it

- A scan of the 24 April 1952 AFOIN-X-SG letter, showing Dunn's full name, office and any copy to Blue Book.
- Blue Book's own files for the nine submarine reports. If they're there, the special group's material reached Blue Book.
- ATIC's internal distribution for 200-2 messages from 1954 to 1957, showing whether reports went first to Blue Book or to other parts of ATIC.
