[[Lockheed Martin]] reportedly tried more than once to hand recovered crash material to [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]], Robert Bigelow's company that ran a Pentagon-funded UFO study. The last attempt went through [[Kona Blue]], a proposed Homeland Security program. The CIA blocked the transfer. In his Sandia video, Gerb offers a working theory about why. He says recovered material is supposed to be kept by federally funded research centers, known as FFRDCs: "FFRDCs like the MITRE Corporation or Sandia". Lockheed, a private company, was keeping its share in its own warehouse. But the same video says Lockheed ran [[Sandia National Laboratories|Sandia]], the nuclear weapons lab, from 1993 to 2017. So during the whole Kona Blue episode, the company Gerb says held the material in the wrong place was also running one of the labs he names as the right place. None of his videos puts those two facts together.

## The finding

From 1993 to April 2017, Sandia was run for the Department of Energy by a company called Sandia Corporation. Sandia Corporation was wholly owned by Martin Marietta. After the 1995 merger that formed Lockheed Martin, it was owned by Lockheed Martin. Gerb dates the attempted transfers to 2008 to 2011. AARO, the Pentagon's UFO office, says Kona Blue itself was set up in 2011 and ordered shut down about six months later. Both sets of dates fall inside the years when Lockheed ran Sandia.

Gerb's theory rests on a clean split: corporations on one side, government-owned FFRDCs on the other. In Lockheed's case that split doesn't hold. Lockheed was a corporation holding material, and it also operated an FFRDC that, on Gerb's account, should have been holding that material.

That is the whole claim. This post does not say the Kona Blue material came from Sandia or passed through it.

## How it fits together

Across these videos Gerb's custody model gets firmer. In September 2025 he wonders whether Lockheed is treated with the "access to information that something like an FFRDC" would have. By November, drawing on [[Andrew Kissner]]'s claim that a 1948 executive order put recovered discs in the custody of the AEC laboratories, Lockheed's corporate holding has become the likely reason the transfer was killed. By August 2026, contractor custody is "incredibly security vulnerable". That reads as Gerb's understanding developing as he researched the FFRDCs and took in Kissner's account, not as a reversal.

All along, though, he says the labs are contractor-operated and that Lockheed operated Sandia. Read together, the videos raise a question none of them asks. If FFRDC custody exists to keep material away from contractors, what did that mean between 1993 and 2017, when the biggest defense contractor ran Sandia? And if Kingman went to Sandia, as the MITRE video guesses, while Lockheed held Kingman material in 2008, how did it get from one to the other? This post suggests no answer, but Gerb's theory would need to give one.

## What cuts against it

- The cleared document Gerb cites calls [[James T. Ryder]] a vice president of [[Lockheed Martin Space Systems]], not of Sandia Corporation, so he and Sandia sat in separate parts of Lockheed. Under the contract, what Sandia held belonged to DOE, not to Lockheed. The company didn't own Sandia's holdings, so it couldn't have given them away.
- In a clip in the Lockheed video, an [[AAWSAP]] insider says the company had held "their collection of crash retrieval materials that they've had since before it got shut down in '89". That is four years before Martin Marietta took over Sandia, so on this account the material predates the Sandia contract. In the MITRE video, a speaker in a clip whom the captions don't name describes being on site at "that facility that they were going to transfer from Loheed Martin to Bigalow Airspace" at [[Naval Air Station Patuxent River|Pax River]], a Navy site, not a DOE one.
- Gerb himself calls the custody theory "still a working theory in its infancy". The theory also doesn't depend on Lockheed being an outsider to FFRDCs. The CIA could have objected to material held outside DOE custody whoever ran Sandia.
- The official record gives a different reason. [[AARO]] says the DHS deputy secretary turned Kona Blue down because the justification and supporting information were too thin, and it mentions no CIA veto and no question of custody.

## Already known?

Nothing turned up. Sandia's own history and DOE's contract records make the management dates easy to find, but no article, forum thread or podcast description found in searches sets Lockheed's Sandia contract beside the Kona Blue transfer or the custody argument. The link between Lockheed and Kona Blue itself rests on Christopher Sharp's reporting and the documents entered into the record at the November 2024 hearing. The search-result summaries of AARO's report don't mention Lockheed at all.

## What would settle it

The facility address [[David Grusch]] says he gave the Inspector General would show whether the material sat on Lockheed corporate property or on a DOE or Navy site. The full text of Ryder's "UAP material divestment plan" might say which Lockheed entity held the material. A statement from Gerb on how Sandia Corporation fits his custody model would answer the question directly.
