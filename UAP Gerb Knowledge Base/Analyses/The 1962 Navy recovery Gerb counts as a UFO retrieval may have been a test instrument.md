In October 1962 the United States set off a nuclear warhead high above the Pacific as a test. In his video on Navy crash retrieval programs, Gerb says the Navy then pulled a downed craft out of the sea. He calls this the [[Bluegill Triple Prime UFO Recovery|Bluegill Triple Prime recovery]], after the name of the test. To him it proves that the [[US Navy]] "has indeed participated in at least one acknowledged USO retrieval operation". A USO is an unidentified submerged object, a UFO found in or under water. His evidence is the logbook of the USS Safeguard, a Navy salvage ship. The log records a search, divers and the recovery of a "pod".

Gerb doesn't mention that the test was meant to put pods in the sea. The Thor rocket that carried the warhead also released three instrument pods, each weighing about 1,200 pounds. They had heat shields and came down by parachute, and Navy ships and helicopters were there to pick them up. So a pod in a recovery ship's log that morning is what the test plan called for.

## The finding

The log entries Gerb describes match the normal recovery of the test's own instrument pods. On their own, they don't record the recovery of an unknown craft, and they don't show the Navy admitting to one. So the case for a UFO now depends on the people who say a craft came down. One is [[Harold Malmgren]], a former presidential adviser. The other is the researcher Geoff Cruickshank. His study of the ship logs is where the logbook argument comes from.

This post does not show that the Safeguard's pod *was* one of the instrument pods. No primary log was read for it. It shows that the logs, as Gerb describes them, can't carry the word "acknowledged". It says nothing about whether Malmgren or Cruickshank are right about a craft.

## The evidence

1. In [[Videos/US Navy UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering Programs/summary|the Navy programs video]], Gerb says: "Recall the 1962 bluegill triple prime detonation and subsequent UFO recovery discussed by late presidential adviser Harold Malmgrren. This event took place during Operation Fishbowl, a series of highaltitude nuclear tests."
2. The documentary basis, in the same passage: "The downed craft or quote unquote pod was searched for and recovered by the USS Safeguard. Historical logs from the safeguard discuss the vessel alongside others such as the USS Engage steaming to the water crash location, searching in a square grid pattern and employing divers to recover a quote unquote pod. During net recovery of this pod, the safeguard actually lost power and the pod was released for helicopter pickup."
3. He builds on it later in the video: the [[Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP)]] "occurred just 2 years after the October 1962 bluegill triple prime US Navy UFO recovery". The same timing point appears on the [[John P. Craven]] and [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] pages.
4. The Fish Bowl project officer's report on the pods (DTIC ADA995471, *Pod and Recovery Unit Fabrications*, General Dynamics/Astronautics) describes 30-inch by 80-inch pods of about 1,200 pounds carried by the Thor on the Star Fish, Blue Gill and King Fish shots. As quoted in search results, it says that on Blue Gill Triple Prime two of the three pods were recovered in good condition and the third had impact damage after a recovery-system fault. A second DTIC report says instruments were placed in each of three pods for Blue Gill Triple Prime, King Fish and Starfish Prime.
5. The researcher Douglas Dean Johnson, who read the recovery ships' logs, writes that the first pod "was located and picked up by the *Safeguard* shortly after the detonation, and returned to Johnston Island before daylight", and that the other two "were returned by helicopter after daylight". A write-up of Cruickshank's own case says the Safeguard lost power "whilst hauling a pod aboard". So both sides agree that the Safeguard brought a pod in.

## How it fits together

The test's own record says pods were coming down near Johnston Island that morning, were expected to be found, and were found. The ships in the area were there to find them. Gerb's sequence (a search, divers, a net, a helicopter) is the sequence you would expect for a heavy, parachuted, radioactive canister in open water. Even the word "pod" belongs to the test program, and Gerb puts it in quotation marks himself. Before these entries could count as a Navy acknowledgement, something in them would have to separate the Safeguard's pod from the three the test released. Nothing Gerb quotes does.

The point reaches past this one case, because Gerb's DSSP timing argument begins with a "US Navy UFO recovery" in 1962. If the logs only show the test's pods being picked up, that argument starts from an alleged recovery, not an acknowledged one.

## What cuts against it

- Cruickshank argues that the logs don't add up. In his account five ships kept searching for days after the official record has all three pods back by 09:00 on 26 October, some debris transfers appear in one ship's log and not in the logs of the ships involved, and the USS Engage logged a "black ball" and a radioactive "green tube". If the Safeguard's pod was recovered after all three instrument pods were accounted for, it can't be one of them.
- Cruickshank says the power loss comes from a diver's account rather than a log entry, and that the Safeguard's watch log shows nothing unusual. If so, part of what Gerb attributes to the "historical logs" may come from testimony instead.
- The Safeguard-and-pod identification rests on one researcher's reading of logs that weren't checked here, and search snippets disagree on which pod was damaged.
- Malmgren's claim that he handled debris from the event stands apart from the logs and isn't affected by this post.

## Already known?

Yes. Johnson's [rebuttal](https://douglasjohnson.ghost.io/bluegill-triple-prime-nuclear-ufo/) identifies the Safeguard's pod as instrument pod B1 and finds nothing in the logs suggesting the crews thought anything they recovered was anomalous. Cruickshank answers him on his [Substack](https://geoffcruickshank.substack.com/p/the-bluegill-triple-prime-ufo-shootdown), and the case is also argued over on [Metabunk](https://www.metabunk.org/threads/australian-intelligence-officer-argues-large-ufo-knocked-down-by-the-bluegill-triple-prime-nuclear-test.13888/). Gerb's videos don't mention the instrument pods or this debate.

## What would settle it

The deck logs of the Safeguard and the Engage for 26 to 31 October 1962, which are held in the National Archives, read alongside the pod recovery times in the Fish Bowl project reports. If the Safeguard's pod entry is timed and placed where pod B1 came down, the entry is the instrument pod. If it falls after all three pods are logged back on Johnston Island, Cruickshank's question stays open.
