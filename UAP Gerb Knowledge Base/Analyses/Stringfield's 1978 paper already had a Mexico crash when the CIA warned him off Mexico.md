In his video on the [[1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval|1974 Coyame crash]], Gerb gives two reasons to think [[Leonard Stringfield]] knew of the case long before the anonymous [[Denb Report]] reached him in 1993. One is Stringfield's own vague memory. The other is a warning: several days before his 1978 [[MUFON]] talk, he says, the [[CIA]] let him know "circuitously" that it would be inadvisable to discuss crash information in Mexico or West Germany. But the paper he gave that week already contained a Mexican crash, and it wasn't Coyame. Stringfield's 1978 paper told of a 1948 crash about 30 miles inside Mexico, across from Laredo, recovered by US troops. He had it from [[Todd Zechel]], the researcher who took [[Robert Willingham]]'s affidavit about a crash south of the border, the story at the heart of Gerb's Del Rio video.

## The finding

The 1978 warning does not single out Coyame. Stringfield already had a Mexican crash with an American recovery team in hand, and he was about to present it. A warning about "crash information in Mexico" fits that case at least as well as a 1974 crash that, on Gerb's own timeline, people only started talking about in the early 1980s. That makes the warning weak evidence for Coyame, and it can't count as independent support for it.

None of this shows that Coyame didn't happen, or casts doubt on Stringfield's memory of hearing about a US team crossing into Mexico. It removes only one of the supports Gerb gives the case.

## The evidence

1. In [[Videos/The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash/summary|the Coyame video]], Gerb reads Stringfield's 1994 recollection: "I heard of the Chihuahua case before either in the late 1970s or early 1980s the only detail I vaguely recall is that the US military team had covertly crossed into Mexico to retrieve the object end quote". Beyond calling it the Chihuahua case, Stringfield gives no date and no site for the crash.
2. In the same video, Gerb adds the warning: "what is crucial to remember here is Stringfield had heard rumors of this case around a decade prior and let's note in stringfield's own words when presenting his initial retrievals of the Third Kind at muon in 1978 he was quote unquote informed circuitously by the CIA several days in advance it would be inadvisable for him to discuss crash information in Mexico or West Germany".
3. Earlier in that video, Gerb dates the first Coyame stories: "in the early 1980s Whispers of a 1974 collision between a small private plane in disc-shaped UFO occurring near coyame Chihuahua Mexico began circulating".
4. Kevin Randle quotes Stringfield's 1978 paper as describing a crash that "occurred about 30 miles inside the Mexican border across from Laredo, Texas, and was recovered by U.S. troops after it was tracked on radar screens". According to Randle, Stringfield got this from Zechel in 1977, and Zechel's source was a technician whose uncle, a provost marshal at Carswell, had helped seal off the site in 1948. Noe Torres's NOUFORS site gives the same Laredo account and credits it to Stringfield's 1978 report.
5. In [[Videos/The 1950s Del Rio, Texas UFO Crashes/summary|the Del Rio video]], Gerb tells how clippings of Willingham's story turned up in the files of the [[National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena]] and how Zechel "Tracked Down Willingham and made him signed an affidavit", which Gerb dates to 1977. In Willingham's account, a Mexican officer told him, "we can't let anyone close to it the American Air Force will be here very soon to clean this all up". Gerb also notes that Stringfield later carried the case himself, as "an entry titled Del Rio Texas 1950".

## How it fits together

The Coyame crash is dated to August 1974. On Gerb's own account, the stories about it began circulating in the early 1980s, and Stringfield could only place his first hearing of it in "the late 1970s or early 1980s". The warning came in July 1978. That's before the earliest date Gerb gives for the Coyame stories, and in the same weeks Stringfield was preparing a paper that included a crash 30 miles inside Mexico, recovered by US troops.

By 1977 two stories had reached the crash-retrieval researchers through Zechel: the Laredo crash Stringfield published, and Willingham's affidavit about a crash across from Del Rio. Both were dated 1948, and both had American teams handling wreckage on Mexican soil. Whether they're one event retold or two separate ones is disputed. Either way, a CIA warning about Mexico had a Mexican case sitting in the paper it preceded. Gerb doesn't connect the warning to either of them, and he never sets the Coyame and Del Rio videos side by side.

## What cuts against it

- Stringfield's own words around the warning aren't available here. If Status Report VII says outright that the 1978 warning concerned Chihuahua, or gives detail only Coyame fits (a 1974 date, a collision with a light aircraft, dead soldiers), Gerb is following Stringfield and this finding shrinks.
- The [[Denb Report]] says its facts were gathered "in 1978 by a person who is now dead", the same year as the warning. That coincidence fits Coyame.
- The warning also covered West Germany. Whether the 1978 paper carried a German case wasn't checked. If it did, that would support the reading that the warning was about the paper's own contents. If it didn't, the warning may have had other targets.
- The Laredo account in the 1978 paper is known here only through Randle's and Torres's quotations of it, not from the paper itself.
- Nobody has confirmed that a warning was given at all. It's Stringfield's account, and it's reported here as his.

## Already known?

No source found sets the 1978 CIA warning against the Laredo case in Stringfield's own paper. Randle has written about the Laredo entry and its source in Zechel, and about how the date of Willingham's story moved from 1948 to 1950 after Zechel became involved, but he doesn't connect either to the warning or to Coyame. The Coyame retellings found online repeat Stringfield's late-1970s memory without asking which Mexican case he had in 1978.

## What would settle it

Two documents would settle it: the passage in Status Report VII where Stringfield brings up the warning, and the full proceedings of the 1978 MUFON Symposium. If Status Report VII ties the warning only to Chihuahua, or if the Laredo case turns out to have been cut from the talk, the finding weakens. If the proceedings also carry a West German case, the warning reads as a response to the paper itself.
