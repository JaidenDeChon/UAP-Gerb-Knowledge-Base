In his Sandia video Gerb reads from two documents about the 1947 New Mexico recoveries. One is [[Andrew Kissner]]'s 1994 statement. The other is the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] report, whose authenticity is contested. He sets them side by side on one point only: both put the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] at [[Sandia National Laboratories|Sandia Base]] in the story. He doesn't mention that both also say people died. Kissner's sources talk of dead US servicemen between May and July 1947. The IPU report talks of three dead technicians from Sandia after the July recoveries.

## The finding

Two documents with no agreed common source each claim that US personnel died in connection with disc recoveries in New Mexico in the summer of 1947, and both tie the recoveries to Sandia Base. Gerb reads out both claims in the same video but never connects them.

The finding goes no further than that. The two claims don't agree on who died, how many died or how they died. Nothing here shows that anyone died at all.

## What cuts against it

- The details differ. Kissner's sources say discs "may in fact have contributed to the deaths of several US servicemen", in a passage about the Army "offensively acting and downing" discs. That reads more like deaths during hostile encounters than contamination. The IPU report names a cause (contamination), a number (three) and a group (technicians from Sandia).
- "Technicians" need not mean servicemen. The report as Gerb reads it sends a radiobiological team, a Special Engineer Detachment and a security detail. SEDs were uniformed soldiers, as the [[Videos/The Manhattan Project 2.0 - the Secrecy of UFO Crash Retrieval Programs Vol.1/summary|Manhattan Project 2.0]] video explains, but Sandia's Z Division also had civilian staff. The scout's idea that the dead technicians *were* Kissner's dead servicemen is a possible reading, but nothing in the evidence supports it.
- Dead recovery crews are a staple of crash lore. Gerb's Coyame video has a Mexican team killed by contamination, and his SAIC video repeats a claim of 18 US servicemen killed by a craft's occupants. A shared death claim is weak evidence on its own.
- Copying can't be ruled out. Gerb raises the possibility that "the IPU docs actually ripped off Kisner's disclosures". Kissner's statement wasn't presented publicly until 2004, but by Gerb's own account Kissner shared it with Representative [[Steven Schiff]] in 1994, so it was already circulating privately. One search snippet, from a hobbyist newsletter of the period, says [[Linda Moulton Howe]] was given the IPU document in the 1990s. She later presented Kissner's statement in 2004. If both papers passed through the same hands, a shared detail proves nothing about independence.

## The evidence

1. [[Videos/Sandia National Laboratories - UFO Reverse Engineering, Material Exploitation, & Legacy Programs/summary|Sandia video]], on Kissner: "Kistner's sources detail the series of events beginning 15 May 1947 that led to elements of the United States Army offensively acting and downing two to four {quote unquote} flying discs. One of these incidents being the infamous Roswell event. According to these sources, flying discs may in fact have contributed to the deaths of several US servicemen from 9 May to 31 July 1947."
2. The same video, on the IPU report: "a special radiobiological [music] team, accompanied by a special engineering detachment, or SED, a term used to describe a group of technical personnel assigned to the Manhattan Project", and then: "According to the report, multiple MPs suffered nervous breakdowns and three technicians from Sandia died due to some sort of ambiguous [music] contamination." Gerb compares this with [[1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval|Coyame]], not with Kissner.
3. The only link Gerb draws between the two is AFSWP at Sandia, followed by "there's a possibility that the IPU docs actually ripped off Kisner's disclosures."
4. [[Videos/The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash/summary|Coyame video]]: "ground Personnel from Sandia experienced some form of contamination resulting in the deaths of three technicians". [[Videos/The Majestic-12 Documents With Ryan S. Wood/summary|MJ-12 video]]: the IPU report "first made its way into the public on July 16th 1995".

## How it fits together

If the documents are independent, the deaths are a second point of agreement alongside AFSWP at Sandia. If the IPU papers drew on Kissner, as Gerb suggests they might, the deaths are the kind of detail a copier would take and sharpen into a number and a cause. Either way, the overlap bears on the question Gerb raises, and he doesn't use it.

## Already known?

Searches found nothing comparing the two casualty claims. Coverage of both documents is thin: no result confirmed Kissner's legislative service, and the 2004 conference proceedings, on the Internet Archive, couldn't be opened from here. On the IPU report's origins the results add nothing to the vault's page: it reached Timothy Cooper from a source calling himself Cantwell in the mid-1990s, with no independent confirmation. Press coverage confirms Schiff's GAO inquiry was under way by January 1994.

## What would settle it

Kissner's full 1994 text, as printed in the 2004 conference proceedings, would show whether his deaths came from contamination or from the downings, and whether he mentions Sandia technicians. A dated record of who held the statement before July 1995 (Schiff's office files, for example) would show whether the IPU report could have drawn on it.
