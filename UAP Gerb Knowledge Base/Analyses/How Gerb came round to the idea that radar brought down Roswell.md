Some researchers think military radar may have knocked the 1947 [[Roswell Crash|Roswell]] craft out of the sky. In January 2025, Gerb didn't buy it. In his video on [[Philip J. Corso]], an Army officer known for his Roswell claims, he said: "I am still very skeptical on this point". Twenty months later, in his 80-year timeline video, he said it was "probably likely" that radar or electromagnetic interference brought the Roswell craft down. He never tells viewers that his view changed. The videos in between show it changing one step at a time. His understanding may simply have changed as new information reached him. Two sources seem to have mattered: the [[Scott and Suzanne Ramsey|Ramseys']] book on the Aztec crash in New Mexico, and a 1994 statement by the researcher [[Andrew Kissner]].

## The finding

Across five videos, from January 2025 to September 2026, Gerb's view of the radar theory moves in three steps. He starts out very skeptical. Then he says "perhaps there is credence" to it. In the end he calls it "probably likely". The transcripts show this directly. On this analysis's own reading, they also show what came along with the shift. The first source is the [[Scott and Suzanne Ramsey|Ramseys']] story of an Aztec witness who said radar had killed ducks. Gerb takes it up in July 2025 and cites it again in 2026. The second is [[Andrew Kissner]]'s 1994 statement. Gerb first discusses its point about radar in November 2025, and by May 2026 he says he firmly agrees with it. Changing a view as new sources arrive is ordinary. The point is only that viewers of the Corso video were never told it had changed.

## The evidence

1. [[Videos/Philip J. Corso - US Army UFO Technology Research & Development/summary|Corso]] (January 2025), after Corso's story of a being whose craft was held down by radar: "a common theory by corso and some Roswell researchers is radar systems interfered with craft navigation leading to crashes I am still very skeptical on this point".
2. [[Videos/The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval/summary|Aztec]] (July 2025), on the undamaged [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|Aztec]] craft: "But perhaps there is credence to the theory the craft was down via high-powered radar". He then gives the Ramseys' account of Lee Crane, who said airmen from the El Vado radar site brought "freshly killed half-cooked ducks" into a diner, knocked down by the radar, and ties it to the [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)|SDI]] weapons he has covered before.
3. [[Videos/Sandia National Laboratories - UFO Reverse Engineering, Material Exploitation, & Legacy Programs/summary|Sandia]] (November 2025), on Kissner's statement: "Now, I know radars bringing down craft is very, very, very bizarre. However, viewers of my channel will recall..." and he goes on to the SDI programs again.
4. [[Videos/The Manhattan Project 2.0 - the Secrecy of UFO Crash Retrieval Programs Vol.1/summary|Manhattan Project 2.0]] (May 2026): "Kisner's disclosures are intriguing, most of which I am in firm agreement with, including ... the presence of high-powered triangulated radar arrays, such as the lash-up network, possibly being incidentally responsible for early UFO crash retrievals, such as 1948's Aztec."
5. [[Videos/80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering - A Timeline/summary|80 Years]] (September 2026), after retelling the duck story from "the work of Scott and Suzanne Ramsay": "so I think it probably likely that the Roswell craft had some sort sort of interference with uh radar systems or sort of EM interference at the time and thus one or several crashes occurred in the American Southwest." He goes straight on to Kissner, "who gosh do I believe and gosh, do I know well at this point".

## How it fits together

The order matters. The softening starts in the Aztec video, four months before Kissner's radar claim appears on the channel, and it rests on the Ramseys' duck story plus Gerb's existing view of SDI as an anti-UFO weapons effort. Kissner then supplies a named source for the radar network itself. By the timeline video, Gerb gives both as his reasons in the same breath. So reading the shift as simply tracking Kissner is too narrow. The transcripts point to two sources, the Ramseys first.

## What cuts against it

- The middle statements are hedged ("perhaps", "possibly", "bizarre"), and the May 2026 line is about Aztec, not Roswell. Only the January 2025 and September 2026 lines are clear opposites.
- The 80 Years line comes from a loose, spoken section at the end of a long video, not a scripted narration.
- Gerb's 2025 skepticism was voiced right after Corso's mine story, which Gerb treats with doubt throughout that video. Some of that doubt may have been about Corso rather than the radar idea itself.
- The link between the shift and its sources is inferred from where Gerb places them. He never says "this changed my mind".

## Already known?

The radar theory itself is old and widely repeated in Roswell and Aztec literature. Searches found nothing noting the change in Gerb's own coverage, so the point here is about the channel.

## What would settle it

A statement from Gerb on when and why his view changed. On the theory itself, the Ramseys' book *The Aztec UFO Incident* for the Crane account, and the text of Kissner's 1994 statement for its radar passage.
