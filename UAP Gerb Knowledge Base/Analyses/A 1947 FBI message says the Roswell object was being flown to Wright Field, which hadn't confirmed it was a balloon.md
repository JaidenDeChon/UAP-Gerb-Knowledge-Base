In several videos Gerb says that what was recovered near Roswell in July 1947 was taken to [[Wright Field]] in Ohio, the Army Air Forces' research and testing base that later became [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. His evidence is witness testimony, a later affidavit from an officer at Fort Worth, and an email in which the musician [[Tom DeLonge]] relays what a retired general told him. There is also a record from the day itself. On the evening of 8 July 1947, the FBI's Dallas office sent an urgent teletype to FBI headquarters and its Cincinnati office. It says the object was being flown to Wright Field "BY SPECIAL PLANE", and that Wright Field had not backed up the Air Force's view that it was a weather balloon. The teletype is reprinted in full in the [[GAO Roswell Report (1995)|1995 Roswell report]] by the General Accounting Office, Congress's auditors, which Gerb cites. No video mentions it.

## The finding

A record made at the time, and not by any witness, states the same destination Gerb gives: the object was on its way to Wright Field for examination. The same message says a phone call with Wright Field "HAD NOT ... BORNE OUT" the balloon identification. When the auditors summarised the teletype in their letter, they kept the balloon description and left that clause out.

That's the whole of what it confirms. It records what an officer at Eighth Air Force headquarters in Fort Worth told the FBI on 8 July about a flight that was under way or planned. It doesn't show that the object arrived, what it was, or anything about bodies or exotic material.

## The evidence

1. **The claim.** In [[Videos/80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering - A Timeline/summary|the 80-year timeline]], Gerb says recovered craft were "processed at the nearby Roswell Army airfield, and biologics and materials were primarily taken to then Wright Field, now Wright Patterson Air Force Base." In [[Videos/UFOs in the Private Sector - Battelle Memorial Institute/summary|the Battelle video]] he hedges: the debris "was supposedly shipped to none other than you guessed it right Patterson Air Force Base". In [[Videos/Philip J. Corso - US Army UFO Technology Research & Development/summary|the Corso video]] he notes that "quite often Roswell reports State wreckage was flown to right Patterson Air Force Base".
2. **Gerb's sources for it.** In [[Videos/The Hidden Wing - US Air Force UFO Reverse Engineering Programs/summary|The Hidden Wing]] he quotes DeLonge's email to John Podesta about [[Neil McCasland]], the retired general who once ran the Air Force's research laboratory: "When Roswell crashed, they shipped it to the laboratory at Wright Patterson Air Force Base." From that, Gerb concludes: "if Maclin's words to Tom Dong are true, seen as AMC operated out of Wright Patterson Air Force Base, it is not only a safe bet, but highly likely Air Material Command was the unit at Wright Patterson given custody of the Roswell wreckage." In [[Videos/UFO Whistleblowers Vol.1/summary|UFO Whistleblowers Vol. 1]] he cites the affidavit of [[Thomas J. DuBose]], chief of staff to General [[General Ramey|Roger Ramey]] at Fort Worth, who "states he was in charge of arranging the requested materials be sent from raaf to Fort Worth which were then sent on to General chidlaw at right field".
3. **The record.** Page 16 of the [[GAO Roswell Report (1995)]] (appendix II) is a facsimile of the FBI teletype, sent from Dallas at 6:17 PM on 8 July 1947 and marked URGENT. The name of the Eighth Air Force officer who phoned is blacked out. The transcription reads:

   > THE DISC IS HEXAGONAL IN SHAPE AND WAS SUSPENDED FROM A BALLON [sic] BY CABLE, WHICH BALLON WAS APPROXIMATELY TWENTY FEET IN DIAMETER. [redacted] FURTHER ADVISED THAT THE OBJECT FOUND RESEMBLES A HIGH ALTITUDE WEATHER BALLOON WITH A RADAR REFLECTOR, BUT THAT TELEPHONIC CONVERSATION BETWEEN THEIR OFFICE AND WRIGHT FIELD HAD NOT [struck out, illegible] BORNE OUT THIS BELIEF. DISC AND BALLOON BEING TRANSPORTED TO WRIGHT FIELD BY SPECIAL PLANE FOR EXAMINAT[ION].

   It ends: "[redacted] ADVISED WOULD REQUEST WRIGHT FIELD TO ADVISE CINCINNATI OFFICE RESULTS OF EXAMINATION. NO FURTHER INVESTIGATION BEING CONDUCTED." GAO says an FBI spokesperson confirmed the message is authentic (page 6).
4. **The auditors' summary.** On page 6 of the report the GAO sums the message up: "According to the Eighth Air Force official, the recovered object resembled a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector." The clause about Wright Field isn't there, and neither is it in the "Results in Brief" on page 4.
5. **What GAO found at Wright Field.** On page 7, GAO says it searched the Cincinnati office's abstracts, where the examination results were to be sent, and "There was no mention in the Cincinnati office abstracts of the crash or recovery of an airborne object near Roswell." It then reviewed [[Air Materiel Command]] (Wright Field) records from 1947 to 1950: "We found no records mentioning the Roswell crash or the examination by Air Materiel Command personnel of any debris recovered from the crash."

## How it fits together

Gerb's case for the Wright Field leg rests on people speaking decades later: DuBose in the 1990s, DeLonge in a January 2016 email relaying McCasland, and Corso's convoy story. The teletype is independent of all of them. The FBI's Dallas office wrote it on the day, from a phone call with the headquarters that held the debris. It names the same base. It is the same headquarters DuBose served at, but it is a record from 1947, not his later memory.

The auditors' summary drops the one line in the message that cuts against the balloon reading. GAO doesn't say the line is wrong. It simply doesn't repeat it, and a reader who only sees the letter would think the message was a plain balloon identification. GAO's own searches then come up empty at the two places the message says the results would go: Wright Field and the FBI in Cincinnati.

## What cuts against it

- **The flight may never have happened.** An Associated Press report from Fort Worth on 8 July 1947, printed in the next day's papers, says "Plans to fly the object to Wright Field for further investigation were canceled." The same report says Warrant Officer Irving Newton of the base weather station identified the material as a weather balloon's radar target. If the flight was cancelled, there would be nothing for Air Materiel Command to file, which explains GAO's empty search as well as a cover-up would.
- **A later account says some did reach Wright Field, as balloon gear.** Charles B. Moore, an engineer on the Project Mogul balloon program, reported that Colonel Marcellus Duffy at Wright Field told him the "flying saucer" was being flown in and brought to his home for identification. That's one second-hand account, but if it's right, the material reached Wright Field and was taken for balloon equipment.
- **"Had not borne out" means unconfirmed, not contradicted.** The words say the phone call hadn't supported the balloon idea. They don't say Wright Field thought it was anything else. Wright Field may simply not have seen the object yet, and the officer still described it as a balloon with a radar reflector.
- **The object described sounds like a radar reflector.** A hexagonal "disc" hung by cable from a balloon about 20 feet across matches a radar target. GAO's own report notes that Project Mogul, the classified program the Air Force's 1994 report blamed for the debris, used "balloons that carried radar reflectors and acoustic sensors" (page 8).
- **The doubtful word.** The scan on page 16 shows a word typed over between "NOT" and "BORNE", which can't be read. It was struck out, and "NOT" and "BORNE OUT THIS BELIEF" are clear on the image either side of it. "EXAMINAT" runs off the edge of the line, and the completion is plain.
- **It wasn't the only 1947 report of the destination.** Press reports on 8 July also said Ramey had ordered the object flown to Wright Field, before the AP said the plan was cancelled. What the teletype adds is the official channel, the special plane, and the line about Wright Field.

## Already known?

Yes. The teletype has been public since the FBI's releases under the Freedom of Information Act, it's in the FBI's online FOIA library, the Vault, and Roswell researchers and writers have quoted the "had not borne out this belief" line for years. Roadside America's write-up reads it as the memo dismissing the balloon story. A web search found no source pointing out that GAO's summary leaves the clause out, but the omission is plain from the report itself. What's new here is only that Gerb's videos, which cite the GAO report, never use the record in it that most directly supports his Wright Field claim.

## What would settle it

The Air Materiel Command intelligence files for July 1947 at Wright Field, if any survive, and any flight record for a special flight from Fort Worth to Wright Field on 8 or 9 July 1947. An FBI Cincinnati file showing whether Wright Field ever sent the promised results would also settle whether the examination took place. The unredacted name of the officer who phoned Dallas would show who at Eighth Air Force was speaking.
