In his NRO video Gerb plays a clip of [[Tom DeLonge]] on Joe Rogan's show in October 2017: "one of my adviserss was from the National Reconnaissance Office. High up". Gerb argues that this adviser told DeLonge about [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)|Immaculate Constellation]] tasking, and that it was "likely Luis Alzando himself". [[Luis Elizondo]] never worked for the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]], though. One person on Gerb's own list of DeLonge's advisers did: Major General [[Neil McCasland]]. In *The Hidden Wing*, Gerb himself says McCasland "performed much work closely with the National Reconnaissance Office". That sentence comes a few lines after he repeats the Elizondo theory, and he never connects the two.

## The finding

DeLonge said his adviser was "from" the NRO. Among the advisers Gerb names (McCasland, Michael Carey, [[John Podesta]], [[Hal Puthoff]], [[Chris Mellon]], Elizondo, Rob Weiss and Steve Justice), McCasland is the only one whose published career includes NRO assignments. Elizondo's published career has none. His only NRO tie in Gerb's account is the liaison work he did for [[To The Stars Academy]] after leaving the Pentagon. When the episode came out, he had been out of government for about three weeks.

This post does not say McCasland was the adviser. DeLonge never named that person, and not all of his advisers were public. The claim is narrower: the description DeLonge gave fits McCasland's record and doesn't fit Elizondo's, so the clip doesn't support Gerb's identification as well as he suggests.

McCasland's name comes up in the same section, but not as a possible source.

## How it fits together

McCasland's official Air Force biography and his Wikipedia article give the same record. He was a payload development engineer and later an assistant director for systems engineering in the Secretary of the Air Force Office of Special Projects in Los Angeles, the Air Force arm of the NRO (also called Program A). From 1992 to 1994 he was director of mission planning at the Aerospace Data Facility at Buckley, an NRO satellite ground station, and from 1995 to 1997 he commanded its operations squadron. The biography describes his career as spanning "the Air Force and the National Reconnaissance Office". Later, at the Pentagon, he was director of special programs. He commanded the Air Force Research Laboratory from 2011 and retired in 2013. DeLonge's 2016 emails to Podesta say McCasland had been working with him for four months and had "helped assemble my advisory team".

Elizondo's record is different. A Pentagon memo released through The Black Vault puts him in the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence from 28 September 2008 to 4 October 2017. His own biography for the November 2024 House hearing lists Army service, Defense Department counterintelligence work, the Office of the National Counterintelligence Executive and the ODNI. Neither source mentions the NRO. Rogan's episode 1029 went out on 26 October 2017, three weeks after Elizondo left the Pentagon.

None of the other advisers has an NRO career on the record either. Michael Carey's Air Force biography covers space, missile and nuclear staff posts and command of 20th Air Force, the ICBM force. Mellon was a Senate intelligence committee staffer and a deputy assistant secretary of defense; his NRO connection is a Gold Medal the agency awarded him, not a job there. Puthoff's background is SRI and contract work for the CIA, and Justice and Weiss came from Lockheed Martin's Skunk Works.

If DeLonge meant one of these people, McCasland is the only one who fits. This also bears on Gerb's argument about Immaculate Constellation (IMCON), which he dates to "the same year of Imcon's establishment 2017". McCasland had been retired for four years by then, so if he was DeLonge's source, the "episodic visits" talk would be older than IMCON rather than taken from its tasking.

## What cuts against it

- DeLonge never named the adviser. Anyone from the NRO who was talking to him and never became public would fit as well as McCasland does.
- McCasland's NRO posts came in the middle of his career, from the 1980s to 1997, not at the top. "High up" might just reflect his final rank, or it might point to someone else.
- How long McCasland stayed involved is disputed. His wife has called him an unpaid consultant on DeLonge's fiction and media projects, and Newsweek found no official record of him advising DeLonge. Gerb himself says McCasland "got a slap on the wrist" after the emails leaked, so he may have stepped back before the Rogan interview.
- Gerb may mean "connected to the NRO" in the liaison sense, and he asks whether "someone connected to the NRO" informed DeLonge. But DeLonge's word was "from".
- DeLonge's wording is loose throughout the clip. Even in the summary that follows it, Gerb says "advisers" in the plural.

## Already known?

The 2026 coverage of McCasland's disappearance, including The Debrief, Newsweek, Fox News and ABC, notes both his NRO work and his ties to DeLonge, and some commentators link him to "the general" DeLonge has talked about. No source turned up that connects McCasland to the NRO adviser in the 1029 clip, and none questions Gerb's identification of Elizondo.

## What would settle it

DeLonge could name the adviser, or the full 1029 episode might say more around the clip than Gerb plays. On the Elizondo side, an official record of any NRO assignment, or of TTSA liaison contacts with the NRO before 26 October 2017, would bring him back into the picture.
