[[Philip J. Corso]] said that the Army's research chief, Lieutenant General [[Arthur Trudeau]], gave him crash material from Roswell to pass on to industry. Almost everything we know about Trudeau and UFOs comes from Corso himself. In his Corso video, Gerb points to one exception. It's a story in the journals of [[Jacques Vallee]]. The astrophysicist [[Eric Davis]] says George H. W. Bush told him that Trudeau briefed him on legacy programs, and that Corso's testimony to Congress was stopped as a result. Set beside the public record, the story's timing doesn't work. By the date it gives, Trudeau had been out of the Army for six years. The best-known hearing it could refer to took place before Bush was in Congress.

## The finding

As Gerb reads it from Vallee, the briefing happened "at the time of the 1968 Mandel Rivers Congressional hearings". L. Mendel Rivers chaired the House Armed Services Committee from 1965 to 1970. There are two ways to read the date, and neither fits an official briefing:

- **If it means Rivers's UFO hearing,** that was held on 5 April 1966. Bush was still a private citizen in Houston, campaigning for a House seat. He took his seat on 3 January 1967.
- **If it means some other hearing Rivers's committee held in 1968,** Bush was a congressman by then. But Trudeau had retired from the Army in 1962, and Corso in 1963. Neither held a government post. Bush, a freshman on the Ways and Means Committee, didn't sit on Armed Services either.

So the story only works as a private conversation, held in 1968 or later, between a retired general working in private industry and a first-term congressman. Or a detail got garbled somewhere between Bush and Gerb. It doesn't work as the Army's research chief briefing a congressman about a serving officer. This doesn't show that the conversation never happened. It shows that the story, as told, can't be what it sounds like.

## The evidence

1. In [[Videos/Philip J. Corso - US Army UFO Technology Research & Development/summary|the Corso video]], Gerb reads from *Forbidden Science: Pacific Heights*: "when the two spoke again Bush told Davis he was briefed on Legacy programs by Trudeau at the time of the 1968 Mandel Rivers Congressional hearings the book says Colonel Trudeau however I do think this is a typo referencing General Arthur Trudeau".
2. The same passage gives what Trudeau is said to have told Bush: "Bush senior claimed Trudeau told him his man Corso had been asked to testify before Congress and was able to reveal Rumblings of alien Hardware that had been distributed to Industrial Labs the Corso testimony was squashed as a result of this conversation".
3. Later in the video, Gerb gives both men's retirement dates: "Lieutenant General Trudeau retired in 1962 and the lieutenant colonel followed him shortly in 1963".
4. A few sentences after that, he calls the Bush story the only outside link: "outside of Corso we have only one interesting piece to link the general to UAP let's remember all the way back to Forbidden Science where Dr Eric Davis claimed Bush senior stated he was indeed briefed by Trudeau at the 1968 mendal Rivers Congress Congressional hearings".
5. In [[Videos/The Origin of the UFO Stigma/summary|The Origin of the UFO Stigma]], Gerb dates the Rivers committee's UFO hearing correctly: "on the 5th of April 1966 Congress held its first closed session on UFOs in a hearing by the house armed services committee". He never sets that date beside the Bush story.

## How it fits together

The public record, checked against at least two sources for each point:

- **The Rivers UFO hearing:** House Armed Services Committee, 5 April 1966, Rivers presiding (the printed hearing on govinfo; The Black Vault). Congressman [[Gerald Ford]] had asked for it.
- **The only UFO hearing in 1968:** a one-day symposium on 29 July 1968, held by a different committee, Science and Astronautics. Representative J. Edward Roush chaired it (the printed hearing on govinfo; the Sign Historical Group at project1947.com).
- **Bush:** in the House from 3 January 1967 to 3 January 1971, and the only freshman put on Ways and Means (Wikipedia; the Ways and Means Committee's own history; the Miller Center).
- **Trudeau:** Chief of Army Research and Development from 1958 to 1962, then retired (National Archives/SNAC record; Wikipedia). Wikipedia says he then headed Gulf Oil's research labs in Pittsburgh until 1968 and advised the chairman of Rockwell until 1972. The National Archives record calls him a management consultant.
- **Corso:** he left the Army in March 1963 to join Senator [[Strom Thurmond]]'s staff (a CIA reading-room file; Gerb's own retirement date). In 1964–65 he was being vetted for a House subcommittee job, as Gerb notes from FBI files.

Read with "1968", the story has Trudeau briefing a congressman on programs he had left six years earlier. It also has Bush, a freshman with no seat on Armed Services, holding a conversation that stopped testimony to Congress. Read as the 1966 Rivers UFO hearing, Bush wasn't in Congress. If the 1966 hearing is what Bush meant, he could only have been a candidate hearing it from a retired general.

## What cuts against it

- **The book wasn't read here.** Everything rests on Gerb's reading of Vallee. Vallee's journal records what Davis said, and Davis was recalling what Bush told him around 2003 or 2004 about events nearly forty years earlier. Reports on the journal say the Bush material may also have passed through [[Hal Puthoff]]. Any of those steps could have changed the year or the committee. The point of this post is the story's timing, not the honesty of anyone who passed it on, Gerb included. He reports the book's date both times he mentions it.
- **"Colonel Trudeau".** Gerb says the book calls him Colonel and takes it for a typo. If Vallee really meant a different, serving colonel named Trudeau, a 1968 briefing could have been official. But "his man Corso" points strongly to the general.
- **A retired general still had contacts.** Trudeau ran a major industrial lab and stayed close to the Army. Nothing stopped him from talking privately to a congressman. The private reading of the story is possible, just much less official than "briefed on Legacy programs" suggests.
- **Rivers held many hearings.** In 1968 his committee met often. "The Rivers hearings" may never have meant the UFO hearing at all, and then the year by itself is no problem for Bush.

## Already known?

Searches turned up the Bush–Davis material in Vallee's fifth volume and the documentary claims about Holloman (see [[The Holloman landing George Bush confirmed wasn't Eisenhower's 1954 meeting]]). Nothing turned up that discusses the Trudeau passage or checks its timing against Bush's or Trudeau's careers.

## What would settle it

The passage in *Forbidden Science 5: Pacific Heights* (Anomalist Books, 2023) itself: whether it says 1968, Mendel Rivers, and Colonel or General Trudeau. After that, any record of Corso being asked to testify before a House committee between 1966 and 1968, or of Trudeau and Bush meeting in those years.
