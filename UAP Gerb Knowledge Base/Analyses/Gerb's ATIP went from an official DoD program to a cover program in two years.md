If you watched Gerb's early 2024 videos, you heard [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|ATIP]] described as an official Defense Department UFO program, with [[Luis Elizondo]] as its former director and one of the people vouching for the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]]. If you watch the 2026 videos, ATIP is "the fake ATIP cover program", an unfunded working group fronting for the [[National Security Council]], and Elizondo is someone Gerb says he does not trust. The change happened in steps across at least eight videos. Gerb never puts the old framing next to the new one, and a viewer who only saw the 2024 videos would not know it had changed.

## The finding

The transcripts show that between January 2024 and June 2026, Gerb's description of ATIP and of Elizondo's place in it changed from an official program with a former director to a cover program that was never a real one. The claim here concerns that framing only and says nothing about how far Gerb once trusted Elizondo. In 2024, "former atip director" may have been the standard label of the time rather than a view Gerb had weighed. The dated videos also point to what moved him: Elizondo's CIFA background and his changing account of his ATIP role (both named in September 2025), *[[The Age of Disclosure]]* (released 21 November 2025, during the making of the NRO video), and Elizondo's own interview answers that Gerb plays in 2026.

In his own voice, Gerb calls ATIP "the department of defense's former publicly known UFO investigation group", and lists among the memo's backers "the former atip the advanced Aerospace threat identification program director Lou alzando".

Gerb sets out [[Sean Kirkpatrick]]'s charge that the reverse-engineering story "is a 2008 conspiracy perpetuated by atip under a small group of individuals" and calls such claims "blatantly false". Part of his answer: "a tip under Lu alzando which by the way is also a former official UFO investigation program contracted by the dod".
3. [[Videos/Incredible UFO Footage - FLYBY/summary|FLYBY]] (5 February 2024) opens on "former atip director Lou alzando" and his remark about leaked footage.

## How it fits together

The change comes in three steps. Through 2024, ATIP is an official program and Elizondo its former director, a label Gerb uses to give weight to the memo and to his answer to Kirkpatrick. By June 2025, ATIP is "likely a white world cover" tied to [[AAWSAP]]'s classified work, and Gerb calls the office one Elizondo "claimed" to direct. From September 2025, Gerb gives his reasons for distrusting Elizondo (the CIFA role and the shifting account of his ATIP role). After *The Age of Disclosure* he moves ATIP out of the AAWSAP picture altogether and places it in the National Security Council under [[James Clapper]]'s top cover. In *The Hidden Wing* he credits [[David Grusch]] with naming Clapper.

This suggests that Gerb's view changed as new material reached him, rather than that he misspoke. Two of his 2024 arguments did rest in part on the older framing. Elizondo was one of the four backers he named for the Wilson-Davis Memo, and ATIP's official standing was part of his answer to Kirkpatrick. His 2026 description of ATIP as a narrative-control effort run by a small circle also moves partway toward the kind of charge he called "blatantly false" in 2024. The overlap has a limit: Kirkpatrick says the story was invented, while Gerb says ATIP let out part of a real one.

## What cuts against it

- In 2024 Gerb may only have been using the common label. The *New York Times* had described ATIP as a Pentagon program since 2017, and the January 2024 Kirkpatrick video quotes Mellon calling Elizondo "the former director of AIP program".
- "official UFO investigation program contracted by the dod" fits AAWSAP, which was contracted, better than ATIP. At the time Gerb ran the two together, as the Coyame line shows.
- The June 2025 "cover" sentence links ATIP to OAP without saying ATIP sat inside it, so it does not clearly match the sub-SAP view Gerb later rejects.
- Gerb never says why his framing changed. The reasons above are inferred from what each video names and when it was published.

## Already known?

The underlying dispute is well documented. In 2019 a Pentagon spokesperson said Elizondo had no assigned AATIP responsibilities, and The Black Vault has published the records on both sides. AARO's March 2024 historical report and later coverage describe AATIP as an informal follow-on to AAWSAP rather than a funded program. A 2007 account of CIFA's contracting puts an employee named Luis Elizondo at CIFA, though it says nothing about a contracting officer role. No source turned up that tracks how Gerb's own framing changed.

## What would settle it

A statement from Gerb on when and why his view of ATIP changed. On the dispute itself, the full AARO Volume I passage on AATIP and the unredacted 2009 Reid letter describing an AATIP "in SAP" status would show whether ATIP had any formal standing.
