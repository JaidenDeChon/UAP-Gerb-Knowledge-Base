In his Corso video Gerb picks out "an interesting line": [[Philip J. Corso]]'s 1998 affidavit calls the Fort Riley body an [[Extraterrestrial Biological Entity|extraterrestrial biological entity]], "the same phrase" used in the [[Majestic 12]] [[Special Operations Manual (SOM 1-01)|Special Operations Manual]]. It reads like two sources agreeing. They disagree, though, on where the name came from, and by the time Corso used it in public the term had been in circulation for about ten years, in a document Gerb says Corso knew about. So the match tells a viewer nothing about whether either source is genuine.

## The finding

There are three accounts of where the name came from. Corso, in an interview clip, says his own group arrived at it after deciding the beings were clones, which Gerb places in Corso's Army research years from 1961. Corso's affidavit says the 1947 Walter Reed autopsy reports already used it. The [[Eisenhower Briefing Document]], dated 1952, credits [[Detlev Bronk]]'s team in 1947.

Whichever account is right, the term was public by 1987, when the briefing document was released, and the manual followed in 1994. Corso's book came out in 1997 and his affidavit in 1998. So when the term turns up in his account, that is not independent support for the MJ-12 papers, and the MJ-12 papers don't support him either. This post doesn't argue that Corso took the term from them, or that any of these sources is genuine or fake.

## The evidence

1. [[Videos/Philip J. Corso - US Army UFO Technology Research & Development/summary|Corso video]], Gerb on the affidavit: "the body was referred to as an Ebe extraterrestrial biological entity the same phrase us used in The Majestic 12 special operations manual this manual describes two sets of Ebe Ebe 1 and 2 ebe1 closely matches the description of Corso's observed body".
2. The same video, reading the [[Citizens Against UFO Secrecy (CAUS)|CAUS]] affidavit: the autopsy reports in the [[Roswell File]] were from Walter Reed, and "the autopsy report referred to the creature as an extraterrestrial biological entity".
3. The same video. Gerb first summarises the manuscript: "corso and the ocrd believed these beans to be cloned or at the very least Expendable creatures". Then comes a clip of Corso: "so when I start putting all this on the exur together we finally gave it a name I told her looks like you know these are clones and so the name came out extra extraterrestrial biological entity".
4. The same video, on Corso's manuscript *Dawn of a New Age*: it "sees Coro recognizing the Eisenhower briefing document report but how he never saw the report at the NSC and White House". Gerb also lists the book's links to [[Roscoe H. Hillenkoetter|Hillenkoetter]] and the MJ-12 personnel among the details that set *[[The Day After Roswell]]* apart from the manuscript.
5. [[Videos/The Majestic-12 Documents With Ryan S. Wood/summary|MJ-12 video]], Gerb on the briefing document: "Dr detlev Bron performed autopsies on the bodies and coined the term Ebe or extraterrestrial biological entity". In the same video [[Ryan S. Wood]] says the manual reached [[Don Berliner]] in 1994.

## How it fits together

1. If the name was coined in 1947, by Bronk's team or by the Walter Reed pathologists, then Corso's clip, which says it came out of his own group's clone conclusion, is wrong about its origin.
2. If Corso's group coined it in the 1960s, then a document dated 1952 and a manual dated 1954 use it before it existed.
3. In both cases the term was public long before Corso went public: the briefing document from 1987, the manual from 1994. By Gerb's own reading, Corso's manuscript mentions the briefing document. So two sources sharing the term proves nothing about either one.

## What cuts against it

- The clip is loose speech. "We finally gave it a name" could mean that his group settled on a term already in the file rather than invented one, and "the name came out" could mean the term emerged from the material. On that reading, the clip and the affidavit fit together, and only the briefing document's credit to Bronk is left as a separate origin.
- A garbled line follows in the captions: "we never used the letters each there was no such thing". It may say the group didn't use the abbreviation, but it's too unclear to rely on, and so is "I told her".
- If the term really was classified jargon from 1947, a genuine Corso and a genuine MJ-12 would use it anyway. The finding doesn't rule that out. It shows only that the match can't tell that case apart from one where one source took the term from the other.
- "Extraterrestrial biological entity" is a plain description. Two people could reach it separately.
- Gerb presents the match only as an interesting line, never as proof, and elsewhere in the video he treats the book's MJ-12 material with suspicion.

## Already known?

The briefing document's wording is quoted on several sites: Bronk's team "has suggested the term 'Extra-terrestrial Biological Entities', or 'EBEs', be adopted as the standard term of reference". Wikipedia and other accounts date its public release to spring 1987. A skeptical review of *The Day After Roswell* argues that the book leans on MJ-12, which surfaced from 1984, and stands or falls with it. No source found discusses the EBE term specifically, or sets Corso's account of coining it against the briefing document or his own affidavit.

## What would settle it

- The full, dated interview the clip comes from, to show whether Corso claimed to have coined the term or only to have used it.
- The passage in *Dawn of a New Age* where Corso discusses the briefing document, and whether the manuscript uses "EBE" before 1987 in any dated draft.
- Any dated use of "extraterrestrial biological entity" before December 1984 in a record whose date can be checked.
