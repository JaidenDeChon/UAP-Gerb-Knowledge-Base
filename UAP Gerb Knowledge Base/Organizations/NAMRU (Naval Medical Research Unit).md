The Naval Medical Research Unit (NAMRU) is a network of US Navy biomedical research laboratories that conduct research and development on infectious diseases, biodefense, and military public health. NAMRU operates under the Navy Medical Research Command and has installations around the world that serve the medical research needs of the Navy and Marine Corps.

## Global Installations

- NAMRU SA: Joint Base San Antonio, Texas
- NAMRU-3: formerly in Egypt, now at Naval Air Station Sigonella, Sicily, Italy
- NAMRU Indo-Pacific: Singapore Naval Base
- NAMRU South (formerly NAMRU-6): offices in Lima and [[Iquitos, Peru]]
- NAMRU-D: Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio

NAMRU installations are multicultural, with researchers and military personnel from many countries. German military and diplomatic visits to NAMRU South's Lima office are documented as recently as 2023.

## Theorized Role in the Peru UFO Crash Incident

Gerb theorizes that NAMRU South in [[Iquitos, Peru]] was where [[Jonathan Weygandt]] was taken, held and quarantined after he was exposed to an unknown viscous liquid leaking from a crashed [[Egg-shaped UAP|non-human craft]] during the 1997 [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]]. Several points support the theory:

- NAMRU South is in Iquitos, within driving distance of the theorized radar station (USAF Detachment 5) where Weygandt was based
- Weygandt described permanent structures at the holding location, not makeshift forward operating bases, which fits an established research facility
- People of several nationalities were at the location (Americans, Chinese, Germans, Peruvians), which matches NAMRU's international research setting
- NAMRU's biodefense research makes it a logical place to quarantine a Marine exposed to potentially hazardous materials from a non-human craft
- After his release, Weygandt was driven back to his original base, which indicates the holding location was within local driving distance

The theory also has acknowledged limitations. Weygandt said the location was underground and that he received no medical procedures despite the potential contamination. He also described the multinational personnel as armed operators rather than diplomats or researchers.
