Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane (NSWC Crane), located in Crane, Indiana, is the US Navy's premier federal laboratory for sensors, electronics, electronic warfare, and special warfare weapons, sometimes described by Senator Joe Donnelly as "the Pentagon's best-kept secret." Spanning 64,000 acres with over 3,000 buildings and extensive underground infrastructure, NSWC Crane is part of Naval Support Activity Crane. Green Beret witness [[RA]] has identified it as the alleged location of the [[Off-World Technologies Division]], which he describes as a classified program for exploiting recovered non-human intelligence technology.

## Overview and Mission

NSWC Crane is part of the Naval Support Activity (NSA) Crane complex, which also includes the US Army Ammunition Activity, the unit that stockpiles much of the US mainland's munitions. The facility was established during World War II. Crane, Indiana was chosen because its landlocked Midwestern location would be hard for enemy forces to target.

### Official Mission

According to official documentation, NSWC Crane's mission is to "provide acquisition and in-service engineering and technical support for sensors, electronics, electronic warfare, and special warfare weapons." It is a Navy federal laboratory with:

- A reputation for national technical leadership
- Direct work with warfighters to build comprehensive, innovative solutions "at the speed of relevance"
- A focus on advanced concepts and technologies as well as conventional military systems

### Operational Focus Areas

Operations at Crane fall into three categories:

1. Strategic Missions
2. Electronic Warfare
3. Expeditionary Warfare

The laboratory puts particular weight on advanced concepts and technologies. One example is the Duke University research funding announcement titled "Long Range Broad Agency Announcement for NSWC Crane."

## Physical Infrastructure

### Surface Facility

The above-ground installation has:

- Over 3,000 buildings across the 64,000-acre complex
- A deliberately run-down, outdated look. According to [[RA]], the surface buildings "look old and dilapidated and beat up... like a 1970s facility"
- Little or deliberately vague labeling on buildings
- The US Army Ammunition Activity's munitions stockpiles on the same site

### Underground Infrastructure

NSWC Crane has extensive underground facilities, including:

- Many underground bunkers for munitions storage
- Underground firing ranges, among them a 100-meter small arms testing range
- Deep underground laboratories and R&D facilities, which [[RA]] described as "so much more advanced than the stuff up top... it looked like a spaceship". They were modern and pristine compared with the surface
- Several security levels, with compartmentalized access, checkpoints to pass and electronic devices to hand over
- Underground areas large enough to need directional signs

According to RA's testimony, an elevator took him down to a depth where the temperature noticeably dropped, which he took to mean "some significant amount below the surface."

## Workforce and Contractor Presence

The people working at NSWC Crane include:

- Federal government personnel who run the Navy laboratory
- Over 2,489 private contract workers, with [[SAIC]] as the largest contractor
- Specialized weapons testing staff, electronic warfare experts and R&D scientists

The facility works closely with private defense contractors on advanced technology.

## Reverse Engineering and Foreign Technology Analysis

NSWC Crane has a documented, acknowledged history of reverse engineering unknown technology.

### AN/SLQ-32 "Slick 32" Black Box

NSWC electromagnetic warfare experts reverse engineered a critical black box component of the AN/SLQ-32 shipboard electronic warfare suite. At first, nobody knew how the device was built, what it did or how to repair it. It was designated "critical technology to maintain fleet readiness".

Reverse engineering this "unknown" device established NSWC Crane's institutional capability and mission to analyze technology whose origins, construction and operating principles are at first incomprehensible. Those are the same skills that analyzing non-human technology would require.

### Foreign and Adversarial Technology

According to [[RA]]'s testimony, NSWC Crane routinely receives:

- Foreign weapon systems captured by CIA operatives in the field
- Adversarial technologies for analysis and assessment
- Experimental systems such as alleged Russian bullets that incorporate biologics

RA asked: "Whatever mechanism they have logistically to get that [foreign tech] from wherever they found it to Crane, why would they not use that same apparatus to get whatever else they find?" His point was that the same CIA-to-Crane logistics chain could deliver recovered UAP materials.

### DoD Reverse Engineering Protocols

NSWC Crane follows DoD guidebooks for "situations such as this", meaning guidance on reverse engineering unknown recovered technology. This indicates that the institution has frameworks for handling devices whose origin and function are unknown at first.

## Special Operations and JSOC Connection

NSWC Crane has close ties to special operations forces:

- It has long worked with the US Army on weapons testing, training and defensive capabilities
- It supported efforts to counter improvised explosive devices in Iraq
- In 2010, SAIC received a $351 million contract specifically to support the Joint Special Operations Response Department (JSOC) at Crane
- JSOC has been identified as the department named, alongside the CIA's Office of Global Access, as tasked with UAP crash retrieval and collections

These ties give NSWC Crane direct institutional links to the operations allegedly responsible for recovering UAP materials, as well as the infrastructure to receive and analyze such materials.

## The Alleged Off-World Technologies Division

According to the firsthand testimony of Green Beret [[RA]], NSWC Crane houses a classified program explicitly called the "Off-World Technologies Division" in a deep underground section of the facility.

### RA's Account (2013-2015)

Between 2013 and 2015, RA, a US Army Special Forces 18 Bravo weapons sergeant with TS/SCI clearance, was sent to Crane for advanced weapons training. During this assignment:

- RA and one other TS/SCI-cleared soldier were separated from their class and taken deep underground
- They had to pass through several checkpoints and hand over all electronics (SCIF-like security)
- RA described the underground area as far more advanced than the deliberately run-down surface buildings
- Directional signs suggested the underground area was large and complex
- A private contractor guide (likely from [[SAIC]], based on who the contractors were) casually called the area the "Off-World Technologies Division"

### Technology Observed

RA reported observing two pieces of alleged non-human technology:

1. A seamless, basketball-sized metallic sphere that appeared to levitate, or to be repelled off its podium
2. A gauntlet-like device, identified as a weapon system, that had discharged an energy pulse during recovery

Both objects were described as:

- Consciousness-interactive, responding to some people but not others
- Beyond the understanding of Crane's "best and brightest" scientists
- Bearing symbols like hieroglyphics that match independent descriptions from [[Daniel Sheehan]]'s 1977 review of classified [[Project Blue Book]] photographs

The private contractor staff talked about the technology casually, which suggests that handling non-human materials was routine for them.

## Connection to SAIC and Consciousness Research

The alleged Off-World Technologies Division's focus on consciousness-interactive technology matches:

- [[SAIC]]'s documented research into anomalous cognition (remote viewing, extrasensory perception)
- SAIC's continued consciousness research after the [[Stargate Program]] ended in 1995
- The U.S. Navy's documented interest in "sixth sense" research (Office of Naval Research, 2012)
- The need for specialized personnel who can interact with or operate recovered technology

This suggests NSWC Crane may be a testing and evaluation facility for consciousness-based technology interfaces derived from recovered UAP systems.

## Hypersonics and Advanced Propulsion Programs

NSWC Crane does advanced propulsion research:

- In 2024, SAIC received a $63 million contract for hypersonic advanced concepts
- In 2023, Crane, [[Sandia National Laboratories]] and other participants took part in a technology transfer for advanced hypersonic technology
- The work connects to [[Salvatore Pais]]' patents and to anti-gravity craft research at [[Naval Air Station Patuxent River|Naval Air Station Pax River]]
- Reverse-engineered UAP propulsion principles could potentially be applied to human-made systems

Attorney [[Daniel Sheehan]] has accused defense contractor [[Radiance Technologies]] of using reverse-engineered UAP technologies in its prompt global strike hypersonic program. SAIC and Sandia are both connected to Crane's hypersonics programs, which suggests a potential technology transfer pipeline.

## Secrecy and Compartmentalization

NSWC Crane uses several layers of secrecy:

- Surface buildings carry no labels, which hides what they are for
- Sensitive programs require TS/SCI access
- Surface infrastructure is deliberately left to deteriorate so it draws no attention
- Several SAIC contract announcements for Crane were scrubbed from the company's website around 2024
- Underground compartments are SCIF-like, with restrictions on electronic devices
- Access is on a need-to-know basis: out of RA's training class of ~10 soldiers, only the 2 with TS/SCI clearance were taken underground

Senator Joe Donnelly's description of Crane as "the Pentagon's best-kept secret" appears increasingly literal rather than metaphorical.

If RA's testimony is accurate, Crane carries that mission further, from analyzing reports to reverse engineering and technologically exploiting recovered materials.
