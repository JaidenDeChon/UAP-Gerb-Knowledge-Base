The USAF Scientific Advisory Group was a body of civilian scientific consultants who advised the United States Air Force on technical and scientific matters relevant to its operations. In UAP history, Gerb names it as one of three bodies that had to receive every [[Project Sign]] report, the other two being the [[Army and Navy Research and Development Board]] and the [[Atomic Energy Commission]]. The names come from Lieutenant General [[Nathan Twining]]'s [[Twining Memo|letter of 23 September 1947]], which recommended that the data of a detailed study be made available for comment to the Army, the Navy, the AEC, the JRDB, the Air Force Scientific Advisory Group, NACA, and the RAND and NEPA projects. Gerb reads the list as a sign that UFO investigations in the late 1940s were thought to need high-level scientific review by both military and nuclear agencies.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the group was one of three bodies that had to receive every Sign report, and treated that as a distribution rule. The list is the recommendation in Twining's letter of 23 September 1947 (paragraph 3a), which proposed sharing the study's data with the Army, the Navy, the AEC, the JRDB, the Air Force Scientific Advisory Group, NACA, and the RAND and NEPA projects; it was not a rule for Sign's reports.
