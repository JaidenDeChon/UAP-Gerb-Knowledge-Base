The Wackenhut Corporation (now G4S Solutions) is a prominent private security firm, founded in 1954, that is closely associated with UAP legacy program operations. The company provided professional security, especially perimeter security, for many US Department of Defense and [[Department of Energy]] installations, including the [[Nevada Test Site]], [[Area 51|Groom Lake (Area 51)]] and [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]]. Wackenhut allegedly acted as a CIA front during the Cold War. The allegation rests on a released March 1963 memo describing the corporation's desire to provide cover for the [[CIA]].

## Board of Directors and Intelligence Connections

Wackenhut's board included many senior US military and intelligence officials. Among the best known:

- [[Bobby Ray Inman]], a four-star US Navy admiral, is a former Director of the NSA, Deputy Director of the CIA, Director of Naval Intelligence and Director of the [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office (NURO)]]. UAP researchers name him as one of the most central alleged gatekeepers of UFO legacy program infrastructure.
- [[Frank Carlucci]] was US Secretary of Defense and Deputy Director of the CIA. Gerb has speculated that Carlucci may have been with [[Brad Sorensen]] in the classified section of the 1988 [[Norton Air Force Base]] air show, where [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|flux liner alien reproduction vehicles]] were seen.
- [[William F. Raborn]] also sat on the board of [[SAIC]] and directed the US Navy's Polaris submarine program. The Polaris program matters because Navy chief scientist [[John Piña Craven]] stated he was ordered to carry out the [[Deep Submergent Systems Project]]. That program was meant to push deep-ocean engineering to far greater depths to meet the needs of [[Project Sand Dollar]], which allegedly cataloged every item of US national security interest on the seafloor that was tagged for retrieval.

According to Edward S. Herman's 1989 book *The Terrorism Industry*, Wackenhut was a revolving door for accomplished military and intelligence professionals, similar to [[SAIC]].

## DOE Special Response Teams

Wackenhut trained and staffed [[DOE Special Response Teams|Department of Energy Special Response Teams (SRTs)]], elite forces trusted with moving US nuclear arsenals across the continental United States. These units could make arrests on site and, at least in the 1980s, wore all-black outfits and gear. According to the General Accounting Office, Wackenhut began subcontracting DOE guard services in 1987. The work included controlling access to sites, patrolling, responding to security alarms, searching property and protecting classified materials. Wackenhut also supplied special response teams to act on the DOE's orders.

## UAP Involvement

Gerb theorizes that the secret armed team [[Rodrik Castle]] ran into during the 1997 [[Hunter Warrior Advanced Warfighting Experiment]] may have been a DOE SRT trained and staffed by Wackenhut. In this theory, the team worked alongside Navy and Air Force alien reproduction vehicle (ARV) programs on domestic UFO crash retrieval exercises. The theory rests on these points:

- The operators' all-black, unmarked appearance matches historical descriptions of DOE SRTs.
- Wackenhut recruited Castle for helicopter teams in 2002, and the company seemed to know non-public details of his service record.
- Wackenhut had an established role providing security at UFO legacy program sites.
- The SRTs had authority across the continental US and could classify operations as transclassified foreign nuclear information under the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]].

The crash retrieval team that detained [[Jonathan Weygandt]] in Peru in 1997 may also have included Wackenhut-trained DOE SRTs, since Wackenhut had set up forward operating bases in Peru in the late 1980s and early 1990s.

In UFO lore, Wackenhut is also known for guarding the perimeter of Area 51. Its guards there famously caught [[Bob Lazar]] and friends as they tried to watch test flights of the "sports model" UFO from nearby Bureau of Land Management lands.

## Richard Mingus Incident

In 1982 Richard Mingus, a Federal Services security guard at the Nevada Test Site who often worked at Area 51, was securing an underground nuclear test area at Area 6. While a live nuclear bomb sat exposed above ground, armed combatants attacked the base. The breach went up the chain of command to President Ronald Reagan as a significant national security issue. The attackers later turned out to be Wackenhut personnel running a drill.
