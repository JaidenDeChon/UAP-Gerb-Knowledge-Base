The West Desert Test Center (WDTC) is one of the United States Army's eight Major Range and Test Facility Bases (MRTFBs). It is located at Dugway Proving Ground in Utah and operated by the US Army Test and Evaluation Command (ATEC). The 23 designated MRTFBs form the "critical core" of DOD test and evaluation infrastructure. WDTC handles chemical and biological defense testing, nuclear, biological and chemical (NBC) survivability evaluation, and aerospace systems testing across more than 1,250 square miles of restricted range in Utah. According to a 2013 GAO report, about 77% of acknowledged WDTC work is done for the Joint Services Chemical and Biological Defense Program (CBDP), and the remaining 23% is made up of other classified acquisition test and training programs. UAP researchers allege that WDTC is the Army component of a joint Army-Air Force UAP legacy program. In their account, its materiel exploitation is centered at Avery Technical Center, with underground storage and study in the alleged DUMB beneath Granite Peak.

## Structure and Organization

WDTC operates under Army Test and Evaluation Command (ATEC). Its operations are led by a Test Director, a post currently held by Ryan W. Harris, a former Battelle Memorial Institute project manager. WDTC sits outside the conventional Army research and development test and evaluation (RDT&E) chains. The Office of Management and Budget and the Office of the Secretary of Defense mandated this separation, ostensibly to ensure unbiased evaluation and warfighter safety. The same independence may also leave WDTC programs with less oversight.

ATEC reports to the Secretary of the Army, the Chief of Staff and the Vice Chief of Staff. It also has direct lines to the US Secretary of Defense through the Director of Operational Test and Evaluation, which bypass traditional RDT&E oversight mechanisms.

## Major Facilities

WDTC includes several important installations at Dugway Proving Ground (DPG).

[[Ditto Technical Center]] is the operational heart of WDTC. It houses range operations, contractor facilities (including Jacobs Solutions), material test facilities, defensive test chambers and the Garrison building, where witness MS was interrogated for six hours after his 2009 encounter at Avery.

Avery Technical Center is a 40-acre restricted facility bordering Michael Army Airfield. It was historically used for radiobiological warfare studies and for handling radioactive materials for the Atomic Energy Commission. Avery has an internal rail system, clean room facilities, classified storage (Building 1016), test operations (Building 1010) and a tunnel entrance (Building 102) that may connect to the alleged DUMB. Although it is a major installation, it is conspicuously absent from DPG visitor guides.

Michael Army Airfield is shared by the Army and the Air Force and is used for aerospace testing, including past support for the Lockheed Martin X-33 hypersonic program. It is operated jointly with Hill Air Force Base's 388th Range Squadron.

## Mission and Acknowledged Programs

WDTC's main acknowledged mission is chemical and biological defense testing for the Joint Services Chemical and Biological Defense Program (CBDP), which a 2013 GAO report put at about 77% of operations. This work includes:

- Testing chemical and biological defense equipment and systems
- Nuclear, biological, and chemical (NBC) survivability evaluation
- Development and validation of defense materiel and protective systems
- Training operations for Special Forces and other military units
- Use of chemical and biological agents and simulants under controlled conditions

The other 23% of acknowledged operations is described as "acquisition test and training programs". That phrasing is vague enough to leave room for classified aerospace, materials science or compartmented programs beyond standard biochem defense work.

## Major Range and Test Facility Base Designation

WDTC is one of eight Army MRTFBs, out of 23 across DOD. The designation makes it part of the "critical core" of DOD test and evaluation infrastructure that must be preserved as a national asset. The MRTFB network has:

- Over 10 million acres of land in the United States
- Over 25,000 employees annually
- Over $4 billion in acknowledged annual funding
- 23 installations across Army (8), Navy (6), Air Force (7), and Defense Agency (2) facilities

## Leadership and Battelle Connections

Current and former WDTC leaders have extensive personnel ties to Battelle Memorial Institute.

Ryan W. Harris, the current WDTC Director, was a Battelle project manager at Dugway (2000-2004) before moving to direct employment at DPG, and he had risen to WDTC Test Director by 2012. He holds a bachelor's in chemical engineering and served as an Army chemical officer.

Greg Frank, a former WDTC chemist, worked as a chemist for Dugway's Chemical Laboratory Division (1984-85). He then joined Battelle as a project manager and principal investigator, and eventually became Executive VP for Battelle Science and Technology International, overseeing 5,000+ scientists and engineers.

Anders Woborg, a former director of WDTC's Special Programs Division, was a Battelle test director for chemical and biological defense equipment before he led that division. He now works for DOE.

This two-way flow of people between WDTC and Battelle, especially in chemistry-heavy roles, suggests deep operational integration between Dugway's chemical testing infrastructure and Battelle's broader defense programs.
