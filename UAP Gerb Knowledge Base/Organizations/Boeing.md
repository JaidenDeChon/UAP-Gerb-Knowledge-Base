Boeing is an American multinational aerospace and defense corporation and one of the major contractors alleged to have participated in the development of the [[TR-3B]] and other reverse-engineered craft. [[Edgar Fouché]] identified Boeing alongside [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Northrop Grumman]], and Teledyne Ryan as primary contractors involved in compartmentalized R&D for the TR-3B program.

Boeing is one of three contractors with classified RDT&E access to [[Air Force Plant 42]] in Palmdale, California, alongside Northrop Grumman and Lockheed Martin. Director of [[Edwards Air Force Base]] 412th Electronic Warfare Group, witness "Ed," stated he worked alongside Boeing and Lockheed on reverse-engineered craft systems at the [[Nellis Air Force Base|Nellis range]]. Stealth aircraft researcher John Joseph claimed Boeing served as the primary contractor in Project Brilliant, a [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)]] program with the goal of deploying an electromagnetic shield around the United States using a boomerang-shaped craft capable of loitering over cities.

## Corporate Consolidation During the "Last Supper" (1996–1997)

UAP Gerb's *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* identifies Boeing's acquisitions of Rockwell (its aerospace and defense units) and McDonnell Douglas in 1996–1997 as part of the defense industrial base "Last Supper" — the 1993–1997 consolidation, prompted by Secretary of Defense Les Aspin and Deputy Secretary William Perry's 1993 warning that only a handful of contractors in each defense sector would survive post–Cold War budget cuts, that shrank the number of major US defense contractors from 51 to five. Boeing emerged as one of the resulting "big five" alongside [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Northrop Grumman]], Raytheon, and General Dynamics.
