The Strategic Air Command Elite Guard was a US Air Force security police unit created in December 1956 to guard the headquarters of Strategic Air Command (SAC) at Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska. General [[Curtis LeMay]], then commander of SAC, proposed it. Its duties were to secure the headquarters, to protect SAC's commander, vice commander and their staff, and to represent the command at military and civic events. LeMay personally chose a blue beret for the unit, which became the first Air Force unit to wear one. It was inactivated in 1966.

## The blue berets at Kecksburg

Gerb discusses the Elite Guard in his video on the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg crash]]. Residents described several kinds of military men at the site, among them Air Force personnel in blue fatigues and blue berets, and volunteer fireman [[Jim Romansky]] said the military arrived within about 15 minutes of him. Gerb offers the Elite Guard as one of three candidates for the blue-bereted men. In his description they were air police in blue fatigues and blue berets who guarded SAC headquarters and bases from 1956 to 1966, so they were still active in December 1965. One SAC base was [[Lockbourne Air Force Base, Columbus, Ohio|Lockbourne Air Force Base]], where a guard later said the Kecksburg object was taken.

The other two candidates are the rapid-reaction crash retrieval unit that [[Leonard Stringfield]] called the [[Blue Berets]], and the 1041st Security Police Squadron, which made the Air Force blue beret famous in Vietnam during Operation Safeside. Gerb leaves open which, if any, took part in the retrieval.
