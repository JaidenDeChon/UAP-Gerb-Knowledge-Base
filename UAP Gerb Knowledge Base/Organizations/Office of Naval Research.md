The Office of Naval Research (ONR) is the US Navy's science and technology agency, which funds and manages basic and applied research for the Navy and Marine Corps through its headquarters in Arlington, Virginia and regional offices. In UAP research it is alleged to be the first custodian of craft recovered from the sea. According to sources cited by journalist [[Christopher Sharp]] in 2024, undersea craft are "transferred to the Office of Naval Research, which subsequently hands them over to defense contractors for detailed analysis".

## Alleged role in naval UFO programs

In UAP Gerb's four-pillar model of Navy UFO legacy programs (monitor, collect, store and exploit), ONR is the **store** stage. Craft retrieved at sea by the [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]], the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]], [[SOCOM]] and contractor submersibles are allegedly taken to ONR locations. From there they are passed, through [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)|FFRDCs]] and university-affiliated research centers, to defense contractors. The host theorises that naval programs are run out of ONR and the [[Office of Naval Intelligence]].

The host links several witness accounts to this pipeline:

- **Great Lakes, 1973.** A witness identified as RK told [[Leonard Stringfield]] that he saw a teardrop-shaped craft in a Quonset hut at Great Lakes Naval Base, allegedly shot down off Hawaii and recovered by the Glomar Explorer (see [[Project Azorian]]). The host notes that ONR's Chicago office lies less than 40 miles away.
- **Kissner's naval sources.** In 1994 former New Mexico state representative [[Andrew Kissner]] said sources at the Naval Research Laboratory and White Sands told him that discs recovered in 1947 went to the [[Atomic Energy Commission]]. He also said the Naval Research Laboratory supported the program established under Truman.
- **Project Sundance.** The [[Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution]], named by Sharp as supplying deep-submergence vehicles for retrievals, manages ONR projects such as Project Sundance.

The host also asks whether the Army and Air Force stage their own recovered craft at naval or ONR sites, calling ONR "almost a staging point for housing craft" if Sharp's reporting is correct.

## Public statements on UFOs

In 1951 ONR physicist Urner Liddel stated in a magazine article that all flying-saucer reports were mirages or balloons that fooled trained observers. The host cites this as the start of decades of official Navy denial. In 2013, by contrast, ONR published what it called a "revolutionary" program studying a human sixth sense to train personnel in intuitive decision-making. The host connects this to [[Eric A. Walker]]'s claim that working in UFO programs required knowledge of ESP.
