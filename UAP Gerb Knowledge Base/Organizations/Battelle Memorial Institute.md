Battelle Memorial Institute is a private nonprofit applied sciences and technology development company headquartered in Columbus, Ohio. It was founded in 1929 with a bequest from industrialist Gordon Battelle and does contract research for government, military, and commercial clients. Its stated mission is to "deliver scientific discovery and applied research that makes the world a better place to live." Battelle manages several U.S. Department of Energy national laboratories, including [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]], Oak Ridge National Laboratory, and the National Renewable Energy Laboratory, as well as the National Biodefense Analysis and Countermeasures Center. Its work has also included uranium fabrication research for the [[Manhattan Project]], development of the photocopy machine and the UPC barcode, and military research into defense against biological and chemical weapons. Within UAP research, Battelle is alleged to have been deeply involved in analyzing UFO material, in classified investigations, and in suppressing the narrative, from at least 1949 through the modern era.

## UFO Investigation — Project Stork (1952–1955)

Battelle's earliest documented link to organized UFO investigation is [[Project Stork]], a classified system for machine-indexing official UFO sighting reports. The [[Air Force Technical Intelligence Center]] (ATIC) commissioned it in 1952. Dr. [[Howard C. Cross]] of Battelle wrote a memorandum dated January 9, 1953, and classified Secret, proposing that Project Stork "assist the Air Force in reassuring the public that everything is well under control with respect to the UFO problem." The memo mentioned Captain [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], who was then director of [[Project Blue Book]].

Project Stork ran alongside Blue Book and analyzed Air Force UFO cases independently, outside the official panel structure. Dr. [[J. Allen Hynek]] tried to discuss it when he testified at the [[Robertson Panel]] in January 1953. The Computer UFO Network (CUFON) later recovered seven Project Stork status reports.

On May 5, 1955, ATIC issued [[Project Blue Book Special Report No. 14]], a statistical analysis of 3,201 UFO cases prepared with "a panel of scientists both in and out of the Air Force". The report does not name Battelle, though Gerb describes it as published by Battelle and ATIC. It found it "highly improbable" that any of the reports represented technology "outside the range of present-day scientific knowledge", and emphasized that there was "a complete lack of any valid evidence consisting of physical matter" in any case it studied. UAP researchers, Gerb among them, argue that Battelle's own research on [[Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium Alloy)]] in 1949 directly contradicts this.

## Nitinol Research and the Roswell Connection (1949)

In 1949, [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] contracted Battelle for titanium-alloy research under contract AF 33(038)-3736 (which Gerb reads as 33-38-3736). The progress reports, titled "Research and Development on Titanium Alloys", cover September 1 to October 21, 1949 by Gerb's reading, and he says they deal at length with nitinol, a nickel-titanium alloy with superelastic and shape-memory properties. Nitinol was not publicly discovered until 1961, when Dr. William J. Buehler documented it at the Naval Ordnance Laboratory. According to Gerb, the 1949 Battelle research carried the [[Atomic Energy Commission]]'s "restricted" label rather than a classification, a private-sector classification tier that even people with Special Access Program clearances could not access. It was not declassified until 2010.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions stated as fact that Battelle analysed shape-memory titanium alloys under contract 33-38-3736 and that the work was classified. The reports are catalogued as "Research and Development on Titanium Alloys", contract AF 33(038)-3736. That they deal with nitinol is Gerb's reading, and Gerb says the work was labelled "restricted", not classified.

Nitinol is a metal that cannot be permanently deformed and returns to its original shape under stress. These properties match intelligence officer [[Jesse Marcel]]'s descriptions of the anomalous debris recovered from the alleged [[Roswell Crash|1947 Roswell crash]]. Marcel inspected the debris field with rancher [[Mac Brazel]], and the recovered material was later taken to [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. UAP researchers argue that Battelle's 1949 contract, which came from Wright-Patterson and covered an alloy with exactly these properties a decade before nitinol's official discovery, indicates that the research was done on Roswell crash material.

## EJ Center

EJ Center (Elroy John Center), a senior research chemical engineer at Battelle from 1939 to 1957, was one of the authors of the "Analytical Methods for Titanium Based Alloys" subsection of the 1949 Battelle progress report. In 1992, a witness told the [[MUFON|Mutual UFO Network]] (MUFON) that in the late 1950s, while he was dating Center's daughter, Center told him he "was responsible for a project which required him to study Parts retrieved from a flying saucer." Dr. [[Irena Scott]] published the account in "The Ohio UFO Crash Connection and Other Stories", a 1994 article in the Ohio UFO Notebook.

The account matters because of when Center gave it: around 1958, the year after he left Battelle, and more than five decades before the 1949 nitinol research was declassified in 2010. The 1994 MUFON report therefore could not have been fabricated to fit the classified documents, because those documents were unavailable. Center's private disclosure and the later declassification of his actual 1949 contract work on the same material came about independently. Together they are cited as the strongest single piece of evidence linking Battelle to the analysis of Roswell crash material.

## Vallée and Fox Identification

On December 4, 2020, on episode #1574 of the Joe Rogan Experience, computer scientist and UAP researcher [[Jacques Vallee]] mentioned a private contractor in possession of recovered UFO materials but declined to name it. Documentary filmmaker [[James Fox]] then hinted at the organization's identity by mentioning its connection to the 1953 [[Robertson Panel]]. Only Battelle Memorial Institute fits that clue, because it was the contractor running Project Stork at the time of the panel.

## Modern Connections — Ronald Moultrie and AARO

[[Ronald S. Moultrie]], who served as Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USD I&S), has been a member of Battelle's advisory board since 2016. As USD I&S, Moultrie announced the creation of [[AARO]] in July 2022 and appointed [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] as its director. Both AARO and Kirkpatrick reported directly to Moultrie's office.

In December 2022, Moultrie's office oversaw the award of a $1.9 million "AARO Support Services" contract to [[Sand Corp]], a firm that specializes in whistleblower suppression and leak prevention. On December 16, 2022, Moultrie and Kirkpatrick held a joint public press conference at which they said they had found no evidence of crashed UFOs of extraterrestrial or extra-dimensional origin.

POGO.org documented Moultrie's seat on Battelle's advisory board and saved screenshots of his online professional biography before he removed the reference from his LinkedIn profile. UAP researchers, including Gerb, argue that Moultrie had a conflict of interest: he was suppressing UAP disclosure through AARO while serving on the board of the organization alleged to hold recovered UAP materials. They argue that this conflict is a structural foundation of the continuing secrecy around UAP.
