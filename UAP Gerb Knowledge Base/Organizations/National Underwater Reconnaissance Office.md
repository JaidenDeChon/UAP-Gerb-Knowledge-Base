The National Underwater Reconnaissance Office (NURO) is a classified US intelligence agency established in 1969 through an agreement between the Central Intelligence Agency and the United States Navy. It is the underwater counterpart to the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)]], and one of very few US government agencies protected in their entirety by a [[Special Access Programs (SAPs)|Special Access Program]]. Its existence was not publicly acknowledged until fragments of information began to appear in intelligence publications and congressional documents. As of the most recent available information, it remains classified.

## Origins and Mission

NURO was established in 1969, and its first director was Secretary of the Navy John Warner. According to author Jeffrey T. Richelson's *The US Intelligence Community*, NURO was created "as a means of managing the conduct of submarine intelligence missions and exploitation of their product." Its founding missions included:

- Recovery of sunken submarines (most prominently the Soviet K-129, recovered under [[Project Azorian]] by the Hughes Glomar Explorer in 1974)
- Tapping of Soviet underwater communication cables (the IVY BELLS program)
- Ocean floor mapping under a program designated Project Desktop
- Imagery and SIGINT collection from submarines under a program once called the Special Navy Control Program

The CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology was one of NURO's two founding agencies. According to the book *Blind Man's Bluff: The Untold Story of American Submarine Espionage*, "From the day NURO was formed, the CIA took charge," though the Navy regained some operational authority by 1972 when Warner formally assumed the directorship.

## Organizational Structure and Secrecy

The Association of Former Intelligence Officers describes NURO as one of very few examples of "an entire government agency" protected by a Special Access Program, which puts it in the same category as special mission units of the Joint Special Operations Command. The agency appears in declassified documents only in fragments. A 1975 submission to the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence lists NURO alongside the NRO in a National Security Decision Memorandum 40, and CIA director Richard Helms's tenure is identified as coinciding with NURO's founding. Gerb argues that NURO was hidden within the [[Navy Special Program]], which a 1974 National Security Council intelligence directive lists beside the National Reconnaissance Program.

Author Jeffrey T. Richelson's FOIA requests about NURO were denied. The agency's then-current director, [[Bobby Ray Inman]], was warned in a "pretty hot letter" not to discuss NURO publicly even after his retirement, because "all [activities] were still classified."

## Key Personnel

Known or claimed NURO directors include:

- **John Warner**: Secretary of the Navy; first director, from NURO's founding in 1969
- **[[Bobby Ray Inman]]**: Director of Naval Intelligence; became NURO's director on 13 September 1974, and is the only person to have publicly admitted serving in that role
- **John Lehman**: Secretary of the Navy; NURO director from about 1981 to 1987, according to a senior CIA officer who confirmed covert submarine operations in Scandinavian waters under Lehman's direction

## Key Assets

NURO has used several specialized vessels for clandestine undersea operations:

- **USS Halibut**: ran clandestine SIGINT and recovery missions, including the first IVY BELLS cable-tapping operations
- **USS Seawolf**: deployed for classified intelligence collection in restricted waters
- **USS Parche**: succeeded the Halibut in undersea special operations
- **Hughes Glomar Explorer**: built by the CIA at an estimated modern cost of $1.68 billion and used to recover the K-129 submarine under Project Azorian; later leased to Lockheed Missiles and Space Company for ostensible "deep-sea mining" operations through Ocean Minerals Company
- **NR-1**: the US Navy's first nuclear-powered deep submergence vessel, launched in 1969, the year NURO was founded. Its official missions were search, object recovery, geological survey and oceanographic research, including the publicly acknowledged 1976 recovery of an F-14 lost from a carrier
- **USS Jimmy Carter**: a modified Seawolf-class submarine with a multi-mission platform section, which Gerb identifies as a likely current NURO asset

## Role in Alleged UFO and USO Retrieval

In a September 2024 article, [[Christopher Sharp]] of *Liberation Times* named NURO as a participant in undersea craft retrieval programs. Sharp first redacted this reporting and then restored it because of threats. It described NURO as working with the CIA's Directorate of Operations and Directorate of Science and Technology, the US Navy, SOCOM, and the [[Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution]] to retrieve objects from beneath the oceans.

[[Bobby Ray Inman]] described NURO's mission as covering "imagery of the ocean floors, looking for anything which might be desirable from an intelligence point of view". UAP researchers read that wording as broad enough to include non-human craft. In separate private disclosures in 1989, Inman referred NASA mission specialist [[Bob Echler]] to [[Everett Heinman]], Deputy Director of the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology (DS&T), as the right contact for questions about recovered craft. These disclosures are treated as connecting the NURO director directly to the CIA DS&T's alleged management of recovered non-human vehicles.

Gerb's video also presents an alleged 1973 UFO retrieval in waters between Hawaii and the mainland, attributed to the Glomar Explorer, as a likely NURO operation. The timing of NURO's founding (1969), the creation of the [[Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicle (DSRV)|DSRV]] program under the [[Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP)]] (1964–1972) and the alleged [[Bluegill Triple Prime UFO Recovery]] (1962) are cited together as evidence that the Navy and the CIA were systematically building infrastructure to retrieve non-human craft from under the sea.

## Relationship to the Navy Special Program

Gerb argues that NURO is likely part of the [[Navy Special Program]] (NSP), an umbrella control system for the Navy's clandestine undersea operations. The NSP first appears in a 1974 National Security Council intelligence directive beside the National Reconnaissance Program. An 11 December 1975 memo for the Deputy Director of Central Intelligence describes it as running parallel to the National Reconnaissance Program under the Executive Committee for the Director of Central Intelligence and the Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence. Under this theory, craft recovered alongside NURO and sent to the [[Office of Naval Research]] would be handled by programs compartmented within the NSP. The overlap in timeframe, mission scope and personnel supports this thesis: Secretary of the Navy Lehman was both NURO director and a figure referenced in NSP documents.
