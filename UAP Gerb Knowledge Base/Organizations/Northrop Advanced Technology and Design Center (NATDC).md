The Northrop Advanced Technology and Design Center (NATDC) was an advanced aircraft design organization within the Northrop Corporation, and later [[Northrop Grumman]], based at Northrop's Pico Rivera plant in Los Angeles County, California, the main site of B-2 bomber development and production. It existed by at least 1991 and was formed to keep Northrop's post-B-2 design expertise together and apply the stealth bomber's breakthroughs to new airframes. A 1995 book described it as responsible for "originating, coordinating, and directing all Northrop research, technology development, and design efforts" in future manned and unmanned aircraft. UAP Gerb calls it Northrop's answer to [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works|Lockheed's Skunk Works]].

## Known Projects and People

A handout at the 1991 Dayton Air Show and a 1992 *Aviation Week & Space Technology* article show NATDC concepts including an F-16 derivative, multirole and high-reliability fighters, a low-cost fighter, AX and CASX designs, an arrowhead AX, a composite short-takeoff-and-vertical-landing (STOVL) wing, and a special-purpose stealth transport for special operations forces. UAP Gerb could find only three people tied to the center:

- [[Irving T. Waaland]], leader of the B-2 design team, who gave the 1991 AIAA Wright Brothers Lecture as NATDC's vice president and chief designer.
- Delbert H. Jacobs, a retired Air Force brigadier general, listed in 1995 as NATDC's vice president and center manager.
- Richard Carrión, a propulsion engineer who led propulsion or airframe teams on NATDC's affordable lightweight fighter, a STOVL joint tactical aircraft funded through IRAD, and a multirole fighter.

## Disappearance

Mentions of NATDC thin out after 1992; the last the video finds are a 1994 NASA report, a 1996-1997 directory entry and a 1999 NASA paper. In 2000 or 2001 Pico Rivera's laboratories moved to El Segundo under a new Advanced Systems Development Center, which worked on the "company-funded" X-47A Pegasus. By 2003 both names were gone.

UAP Gerb alleges that NATDC did not close but went fully black, continuing inside Northrop's aeronautics division at [[Air Force Plant 42]] as the company's in-house builder of [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicles]] and derivative airframes, funded through [[Independent Research and Development (IRAD)]] with little accountability to a government sponsor. Gerb also compares NATDC's concepts to the airframes [[Brad Sorenson]] described at the [[Norton Air Force Base Air Show]].
