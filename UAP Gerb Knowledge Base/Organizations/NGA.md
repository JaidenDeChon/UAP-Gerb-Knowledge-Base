The National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA) is a US intelligence agency and Department of Defense combat support agency that collects, analyses and distributes geospatial intelligence (GEOINT): imagery, imagery intelligence and geospatial information describing physical features and activities on Earth. It is one of the large US intelligence agencies, overseen within the DoD by the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security, and it operates the imagery that satellites built by the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|National Reconnaissance Office]] collect. [[David Grusch]] served there as a senior intelligence officer, and reviewing overhead collection was, he has said, one of his primary tasks.

## Role in Gerb's videos

The NGA has been speculated to be a possible source agency for the information in the Coyame (Tenet) report, and theorized to be a combat support agency providing intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance for UFO crash retrieval operations.

In [[Videos/NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering/summary|NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering]], Gerb explains GEOINT as the foundation other intelligence is layered on: a shared map of what is happening at a given place and time, drawn from satellites and drones but also from elevation data, infrared and vector maps. In 2015 the NGA entered a partnership with the [[National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)|National Air and Space Intelligence Center]] and embedded an NGA Support Team (NST) at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. The team gives NASIC and the Air Force advocacy, oversight and guidance on GEOINT policy and programs and serves as the GEOINT functional manager at Wright-Patterson, with image analysts, image scientists, geospatial analysts and computer-aided design specialists. It supports NASIC's Geospatial Intelligence Analysis Squadron (GSI), the unit of Grusch's late friend [[Matthew Sullivan]].

Gerb calls GEOINT "the crown jewel of intelligence" for crash retrieval: a recovery team needs it to locate a downed craft and to plan where to stage, deploy and rig the craft for transport. He believes the overhead imagery of crash sites that Grusch would not discuss in open session in 2023 came from NASIC's GSI, and that GSI and the NGA team are core contributors to [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)|Immaculate Constellation]], supplying GEOINT and signature intelligence while diverting detections away from standard channels.
