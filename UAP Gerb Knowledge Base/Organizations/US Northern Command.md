United States Northern Command (USNORTHCOM or NORTHCOM) is a unified combatant command of the United States Department of Defense responsible for homeland defense, civil support, and security cooperation in the North American theater. NORTHCOM was established on October 1, 2002. Its area of responsibility includes the continental United States, Alaska, Canada, Mexico, and surrounding waters. The command is headquartered at Peterson Space Force Base in Colorado Springs, Colorado. [[NORAD]] has its headquarters at the same location, and the two commands coordinate closely on operations.

## Oversight of NORAD

US Northern Command is the parent command of [[NORAD]] (North American Aerospace Defense Command). NORAD's aerospace warning and space surveillance missions are folded into NORTHCOM's wider homeland defense responsibilities. Because of this relationship, NORTHCOM has oversight authority over NORAD's [[Fast Walkers|Fast Walker]] detection operations and classified satellite tracking data.

## 2013 FOIA Denial to John Greenwald

In 2013, [[John Greenewald]] of [[The Black Vault]] submitted a FOIA request to US Northern Command for documentation on Fast Walkers, which are objects detected by [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites as they enter or leave Earth's atmosphere. NORTHCOM replied that Fast Walker documentation is "currently and properly classified" under Executive Order 13526. It said such information is "considered critical to National Defense or foreign policy and is thus controlled by the US government."

Although it withheld the Fast Walker documentation as classified, NORTHCOM did release two unclassified research papers:
1. "Orbit Determination of Sunlit Illuminated Objects Detected by Overhead Platforms" (1989) by [[Richard P. Osedacz]]
2. "Space-Based Satellite Tracking and Characterization Utilizing Non-Imaging Passive Sensors" (2008) by [[Bradley R. Townsend]]

Both papers confirmed that ballistic missile early warning satellites (DSP) have been detecting Fast Walkers since 1972. The specific detection events and data remain classified.

## Pattern of Denial

NORTHCOM's 2013 denial follows the pattern that [[NORAD]] set in 1979, when it answered [[Barry J. Greenwood]] and [[Lawrence Fawcett]], and that [[US Space Force]] continued in its 2023 denial to Greenwald. The answers have stayed the same across decades and organizational changes, which shows an institutional policy of withholding Fast Walker data regardless of public disclosure trends on other UAP topics.
