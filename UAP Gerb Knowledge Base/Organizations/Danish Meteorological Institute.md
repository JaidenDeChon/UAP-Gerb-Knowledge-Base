The Danish Meteorological Institute (DMI; Danish: *Danmarks Meteorologiske Institut*) is Denmark's national meteorological service. It handles weather forecasting and climate research, and it collects maritime meteorological data from vessels in Danish and international waters. In UAP and USO (unidentified submerged object) research, the Institute is known for publishing the nautical meteorological annual that preserved the 1909 account of the [[Maritime Light Wheel]] seen by Captain Gabe of the SS Bintang in the Strait of Malacca. *Scientific American* later cited the case in issue 1065 as a significant anomalous maritime phenomenon.

## Maritime Observation Archive

As part of its operational mandate, the Danish Meteorological Institute collected observations sent in by ships' captains on international shipping lanes. Its nautical meteorological annuals gathered reports of weather, sea state and unusual phenomena from a wide geographic range. The 1909 Bintang light wheel case in the Institute's annual shows how anomalous maritime phenomena of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries came to be recorded by national meteorological bodies. These scientific bodies had no particular interest in UAP-type events and no mandate to document them, yet they kept the accounts because the reports were observational data of possible relevance to atmospheric and oceanographic science.

DMI records also cite the 1910 case of the Dutch steamship Valentin, in which Captain Brier and multiple officers watched a hovering, horizontal, wheel-like object above the South China Sea. That makes the Institute's annual a documentary source for at least two separate [[Maritime Light Wheel]] encounters from the same period.
