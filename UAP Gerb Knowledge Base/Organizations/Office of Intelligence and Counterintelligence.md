The **Office of Intelligence and Counterintelligence** (OICI) is the intelligence arm of the [[Department of Energy]] and the DOE's primary element within the U.S. Intelligence Community. OICI traces its institutional roots to World War II, when the government sought to collect intelligence on German nuclear weapons efforts while protecting the secrecy of the [[Manhattan Project]]. The office is responsible for protecting DOE's national laboratories, plants, and other facilities from foreign intelligence threats, and provides scientific and technical expertise to the broader intelligence community. In 1992, OICI was formally integrated into the Intelligence Community through a provision of the Intelligence Authorization Act.

OICI's mission is to protect the vast scientific and technical knowledge held in DOE's laboratories and secure facilities, which is intellectual property of significant national security value, while also enabling and informing national intelligence efforts. The office has staff embedded at DOE's major national laboratories and works closely with the [[National Nuclear Security Administration]] (NNSA) and its subordinate institutions.

## Alleged Role in UAP Legacy Programs

Within UAP research, OICI is named as the DOE body alleged to run UAP legacy program operations directly at [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)|Federally Funded Research and Development Centers (FFRDCs)]] within the [[Nuclear Security Enterprise (NSE)]]. According to this claim, OICI's intelligence mandate and its deep operational presence within DOE national laboratories, together with the DOE's unique classification authority under the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]], make it the natural choice to manage highly compartmented, non-standard programs that sit outside normal intelligence community structures.

[[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]] is named as a DOE FFRDC where OICI allegedly runs UAP legacy program operations, and [[Sandia National Laboratories]] is cited in the same way. The reasoning offered is that OICI's embedded presence inside DOE labs lets it manage compartmented programs under "Restricted Data" classification. This legal category, established by the Atomic Energy Act, places covered materials beyond presidential Executive Order authority and outside FOIA reach. This would make OICI-managed programs significantly harder to access or audit than comparable programs run by the Department of Defense or the CIA.

### Classification Mechanisms and the Oak Ridge Claim

Gerb, who calls the office "DOE Int", presents its existence as the one takeaway from his Sandia investigation: "the Department of Energy has its own intelligence agency." He notes that OICI supports all 17 DOE national laboratories and offices, including the three NNSA laboratories, and that the NNSA conducts some intelligence activities on its behalf. OICI manages foreign intelligence information (FII), which can carry caveats such as WNINTEL, ORCON and NOCONTRACT, the last barring release to contractors and consultants. Gerb asks whether NOCONTRACT material may still be released to government-owned FFRDCs such as Sandia, which would make it a channel for passing recovered material, and even bodies, to the laboratories while keeping it from contractors.

The Oak Ridge claim comes from sources Gerb describes as intimately involved with the laboratory. They say two recovered non-human discs are held in a top secret facility beneath the [[Y-12 Complex]], that one program there studies the "skin" of UFOs, possibly involving Battelle and MITRE, and that OICI is the program manager. Gerb stresses that he cannot yet support this and that the source is still being vetted.
