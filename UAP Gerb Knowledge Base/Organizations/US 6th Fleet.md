In 1979, during the Iranian Hostage Crisis, the US 6th Fleet was stationed near the Mediterranean Sea. It was proposed as the source of the electromagnetic interference that scrambled the avionics of Spanish Air Force pilot [[Fernando Cámara]]'s Mirage F1 while he pursued UFOs in the [[Manises UFO Incident]].

## Role in Manises Incident Explanations

[[Fernando Cámara|Cámara]]'s instruments failed during his intercept mission on November 11, 1979. Official explanations blamed the avionics jamming on powerful electronic signatures from two LPH Iwo Jima-class helicopter carriers of the US 6th Fleet, which was in the Mediterranean as part of U.S. naval operations during the Iranian Hostage Crisis.

Cámara rejected this explanation outright. He said he was too far from the fleet for its electronic systems to interfere with his flight navigation, radio and emergency control systems. The US 6th Fleet explanation also did not account for:

- The truncated cone-shaped craft Cámara saw with his own eyes
- The object's evasive behavior at supersonic speeds (Mach 1.4)
- The active jamming of Cámara's infrared missile targeting systems when he tried to lock onto the UFO
- The onboard alert warning that continuous wave missile radar was locking onto him

Nor did it address the commercial aircraft encounter that came before the military intercept, or the three radar signatures, each with an estimated diameter of 200 meters.

## Significance

The US 6th Fleet explanation follows a common pattern in explanations of UFO cases, in which nearby military operations are put forward as the mundane source of an anomalous event even when pilot testimony and technical details contradict that attribution.
