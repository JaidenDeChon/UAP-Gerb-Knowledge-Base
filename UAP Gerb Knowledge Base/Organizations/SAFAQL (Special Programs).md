SAF/AQL, the Directorate of Special Programs, is the office under the Assistant Secretary of the Air Force for Acquisition, Technology and Logistics ([[SAFAQ|SAF/AQ]]) that manages the Air Force's most sensitive acquisition work. In its own mission language, it directs special access program (SAP) advanced technology development programs and monitors new technologies that may meet operational requirements, in coordination with the science-and-technology directorate [[SAFAQR (Science, Technology and Engineering)|SAF/AQR]]. It is the principal acquisition adviser to the Secretary, Under Secretary and SAF/AQ on all SAP matters, and it liaises with other services, defense agencies and federal departments to "cross-fertilize" advanced technologies and concepts.

## Role in the Hidden Wing thesis

In *The Hidden Wing*, UAP Gerb names SAF/AQL as one of three SAF/AQ directorates he believes are central to Air Force UFO legacy programs, alongside SAF/AQR and [[SAFAQX (Acquisition Integration)|SAF/AQX]]. Gerb stresses that AQL plans, programs, directs and accomplishes research, development, test and evaluation ([[RDT&E (Research, Development, Test and Evaluation)|RDT&E]]) for advanced air, space and cyber capabilities. He reads the published statement that AQR and AQL jointly coordinate RDT&E SAPs and "monitor new technologies" as the place where deep, waived programs on technologies of unknown origin would sit. He argues that the directorates carry out that tasking through the [[Rapid Capabilities Office (RCO)]].

The video also notes that [[Randall G. Walden]], now director of the RCO, previously held several posts in the Air Force Directorate of Special Programs, including deputy chief of its advanced technology division, chief of its special studies division and technical director.
