Space Technology Laboratories (STL) was the Ramo-Wooldridge, and later [[TRW]], organization that led the systems engineering of the first US intercontinental ballistic missiles and, later, the Titan missile programs. It began as a subsidiary and soon became a division of TRW. In 1960 roughly half of it was spun off as the [[Aerospace Corporation]], because rival contractors complained that STL's Air Force advisory role gave TRW unfair access to technology. The remainder was renamed TRW Systems Group, which ran TRW's entire electronics and defense sector and whose units now form most of [[Northrop Grumman]]'s Mission Systems and Space Systems sectors.

## Alleged Legacy Program Relevance

Gerb treats STL as the corporate bridge between 1950s crash retrieval and today's Northrop Grumman. Kingman witness [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]] named [[Edward Bushnell Doll]] as the man who summoned him to the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]]. Doll joined STL in 1955 and was elected its vice president in 1959. He stayed on as the division became TRW Systems Group, rising to senior vice president in 1966 and retiring as executive vice president in 1977. The video argues that whatever legacy knowledge Doll brought to STL passed through TRW Systems Group into Northrop Grumman after the 2002 acquisition.

The same video likens the 1960 split of STL and the Aerospace Corporation to [[SAIC]]'s 2013 spin-off of [[Leidos]]: two organizations with shared personnel, practices and access to government special access programs, which Gerb alleges includes UFO legacy programs.

## Project West Wing

In [[Videos/NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering/summary|NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering]], Gerb says that STL staffed Project West Wing, an Air Force effort to analyse Soviet intelligence and technology, established with the help of General Bernard Schriever's Western Development Division and funded and administered through the [[Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC)|Air Technical Intelligence Center]] at Wright-Patterson; he cites a 1990 National Air and Space Museum interview with Schriever. STL's vice president at the time was [[Edward Bushnell Doll]], named as coordinator of the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|1953 Kingman retrieval]]. Gerb says West Wing passed to the [[Aerospace Corporation]], spun off from STL in 1960, where it continues as the corporation's strategic and global awareness directorate, and asks whether West Wing had an off-books UFO element run by TRW, connected to the alleged [[Operation Zodiac|Zodiac]] program.
