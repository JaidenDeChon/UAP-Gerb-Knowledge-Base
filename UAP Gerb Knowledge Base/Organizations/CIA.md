The Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) is the United States' primary civilian foreign intelligence service, established in 1947 under the National Security Act. UAP researchers study the CIA because it was involved early in assessing what UFO reports meant for national security and helped shape public policy on the phenomenon. It is also alleged to have been connected to crash retrieval programs and reverse engineering efforts through specialized directorates.

## Robertson Panel and UFO Policy

In January 1953, the CIA convened the [[Robertson Panel]], a scientific advisory committee tasked with evaluating the national security risks posed by public interest in UFOs during the [[UAP Mass Wave of 1952–1954]]. The panel recommended a systematic campaign to reduce public fascination with UFOs through education and media management, a policy widely cited as the institutional origin of the UFO stigma. The recommendation was carried out in coordination with the [[US Air Force]], [[Project Blue Book]] and public affairs operations. It was followed in August 1953 by [[USAF Regulation 200-2]], which centralized UFO reporting away from public oversight.

## Alleged UAP Program Involvement

UAP researchers allege that the CIA's [[Directorate of Science and Technology]] (DS&T), under deputy directors including O'Sullivan, was involved over the long term in exploiting non-human technology and coordinating crash retrievals. The agency co-founded the [[National Reconnaissance Office]] with the [[US Air Force]], which set up institutional channels for compartmented aerospace surveillance and materials analysis programs. The CIA is also alleged to operate an underground facility (STIFF) beneath the U.S. Army training center in Warrenton, Virginia. Some researchers connect the facility to classified UAP-related operations.

## National Reconnaissance Office Connection

The CIA played a leading part in creating the [[National Reconnaissance Office]] (NRO) in 1961, and in the NRO's early years the CIA Deputy Director also served as NRO Deputy Director. Because one person held both posts, the satellite reconnaissance programs had continuity with any covert intelligence operations on UAP tracking or retrieval. Researchers allege that the NRO was a main gatekeeper of UAP crash retrieval knowledge and of satellite detection of anomalous objects.
