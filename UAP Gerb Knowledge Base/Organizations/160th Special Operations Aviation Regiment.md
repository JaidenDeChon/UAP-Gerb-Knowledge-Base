The 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment (SOAR), known as the Nightstalkers, is a US Army special operations aviation unit headquartered at Fort Campbell, Kentucky. It specializes in night operations, low-altitude flight, and the insertion, extraction and resupply of special operations forces. Its fleet of modified aircraft includes MH-60 Black Hawks, MH-47 Chinooks and MH-6 Little Birds, and it supports the most sensitive US special operations missions around the world. The regiment is made up of several battalions, each assigned to support particular geographic combatant commands and special operations elements.

## Notable Operations

The Night Stalkers have deployed around the world on sensitive missions, including the recovery of a downed Soviet helicopter in June 1988 and support to Operation Desert Storm from Panama in 1991. They often work with [[JSOC (Joint Special Operations Command)|Joint Special Operations Command]].

## Alleged UAP Involvement

According to claims examined in UAP research, only the 1st Battalion of the 160th SOAR is allegedly involved in UFO crash retrieval operations, and the other battalions are reportedly unaware of this mission. The 1st Battalion is alleged to provide helicopter transport (CH-47s and MH-47s) for crash retrieval rapid reaction teams, carrying personnel and recovered materials to secure facilities. This compartmentalization inside a single regiment, with one battalion conducting crash retrievals while its sister battalions know nothing of them, is cited as an example of the extreme siloing alleged to characterize UFO legacy program operations. The umbrella authority directing these missions is alleged to be the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)]], rather than the standard JSOC or Air Force special operations chains of command.

### The 1997 Peru retrieval

In his video on the 1997 [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]], Gerb considered the 160th SOAR as a possible source of the US Army CH-47 Chinooks that carried the [[Department of Energy]] [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|NEST]] team to the crash site witnessed by [[Jonathan Weygandt]]. He favored the [[1st Battalion 228th Aviation Regiment]] of [[Joint Task Force Bravo]] instead. 1-228th Aviation had a provable CH-47 presence in the [[US SOUTHCOM]] theater, while the Night Stalkers were based at Fort Campbell, Kentucky, too far away to reach Peru within the roughly nine-hour window of the retrieval. He left open the possibility that Night Stalker elements were pre-positioned in the region as a standing rapid-recovery capability. In his later investigation of the NRO, he returned to the 160th and named its 1st Battalion as the likely transport arm of an NRO-directed crash retrieval task force.
