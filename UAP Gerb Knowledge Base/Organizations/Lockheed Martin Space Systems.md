Lockheed Martin Space Systems Company was a division of [[Lockheed Martin]] overseeing roughly $9 billion in program infrastructure spanning space science, remote sensing, and defensive and strategic systems research and development. The division is alleged to have held recovered UAP craft and materials since the 1950s and, under vice president [[James T. Ryder]], to have sought to transfer some of that material to government custody via the proposed [[Kona Blue]] program.

## Alleged UAP Involvement

UAP Gerb's *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* identifies [[James T. Ryder]] as "Lockheed Martin Space Systems company vice president" at the time he championed the divestment of UAP materials to [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]] via the proposed [[Kona Blue]] prospective special access program, facilitated through the [[DHS (Department of Homeland Security)|Department of Homeland Security]]'s Directorate of Science and Technology. The video theorizes that Ryder — described elsewhere as something of a maverick, citing post-retirement speeches such as "Garments of the Gods" — sought outside expertise to help crack a particularly difficult set of materials that Lockheed's own Space Systems division had reportedly failed to make exploitation progress on for decades.
