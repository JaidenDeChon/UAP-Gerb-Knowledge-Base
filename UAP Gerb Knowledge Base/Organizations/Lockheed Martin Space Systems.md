Lockheed Martin Space Systems Company was a division of [[Lockheed Martin]] that oversaw roughly $9 billion in program infrastructure, covering space science, remote sensing, and research and development of defensive and strategic systems. The division is alleged to have held recovered UAP craft and materials since the 1950s. Under its vice president [[James T. Ryder]], it allegedly sought to transfer some of that material to government custody through the proposed [[Kona Blue]] program.

## Alleged UAP Involvement

UAP Gerb's *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* identifies [[James T. Ryder]] as "Lockheed Martin Space Systems company vice president" at the time he pushed for UAP materials to be divested to [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]]. The divestment was to go through the proposed [[Kona Blue]] prospective special access program, handled through the [[DHS (Department of Homeland Security)|Department of Homeland Security]]'s Directorate of Science and Technology. The video theorizes that Ryder sought outside expertise to help crack a particularly difficult set of materials, on which Lockheed's own Space Systems division had reportedly failed to make any exploitation progress for decades. Ryder has been described elsewhere as something of a maverick, on the strength of post-retirement speeches such as "Garments of the Gods".
