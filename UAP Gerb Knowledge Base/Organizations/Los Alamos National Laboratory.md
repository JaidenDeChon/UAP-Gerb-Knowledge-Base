Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) is a United States Department of Energy national laboratory and federally funded research and development center in Los Alamos, New Mexico, operated for the [[National Nuclear Security Administration]]. It was founded in 1943 as Project Y of the [[Manhattan Project]], the secret laboratory where [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]]'s scientists designed the first atomic bombs. It remains one of the three primary US nuclear weapons laboratories, alongside [[Sandia National Laboratories]] and [[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory]]. In UFO research, and especially in Gerb's work, Los Alamos is named as one of the national laboratories that allegedly took custody of recovered non-human craft and biological remains from the late 1940s onward.

## History

General [[Leslie Groves]] chose the remote Los Alamos Ranch School on the Pajarito Plateau for the weapons laboratory Oppenheimer requested, and named Oppenheimer its scientific director. Its ordnance engineering arm, **Z Division**, was set up at Sandia Base in Albuquerque in 1945 and later became Sandia. After the war the laboratory passed to the [[Atomic Energy Commission]] and then to the [[Department of Energy]]. It was managed by the University of California for most of its history, and since 2018 has been managed by Triad National Security, a partnership that includes the [[Battelle Memorial Institute]]. The laboratory had a permanent security presence during the Manhattan Project. Gerb points to the heavily armed Los Alamos Protective Forces of the 1980s as its modern equivalent.

## Alleged role in UFO legacy programs

According to former New Mexico state representative [[Andrew Kissner]], recovered UFOs of the 1940s and 1950s were analyzed by teams under [[Vannevar Bush]] in cooperation with the AEC's Los Alamos Scientific Laboratory and its Z Division at Sandia Base. Kissner also says that a classified executive order signed by [[Harry Truman]] in July 1948 vested custody of recovered technical vehicles in the AEC and its national laboratories. Gerb treats Los Alamos, with Sandia and [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory|Oak Ridge]], as part of the FFRDC and GOCO layer of his legacy program "pyramid," which holds material while controlling what contractors learn. An alleged 1947 Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit report names AEC scientists, including Oppenheimer, as part of the Roswell retrieval effort.

Other accounts point the same way. Engineer [[Bill Uhouse]] said the occupants of the craft recovered near Kingman, Arizona in 1953 were taken to Los Alamos, while the craft went to [[Area 51]]. [[Oak Shannon]], a former manager of special projects at Los Alamos, facilitated the meeting described in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] and attended the 1985 Advanced Theoretical Physics conference, whose notes discussed legacy programs. Los Alamos also designed the [[Nuclear Subterrene|nuclear subterrene]] tunneling concept linked to claims about deep underground facilities, and green fireball reports around the laboratory prompted the Air Force's [[Project Twinkle]].

Gerb also notes that Los Alamos is one of the facilities that receives non-regulatory nuclear material collected by the NNSA's Nuclear Forensics Material Analysis Program within [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|NEST]]. He theorizes that the NNSA uses such channels, together with the [[Office of Secure Transportation (OST)|Office of Secure Transportation]] and [[DOE Special Response Teams (SRTs)|special response teams]], to recover and move UFO material under the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]].
