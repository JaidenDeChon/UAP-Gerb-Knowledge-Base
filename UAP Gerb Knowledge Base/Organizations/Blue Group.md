The Blue Group is one of two distinct factions within the United States Government studying unidentified anomalous phenomena, according to disclosures made by an anonymous insider to whistleblower [[Michael Herrera]]. Unlike the [[Black Group]], the Blue Group operates with proper congressional oversight and represents the publicly acknowledged (or semi-acknowledged) face of government UAP investigation.

## Characteristics

The Blue Group consists of UAP investigation programs that operate within legal and constitutional frameworks:

- **Congressional Oversight**: All Blue Group programs answer to Congressional oversight committees and operate within acknowledged government structures
- **Public Acknowledgment**: While specific details may be classified, the existence of Blue Group programs is generally known to the public and to elected representatives
- **Institutional Legitimacy**: Blue Group programs operate through official channels and are subject to standard government accountability measures

## Known Blue Group Programs

According to the insider's framework, Blue Group programs include:

- **[[AARO]]** (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office) — Current DOD/intelligence community UAP investigation office
- **[[AATIP]]** (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program) — Previous Pentagon UAP investigation program
- **[[Project Blue Book]]** — Historical Air Force UAP investigation program (1952-1969)

## Relationship to Black Group

The Blue Group operates in parallel to but separate from the [[Black Group]], which was allegedly founded in the 1940s to reverse engineer non-human intelligence technology and operates without congressional oversight. The existence of two separate groups studying the same phenomenon suggests institutional fragmentation and potential information compartmentalization, with the Black Group allegedly possessing significantly more advanced knowledge and technology than the Blue Group has access to.

## Credibility and Context

The Blue Group/Black Group framework was disclosed to Michael Herrera by an anonymous insider following Herrera's public testimony about his 2009 Indonesia UAP encounter. While investigator [[Joey Is Not My Name]] verified that Herrera was indeed taken to a secure facility to meet this insider, the truthfulness of the insider's disclosures remains unverified. The framework may represent:
- Accurate insider knowledge of USG UAP program structure
- Disinformation designed to shape public understanding
- The insider's personal interpretation rather than official nomenclature
