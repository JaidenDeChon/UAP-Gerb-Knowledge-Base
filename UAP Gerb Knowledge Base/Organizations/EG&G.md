EG&G (Edgerton, Germeshausen, and Grier) was an American defense and technology corporation and a major contractor for the [[Department of Energy]] and the US military. It managed operations at the Nevada Test Site and is widely associated with classified programs at Area 51.

## UAP Connections

EG&G turns up in UFO lore in several ways:

- [[Bob Lazar]] claimed EG&G employed him to try to reverse-engineer UFO propulsion devices at Area 51's S-4 facility.
- EG&G's Nevada parking lot was allegedly where the meeting recorded in the 2002 Wilson-Davis meeting notes took place. The notes are disputed but likely accurate, and they are an important document in UAP disclosure research.
- EG&G was a corporate team member of [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|NEST]], the Nuclear Emergency Support Team, alongside [[Los Alamos National Laboratory|Los Alamos National Labs]], [[Sandia National Laboratories]], [[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory|Lawrence Livermore National Labs]], and Raytheon. That puts EG&G in the same operational framework that is theorized to have responded to the 1997 [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]].
- EG&G allegedly took part in Project Redlight on behalf of the DOE.

## Connection to DARC and Project Red Light

EG&G was awarded an indefinite contract for [[Project Redlight|Project Red Light]] to support the [[Department of Energy]] and the US military. Under it, EG&G was responsible for helping recover nuclear materials after mishaps and for providing aerial and ground security at highly classified government and military sites. A witness described Project Red Light to [[Leonard Stringfield]] in his crash retrieval status reports, claiming that the project tested NHI and reverse-engineered craft on the [[Nellis Air Force Base|Nellis test range]] after the craft had been shipped from [[Edwards Air Force Base]]. EG&G was also involved in building and operating the [[Defense Advanced Research Center (DARS)|Defense Advanced Research Center (DARC)]], the underground facility at [[Area 51]] where [[Edgar Fouché]] worked on reverse-engineered craft technologies.

## Sandia and the Nevada Test Site

At the Nevada Test Site, EG&G and its special projects division provided timing, control and high-speed photography for nuclear tests. They worked closely with [[Sandia National Laboratories]] and also worked on numerous classified special access programs. From 1972 to 1992 EG&G's test-site photo lab documented Sandia's unclassified work in some 20,000 images, which Gerb went through for his Sandia investigation. The images include tunnels, weapon depots, control rooms at [[Area 6 (Nevada Test Site)|Area 6]] and Area 19, and Janet Airlines aircraft. In 1990 senior EG&G manager [[Alfred O'Donnell]] told [[George Knapp]] that a recovered saucer and a live being were held at [[Indian Springs Air Force Base]] until 1955 and then moved beside Area 51. Gerb describes [[Donald M. Kerr]] as the person who would know whether EG&G hired Bob Lazar. Kerr, a former Los Alamos director and a MITRE trustee and SAIC director, was EG&G's president from 1989 to 1992. Through a series of transactions, EG&G's assets have since passed to Amentum.
