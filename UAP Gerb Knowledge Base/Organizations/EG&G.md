EG&G (Edgerton, Germeshausen, and Grier) was an American defense and technology corporation that operated as a major contractor for the [[Department of Energy]] and the US military. The company managed operations at the Nevada Test Site and is widely associated with classified programs at Area 51.

## UAP Connections

EG&G is deeply embedded in UFO lore through multiple connections:

- **[[Bob Lazar]]** — Lazar claimed EG&G employed him to attempt to reverse-engineer UFO propulsion devices at Area 51's S-4 facility
- **Wilson-Davis Meeting** — EG&G's Nevada parking lot allegedly served as the meeting place for the disputed but likely accurate 2002 Wilson-Davis meeting notes, a key document in UAP disclosure research
- **[[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|NEST]] Partnership** — EG&G was a corporate team member of the Nuclear Emergency Support Team alongside [[Los Alamos National Laboratory|Los Alamos National Labs]], [[Sandia National Laboratories]], [[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory|Lawrence Livermore National Labs]], and Raytheon. This connection places EG&G within the same operational framework theorized to have responded to the 1997 [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]]
- **Project Redlight** — EG&G has been alleged to have participated in Project Redlight on behalf of the DOE

## Connection to DARC and Project Red Light

EG&G was awarded an indefinite contract for [[Project Redlight|Project Red Light]] to support the [[Department of Energy]] and the US military. This contract gave EG&G responsibility to assist in the recovery of nuclear materials in cases of mishaps and to provide aerial and ground security for highly classified government and military sites. Project Red Light was described to [[Leonard Stringfield]] in his crash retrieval status reports by a witness claiming the project served to test NHI and reverse-engineered craft on the [[Nellis Air Force Base|Nellis test range]] after craft had been shipped from [[Edwards Air Force Base]]. EG&G was additionally involved in the construction and operations of the [[Defense Advanced Research Center (DARS)|Defense Advanced Research Center (DARC)]], the underground facility at [[Area 51]] where [[Edgar Fouché]] worked on reverse-engineered craft technologies.

## Sandia and the Nevada Test Site

At the Nevada Test Site, EG&G and its special projects division provided timing, control and high-speed photography for nuclear tests and worked closely with [[Sandia National Laboratories]], as well as on numerous classified special access programs. From 1972 to 1992 its test-site photo lab documented Sandia's unclassified work in some 20,000 images, which UAP Gerb went through for his Sandia investigation. They include tunnels, weapon depots, control rooms at [[Area 6 (Nevada Test Site)|Area 6]] and Area 19, and Janet Airlines aircraft. In 1990 senior EG&G manager [[Alfred O'Donnell]] told [[George Knapp]] that a recovered saucer and a live being were held at [[Indian Springs Air Force Base]] until 1955 and then moved beside Area 51. [[Donald M. Kerr]], EG&G's president from 1989 to 1992, a former Los Alamos director, and a MITRE trustee and SAIC director, is described by the host as the person who would know whether EG&G hired Bob Lazar. EG&G's assets have since passed, through a series of transactions, to Amentum.
