The Defense Threat Reduction Agency (DTRA) is a US Department of Defense combat support agency responsible for countering weapons of mass destruction and chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear and high-explosive (CBRNE) threats. Established in 1998, it supports the US nuclear enterprise alongside the [[National Nuclear Security Administration]] and its national laboratories, and has a substantial presence at White Sands, the [[Nevada National Security Site (Nevada Test Site)|Nevada National Security Site]] and [[Kirtland Air Force Base, New Mexico|Kirtland Air Force Base]]. According to UAP Gerb, it employs more than 2,100 people, over two thirds of them contractors, on an annual budget of about $2 billion. Its stated mission is to deter strategic attack on the United States and its allies, to prevent, reduce and counter WMD and emerging threats, and to prevail against WMD-armed adversaries.

## Lineage

DTRA is the latest in a line of agencies that began with the [[Manhattan Project]]:

- [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]], 1947-1959, headquartered at Sandia Base in Albuquerque
- Defense Atomic Support Agency, 1959-1971
- Defense Nuclear Agency, 1971-1996, which began supporting the [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|Nuclear Emergency Support Team]] in 1974
- Defense Special Weapons Agency, 1996-1998
- Defense Threat Reduction Agency, from 1998

## Alleged UFO Connections

Whistleblower [[Dylan Borland]], who worked as an all-source analyst at DTRA, said on the *Weaponized* podcast that on his last day there, the official collecting DTRA's records on extraterrestrials in response to congressional records legislation pulled documents from the systems in Borland's SCIF, including old [[Atomic Energy Commission]] files. Those files, he said, concluded that UFOs were extraterrestrial and that "this isn't exactly for our benefit."

In his investigation of [[Sandia National Laboratories]], UAP Gerb argues that DTRA's descent from AFSWP, the organization that former New Mexico state representative [[Andrew Kissner]] and the disputed [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] documents both place at the 1947 recoveries, adds weight to Borland's account. He also notes that DTRA is among the customers of the top secret sensor and drone testing at [[Area 6 (Nevada Test Site)|Area 6]], that it works with Sandia and the NNSA on programs such as Global Threat Reduction, and that it works with the NNSA's [[Office of Secure Transportation (OST)|Office of Secure Transportation]] and [[DOE Special Response Teams (SRTs)|Special Response Teams]] on "safe havens". He recalls that [[Ryan W Harris]], director of the West Desert Test Center at [[Dugway Proving Ground]], was once a project engineer on DTRA-sponsored work. The host concludes that DTRA is probably a support agency, acting under the cover of national nuclear security, for the NNSA's alleged crash-retrieval and transport logistics.
