The Defense Support Program (DSP) is a United States Air Force satellite constellation originally designed to detect ballistic missile launches with infrared sensors. DSP satellites operate in geosynchronous orbit approximately 22,000 miles above Earth. They have been a central part of America's early warning system since the first one (DSP-1) was launched in 1970. TRW (later acquired by [[Northrop Grumman]]) was heavily involved in developing them.

## Technical Capabilities

DSP satellites carry extremely sensitive infrared sensors that can detect small heat sources and reflected light, both on Earth's surface and in space. The sensors were built to spot missiles, but they have proved able to track much smaller objects as well, including ones moving through space at extreme speeds. DSP data is relayed to [[NORAD]] and has been recorded since at least 1972.

## UAP Detection and Fast Walkers

Besides their stated mission of detecting missiles, DSP satellites have been tracking anomalous objects that enter or leave Earth's atmosphere at extreme speeds. [[NORAD]] and [[US Northern Command]] designate these objects "[[Fast Walkers]]". Multiple Defense Department papers from 1972 onward document the system's ability to detect them.

[[Bob Fish]], a [[US SOUTHCOM|USG]] contractor who managed highly classified government communications systems in the 1980s and 1990s, wrote to Clinton campaign manager [[John Podesta]] in a 2015 email that later came out in the [[WikiLeaks]] leaks of Clinton campaign emails: "One of the government programs that collects hard data on unidentified flying objects is the USAF DSP satellite program."

Fish described an incident in which DSP personnel detected a [[Fast Walkers|Fast Walker]] that "entered our atmosphere from deep space" and "made a 30° course correction turn," which pointed to controlled flight rather than the ballistic path of a meteor.

## Notable Detection Cases

[[Joe Stefula]], a former Army Criminal Investigation Command contact, relayed a [[1984 DSP Indian Ocean Detection|May 5, 1984 incident]] in which a DSP satellite allegedly detected a UAP heading toward Earth at 22,000 mph. According to the account, the object passed within 1.8 miles of the satellite before abruptly changing course and flying back into outer space.

Satellite sensor technicians [[Lee Graham]] and [[Ron Regehr]] at [[Aerojet]] confirmed that DSP satellites tracked the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]]. They found a computer printout showing 238 scans (approximately 39.7 minutes of continuous tracking) of an anomalous object in Iranian airspace.

## Role in Recovery Operations

DSP satellites, managed by the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|National Reconnaissance Office]], have a documented record of tracking objects for recovery operations. In a 1996–1997 incident, [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|DOE NEST]] used DSP satellite intelligence from US Space Command to track debris from the Russian Mars 96 probe over South America. The incident established a pattern of DSP/NEST coordination for recovery operations.

## Aerospace Corporation Partnership

The 2014 DSP fact sheet notes that "researchers at the [[Aerospace Corporation]] have used DSP to develop portions of a hazard support system that will aid Public Safety in the future." Researchers including [[Jacques Vallee]], [[Ross Coulthart]], [[Kit Green]], [[Hal Puthoff]], and [[Eric Davis]] suspect that the Aerospace Corporation, based in [[Long Beach, California]], is one of the legacy private contractors engaged in covert UAP research.

## Classification and FOIA Denials

All requests for DSP data related to [[Fast Walkers]] have been denied under Executive Order 13526, which protects information "critical to National Defense or foreign policy." Both [[US Space Force]] and [[US Northern Command]]/[[NORAD]] have repeatedly denied [[John Greenewald]] of [[The Black Vault]] access to Fast Walker data, citing national security exceptions.
