Martin Marietta was an American aerospace and defense corporation, founded in 1961 when the Glenn L. Martin Company merged with the American-Marietta Corporation. Lockheed Corporation acquired it in a merger on March 15, 1995, forming [[Lockheed Martin]], the world's largest defense contractor. Martin Marietta and its predecessor, the Glenn L. Martin Company, are implicated in early US anti-gravity research, and their documented corporate lineage is relevant to alleged UAP reverse engineering programs.

## Glenn L. Martin Company and Anti-Gravity Research

The Glenn L. Martin Company, the predecessor that helped create Martin Marietta, was a site of documented anti-gravity research in the mid-1950s. In 1955, George Trimble, the company's Vice President for Aviation and Advanced Propulsion Systems, recruited physicist [[Lou Whitten]] into the Research Institute for Advanced Studies (RIAS), the company's internal advanced research organization. Trimble's explicit brief was to develop anti-gravity propulsion. Whitten later stated that the research team discovered techniques to harness anti-gravity, including experiments with isotopes of bismuth. Journal records confirm that the research took place.

## Mergers and UAP-Relevant Acquisitions

Before it merged with Lockheed, Martin Marietta made several acquisitions in the early 1990s that are relevant to alleged UAP legacy programs:

- 1993: Acquired General Electric Aerospace. GE has been implicated in UAP-related programs since World War II.
- 1993: Acquired General Dynamics Space Systems Division.
- 1993: Acquired management contracts for [[Sandia National Laboratories]], a Department of Energy FFRDC that has repeatedly been implicated in UAP material exploitation and in the alleged reverse engineering of TR-3B propulsion systems.

The 1995 merger of Lockheed and Martin Marietta therefore put two corporations under single management, each with its own decades-long alleged involvement in UAP programs, and the management of Sandia National Laboratories came with them.
