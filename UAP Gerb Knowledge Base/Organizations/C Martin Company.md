C Martin Company is a Department of Defense contractor that handles facility operations and maintenance, logistics, construction, and range operations management at military installations. At Dugway Proving Ground it works as a prime subcontractor based near the administration center, providing maintenance, painting, signage, electrical services, and facilities support across the 1,250-square-mile installation. C Martin employed the witness "MS" from 2009-2013 as a maintenance painter and sign technician, during which time MS was assigned routine meter-reading duties that led to his encounter with an alleged extraterrestrial craft at Avery Technical Center in 2009. C Martin's mission is to "provide a fine-tuned approach to managing people, time, and resources to provide quality services while creating value for our customers." The company works extensively in DOD facility operations, range operations and maintenance, construction, and logistics support.

## Services and Capabilities

C Martin Company provides facility and range support services to Department of Defense installations. Its core capabilities are:

- Facility operations and maintenance: day-to-day upkeep, repair, and maintenance of the buildings, utilities, and infrastructure on military installations
- Logistics: supply chain management, materiel handling, and logistics support for military operations
- Construction: design-build and construction management for military facilities
- Range operations and maintenance: support for test ranges, training areas, and evaluation facilities, which matters most at installations like Dugway that have extensive test ranges

The company works as both a prime contractor and a subcontractor on various DOD programs, and it has a documented presence at multiple Army, Air Force, and joint installations.

## Operations at Dugway Proving Ground

C Martin has a significant presence at Dugway Proving Ground. It works from near the administration center, and its responsibilities cover the entire 1,250-square-mile installation. According to a 2021 Dugway mission and capabilities overview, the installation employs 784 primarily Army and Air Force contractors out of 2,120 total personnel. C Martin likely makes up a substantial portion of this contractor workforce.

C Martin's work at Dugway includes:
- Maintenance and painting of facilities in all areas of Dugway, including Avery Technical Center, Ditto Technical Center, and Michael Army Airfield
- Electrical services and meter reading for government billing (approximately 52% of Dugway is contractor-controlled, so usage has to be tracked precisely)
- Signage and wayfinding support
- General facilities maintenance and repair
- Access to numerous facilities across the installation for maintenance purposes

For routine maintenance work, C Martin employees at Dugway typically hold proximity badges (level 5 in MS's case) instead of security clearances, even though they work close to highly classified facilities and programs.

## Witness MS Employment (2009-2013)

C Martin employed former US Navy serviceman [[MS (Dugway Witness)|"MS"]] from 2009 to 2013 as a maintenance painter and sign technician at Dugway Proving Ground. His duties included:

- Facilities maintenance, painting, and signage across the installation
- Reading the electrical meters of approximately 15 facilities so the US government could bill contractors
- Access to numerous buildings and areas across Dugway with his level 5 proximity badge
- Travel to Dugway from Kearsville (Kville), Utah in company vans with other contractors

About four months into his employment in 2009, MS was assigned to read electrical meters in place of his absent apprentice. The routine task took him to Avery Technical Center, where he entered an unmarked "ghost building" (believed to be Building 1010, Test Operations). Inside, he encountered an alleged extraterrestrial disc craft hovering in a clean room, surrounded by scientists in white coats and armed guards.

After six hours of interrogation by men in black suits at Ditto Technical Center's Garrison building, MS was released and kept working at Dugway through 2014. When he left C Martin in 2014, MS lost contact with his source "GP", a senior DOD chemist working in legacy operations.

## Significance in UAP Legacy Program Context

As a facilities maintenance contractor at Dugway, C Martin is relevant to alleged UAP legacy programs in several ways.

C Martin employees like MS had access without clearance. Their proximity badges let them into numerous facilities for maintenance work, but they held no security clearances. Contractors doing routine maintenance could therefore come across highly classified programs by accident, which is exactly what happened to MS at Avery.

C Martin also maintains classified facilities. Its responsibility for facilities across Dugway necessarily includes buildings that house classified programs, clean rooms, and sensitive operations. Its staff would routinely enter restricted areas for electrical, HVAC, painting, and other maintenance, and could be exposed to compartmented programs without meaning to be.

The presence of C Martin maintenance personnel throughout Dugway also gives operational cover for the movement of materials, personnel, and equipment. Maintenance vehicles, contractors entering various buildings, and routine facilities work would not draw attention, even near highly sensitive locations like Avery.

Meter reading is a weak point for intelligence. Tracking electricity usage for government billing requires contractor access to every building, including those that house classified programs, and MS's meter-reading assignment shows that operational security vulnerability. This routine administrative task is what led MS, unintentionally, to his breakthrough encounter.

MS was a maintenance contractor with a proximity badge and no security clearance, yet he could accidentally wander into a building that housed an alleged extraterrestrial craft. That suggests either that security protocols were inadequate, or that so many contractors work at Dugway that full compartmentation is impractical for routine maintenance operations.

## Post-MS Encounter Security Implications

After MS's 2009 encounter, C Martin's operations at Dugway presumably came under greater scrutiny. MS was interrogated for six hours but was released and allowed to keep working at the installation through 2014. This suggests either that his maintenance access was considered too important to operations to revoke, or that the authorities concluded his encounter was an isolated incident.

MS was able to discuss his experience with colleagues (including making contact with "GP") while still employed by C Martin. This indicates that the company's personnel were not subjected to extreme compartmentation or debriefing after the incident, possibly because C Martin's leadership was not fully briefed on the nature of MS's encounter.
