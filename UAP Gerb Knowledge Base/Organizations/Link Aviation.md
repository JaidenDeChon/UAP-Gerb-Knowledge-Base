Link Aviation was an American aviation simulation company best known for making the Link Trainer, the first mass-produced flight simulator widely adopted by the U.S. military for pilot training. Edwin Link founded the company in Binghamton, New York. During World War II and the Cold War era it became a major defense contractor in flight simulation technology.

In UAP research, Link Aviation is known as the company through which [[Bill Uhouse]] claimed he was recruited to work on a flying disc simulator at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. According to Uhouse, a company representative invited him to help build simulators, including ones for the F-102 and the B-47, and later a flying disc simulator modeled on the craft recovered in the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]]. In Uhouse's account, Link Aviation connects conventional defense contracting to classified reverse-engineering programs related to UAP.
