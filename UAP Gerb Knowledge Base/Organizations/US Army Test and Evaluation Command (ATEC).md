The **US Army Test and Evaluation Command (ATEC)** is the Army command responsible for independent developmental and operational testing and evaluation of Army weapons and equipment. It is headquartered at Aberdeen Proving Ground, Maryland, and runs the Army's test centers and ranges, including the [[West Desert Test Center]] at [[Dugway Proving Ground]], White Sands and Yuma, several of which are [[Major Range and Test Facility Bases (MRTFBs)|major range and test facility bases]].

## Independence from Army RDT&E

Army test and evaluation is kept separate from the Army's ordinary research, development, test and evaluation ([[RDT&E (Research, Development, Test and Evaluation)|RDT&E]]) chain. Gerb reports that the separation is mandated by the Office of Management and Budget and the Office of the Secretary of Defense, to keep evaluations unbiased, protect warfighters and preserve credibility with decision-makers. Above ATEC, the Secretary of the Army and the Army Chief and Vice Chief of Staff answer to the Secretary of Defense, alongside OSD's own Director of Operational Test and Evaluation.

## In UAP research

Gerb argues that ATEC's independence could also mean weaker oversight of its programs, including those at Dugway's West Desert Test Center, which he believes host a UFO legacy program. He suggests the white-coated technicians witness [[MS (Dugway Witness)|MS]] saw around a hovering saucer at [[Avery Technical Center]] in 2009 were ATEC and [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] scientists. He also cites an ATEC security specialist posting at Dugway whose duties include serving as alternate special security officer for sensitive compartmented information programs.
