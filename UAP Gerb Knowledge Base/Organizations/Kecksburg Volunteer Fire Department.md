The Kecksburg Volunteer Fire Department is the volunteer fire company of [[Kecksburg, Pennsylvania]], in Mount Pleasant Township, Westmoreland County. Its firemen were among the first people to reach the object that came down in the woods near the village on 9 December 1965, in the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|Kecksburg crash]].

## In Gerb's video

In *The 1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania UFO Crash*, Gerb tells how the Kecksburg company was sent to search the woods for what was thought to be a downed aircraft. Volunteer fireman [[Jim Romansky]] heard by walkie-talkie that the first rescue team had found the site. When his team arrived, they found an object half-buried at the end of a 25-foot trench, a seamless bronze acorn or bell with a raised band of hieroglyph-like markings. After 10 or 15 minutes, two crew-cut men in trench coats declared the area restricted and ordered the firemen out, and uniformed troops came marching down through the woods as they left. Romansky said that when they walked the mile or so back to the fire hall it was "wall to wall military", with trucks, jeeps, cars and guards on the door.

[[Bill Bully Bush|Bill "Bully" Bush]], who reached the object first, said he fled the woods when he heard the firemen approaching. A clip in the video shows a man who had been a Kecksburg fireman for 28 years saying that "the military was here" and that everybody was right about what they saw and heard.

Gerb also mentions an assistant chief of the fire department who got within 75 yards of the object without a clear view, and who wondered whether it was a man-made capsule such as a Gemini. Gerb asks whether the men in trench coats who ordered the firemen away might have been [[Eric A. Walker]]'s party.

## Background

The fire department hosts the annual Kecksburg UFO Festival each July on its grounds, as a fundraiser for the fire company. A replica of the acorn-shaped object, built for the 1990 *Unsolved Mysteries* segment on the case and later given to the town, is displayed at the fire hall.
