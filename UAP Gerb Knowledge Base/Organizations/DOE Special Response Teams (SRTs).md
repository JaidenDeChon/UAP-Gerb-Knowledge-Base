Department of Energy Special Response Teams (SRTs) are elite tactical units that guard US nuclear weapons and materials. They were trained and historically staffed by [[Wackenhut Corporation]] (now G4S Solutions). SRTs are entrusted with transporting the nation's nuclear arsenals across the continental United States, and they have the authority to make arrests on site. At the height of their operations in the 1980s and 1990s, SRT members reportedly wore all-black clothing and gear with no identifying insignia.

## Operations and Authority

According to a General Accounting Office report, Wackenhut began subcontracting DOE guard services in 1987. The work included controlling access to sites, patrolling, responding to security alarms, searching property, protecting classified materials, and deploying special response teams when the Department of Energy asked for them. With this broad mandate, along with arrest authority, weapons training and access to nuclear facilities, SRTs were among the most capable domestic security forces outside dedicated military special operations units.

SRT operations are classified under the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]]. The act gives a legal basis for classifying information about nuclear weapons and materials as "Restricted Data" or "Formerly Restricted Data," under a system that is separate from the standard military classification system and overrides it. As a result, SRT activities can be designated as transclassified foreign nuclear information, which in effect puts them beyond the reach of normal congressional oversight and Freedom of Information Act requests.

## UAP Significance

Gerb theorizes that DOE SRTs, or units modeled on them, may be the clandestine armed operators that multiple military witnesses encountered during UAP-related incidents:

- [[Rodrik Castle]] (1997): During the [[Hunter Warrior Advanced Warfighting Experiment]] at [[Marine Corps Air Ground Combat Center 29 Palms|29 Palms]], Castle encountered armed operators in unmarked all-black gear next to a triangular [[TR-3B]]-type craft. Their appearance and equipment are consistent with historical descriptions of DOE SRTs.
- [[Jonathan Weygandt]] (1997): In Peru, Weygandt encountered a similar clandestine team during a UAP recovery operation. Wackenhut had set up forward operating bases in Peru in the late 1980s and early 1990s, which gives a plausible way for SRTs to have been deployed there.
- [[Michael Herrera]] (2009): In Sumatra, Herrera encountered armed operators in unmarked gear next to an octagonal craft, the same pattern as the other two cases.

Several factors support the theory that these operators were DOE SRTs and not conventional military special operations forces. They wore no identifying insignia and apparently had the authority to operate independently of the acknowledged military chain of command. Witnesses were given [[Anthrax Vaccine|anthrax boosters]], which suggests proximity to nuclear materials. The classification framework of the Atomic Energy Act would also explain the extreme secrecy surrounding their operations.
