To The Stars Academy of Arts & Science (TTSA) is a private research, media, and entertainment company founded in October 2017 by musician [[Tom DeLonge]]. Its formally credited co-founders are DeLonge, physicist [[Hal Puthoff]], and former CIA officer Jim Semivan; [[Luis Elizondo]], former Minority Staff Director of the Senate Armed Services Committee [[Chris Mellon]], and former Lockheed Martin engineer Steve Justice joined the company's leadership team at its public launch. TTSA's stated mission combined UAP research and public engagement with entertainment media production. Its 2017 launch coincided with a *New York Times* article that revealed the existence of the Pentagon's [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]] program, and the company was widely credited with helping to spark renewed mainstream and congressional interest in UAP after that launch.

## Alleged UAP Involvement

Gerb is highly critical of TTSA in *Special Access Required Vol.2*. He describes the company as a vehicle for what he theorizes was a partial-disclosure campaign, which he connects to [[Luis Elizondo]]'s earlier role directing the National Program Special Management Staff (NPMS) under [[James Clapper]]'s former office. The video links TTSA to [[John Podesta]] (Hillary Clinton's 2016 campaign chairman) and speculates that the company's formation and messaging were aimed at positioning Hillary Clinton as a "disclosure president" ahead of the 2016 election. Gerb also names Lockheed Martin Skunk Works executive Rob Weiss and retired Air Force Major General William Neil McCasland, alongside [[Hal Puthoff]] and Elizondo, as people connected to TTSA's formation. Leaked email correspondence documents that McCasland briefly and informally advised DeLonge shortly after his own 2013 retirement from the Air Force. Gerb argues that TTSA's 2025 documentary *Age of Disclosure* continued this alleged pattern of managed, partial disclosure. In his account, the film does this by presenting an organizational chart of the legacy program structure that the video describes as both historically inaccurate and self-serving toward individuals connected to TTSA.
