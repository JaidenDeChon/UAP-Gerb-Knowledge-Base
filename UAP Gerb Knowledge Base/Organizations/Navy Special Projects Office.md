The Navy Special Projects Office (also referred to in some contexts as the Navy Special Program Office) is a classified naval office that has served as the organizational home for several of the US Navy's most sensitive clandestine programs. In UAP research, the office is significant both as the institutional base from which [[John P. Craraven]] managed the [[Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP)]] and [[Sand Dollar]], and as the office through which CIA scientific intelligence officer Samuel Gene Potit managed [[Project Palladium]]. The Navy Special Program — a broader umbrella structure potentially subsuming the [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] (NURO) — is referenced in NSC intelligence directives from the mid-1970s alongside the National Reconnaissance Program.

## Key Functions and Personnel

[[John P. Craraven]], chief scientist of the Navy Special Projects Office, used the office as his operational base for the 1964 [[Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP)]], which produced the Navy's Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicles (DSRVs) and other clandestine undersea platforms. Craraven received his briefing on the classified [[Sand Dollar]] program through naval intelligence channels connected to this office.

Samuel Gene Potit served as technical director of the Navy Special Program Office, where he directed [[Project Palladium]] in the 1960s — a CIA program using naval assets to create false UAP radar signatures and map Soviet air defenses. Potit also served in the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology and as executive director of the Intelligence Research and Development Council, exemplifying the cross-agency staffing pattern seen throughout naval UFO legacy program infrastructure.

## Relationship to the Navy Special Program

UAP Gerb distinguishes between the Navy Special Projects Office (a specific administrative office) and the Navy Special Program (a broader umbrella classification structure referenced in NSC intelligence directives). The Navy Special Program appears in a 1974 NSC intelligence directive alongside the NRO, running parallel to the National Reconnaissance Program under the Executive Committee for the Director of Central Intelligence. A 1975 NSC document describes it as performing intelligence collection functions analogous to the NRO, DIA, and CIA. UAP Gerb argues the Navy Special Program likely subsumes NURO and other classified naval undersea collection activities, and that UFO/USO recovery programs operating under the NSP would be among the most compartmentalized in the US government.

A 14 December 1984 memo from the staff of the Director of the Intelligence Community to Director of Naval Intelligence Rear Admiral John Butts references a letter from Secretary of the Navy John Lehman (also a NURO director) concerning the Navy Special Program, and suggests including a representative from [[Everett Heinman]]'s CIA DS&T department on a classified special committee — connecting NURO leadership, CIA DS&T, and the NSP in a single document.
