The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) is the official U.S. Department of Defense office responsible for detecting, identifying and attributing unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP). It was established on July 20, 2022, under the authority of [[Ronald S. Moultrie]] in his role as Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USD I&S), and its first director was [[Sean Kirkpatrick]]. AARO sits within the [[Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USDINS)]]. By law it must accept UAP-related disclosures from current and former government employees, including people with knowledge of classified programs. AARO has released a declassified slide deck on the Kona Blue program, and in 2024 it published its Historical Record Report.

## Alleged UAP Involvement

Gerb alleges in "The Hidden Wing" that AARO was never meant to be a genuine investigative body. In his account it was deliberately designed as a whistleblower honeypot, a way to identify and neutralize people with knowledge of UAP legacy programs. [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] is accused of lying, deception and active interference against whistleblowers during his time as director. Gerb also alleges that Kirkpatrick and Moultrie both deleted references to [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] and [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory FFRDC]] from their public biographies. Both organizations are rumored to be involved in analyzing recovered non-human vehicles. Several whistleblowers and witnesses, including [[Dylan Borland]], have indicated that they would not report their experiences to AARO, or chose not to, because they distrust the office.

### Michael Herrera Testimony and Historical Report Misrepresentation

In late April 2023, [[Michael Herrera]] testified to AARO in a secure facility about his 2009 encounter in Indonesia with an octagonal craft and armed operators. Herrera describes his interview with Director Kirkpatrick as more like an interrogation. In his account, Kirkpatrick showed little interest and asked only three questions, over and over, all aimed at identifying the operators. Kirkpatrick claimed AARO had access to satellite imagery of the incident location and promised to follow up, but Herrera says that more than a year later he had still not been contacted to review the imagery.

Herrera's most controversial allegation is that AARO's Historical Report Volume 1 deliberately misrepresented his testimony. He says it falsely attributed two claims to him: that he witnessed an "extraterrestrial vehicle" and that the operators were "US Special Forces." Herrera firmly denies making either statement. He calls the misrepresentation purposeful distortion and rejects the idea that it was an innocent error. He notes that at least one other person present at his SKIF testimony can corroborate that he never used those terms. At the National Press Club and in media interviews, Herrera has publicly testified that he consistently described the craft as clearly man-made with human operators, not extraterrestrial, and that he never identified the armed personnel as US Special Forces.

This alleged pattern of misrepresentation fits broader accusations that AARO, under Kirkpatrick's leadership, actively undermined whistleblower testimony instead of conducting good-faith investigations.

## Sand Corp Contract and Structural Whistleblower Suppression

In 2022, the DOD awarded [[Sand Corp]] a $1.9 million contract for "AARO Support Services." Sand Corp's specialty is preventing leaks and stopping whistleblowers, not UAP analysis or scientific investigation. [[Ronald S. Moultrie]]'s office (Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security) was involved in securing the contract. UAP researchers and commentators, Gerb among them, see this as the most structurally damning evidence that AARO was designed as a disinformation front and a means of suppressing whistleblowers, and not as a legitimate investigative office.

## DOD IG Report: No Overarching UAP Policy

An official press release from the DOD Office of the Inspector General stated: "DOD has no overarching UAP policy and lacks assurance that National Security and flight safety threats to the United States from UAP have been identified and mitigated." The finding directly contradicts AARO's stated mission and Kirkpatrick's public claims of rigorous, science-driven investigation.

## Witnesses Briefed Kirkpatrick; Received No Follow-Up

Kirkpatrick's stated position was that no UAP witnesses had brought evidence to AARO. [[Chris Mellon]], however, publicly stated that he personally introduced Kirkpatrick to [[Luis Elizondo]], [[Eric Davis]] and [[Hal Puthoff]], and that each of them spent hours briefing Kirkpatrick in a classified setting and received no follow-up at all. Elizondo likewise stated that "many people" he knew personally had given AARO detailed information on the record. [[David Grusch]] has also said he tried to approach AARO but received no response.
