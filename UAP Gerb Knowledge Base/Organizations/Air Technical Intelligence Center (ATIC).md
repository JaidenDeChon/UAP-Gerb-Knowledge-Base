The Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC) was the US Air Force's center for scientific and technical intelligence on foreign aircraft, missiles and air technology from 1951 to 1961, based at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], Ohio. It was established on 21 May 1951, when Air Materiel Command's Technical Intelligence Division was made a field activity reporting directly to the Director of Intelligence at Air Force headquarters. ATIC ran [[Project Grudge]]'s final phase and then [[Project Blue Book]], the Air Force's official UFO investigation, and exploited captured Soviet-built aircraft such as the MiG-15. In 1961 it became the [[Air Force Foreign Technology Division|Foreign Technology Division]] under [[Air Force Systems Command]]; its line continues today in the [[National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)|National Air and Space Intelligence Center]].

## In Gerb's account

In [[Videos/NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering/summary|NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering]], Gerb treats ATIC as one link in NASIC's unbroken lineage of foreign materiel acquisition and exploitation, running from the Foreign Data Section of 1917 to today. Points he draws from ATIC's decade:

- **The move to headquarters.** In 1951 the center stopped reporting to a major command ([[Air Materiel Command]]) and answered directly to the Air Force Director of Intelligence. At the same time Air Materiel Command's engineering division became the [[Wright Air Development Center (WADC)|Wright Air Development Center]] under the [[Air Research and Development Command (ARDC)|Air Research and Development Command]], separating the intelligence and engineering sides of the foreign materiel mission while leaving them neighbours at Wright-Patterson.
- **The MiG-15.** Gerb says that in 1953, under what he calls Project Zeta, ATIC captured, exploited and tested a MiG-15 flown out by a defecting North Korean pilot, and that its earlier assessments, made from signatures before it had the hardware, proved 98% correct. He uses this as an example of how good measurement and signature intelligence (MASINT) already was in the 1950s, and of the kind of work NASIC could do on UFOs without hardware in hand.
- **Blue Book and Project Stork.** Gerb notes that Project Stork, which he says was run by [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] under ATIC as a parallel to Blue Book, likely experimented with shape-memory alloys, possibly nitinol from the Roswell crash, and wonders whether UFO recovery photographs reached classified annexes of the programs ATIC and its successors ran.
- **Project West Wing.** Citing a 1990 National Air and Space Museum interview with General Bernard Schriever, Gerb says ATIC funded and administered Project West Wing, which analysed Soviet intelligence and technology, and that it was staffed from TRW's [[Space Technology Laboratories (STL)|Space Technology Laboratories]], whose vice president was [[Edward Bushnell Doll]]. Gerb says West Wing passed to the [[Aerospace Corporation]], where it lives on as the corporation's strategic and global awareness directorate.
- **The 1951 estimate.** Gerb repeats that Blue Book head [[Edward J. Ruppelt]] wrote that in 1951 he saw the [[Project Sign Estimate of the Situation (1948)|1948 Project Sign estimate]] concluding that UFOs were interplanetary, and that General [[Hoyt Vandenberg]] rejected it and ordered it destroyed.
