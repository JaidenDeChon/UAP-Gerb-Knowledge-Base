The Atlantic Undersea Test and Evaluation Center (AUTEC) is a U.S. Navy major range and test facility base (MRTFB) in the "Tongue of the Ocean," a deep-water trench off Andros Island in the Bahamas. The trench drops to roughly 2,000 meters, and the waters right beside it keep falling to 4,000 meters or more in the Blake–Bahama Basin, farther out into the Atlantic. AUTEC tests and evaluates advanced submarines, weapons systems and sonar technology, making use of the area's unusual natural depth and acoustic isolation. In Gerb's investigation, AUTEC's location matters because it puts a major, sophisticated U.S. Navy undersea sensor and test installation right next to the alleged operating area of the "[[Tic Tac Factory]]". Gerb alleges that this is an enormous unidentified submerged object monitored by [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] and the U.S. Navy.

## Alleged UAP Significance

Gerb argues that it is unlikely to be a coincidence that one of the U.S. Navy's most capable undersea test and evaluation facilities sits directly beside the area where the Tic Tac Factory is alleged to be stationed. Because they work in the same waters, he suggests, AUTEC personnel may have direct firsthand exposure to the alleged MITRE program for monitoring unidentified submerged objects (USOs), though this is unconfirmed.
