**Brera Astronomical Observatory** (Osservatorio Astronomico di Brera) is a historic astronomical research facility in Milan, Italy, founded in 1764 by the Jesuit astronomer Roger Boscovich. It is one of Italy's oldest scientific institutions, and it has made significant contributions to astronomical research, especially in stellar astronomy, planetary observation and meteorite studies. During the fascist era it operated under state oversight and had to follow government directives, as did every Italian scientific institution.

## Role in 1933 Magenta UFO Disinformation Campaign

On June 13, 1933, an unknown craft crashed or landed near [[Magenta, Italy]], just west of Milan and within Brera's geographic area. Afterwards the observatory was publicly named as the authority that had identified the object as a meteor. That attribution appears in the second of three classified [[Agenzia Stefani]] telegrams sent on June 13, 1933, which read:

> "By superior order, the news disclosed today in the Stefani dispatch number 633/10 should be treated as follows: the above mentioned aircraft has been recognized as a meteor by the Brera Astronomical Observatory. Give the news the slightest graphic relevance. There is no need to rectify. Minimize."

The directive told Italian media to report that Brera Observatory had identified the object as a meteor and to keep coverage to a minimum. Brera's name gave the cover story scientific legitimacy, while the order to minimize coverage suppressed further investigation.

## Official vs. Actual Involvement

It remains unclear whether Brera Observatory astronomers were genuinely consulted about the Magenta object, whether they issued any formal statement identifying it as a meteor, or whether fascist authorities simply used the observatory's name, through [[Agenzia Stefani]], to give scientific credibility to a cover story decided in advance.

The [[RS-33 (Gabinetto RS 33)]] protocol explicitly barred "pontifical university" and other scientific bodies from examining UAP evidence, and every UFO-related decision needed RS-33 approval. It is therefore plausible that Brera's "identification" was invented by [[Benito Mussolini]]'s government without any input from the observatory's astronomers. The supposed identification also came on the same day as the incident, a speed that suggests a cover story prepared beforehand rather than real scientific analysis.

## Precedent for Government Use of Scientific Authority

The Brera Observatory attribution in the Magenta case set a pattern in which governments cite respected scientific institutions to make UFO debunking stories credible. The practice was repeated in later cases around the world. In the United States, for example, the cover story for the [[1947 Roswell Crash]] identified the object as a meteorological balloon, and Project Blue Book routinely dismissed UAP sightings with scientific-sounding explanations such as temperature inversions, Venus or swamp gas, sometimes without consulting any actual expert.

## Institutional Reputation

In the 1930s Brera Observatory had an international scientific reputation, which would have made it an ideal name to attach to the meteor story. Whether or not its astronomers were actually complicit, the use of the observatory's name served the fascist state's disinformation aims effectively.
