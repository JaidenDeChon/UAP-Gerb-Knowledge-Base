The Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL) is the primary scientific research and development center of the Department of the Air Force, responsible for the discovery, development and integration of warfighting technologies for air, space and cyberspace. Formed in 1997 by consolidating earlier Air Force laboratories, it is headquartered at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], Ohio, and is one of the six centers of [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)]]. Its commander is appointed by the Assistant Secretary of the Air Force for Acquisition ([[SAFAQ|SAF/AQ]]) and works closely with [[SAFAQR (Science, Technology and Engineering)|SAF/AQR]] on the Air Force science and technology program. AFRL has functional directorates, which provide support and oversight, and technology directorates, which are organized by capability. Among the technology directorates are Directed Energy and Space Vehicles at [[Kirtland Air Force Base, New Mexico|Kirtland Air Force Base]] and Aerospace Systems, which has a presence at [[Edwards Air Force Base]]. [[Neil McCasland]] commanded AFRL from 2011 to 2013.

## Lineage

AFRL absorbed the Air Force Weapons Laboratory, which in 1976 had inherited the office-of-primary-responsibility role of the disestablished [[Air Force Special Weapons Center (AFSWC)]] at Kirtland. UAP Gerb's *The Hidden Wing* treats this as one of two routes by which the AFSWC research directorate where Dr. [[Eric Henry Wang]] worked survives inside today's AFMC. The other runs through [[Air Force Systems Command]].

## Alleged role in UFO legacy programs

UAP Gerb calls AFRL "baked into UFO lore". The video quotes a [[Tom DeLonge]] message to [[John Podesta]] saying McCasland had been in charge of the Wright-Patterson laboratory to which the Roswell wreckage was shipped. Gerb notes that AFRL did not exist in 1947 and assigns the original custody to [[Air Materiel Command]]. He also points to statements by physicist [[Eric Davis]], made at the 2024 Sol Foundation conference and in an interview with Gerb, that much of Davis's concrete information on the crash-retrieval portfolio came from AFRL. Gerb disputes Davis's related claim that no [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicles]] have ever been built, calling it program protection that AFRL may have required. In Gerb's [[Hidden Wing]] thesis, AFRL works with the [[Air Force Test Center]] and the [[412th Test Wing]] to reverse-engineer and adapt non-human craft. When discussing alleged underground facilities in the [[Antelope Valley]], Gerb has also brought up AFRL's presence on Haystack Butte at Edwards.
