The **Special Access Program Oversight Committee** (SAPOC) is the senior Department of Defense body that oversees the department's [[Special Access Programs (SAPs)|special access programs]]. It is chaired by the Deputy Secretary of Defense. The SAPOC has visibility of every DoD SAP. It is meant to ensure the SAP enterprise complies with law, regulation and policy and that required information is provided to Congress, and it reviews whether each [[Waived Unacknowledged Special Access Programs (USAPs)|waived]] program still needs waived status. Its principal support is a **Senior Review Group** (SRG) of senior executive officials, and the DoD-wide Special Access Program Central Office (DoD SAPCO) serves as its staff.

In UFO research the SAPOC is best known from the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], which places it, and particularly its Senior Review Group, at the gate of an alleged crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering program that Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] tried and failed to access in 1997.

## Structure

SAP governance runs upward in tiers, as UAP Gerb lays it out in *Special Access Required Vol.2*:

- **Component SAPCOs**: one each for the armed services, the Joint Staff, [[DARPA]] and the Missile Defense Agency, each responsible for the SAPs under its purview. The Air Force's is SAF/AAZ.
- **OSD-level SAPCOs**: offices under the under secretaries of defense for acquisition and sustainment, intelligence and security, and research and engineering, which oversee acquisition, intelligence and research SAPs respectively.
- **DoD SAPCO**: the department-wide central office, the primary point of contact with Congress and the [[National Security Council]], which processes congressional program access requests and exempts waived SAPs from them. Past directors named by the presenter include General [[Marshal Ward|H. Marshal Ward]] and Major General Dawn Dunlop.
- **SAPOC and its Senior Review Group**: the apex. The SRG's senior executive members are meant, among other things, to stop the same program being duplicated across SAP categories.

Past chairs cited in UAP Gerb's work include Deputy Secretaries of Defense [[John Deutsch]] and [[Bill Perry]].

## Alleged role as legacy gatekeeper

According to the Wilson-Davis notes, the UFO portfolio was reorganized in 1994, during Perry's expansion of SAP management and control, into "a special subset of the unacknowledged carve-outs waived programs not belonging to usual SAP divisions." The notes describe a special agreement between the SAPOC's Senior Review Group, a contractor's counsel and a corporate security director (the [[Watch Committee]]) governing access. Wilson was told to "drop the matter" because the program did not fall within his oversight. Air Force Major General Michael Kostelnik, described as SAPOC director at the time, is credited in the notes with estimating legacy security budgets at two to three times the program budget, and once six to seven times.

UAP Gerb treats the SAPOC and SRG as the "skeleton gatekeeping structure and access approval authority" for government access to the legacy programs: able to turn away even agency directors and general officers who lacked need-to-know. He distinguishes them from the programs' actual control group, which he says has since 1994 been a quasi-government and industry group of just over two dozen people, though he suggests the Watch Committee may be connected to it. He quotes [[David Grusch]] describing the decision-makers as "career senior executive officials... both in and out of government." He also argues the SRG's anti-duplication role has failed for the legacy portfolio, which is so siloed that separate services allegedly run duplicate exotic programs.
