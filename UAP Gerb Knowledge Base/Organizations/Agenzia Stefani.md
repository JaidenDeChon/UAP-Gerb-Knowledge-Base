**Agenzia Stefani** was the official state-controlled news agency of Fascist Italy. From 1924 to 1945, under [[Benito Mussolini]]'s regime, it was the only authorized source of news. The agency held a monopoly and worked as a propaganda apparatus: all information in the Italian media passed through Stefani, and no news that contradicted the fascist government's account reached the public unless state authorities had approved and edited it first.

## Role in 1933 Magenta UFO Cover-Up

After an unknown craft crashed or landed near [[Magenta, Italy]] on June 13, 1933, Agenzia Stefani was the main instrument used to impose absolute press silence and to spread disinformation about the incident. Three classified telegrams sent from Stefani headquarters on June 13, 1933 document the suppression campaign. All three carried "Lampo" (highest) priority and the "Riservato" (confidential) classification.

The first telegram, sent on the morning of June 13, ordered "absolute silence" about the "presumed landing of an unknown aircraft on national soil," and it confirmed that only the cover story version was to be published. It threatened "maximum penalties for offenders up to and including appeal to the State Security Court" and demanded immediate confirmation of receipt.

The second telegram, sent at 4:00 PM on June 13, directed that the craft be publicly identified as a meteor recognized by the [[Brera Astronomical Observatory]]. It told the media to give the story "the slightest graphic relevance" and to "minimize" coverage.

The third telegram, sent at 5:07 PM on June 13, ordered an immediate stop to all circulation of news about the "craft of unknown nature" mentioned in the morning dispatch. Every newspaper lead carrying the story had to be "recast immediately", and the telegram again threatened maximum penalties, including prosecution before the State Security Court.

The telegrams show how quickly and completely the fascist state could suppress information. Within hours of the event, the Italian media were under a complete blackout.

## Institutional Structure

During the 1930s, Stefani held a monopoly on official news within Italy. All media outlets had to use Stefani dispatches for their news stories. Departing from the official version could bring severe penalties, including imprisonment, seizure of assets, or referral to the State Security Court, Mussolini's special tribunal for political crimes.

The agency also actively shaped the narrative. In the [[1933 Magenta UFO Crash/Retrieval]] case, Stefani suppressed unwanted stories and supplied a pre-fabricated cover story (the meteor explanation) for journalists to use, so that the disinformation campaign ran in a coordinated way instead of leaving a gap in the news.

## Historical Verification

The three Stefani telegrams from June 13, 1933 were among the authenticated documents that the anonymous source [[Mr. X]] gave to researcher [[Roberto Pinotti]]. Forensic analysis confirmed that the paper and ink dated to the 1930s, and Italian researchers verified that the telegrams' format, language style and bureaucratic procedures matched authentic Stefani communications from the fascist era.

## Parallel to Modern Disinformation

The Stefani model of rapid, centralized media control and coordinated cover stories anticipates modern allegations about UAP-related disinformation campaigns. In a single day, Stefani moved from an order of silence to the meteor explanation to a complete news blackout.
