The Research and Development Board (R&D Board) was a high-level US Department of Defense organization established in the late 1940s to coordinate and oversee military research and development activities across all branches of the armed services. According to several witness accounts, most notably that of [[Robert Sarbacher]], the R&D Board was the institutional home of a small, highly classified group of scientists tasked with studying recovered UAP craft and materials in the late 1940s and early 1950s. Its chairman was Dr. [[Vannevar Bush]], one of the most powerful figures in American science during and after the war.

## Establishment and Purpose

The R&D Board was created in the immediate aftermath of World War II to keep the United States ahead in technology as the Cold War began. It coordinated research across the Army, Navy, and Air Force and oversaw programs on nuclear weapons, jet propulsion, guided missiles, radar, and other advanced defense technologies.

Its members were senior scientists, engineers, and defense officials with top-level security clearances and access to the nation's most sensitive programs. As chairman, [[Vannevar Bush]] had authority over the direction of US military science policy during a critical period.

## Alleged UAP Program

In 1950, [[Wilbert B. Smith]], a senior radio engineer in the Canadian government, met [[Robert Sarbacher]], a physicist who was a consultant to the R&D Board. Sarbacher told Smith that:

1. The study of flying saucers within the US government was classified at a higher level than the hydrogen bomb.
2. Flying saucers exist.
3. Their mode of operation is unknown, but a small group headed by [[Vannevar Bush]] is making concentrated efforts to study them.
4. The entire matter is considered by US authorities to be of tremendous significance.

Sarbacher's testimony placed the R&D Board at the center of the US government's early UAP crash retrieval and reverse engineering efforts. In a 1983 letter to researcher [[William Steinman]], Sarbacher confirmed that he had been invited to several discussions about crash retrievals at the R&D Board, although he did not attend them himself. He named [[Vannevar Bush]], [[John Von Neumann]], and [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] as "definitely involved" in the program.

## Connection to the Wright Field Meeting

The alleged [[Wright Field Crash Retrieval Meeting]] of 1949–1950, which [[Eric A. Walker]] attended and Sarbacher referred to, is believed to have been organized under the R&D Board or a closely related body, the [[Defense Research Board]]. The meeting brought the nation's top scientists together to discuss recovered UAP materials, occupants, and how to reverse engineer them.

Sarbacher's account suggests that the R&D Board's UAP work was compartmentalized even inside the board, with access to the crash retrieval program granted only to a small subset of members who had a need to know. That would explain why the program stayed secret for decades even though the board had a fairly large membership.

## Relationship to Majestic 12

The origin of the [[Majestic 12]] documents can be traced conceptually to the R&D Board's structure. Its precursor, the joint Research and Development Board established in 1941, had 12 members. The [[MJ-12 Documents]], which describe a secret committee that President Truman charged with managing UAP crash retrievals, name [[Vannevar Bush]] as a member. This fits Sarbacher's testimony that Bush headed the small group studying flying saucers within the R&D Board.

Many of the MJ-12 documents are considered forgeries. Even so, the alleged MJ-12 group and the R&D Board overlap in structure and personnel, and Sarbacher and [[Eric A. Walker]] each independently confirmed Bush's involvement. Together these suggest that the R&D Board, or a compartmented subset of it, may have been the real-world basis for the MJ-12 story.

## Participants and Members

Known members or consultants to the R&D Board with alleged connections to UAP programs include:

- **[[Vannevar Bush]]**: chairman; named by Sarbacher as heading the UAP study group.
- **[[Robert Sarbacher]]**: consultant; confirmed his knowledge of the program in 1950 and 1983.
- **[[Eric A. Walker]]**: Executive Secretary of the related Defense Research Board (1950–1951); confirmed that he attended UAP-related meetings.
- **[[John Von Neumann]]**: consultant; named by Sarbacher as involved in UAP analysis.
- **[[J. Robert Oppenheimer]]**: consultant; named by Sarbacher as involved in UAP analysis.

## Dissolution and Legacy

The R&D Board was eventually reorganized, and other DoD bodies took over its functions. Its role in the critical 1947–1952 period, when several UAP crashes allegedly occurred and the US government was setting its approach to the phenomenon, places it at the historical center of American UFO secrecy.

The board's place in UAP research rests on the testimony of insiders like Sarbacher and Walker, who decades later confirmed that the board had indeed overseen a compartmented program to study recovered non-human technology. Its chairman was Vannevar Bush, the architect of the Office of Scientific Research and Development, which preceded the Manhattan Project. That he chaired it shows how seriously the UAP question was treated at the highest levels of US defense science.

## Role in the Manhattan Project 2.0

In his investigation of the Manhattan Project 2.0, Gerb identifies the RDB as the probable "tip of the spear" for the scientific and administrative management of UFO crash retrieval operations from 1947 to 1953. Under the 1947 National Security Act, the RDB was to prepare a complete and integrated program of military research and development, advise on trends in scientific research relating to national security, recommend ways to coordinate the military departments, and set policy for the National Military Establishment on research and development matters involving outside agencies. That mandate made it the ideal institutional home for the Manhattan Project 2.0's scientific apparatus.

In 1953 the RDB was reorganized into the Assistant Secretary of Defense for Research and Development, which today is the Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering (USDR&E). Gerb says this office is still involved in UFO legacy activities, along with the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment (USDANS) and the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USDINS).
