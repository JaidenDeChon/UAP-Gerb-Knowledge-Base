Western Electric Company was the manufacturing and supply arm of the AT&T Bell System, a wholly owned subsidiary of [[AT&T]] that made telephone equipment for most of the twentieth century and co-owned [[Bell Labs]] with its parent from 1925. From 1 November 1949 until 1993, Western Electric's own wholly owned subsidiary, Sandia Corporation, managed [[Sandia National Laboratories]] for the [[Atomic Energy Commission]] and its successors. The arrangement began after President [[Harry Truman]] wrote to AT&T president LeRoy Wilson on 13 May 1949 asking the company to run the laboratory, telling him it had "an opportunity to render an exceptional service in the national interest."

## Significance in UAP Gerb's Work

UAP Gerb returns to the AT&T and Western Electric chain in several investigations as an example of the layered corporate ownership he sees behind government-owned, contractor-operated laboratories. Sandia's operations were modelled on Bell Labs, the joint AT&T and Western Electric research subsidiary that [[Philip J. Corso]] named as a leading contractor in the Army's exploitation of recovered technology. Gerb also notes that [[Vannevar Bush]], whom early crash-retrieval accounts place at the head of the first recovery effort, sat on AT&T's board from 1947 to 1962. In his MITRE video he describes Bell Labs and Western Electric as the "trusted partners" to whom the military would naturally have turned for technical help with recovered craft. In his video on underground bases, he connects AT&T and Western Electric's management of Sandia to reports of underground communication centers in New Mexico.
