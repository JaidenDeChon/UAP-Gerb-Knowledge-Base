TRW Inc. was a major American defense and aerospace corporation. It grew out of the [[Space Technology Laboratories (STL)]], founded in the 1950s, and became a leading U.S. government contractor for spacecraft design, intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) systems, reconnaissance satellites and the [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] early warning satellite constellation. Its space intelligence work made it one of the most sensitive contractors in Cold War national security. In 2002, [[Northrop Grumman]] bought TRW for about $7.8 billion and split its operations between two new Northrop Grumman sectors, Mission Systems and Space Technologies. The [[Aerospace Corporation]], a federally funded research and development center (FFRDC), was spun off from TRW's Space Technology Laboratories in 1960.

## Alleged UAP Involvement

TRW is alleged to have been a central hub for UAP crash retrieval and reverse engineering from the 1950s onward. [[Edward Bushnell Doll]], alleged coordinator of the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]], worked at TRW Systems Group from approximately 1955 to 1977. [[Richard Haver]] and [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]] both worked at TRW before rising to senior government intelligence posts. This pattern is cited as evidence of a deliberate personnel pipeline between the alleged corporate UAP program and the intelligence community's oversight structure.

[[Eric A. Walker|Dr. Eric Walker]], a physicist, former Pennsylvania State University president and member of the [[Research and Development Board]] under [[Vannevar Bush]], was a chairman of TRW. [[Dr. Robert Sarbacher]] named Walker as someone who attended classified UFO crash retrieval meetings in the late 1940s and 1950s, and Walker himself admitted to being present at the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]]. His TRW chairmanship links the company's corporate leadership directly to documented figures in UFO crash retrieval programs.

In 1997, TRW acquired [[BDM International]], a defense contractor headquartered at [[Fort Bliss]] and [[White Sands Missile Range]]. BDM hosted the 1985 Advanced Theoretical Physics Conference on UFO reverse engineering, employed [[Major General Albert Stubblebine]] as vice president and had [[Rear Admiral Sumner Shapiro]] on its board of directors. The acquisition brought BDM's institutional knowledge and personnel networks into TRW, and through TRW into Northrop Grumman after the 2002 purchase.

TRW received a $340.5 million contract for the [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)]]'s Brilliant Pebbles sub-program, which is alleged to have been a vehicle for back-channeling funds to clandestine UAP legacy activities. TRW also used its facilities for high-powered chemical laser testing under SDI.

## TRW Systems Group and Special Projects

TRW's [[Space Technology Laboratories (STL)]] was renamed TRW Systems Group, which ran the company's entire electronics and defense sector. Its three main units were Space and Electronics, Systems, and Aeronautics. The first two are now part of [[Northrop Grumman]], and Aeronautics is now part of Raytheon. Its sub-organizations included a Systems Engineering and Integration Division that contracted with the CIA, and a Special Projects unit. The Special Projects unit, led in 1965 by chief scientist Bill Carson, built signals-intelligence satellites for NRO Program B, operated with the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]] from [[Pine Gap, Australia]]. UAP Gerb's *Northrop Grumman & TRW* argues that TRW's alleged legacy work lived in these units, where [[Edward Bushnell Doll]] was senior and then executive vice president. Other TRW alumni named in the video include [[Donald C. Winter]], [[Dawn Meyerriecks]] and [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]].

## IRAD Record

The same video goes through TRW's record with [[Independent Research and Development (IRAD)]]. A CIA memorandum of 5 April 1982 records the Directorate of Science and Technology's wish to channel contractor IRAD, including TRW's, into its own areas of need. In 1986 a TRW vice president left over overcharging through IRAD and overhead accounts. In 1989 the General Accounting Office investigated TRW for misclassifying costs as IRAD. Between 1990 and 1997 TRW ran five separate schemes, including IRAD mischarging. TRW's company-funded R&D was $468 million in 1999 and $442 million in both 2000 and 2001, more than Northrop's own IRAD. Gerb theorizes that TRW was misappropriating funds for the CIA's DS&T and that the practice continued inside Northrop Grumman.

## The Zodiac Story

TRW also appears in "Zodiac", a retrieval-program account first published in *UFO Magazine* in 1998 under the pseudonym Greg Halifax. [[Richard Dolan]] has linked the pseudonyms to a TRW attorney, Jeffrey W. Griffith, and a participant, Mary Elizabeth Elliott. A 3 March 2001 entry in [[Jacques Vallee]]'s journals records [[Eric Davis]] describing a ten-volume binder at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base containing Roswell data and "a special study done by TRW".

## The 1990s Pentagon Audit and $111 Million Settlement

In 2003, Northrop Grumman paid $111.2 million to settle a False Claims Act case stemming from TRW's overcharging of the government between 1990 and 1997. The lawsuit's roots go back to 1994, the same period the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] refers to. In the memo, Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] was told by the "watch committee" (UAP program gatekeepers) that their programs had been reorganized after an audit in the 1990s that nearly exposed them. The matching dates suggest that TRW's inflated space contracts, which looked to auditors like ordinary overcharging, may have been hiding funding for waived unacknowledged special access programs. After the settlement, [[Catherine Austin Fitts]]'s research into black budget accounting documented the same period as one of widespread unauthorized diversion of spending across the DOD.

In an interview, [[Dr. Eric Davis]] said that only one FFRDC had been the "principal investigator" in UAP programs, for roughly 29 years before transitioning. He identified it only as a TRW spin-off from 1960 (that is, [[The Aerospace Corporation]]). Davis's statement, together with Walker's chairmanship and the BDM acquisition, presents TRW as the central corporate node in UAP legacy operations from the late 1950s through 2002.

After the 2002 acquisition by [[Northrop Grumman]], all of TRW's assets, programs and personnel networks are alleged to have passed to Northrop Grumman's control. UAP Gerb's *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* says that [[Edward Bushnell Doll|Ed Doll]]'s TRW Systems Group contained an alleged "special projects division," which the video argues was absorbed into Northrop Grumman's Mission Systems sector, along with the rest of TRW Systems Group, after the 2002 acquisition.
