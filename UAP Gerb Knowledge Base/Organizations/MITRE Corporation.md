The MITRE Corporation is a nonprofit systems-engineering and research organization founded in 1958. It was spun off from the [[MIT|Massachusetts Institute of Technology's]] Lincoln Laboratory to provide independent engineering support for the U.S. Air Force's SAGE air-defense system. MITRE has joint headquarters in Bedford, Massachusetts, and [[McLean, Virginia]] (immediately adjacent to [[Crystal City, Virginia]]). It operates roughly 43 campuses and field offices across 28 states and has a workforce of over 7,000, more than 65% of whom hold advanced degrees. MITRE manages six of the roughly 42 federally funded research and development centers (FFRDCs, see [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)]]) recognized across the U.S. government. It receives approximately 99% of its funding from federal sponsors, yet has the legal independence of a private nonprofit. In Gerb's investigative framework, MITRE is identified as the "apex" FFRDC within the alleged UFO legacy program architecture: the R&D lead and program manager for two long-running U.S. Navy special access programs, one a technology reverse-engineering effort tracing to material recovered at [[Roswell Crash|Roswell]], and the other a decades-old surveillance program monitoring an enormous unidentified submerged object (USO) in the Atlantic Ocean nicknamed the "[[Tic Tac Factory]]."

## History and Founding

MITRE's origin is inseparable from Project SAGE (Semi-Automatic Ground Environment), the United States' first computer-based national air defense system, conceived in 1951 and deployed by 1963. SAGE coordinated data from hundreds of radar sites, 24 direction centers, and three combat centers into a single unified air picture for [[NORAD]]. The project exceeded the Manhattan Project in both cost and personnel. SAGE began as "Project Lincoln," administered by [[MIT Lincoln Laboratory]], which reopened in 1951 as the direct successor to the MIT Radiation Laboratory of 1940, founded by [[Vannevar Bush|Vannevar Bush]]. Because of SAGE's enormous scale, the Air Force and MIT Lincoln Laboratory spun off a dedicated engineering group in 1958, staffed largely with Lincoln Laboratory research and engineering personnel, and named it MITRE. Contrary to popular assumption, the name does not refer to the ceremonial headwear of bishops. It is simply a play on the initials "MIT," which its founders liked the sound of.

MITRE was modeled directly on the [[RAND Corporation]], another FFRDC created to answer Air Force systems-engineering needs while holding no capital stock, no profit motive, and no hardware to sell. These structural features were intended to keep MITRE free of the conflicts of interest inherent to a defense contractor. MITRE's first technical director, [[Robert R. Everett]], came to the organization from sensitive MIT Navy-funded research, including Project Whirlwind, and many of MITRE's founding scientific personnel had similar Naval Special Projects backgrounds. Gerb argues this detail is significant given the U.S. Navy's later administration of MITRE's alleged legacy program portfolio. Neil McElroy, the sixth U.S. Secretary of Defense under President Eisenhower, is recorded as having personally asked MITRE's founders to build "flexibility to take on the Department of Defense's requirements as we develop them."

From its earliest years, MITRE was caught in a "roles and missions" power struggle among the Army, Navy, and Air Force, each of which feared losing control of its own automatic air, ground, and sea defense systems to a unified command-and-control architecture. Because MITRE was a trusted, neutral, non-competing R&D party, it could work independently and simultaneously with all three services on their own compartmented projects, even though the Air Force alone had formally chartered it. Gerb identifies this climate of severe internal distrust and compartmentation as foundational to MITRE's later legacy-program security culture. Over subsequent decades, MITRE built a reputation in radar systems, [[C4I (Command, Control, Communications, Computers and Intelligence)|C3I/C4ISR]], satellites, computing, and artificial intelligence.

## Modern Structure

Today MITRE is a top-down parent nonprofit that operates six functional FFRDCs, each a division under the MITRE umbrella:

- National Cybersecurity FFRDC, sponsored by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
- Health FFRDC, sponsored by the Department of Health and Human Services
- Homeland Security Systems Engineering and Development Institute, sponsored by the Department of Homeland Security
- Center for Enterprise Modernization, sponsored by the Department of the Treasury and Internal Revenue Service
- Center for Advanced Aviation System Development, sponsored by the Federal Aviation Administration
- National Security Engineering Center (NSEC), formerly the C3I FFRDC, headquartered at MITRE's McLean, Virginia campus and sponsored by the Department of Defense and the Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering (USD R&E). NSEC's primary contracting and administrative agent is the [[Air Force Life Cycle Management Center (AFLCMC)|Air Force Life Cycle Management Center]] (AFLCMC), commanded since early 2024 by Lieutenant General [[Donna D. Shipton]].

Of the six, Gerb identifies NSEC as MITRE's oldest FFRDC relationship and the division through which the organization's alleged UFO legacy program work is administered. NSEC's declared mission spans space warfighting, integrated air and missile defense, joint command and control, and strategic deterrence for the wider DoD and intelligence community. Apart from its FFRDCs, MITRE also operates 15 internal "MITRE Labs" innovation centers. This applied-research arm is distinct from the government-sponsored FFRDC structure and is intended to "inspire breakthroughs in applied science and advanced technology."

## Alleged UFO Legacy Program Involvement

Gerb describes MITRE as the "apex science and technology institution within UFO activities" and states that its own investigation into MITRE's legacy program role concerns two distinct Navy-administered special access programs, with a stated knowledge cutoff of approximately 2019. Gerb says the investigation draws on roughly two years of research and testimony from several MITRE engineers and program managers. Both are alleged to be Waived Unacknowledged Special Access Programs (WOAPs), the most restrictive class described in [[Waived Unacknowledged Special Access Programs (USAPs)]], with MITRE serving as R&D lead and program manager. The sponsor is identified specifically as the United States Navy, with lesser Army involvement, rather than the Air Force that formally chartered MITRE in 1958. [[David Grusch]] has separately described the wider legacy reverse-engineering effort in terms that Gerb argues map directly onto MITRE's role: "This whole program in a nutshell... it is a reverse engineering program to garner some kind of insight... not a lot of the things that we've learned from it are like directly ripped off the technology we found, but it has inspired other innovations that made its way into other US classified programs."

### Program One: Roswell-Derived Reverse Engineering and Technology Exploitation

Gerb alleges that MITRE's founding purpose extended beyond SAGE, and that MITRE's scientific personnel, drawn from MIT Lincoln Laboratory and Naval Special Projects backgrounds, were tasked from the organization's 1958 inception with reverse engineering a suite of capabilities from nonhuman technical vehicles recovered at [[Roswell Crash|Roswell, New Mexico]] in July 1947, wreckage that had been taken into custody by Air Materiel Command and the newly formed Air Research and Development Command (ARDC) at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. In this telling, MITRE's creation came almost exactly ten years after Roswell and roughly ten and a half years after the crash, matching a period in which assessments reportedly determined that certain recovered technologies or systems were exploitable. ARDC, which worked closely and nearly inseparably with MITRE during the SAGE years, is alleged to have played a guiding role in establishing MITRE as a trusted, secure R&D partner for this purpose. Gerb draws a parallel to Lieutenant Colonel [[Philip J. Corso]]'s account of the [[U.S. Army Foreign Technology Division]] under General [[Arthur Trudeau]], arguing that the Army pursued its own aggressive technology-seeding program out of Roswell materials starting in 1958 while the Air Force and Navy took a more conservative route through the fledgling MITRE Corporation.

This reverse-engineering program is described as deriving discrete, asymmetric "war-altering" capabilities, and explicitly not as producing exotic craft, propulsion systems, or directed-energy weapons. These are technologies that, per Grusch's framing, inspired or influenced other classified U.S. systems without being direct copies, and that would be unrecognizable as UFO-derived to anyone without knowledge of the program's full chain of custody. Since 1958, despite MITRE's Air Force founding, the program has been administered and overseen by the U.S. Navy, primarily through the [[Office of Naval Intelligence]] (ONI), [[Naval Air Systems Command]] (NAVAIR), and to a lesser extent [[Naval Sea Systems Command]] (NAVSEA). ONI provides intelligence and counterintelligence support, including scouting for foreign exploitation of the same technologies and monitoring for leaks of program terminology. It works alongside the [[Naval Criminal Investigative Service]] (NCIS) and the [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations|Air Force Office of Special Investigations']] Office of Special Projects (AFOSI/PJ), historically headed by [[Terry Phillips]].

The program's operational and administrative center is [[Naval Air Station Patuxent River]] ("Pax River"), home to NAVAIR's program executive offices, the Naval Air Warfare Center Aircraft Division, and the Navy Test Pilot School. Supporting sites include [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Dahlgren]] (major testing and deployment), [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane]] and [[Naval Undersea Warfare Center Keyport]] (a data-processing, hardware-analysis, and integration triage alongside Pax River), and a highly secretive Navy field at Dallas Love Field, Texas, known as the Bureau of Naval Personnel Sea Duty Component or Naval Air Warfare Center 23 (NAWC-23). Contractors alleged to be involved include [[Raytheon]], [[L3Harris]], and [[Lockheed Martin]]. Gerb also suspects, with less confidence, [[Booz Allen Hamilton]], [[Boeing]], [[Northrop Grumman]], [[BAE Systems]], and [[Aerojet Rocketdyne]]. Contractor personnel on the program are described as almost universally unaware of the technology's true origin, receiving only narrowly compartmented pieces of work.

### Program Two: The Tic Tac Factory — Atlantic USO Monitoring

The second program Gerb attributes to MITRE is a Navy-partnered effort, several decades old, to monitor, track, and task for intercept an enormous, permanent or semi-permanent [[Unidentified Submerged Object (USO)|unidentified submerged object]] stationed in the Atlantic Ocean near the Bahamas. The object is nicknamed the "[[Tic Tac Factory]]" for its alleged behavior of deploying and recovering smaller tic-tac-shaped, cylindrical, discoid, and irregular "blobby" craft capable of transmedium flight. See [[Tic Tac Factory]] for full detail on the object's alleged characteristics, geography, and corroborating testimony, including an anonymous 2023 4chan account and the 2004 USS Nimitz encounter involving Commander [[David Fravor]].

## Security Architecture: The Hidden SSO and the Onion Model

MITRE's security apparatus for both programs is administered through what Gerb terms a "hidden SSO" instead of through its acknowledged Special Security Office (SSO) at its McLean, Virginia campus. The hidden SSO is a parallel, unacknowledged security office located at unmarked MITRE satellite sites near Crystal City, referenced internally only by an "S" designation and a number (with, per UAP Gerb's understanding, several hundred such "S" sites believed to exist below roughly 500). The hidden SSO's personnel and program security officers reportedly operate under nonofficial cover (NOC) — a technique borrowed from CIA tradecraft in which an officer's true institutional affiliation is known only to the MITRE CEO and the SSO director — with UAP Gerb stating it has seen badges for MITRE legacy engineers listing fabricated cover positions and institutions. The hidden SSO is described as exempt from ordinary audits, with personnel reportedly instructed, if questioned during an audit, to state they were "lollipop manufacturers." Funding for the programs is alleged to be skimmed from a swath of acknowledged MITRE and Navy projects rather than carried on any visible program budget line.

A source described to Gerb characterized MITRE's internal security architecture as the literal origin of the "onion" model of nested secrecy later adopted throughout the wider legacy program apparatus (see [[Legacy Program Onion Model]]), and as "the model for all legacy security personnel." Compartmentation within MITRE's own programs is aggressive even among cleared personnel. An engineer investigating an anomalous maritime radar signature, for example, might never be told he is working on a USO-tracking WOAP, while a colleague seated next to him is fully cleared into the imagery, signals, and measurement-and-signature intelligence generated by the same object. This pattern echoes the "seventh veil" analogy attributed to Navy Special Projects Office chief scientist [[John P. Craven]], in which no individual, cleared or not, can ever be certain whether they have reached the innermost layer of a compartmented program or are merely working a cover program in service of another cover program.

## 2007 Briefing Embargo, 2011 Clampdown, and 2017 Loss of Control

UAP Gerb's *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* dates the origin of MITRE's internal briefing restrictions earlier than previously documented. The video states that a briefing embargo was actually introduced within MITRE in 2007, not 2011, and that under it legacy program incumbents stopped being briefed on program history, successes, breakthroughs, records, and milestones. The practical effect of that 2007 policy change is described as showing from 2011 onward, when newcomers to MITRE's legacy program efforts began arriving completely unaware of the program's history, in some cases fully unaware that their programs involved non-human technology at all. The video explicitly frames this as an instance of the broader "catastrophic loss of institutional knowledge" it traces across legacy programs. It speculates that the embargo was likely not isolated to MITRE and was also enacted within other agencies, services, and possibly other FFRDCs.

Gerb identifies 2011 as a further major inflection point for MITRE's legacy program secrecy: top-down orders to the hidden SSO restricted newly incoming engineers, security officers, and scientists to strictly need-to-know operational briefings, eliminating historical briefings on program origins, chain of custody, and past breakthroughs. Newcomers after 2011 were reportedly left unaware that their compartmented work touched programs tracking a USO or exploiting recovered UFO technology. Gerb attributes this shift, at least in part, to the 2011 introduction of intelligence community partners, specifically the CIA, into the reverse-engineering portfolio. It argues that MITRE and DoD leadership did not fully trust the new IC stakeholders with a program that had been exclusively DoD, Navy, and MITRE since 1958.

Gerb further alleges that in 2017 MITRE lost full control of the reverse-engineering program, or saw leadership of it shifted away from the organization. It connects that year to [[James Clapper]]'s tenure as Director of National Intelligence, during which Clapper is alleged to have worked to fold previously siloed DoD legacy activities into a wider National Security Council and intelligence-community crash-retrieval portfolio, potentially including the alleged 2017 Unacknowledged Special Access Program [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)]]. Gerb notes that this timeline also roughly parallels the alleged 2010–2011 origins of [[NRO Sentient]].

## Congressional Scrutiny: The Burlison–Grusch Letter

Representative [[Eric Burlison]], working with [[David Grusch]], sent MITRE Corporation a detailed legal interrogative letter of more than ten pages, dated 22 May 2026. It was received by senior vice president [[Christopher Land]], who had been with MITRE only since February of the year the letter was released. The letter establishes a records preservation obligation on MITRE covering, among other things: contracts, task orders, programs, and sponsor relationships; classified access and Special Access Program oversight visibility; sensor data and domain-awareness records related to UAP/USO detection; materials, technology exploitation, and biological or health-safety records; secure facilities and physical custody arrangements; third-party contractor and subcontractor relationships; and budgeting, funding, and contracting controls. Gerb frames the letter as directly targeting the two programs it has investigated, and specifically calls out MITRE's alleged history of destroying legacy program records at the Fairfax County, Virginia Waste Management Depot (including around 2011 and 2017) as a preservation risk the letter is intended to foreclose.
