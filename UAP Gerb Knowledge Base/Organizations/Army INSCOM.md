The United States Army Intelligence and Security Command (INSCOM) is the Army's primary intelligence organization. It provides intelligence, security, and information operations support to Army and joint force commanders. INSCOM was established in 1977 and has been headquartered at Fort Belvoir, Virginia.

## UAP Connections

INSCOM matters to UAP research mainly because of its former commanding general, [[Albert Stubblebine|Major General Albert Stubblebine]], who pursued unconventional intelligence methods such as remote viewing programs. INSCOM also originally created the Intelligence Support Activity (ISA), a shadowy special missions unit also known as "Task Force Orange". The ISA later became one of [[JSOC (Joint Special Operations Command)|JSOC's]] most secretive component units. Theoretical crash retrieval models cite it as a possible intelligence collection element that could support UAP recovery operations.

Gerb has also linked Stubblebine and INSCOM to [[TREAT]] (Tactical Reconnaissance Engineering Assessment Team), the unit [[Edgar Fouche|Edgar Fouché]] described as the "Men in Black". The link rests on Gerb's reading of a 1997 letter to [[Steven Greer]] that mentions TREAT and the wife of a former head of INSCOM, and he suggests TREAT may be "the real life Men In Black housed within US Army INSCOM". In his Peru video Gerb says Stubblebine has been accused by [[Philip J. Corso]] and Greer of taking part in UFO legacy program operations at Fort Huachuca, Arizona. In his other videos the two claims are narrower: Gerb says Corso claimed a UFO working group set up in the mid-1980s was funded by INSCOM under Stubblebine, and Greer's witness log describes Stubblebine as a former head of Army intelligence at Fort Huachuca, "where ET craft and bodies are stored".
