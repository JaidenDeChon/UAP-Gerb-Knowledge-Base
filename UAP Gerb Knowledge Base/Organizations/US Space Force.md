The United States Space Force (USSF) is the space service branch of the United States Armed Forces, established in December 2019 as the sixth independent military service. It is part of the Department of the Air Force and is responsible for organizing, training, and equipping space forces to protect US and allied interests in space and to provide space capabilities to the joint force.

## DSP and Fast Walker Operations

Space Force inherited oversight of the [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellite constellation from the former Air Force Space Command. DSP satellites have been detecting [[Fast Walkers]], objects entering or leaving Earth's atmosphere at extreme speeds, since at least 1972. The program continues under Space Force management and works with [[NORAD]] on aerospace warning and space domain awareness missions.

[[NORAD]]'s headquarters is at Space Force's Peterson Space Force Base in Colorado Springs, so the two organizations work in close coordination to track objects in near-Earth space.

## FOIA Denials on Fast Walker Data

In late 2023, Space Force issued a final denial to [[John Greenewald]]'s FOIA request for Fast Walker information. The denial cited FOIA Exception One, which covers information "to be kept secret in the interest of National Defense or foreign policy."

The 2023 denial followed a decade of similar refusals by Space Force's predecessor organizations. Together, these refusals establish that Fast Walker data remains among the most highly classified information held by US military space commands.

## Fast Walker Terminology to Evade Disclosure

Space Force, [[NORAD]], and the [[Department of Defense]] use the technical term "Fast Walker" (and "Slow Walker") for objects detected in space. These terms are kept separate from "UFO" and "UAP", which are applied to atmospheric phenomena. Because of this distinction, the organizations can deny FOIA requests for "UFO" data while they keep collecting information on spacebound unidentified objects under different classification protocols.
