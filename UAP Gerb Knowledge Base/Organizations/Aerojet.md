Aerojet is an American maker of rocket and missile propulsion, and has been a major contractor for US military and space programs since World War II. From facilities in California and other locations, it develops propulsion systems for ballistic missiles, space launch vehicles, and satellite systems. Its work on military space programs meant it employed staff with expertise in satellite sensor systems and space-based detection technologies.

## Satellite Sensor Technicians and Fast Walker Research

Aerojet employed the satellite sensor technicians [[Lee Graham]] and [[Ron Regehr]], who researched [[Fast Walkers]]. These are objects that [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites detect as they enter or leave Earth's atmosphere. Graham and Regehr set out to match DSP satellite detection data with documented UFO incidents.

## 1976 Tehran UFO Investigation

Graham and Regehr confirmed that United States military DSP satellites tracked the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]]. In the course of their investigations they found a computer printout from the time of the UFO's overflight of Iran, showing that the DSP satellite had detected an anomalous object in Iranian airspace. The printout records 238 scans, about 39.7 minutes of continuous tracking, and carries the notation "possible SR" (possibly Strategic Reconnaissance).

## Access to Classified Detection Data

As contractor staff working on satellite sensor systems, Graham and Regehr would have had technical access to DSP detection records through their work on the program. That they could find and analyze specific DSP printouts about UFO incidents suggests their roles came with clearance and access to classified satellite detection data.

## Significance

Through its technical staff, who analyzed DSP detection records, Aerojet is the source of a rare confirmation from defense contractor employees that US military satellites have been tracking specific, well-documented UFO incidents. Graham and Regehr's research linked eyewitness UFO reports to classified satellite sensor data. It provided hard evidence that [[NORAD]] and DSP systems were detecting and tracking the same objects that military pilots and civilians saw.
