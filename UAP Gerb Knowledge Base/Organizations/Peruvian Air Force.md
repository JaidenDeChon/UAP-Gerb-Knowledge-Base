The Peruvian Air Force (Fuerza Aérea del Perú, or FAP) is the aerial warfare branch of the Peruvian Armed Forces. During the 1990s, the FAP took part in joint counter-narcotics operations with US military forces in Peru, including [[Operation Laser Strike]].

## Suspected Role in Peru UAP Downing

[[Jonathan Weygandt]] theorized that the [[Peruvian Air Force]] was responsible for downing the [[Egg-shaped UAP|egg-shaped craft]] he encountered during the 1997 [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]]. The FAP operated [[MIM-23 Hawk Missile]] batteries. The US military had phased the system out of general use in 1994 and out of USMC Laser Strike operations in early 1997, but Peru still had it in service. Weygandt had studied Hawk missile ballistics extensively, and he concluded that the catastrophic damage visible on the craft (an enormous gash in the rear, consistent with shrapnel penetrating it and bouncing around inside) matched the air burst fragmentation pattern of Hawk MIM-23 rounds. If the theory is right, the FAP fired on an object that its radar or ground observers had identified as a threat or as an unauthorized aircraft in Peruvian airspace.

## La Joya Airbase UFO Intercept

On April 11, 1980, the Peruvian Air Force ordered Lieutenant [[Oscar Santa Maria Huertas]] to take off in his Sukhoi Su-22 fighter from [[La Joya Airbase, Arequipa, Peru|La Joya Airbase]] to intercept a silvery object in restricted airspace near the end of the runway. The incident led to an official DOD investigation, and a [[DOD Joint Chiefs]] briefing document associated with [[Project Moon Dust]] corroborated Santa María's account.
