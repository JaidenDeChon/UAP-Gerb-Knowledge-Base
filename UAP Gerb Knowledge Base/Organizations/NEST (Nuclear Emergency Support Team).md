The Nuclear Emergency Support Team (NEST) is the Department of Energy's premier rapid response unit for nuclear and radiological emergencies. Operated under the [[National Nuclear Security Administration]] (NNSA), NEST deploys teams of scientists and technical specialists with specialized detection and assessment equipment to respond to incidents involving nuclear materials, improvised nuclear devices, or radiological dispersal events. NEST personnel are trained to rapidly characterize nuclear threats, advise on protective actions, and support law enforcement or military response to nuclear incidents anywhere in the world.

## Alleged UAP Involvement

NEST is alleged to be involved in UAP crash retrieval operations as the radiological assessment component of crash retrieval task forces. At the 1997 Peru crash site described by [[Jonathan Wagant]], DOE personnel in rubber suits, gas masks, and MOPP gear conducted what appear to have been CBR (chemical, biological, radiological) risk assessments — a mission profile consistent with NEST capabilities. UAP researchers argue that the presence of DOE radiological response assets at alleged UAP crash sites is consistent with a standing protocol in which NEST or NEST-adjacent teams deploy alongside [[160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment]] transport and tier 1 Delta Detachment security elements under [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)]] direction.
