The Wright Air Development Center (WADC) was the US Air Force's principal aircraft research, development and engineering center at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], Ohio, from 1951 to 1961. It was formed under the new [[Air Research and Development Command (ARDC)|Air Research and Development Command]] when research and development was taken away from [[Air Materiel Command]]; in Gerb's account it consolidated Wright-Patterson's engineering division, flight test division, office of air research and all-weather flying division. In 1961 it became the Aeronautical Systems Division under [[Air Force Systems Command]], later the Aeronautical Systems Center, which in 2012 became today's [[Air Force Life Cycle Management Center (AFLCMC)|Air Force Life Cycle Management Center]].

## The engineering twin of NASIC

In [[Videos/NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering/summary|NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering]], Gerb presents WADC as the engineering face of the coin whose intelligence face is the [[Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC)|Air Technical Intelligence Center]], an ancestor of [[National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)|NASIC]]. Both trace their roots to 1917 at McCook Field. Before 1951 they were parallel divisions of Air Materiel Command (its engineering division and its technical intelligence division). In 1951 they split, ATIC to Air Force headquarters and WADC to ARDC, but stayed neighbours with a close working relationship: WADC was a prime customer for ATIC's assessments, since its job was to design systems to beat Soviet ones, and ATIC relied on WADC's engineering expertise, test facilities and laboratories for the deep engineering of foreign materiel exploitation, as in the MiG-15 work. In 1961 the two were deliberately reunited under Air Force Systems Command, to put foreign technology intelligence in the hands of the Air Force's research, development, test and evaluation enterprise, and they split again in 1991–1992.

Gerb ties WADC to UFO crash retrieval through two threads:

- **Kingman.** [[Eric Henry Wang]] of WADC was named by [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]] as the scientific and technical lead on the craft recovered at the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|1953 Kingman retrieval]], which [[Edward Bushnell Doll]], later of TRW's Space Technology Laboratories, coordinated. Gerb reads this as WADC and ATIC working the crash retrieval mission together, on engineering and intelligence respectively.
- **The Blue Room.** The one Air Force film labelled "Blue Room", *Blue Room Radar Scope, Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, 1955*, was shot by and received from Wright-Patterson's engineering division, that is, WADC. Hangar 4 at Wright-Patterson, where Gerb places the saucer seen by "PJ" ([[Paul Stafford Johnson Jr.]]), was used both by the Foreign Technology Division and by WADC's flying unit, the 4950th Test Wing. Gerb concludes that the [[Blue Room]] has moved between the intelligence and engineering sides of the base over the decades.
