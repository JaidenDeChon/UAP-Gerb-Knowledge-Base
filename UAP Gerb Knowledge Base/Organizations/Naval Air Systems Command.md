Naval Air Systems Command (NAVAIR) is one of the U.S. Navy's major systems commands (syscoms). It is responsible for the acquisition, development, testing, and life-cycle support of naval aviation systems. NAVAIR is headquartered at [[Naval Air Station Patuxent River]] ("Pax River") in Maryland and is the aviation counterpart to [[Naval Sea Systems Command]] (NAVSEA), which handles ships, submarines, and surface combat systems. Gerb identifies NAVAIR, and specifically its program executive offices (PEOs) at Pax River, as the main administrative and operational home of an alleged [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]]-managed program to reverse-engineer UFO technology, which in his account traces back to the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]].

## Role in Alleged MITRE Legacy Programs

Gerb alleges that NAVAIR's program executive offices at Pax River house the core of a decades-old Navy-MITRE reverse-engineering effort, both administratively and operationally. He argues that this role makes sense because UAP frequently demonstrate transmedium capabilities that span both NAVAIR's aviation domain and [[Naval Sea Systems Command|NAVSEA]]'s maritime domain. Pax River is described as the center of Naval Aviation Research, Development, Test and Evaluation (RDT&E). It is home to NAVAIR, the U.S. Navy Test Pilot School (USNTPS) and the Naval Air Warfare Center Aircraft Division (NAWCAD). NAVAIR is alleged to work alongside [[Office of Naval Intelligence|ONI]] as a primary program administrator, with counterintelligence support coming through ONI's relationship with the [[Naval Criminal Investigative Service]] and the [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations]].

In 2025, Representative [[Eric Burlison]] and White House staff visited Pax River to investigate possible hangar facilities used to store recovered vehicles. Separately, investigative journalist Chris Sharp, writing for the outlet Liberation Times and citing sources, has named Pax River as a site where one or more vehicles of unknown origin were allegedly stored.
