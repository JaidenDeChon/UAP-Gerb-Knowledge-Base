The Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) is the US Department of Defense's main all-source military intelligence agency, founded in 1961 and headquartered in Washington, DC. Its director is the DoD Executive Agent for the [[Foreign Material Program (FMP)|Foreign Materiel Program]], which acquires and exploits foreign weapons and technology, a role set out in [[DoD Directive C-3325.01E]] of 2006. In Gerb's videos the agency recurs in disputes over access to legacy UAP programs and, in his NASIC video, as the owner of the program inside which he argues part of the UFO legacy effort hides.

## Legacy program connections

Thomas Wilson was the agency's deputy director. He is connected to disputes over access to legacy UAP programs.

The agency is mentioned alongside the CIA in connection with the officer who warned about threats.

The agency was possibly involved in a program similar to OAP-L, which may have been meant to receive UAP materials from Lockheed Martin.

James Clapper was DIA Director and allegedly managed crash retrieval issues while in that post.

DIA Colonel Harold E. Phillips allegedly chaired the [[UFO Working Group]], a secret interagency UFO study group formed in February 1987, according to [[Philip J. Corso]]'s manuscript *Dawn of a New Age*.

## The Foreign Materiel Program

In [[Videos/NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering/summary|NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering]], Gerb describes the DIA's Directorate of Science and Technology as the program's executive agent, managing it for the whole DoD under the oversight of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence, with exploitation carried out by the services' centers, including the [[National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)|National Air and Space Intelligence Center]]. CIA reading-room records, he says, show the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]] as its close partner for decades. Gerb notes that the DIA scientist [[James Lacatski]] has said he worked on a program in which the US gained access to the interior of a craft of unknown origin, and places that work under the DIA's purview; he criticizes *[[The Age of Disclosure]]* and [[Jay Stratton]]'s book for leaving the DIA out of their accounts of the legacy programs. In his reconstruction, a foreign intelligence estimate of the US retrieval program, intercepted by the [[NSA]], would go to the DIA as the all-source agency owning the program, which would bring in the [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations]] and cleared NASIC staff. He also cites [[David Grusch]]'s complaint that the DIA has withheld such foreign intelligence from [[Anna Paulina Luna]]'s task force despite lawful requests.
