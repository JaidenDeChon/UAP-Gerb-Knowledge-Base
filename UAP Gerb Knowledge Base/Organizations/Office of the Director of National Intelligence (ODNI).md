The Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) is the US government office that leads and coordinates the intelligence community. It was created by the Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act of 2004, which President George W. Bush signed on 17 December 2004, and it opened on 22 April 2005. It is headed by the Director of National Intelligence (DNI). Gerb's videos deal mostly with a few of the people who ran it and with its acquisition office, which he counts among the gatekeepers of the UFO legacy programs.

## Clapper's ODNI

[[James Clapper]] was Director of National Intelligence from 2010 to 2017. Gerb calls him the closest thing to a [[Dick Cheney]]-level boss of the legacy programs after Cheney left office in 2009. He alleges that Clapper, with deputies [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]] and [[Michael Dempsey]], oversaw a reduced National Security Council crash retrieval portfolio, and that Clapper used his DNI credentials to pull Defense Department legacy programs, [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]]'s among them, into that portfolio. O'Sullivan, formerly deputy director of the CIA and of its Directorate of Science and Technology, became Principal Deputy DNI in 2011 on Clapper's recommendation and held the post until 2017. Gerb believes both had "dual-hatted" roles in the ODNI and the legacy programs. He also ties Clapper's ODNI to the [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]] cover effort and to the attempt to present [[Hillary Clinton]] as a "disclosure president".

Two other ODNI officials appear in the videos. [[Donald M. Kerr]], whom Gerb calls an "MJ-1" equivalent, joined MITRE's board straight after serving as Principal Deputy DNI and later chaired it. [[Robert Cardillo]], then deputy DNI for intelligence integration, is named in research by Gerb's friend Rob Jones and in [[Christopher Sharp]]'s reporting as a possible blocker of Lockheed Martin's proposed transfer of crash retrieval material to the government, a claim the video treats as an allegation.

## The acquisition office

In his [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]] video, Gerb says the offices under the DNI and the Secretary of Defense matter more than the principals. He singles out the ODNI's Deputy Director for Acquisition, Technology and Facilities, which a July 2016 GAO report shows shares milestone decision authority over NRO acquisitions with the Pentagon's acquisition under secretary. He treats the two offices together as a nexus of legacy-program gatekeepers. [[Doug Wolfe]], who founded the [[CIA Office of Global Access]], served as the ODNI's acquisition deputy, and [[Mark Moynihan]] also had ties to the office.
