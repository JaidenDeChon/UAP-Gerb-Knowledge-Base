The Army Ballistic Missile Agency (ABMA) was a US Army rocket and missile organization set up at Redstone Arsenal in Huntsville, Alabama, on 1 February 1956. Major General John B. Medaris commanded it, and [[Wernher von Braun]] was its technical director. On 1 July 1960 its space missions, about 4,000 civilian employees and von Braun's team moved to [[NASA]]'s new Marshall Space Flight Center at Redstone Arsenal. Gerb brings the agency up in two connections: an alleged underground laboratory at Area 51, and early plans for underground facilities near Huntsville.

## The "Defense Advanced Research Center"

[[Edgar Fouche]] said [[Area 51]] held an underground laboratory called the Defense Advanced Research Center, ten stories deep beside a mountain near [[Papoose Lake]]. To the objection that no such center ever belonged to [[DARPA]], Gerb answers with a declassified 1973 historical study of ARPA. It records that advanced aerospace projects were carried out by von Braun's team at ABMA, and that a draft Defense Department directive considered moving the agency into a "Defense Advanced Research Center", which Gerb links to the Jet Propulsion Laboratory. Whether such a center was ever created, and whether any of von Braun's concepts reached it, is unconfirmed (see [[Defense Advanced Research Center (DARS)]]). Gerb also notes that von Braun's mentor, [[Hermann Oberth]], studied UFOs.

## Green Mountain

In his video on [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)|deep underground military bases]], Gerb cites two 1957 articles on an Army plan, proposed by American Machine and Foundry, Redstone Arsenal and ABMA, for a rocket factory inside Green Mountain outside Huntsville, with an underground "junior Pentagon" to direct the defense of the southern United States. It is not known whether it was built. Gerb reads the plan as an early example of private and military cooperation.
