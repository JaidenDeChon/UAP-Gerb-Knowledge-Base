Decision Science Applications, Inc. (DSAI) was an employee-owned U.S. technical services company founded in Virginia on 4 January 1977. It had expertise in military command and control systems, commercial information management systems, software development, electrical engineering, telecommunications and physics. Whistleblower Colonel [[Steve Wilson]] alleged that DSAI was an important contractor in UFO programs hidden inside the [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)|Strategic Defense Initiative]], and that it was effectively the same company as [[SAIC]].

## Steve Wilson's claims

According to Gerb, Wilson claimed that DSAI and SAIC "were one and the same", that DSAI's board included directors of UFO black budget divisions at companies such as IBM, Lockheed, Teledyne Ryan and Aerojet ElectroSystems, and that Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]], an SAIC board member, also ran DSAI. Wilson's "Star Wars City" chart lists DSAI among contractors working on the UFO portfolio within SDI, which he said was paramount to those programs. Gerb is skeptical of Wilson. He says there is no concrete evidence DSAI was spun out of SAIC, though he thinks it likely.

## Corporate lineage

Drawing on posts by a since-deleted Reddit researcher, Gerb traces DSAI's assets through these steps:

1. In 1998 DSAI merged into DSA Acquisitions, Inc., which Steven Myers & Associates (SM&A) bought for almost $31 million in cash and stock, completing the deal on 21 August 1998.
2. SM&A acquired Emergent Information Technologies and took its name.
3. On 6 December 2001 Emergent sold its Virginia-based government services group to [[L3 Communications]].
4. In July 2012 part of L-3 was spun off as Engility, which SAIC bought for $2.5 billion.

Gerb concludes that DSAI's assets reached both SAIC and L3Harris, two contractors he believes are involved in UFO legacy operations.
