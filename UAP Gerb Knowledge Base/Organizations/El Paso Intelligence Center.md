The **El Paso Intelligence Center (EPIC)** is a federal tactical operational intelligence center established at [[Fort Bliss]], Texas, in 1974. It coordinates intelligence gathering and law enforcement operations along the US-Mexico border, focusing mainly on counter-narcotics, counter-terrorism, and border security.

## Establishment and Mission

EPIC was established in 1974, the same year as the alleged [[1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval]]. It is a multi-agency intelligence fusion center, and its participating agencies include:

- Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA), the lead agency
- FBI
- US Customs and Border Protection
- Department of Defense
- US Coast Guard
- Department of Homeland Security
- Various other federal, state, and local agencies

EPIC's mission includes collecting, analyzing, and disseminating tactical intelligence on cross-border criminal and security threats.

## Potential Involvement in Coyame Case

Since EPIC was set up at Fort Bliss in 1974 to gather intelligence along the US-Mexico border, the center may have helped collect intelligence on the Coyame incident. Its possible roles include:

- Coordinating or tasking reconnaissance aircraft to fly over northern Mexico and gather intelligence on the crash site and the Mexican military's recovery efforts.
- Intercepting Mexican military radio communications about the crash and recovery operations, which were then reported "through channels to the [[CIA]]," as the [[Denb Report]] describes.
- Giving the CIA recovery team analytical support, including mapping, route planning, and threat assessment for the cross-border operation.

The Denb Report states that "radio interceptions were reported through channels to the CIA," which points to a process of collecting and passing on intelligence that fits EPIC's mission and capabilities.

## Fort Bliss Staging

The CIA recovery team staged at [[Fort Bliss]], where EPIC was based, so the center was in a position to give the operation direct intelligence support. Unmarked helicopters, specialized personnel, and equipment were assembled at Fort Bliss within 24 hours of the crash. That speed suggests a well-coordinated intelligence and logistics operation, possibly with EPIC acting as an intelligence node.

## Border Intelligence Context

In 1974, anti-drug smuggling operations along the US-Mexico border were a major priority. Its counter-narcotics mission would have given EPIC operational awareness of aircraft crossing the border, including small aircraft engaged in smuggling. The Denb Report speculates that the civilian aircraft that collided with the UAP may have been smuggling drugs, given how low it was flying and how common smuggling flights were.

If EPIC was monitoring cross-border air traffic for counter-narcotics purposes on August 25, 1974, it may have detected, or been alerted to, the civilian aircraft's flight and later disappearance, as well as the unknown object tracked by US Air Defense radar.

## Classification

Any part EPIC played in the Coyame case remains speculative, and no official documentation confirming a role has been released. Still, the center's mission, its location, and the year it was established make it a plausible intelligence support asset for cross-border UAP crash retrieval operations.
