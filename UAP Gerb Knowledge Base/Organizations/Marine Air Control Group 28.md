Marine Air Control Group 28 (MACG-28, often written "MAC G28") is a United States Marine Corps aviation command-and-control unit of the 2nd Marine Aircraft Wing, based at [[Cherry Point, North Carolina Marine Corps Air Station|Marine Corps Air Station Cherry Point]], North Carolina. Its units provide air control, air surveillance and low-altitude air defense for Marine aviation. In UAP research it is the parent unit of Lance Corporal [[Jonathan Weygandt]], who says that in 1997, while deployed with the group to Peru, he came upon a crashed non-human craft.

## Operation Laser Strike

In 1997, Marines of MACG-28 deployed to Peru in support of [[Operation Laser Strike]], a classified [[US SOUTHCOM|US Southern Command]] counter-narcotics operation. According to UAP Gerb, they served as perimeter security for the operation's radar installations and replaced ageing Hawk missile batteries with Stinger man-portable air defense systems and Avenger surface-to-air missile platforms. That MACG-28 Marines from Cherry Point deployed to Laser Strike is documented; which radar site they guarded is not publicly known, and some details of the operation remain classified.

Weygandt served in the group's 2nd Low Altitude Air Defense Battalion as a low-altitude air defense gunner. The non-commissioned officers he names from the night of the [[Peru UFO Crash Incident|crash]] — [[Sergeant Montil|Staff Sergeant Montil]], [[Sergeant Allen]] and [[Sergeant Atkins]] — were members of the same unit, and all three went on to long Marine Corps careers. Weygandt says that after the incident MACG-28 and the Criminal Investigation Division made his remaining service "horrific", until his discharge in March 1998.
