Marine Air Control Group 28 (MACG-28, often written "MAC G28") is a United States Marine Corps aviation command-and-control unit of the 2nd Marine Aircraft Wing, based at [[Cherry Point, North Carolina Marine Corps Air Station|Marine Corps Air Station Cherry Point]], North Carolina. Its units provide air control, air surveillance and low-altitude air defense for Marine aviation. In UAP research it is the parent unit of Lance Corporal [[Jonathan Weygandt]], who says that in 1997, while deployed with the group to Peru, he came upon a crashed non-human craft.

## Operation Laser Strike

In 1997, Marines of MACG-28 deployed to Peru in support of [[Operation Laser Strike]], a classified [[US SOUTHCOM|US Southern Command]] counter-narcotics operation. According to UAP Gerb, they served as perimeter security for the operation's radar installations and replaced ageing Hawk missile batteries with Stinger man-portable air defense systems and Avenger surface-to-air missile platforms. The deployment of MACG-28 Marines from Cherry Point to Laser Strike is documented, but which radar site they guarded is not publicly known, and some details of the operation remain classified.

Weygandt served in the group's 2nd Low Altitude Air Defense Battalion as a low-altitude air defense gunner. He names three non-commissioned officers from the night of the [[Peru UFO Crash Incident|crash]]: [[Sergeant Montil|Staff Sergeant Montil]], [[Sergeant Allen]] and [[Sergeant Atkins]]. All three were members of the same unit and went on to long Marine Corps careers. Weygandt says that after the incident MACG-28 and the Criminal Investigation Division made his remaining service "horrific", until his discharge in March 1998.
