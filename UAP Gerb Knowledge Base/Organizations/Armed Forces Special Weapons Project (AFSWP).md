The Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP) was a joint Army and Navy organization that managed nuclear weapons after the [[Atomic Energy Commission]] took over from the Manhattan Project. Secretary of War Robert P. Patterson and Secretary of the Navy [[James Forrestal]] established it on January 29, 1947. It was responsible for the custody, assembly and testing of nuclear weapons, and for training the armed forces to deliver them. Its first chief was [[Leslie Groves]], who had directed the Manhattan Project. UAP research identifies the AFSWP as one of several organizational bridges between the Manhattan Project's secrecy apparatus and the structure of the early UFO legacy program.

## Establishment and Function

The 1946 Atomic Energy Act gave civilian development and production of nuclear weapons to the newly formed Atomic Energy Commission (AEC). The AFSWP was created alongside it to handle the military, operational side of nuclear weapons that fell outside the AEC's civilian mandate: custody, assembly procedures, and training military personnel to handle the weapons. The AFSWP and Los Alamos Z Division set up joint operations at Sandia Base in Albuquerque, New Mexico, on the same site as [[Sandia National Laboratories]] (then Los Alamos Z Division).

## Alleged UFO Legacy Program Connections

UAP research has identified several specific connections between AFSWP personnel and alleged UFO crash retrieval operations:

**1947 Roswell:** An alleged Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit (IPU) report dated July 22, 1947 says that Colonel [[Sherman V. Hasbrouck]] of the AFSWP ordered a "special radiobiological team, accompanied by a SED [Special Engineer Detachment] and a security detail from Sandia National Labs" to secure the immediate area around the Roswell crash site. Hasbrouck was a real Army officer who helped build up the AFSWP and worked closely with [[Leslie Groves]].

**1948 Aztec:** J. Andrew Kisner, a former New Mexico State Representative, interviewed firsthand sources from the legacy program at length in the early 1990s. He stated that the early operations of the UFO legacy program had military support from Air Material Command and from the AFSWP at Sandia Base.

**1953 Kingman:** [[Edward Bushnell Doll]], a senior executive vice president at TRW and a Manhattan Project veteran (specifically of Project Alberta), was sent to the [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]] nuclear tests in 1953 as chairman of the Stanford Research Institute. He went under a joint DoD and Atomic Energy Commission task force that the AFSWP oversaw. UAP Gerb's *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* says that during Operation Upshot-Knothole, Doll was technical director for two major special weapons programs for the AFSWP. [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]] accused Doll of coordinating the personnel and logistics of the 1953 Kingman, Arizona UFO crash retrieval. In his diary, Stansel wrote that Doll was "directly in charge of the UFO crash retrieval."

**Contentious IPU Documents:** AFSWP personnel and the organization's structure appear in several alleged IPU documents that claim to record how early UFO crash retrievals were coordinated.

## Modern Successor: Defense Threat Reduction Agency

The AFSWP became in turn the Defense Atomic Support Agency (1959-1971), the Defense Nuclear Agency (1971-1996) and the Defense Special Weapons Agency (1996-1998). In 1998 it became the [[Defense Threat Reduction Agency (DTRA)|Defense Threat Reduction Agency]] (DTRA). Former defense analyst [[Dylan Borland]] has stated that while working near DTRA, he saw old Atomic Energy Commission files that concluded some UFOs are of extraterrestrial origin. Such files would be protected under the DOE's statutory classification authority, which the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]] established.

## Sandia Base

In UAP Gerb's investigation of [[Sandia National Laboratories]], AFSWP is what links Sandia Base to the earliest recoveries. The 1994 sources of former New Mexico state representative [[Andrew Kissner]] said AFSWP personnel at Sandia Base supported the analysis of discs recovered in 1947. The IPU report names Hasbrouck of the AFSWP as the officer who ordered the July 1947 recovery teams. The IPU documents appeared in 1995, after Kissner had finished, so Gerb treats the two as separate sources and suggests the documents may have borrowed from Kissner. He also notes that in 1947 AFSWP began building Manzano Base (Site Able), a nuclear weapons store with two underground installations, and that it built much of Sandia's Coyote Test Field in the late 1940s.
