Lockheed Martin Corporation is the largest defense contractor in the world. In 2024 it earned $64.65 billion in revenue and employed approximately 122,000 personnel, and its market capitalization exceeds $136 billion. The corporation was formed on March 15, 1995, when Lockheed Corporation merged with Martin Marietta, creating the leading US aerospace and defense manufacturer. Multiple whistleblowers, researchers, and Congressional witnesses allege that Lockheed Martin is the central private contractor for US government UFO crash retrieval, material exploitation, and reverse engineering programs, from 1947 to the present. They identify its [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works|Skunk Works]] Advanced Development Programs division as the specific part of the company that manages technologies of unknown origin.

## Corporate Structure and Divisions

Lockheed Martin operates in four primary divisions:
1. Aeronautics (39% of revenue): houses [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works|Skunk Works]] and built the F-35 Lightning II, F-22 Raptor, F-16 Falcon, C-130 Hercules, and F-117 Nighthawk
2. Missiles and Fire Control (18% of revenue): surface-to-air missiles, the AGM-114 Hellfire, and special forces support
3. Rotary and Mission Systems (24% of revenue): Sikorsky helicopters (UH-60 Blackhawk, CH-53), Aegis combat systems, and littoral combat ships
4. Space (18% of revenue): Trident II ballistic missiles, the Orion spacecraft, and hypersonic weapons

Lockheed Martin earns 73% of its revenue from US government contracts, mainly with the Department of Defense.

## Formation and Corporate Lineage

Lockheed Corporation (founded 1926):
- Rose to prominence with WWII P-38 Lightning aircraft
- Founded [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works|Skunk Works]] in 1943 at Burbank, California
- Developed the U-2 spy plane, the SR-71 Blackbird, and the F-117 Nighthawk
- A 1986 GAO audit exposed critical security failures in how the Burbank Skunk Works controlled special access program documents

Martin Marietta (founded 1961):
- Focused heavily on missiles and space systems (Titan rockets, Pershing missiles, Viking Mars landers)
- 1993: Acquired General Electric Aerospace (GE has been implicated in UFO programs since WWII)
- 1993: Acquired General Dynamics Space Systems Division
- 1993: Acquired management contracts for [[Sandia National Laboratories]], a DOE federally funded research and development center (FFRDC) repeatedly implicated in UAP material exploitation

The 1995 merger brought two corporations, each with decades of alleged separate involvement in UFO programs, under single management.

UAP Gerb's *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* places the Lockheed–Martin Marietta merger within what it calls the defense industrial base "Last Supper". In July 1993, Secretary of Defense Les Aspin and Deputy Secretary of Defense William Perry told U.S. prime defense contractors that only a handful in each defense sector would survive the post–Cold War drawdown in defense spending. From 1993 to 1997, a wave of mergers and acquisitions cut the number of major U.S. defense contractors from 51 to five. These were the "big five": Lockheed Martin, [[Boeing]], [[Northrop Grumman]], Raytheon, and General Dynamics. The video argues that the mergers also consolidated and further compartmentalized whatever alleged UFO legacy program activity each predecessor company held.

## IRAD and Black Budget Funding

[[David Grusch]] identified Independent Research and Development (IRAD) as one way contractors fund UAP programs. IRAD is research that contractors can bill back to the Department of Defense (DoD) without traditional oversight. In 2021, Lockheed Martin spent $1.5 billion on IRAD. This lets Lockheed pursue classified exotic technology programs and charge the DoD for the work.

## Defense Industrial Base Integration
