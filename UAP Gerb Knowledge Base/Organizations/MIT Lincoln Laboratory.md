MIT Lincoln Laboratory is a federally funded research and development center (FFRDC) affiliated with the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. It reopened in 1951 as the direct successor to [[Vannevar Bush]]'s MIT Radiation Laboratory, established in 1940, which had developed microwave radar technology for navigation and combat during World War II. The laboratory is sponsored by the Department of Defense under the Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering. Within UAP research it is best known as the parent institution of the [[MITRE Corporation]], which was spun off from it in 1958 to provide systems-engineering leadership for the SAGE national air defense program.

## Role in SAGE and MITRE's Founding

Under the internal designation "Project Lincoln," MIT Lincoln Laboratory led early development of the [[Semi-Automatic Ground Environment (SAGE)]], the United States' first computer-based national air defense system. As SAGE grew in scale and complexity, the U.S. Air Force and MIT Lincoln Laboratory jointly created MITRE in 1958. They staffed the new organization largely with people from Lincoln Laboratory's research and engineering group, including MITRE's first technical director, [[Robert R. Everett]]. Gerb identifies Lincoln Laboratory, through this lineage, as MITRE's institutional and scientific "bloodline," and he places the laboratory, via Vannevar Bush, in the same institutional tradition that he credits with custodianship of nonhuman technical materials recovered after the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]].

## Ongoing Relationship with MITRE

Gerb notes that MITRE has an ongoing relationship with MIT Lincoln Laboratory as a jointly used facility partner. He also notes that a 2025 congressional interrogative letter sent to MITRE by Representative [[Eric Burlison]] and [[David Grusch]] specifically requests that MITRE detail its continuing relationship with Lincoln Laboratory as part of a broader accounting of third-party facility and material relationships. According to Gerb, Lincoln Laboratory was reportedly expected to deliver a film associated with Captain Edward J. Ruppelt to Congress through Representative Burlison's office, and as of this reporting the film had not been produced.
