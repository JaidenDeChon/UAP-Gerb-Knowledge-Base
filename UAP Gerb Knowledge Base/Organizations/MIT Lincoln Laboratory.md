MIT Lincoln Laboratory is a federally funded research and development center (FFRDC) affiliated with the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, reopened in 1951 as the direct successor to [[Vannevar Bush]]'s MIT Radiation Laboratory (established 1940), which had developed microwave radar technology for navigation and combat during World War II. Sponsored by the Department of Defense under the Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering, Lincoln Laboratory is best known within UAP research as the parent institution from which the [[MITRE Corporation]] was spun off in 1958 to provide systems-engineering leadership for the SAGE national air defense program.

## Role in SAGE and MITRE's Founding

Under the internal designation "Project Lincoln," MIT Lincoln Laboratory led early development of the Semi-Automatic Ground Environment (SAGE) — see [[Semi-Automatic Ground Environment (SAGE)]] — the United States' first computer-based national air defense system. As SAGE's scale and complexity grew, the U.S. Air Force and MIT Lincoln Laboratory jointly created MITRE in 1958, staffing the new organization largely with personnel drawn from Lincoln Laboratory's research and engineering group, including MITRE's first technical director, [[Robert R. Everett]]. UAP Gerb identifies Lincoln Laboratory, through this lineage, as MITRE's institutional and scientific "bloodline," and — via Vannevar Bush — situates the laboratory within the same institutional tradition UAP Gerb credits with custodianship of nonhuman technical materials recovered following the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]].

## Ongoing Relationship with MITRE

UAP Gerb notes that MITRE maintains an ongoing relationship with MIT Lincoln Laboratory as a jointly used facility partner, and that a 2025 congressional interrogative letter sent to MITRE by Representative [[Eric Burlison]] and [[David Grusch]] specifically requests that MITRE detail its continuing relationship with Lincoln Laboratory as part of a broader accounting of third-party facility and material relationships. UAP Gerb also notes that Lincoln Laboratory was reportedly expected to deliver a film, associated with Captain Edward J. Ruppelt, to Congress via Representative Burlison's office, and that as of this reporting the film had not been produced.
