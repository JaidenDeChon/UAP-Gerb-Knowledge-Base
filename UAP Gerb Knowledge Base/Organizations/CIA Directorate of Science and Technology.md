The CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology (DS&T, sometimes rendered DSMT in captions) is one of the agency's principal directorates. It was founded in 1963 to centralize the CIA's research and development efforts and its technical intelligence collection. Gerb identifies DS&T as a recurring gatekeeper in the administration of the alleged UFO legacy programs. Contrary to claims made in the 2025 documentary *Age of Disclosure*, he does not see it as the sole controlling body or "crash retrieval portfolio director" of the legacy programs; he describes it as an office with its "fingers" in many of the legacy program's administrative pies. DS&T is cited as comparable to the Air Force's SAF/AQ, as an internal directorate that supports classified programs within its parent organization.

## Alleged UFO Legacy Program Role

Gerb states that many of the legacy program gatekeepers and personnel discussed across his research have deep ties to DS&T. He names in particular [[Dawn Meyerriecks]], [[Doug Wolfe]], [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]], [[Glenn Gaffney]], and [[Mary K. Sturdivant|Mary Sturdivant]]. In a 1989 phone call with NASA mission specialist Bob Echler, [[Bobby Ray Inman]] named R. Everett Heinman, CIA DS&T Deputy Director and NRO Program B Director, as the right person to contact about recovered UAP technology becoming available for research. The call is cited as evidence that DS&T leadership was directly connected to the alleged custody of UAP material.

## Blocking of the Kona Blue Transfer (2011)

Gerb attributes the killing of [[Kona Blue]] in 2011 specifically to elements of DS&T. Kona Blue was a proposed special access program that would have transferred [[Lockheed Martin]]-held UAP materials to the [[AAWSAP]] program through the DHS Science and Technology Directorate. He names [[Glenn Gaffney]] and [[Mary K. Sturdivant|Mary Sturdivant]] as the officials who ultimately blocked the technology transfer agreement. The video theorizes that Gaffney and other CIA personnel objected to [[Lockheed Martin Space Systems|Lockheed Martin]] bringing an "uninitiated" contractor, [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]], into the UFO materials space outside CIA control. It frames DS&T's intervention as an effort to preserve centralized gatekeeping over unregulated groups possessing UAP materials.
