Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS) was the Las Vegas company that held the Defense Intelligence Agency's contract for the [[AAWSAP|Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program]]. [[Robert Bigelow]] set it up as an offshoot of his company Bigelow Aerospace. BAASS is central to the alleged attempts by [[Lockheed Martin]] between 2008 and 2011 to transfer recovered non-human material to the government, and it was preparing to receive that material.

## The AAWSAP contract

Senator [[Harry Reid]] started AAWSAP inside the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]] between 2007 and 2008, with about $22 million over five years. According to BAASS's ten-month report of 30 July 2009, contract HHM402-08-C-0072 was awarded on 22 September 2008. It required BAASS to acquire buildings that met the rules for top secret work and to hire staff to research twelve designated areas: lift, propulsion, control, power generation, spatial and temporal translation, materials, configuration and structure, signature reduction, human interface, human effects, armament, and other areas supporting the first eleven. The same report proposed "Project Forum", a plan to "raise the level of awareness of the importance of ET and the potential disclosure in North America", starting in California.

## The underground vault

Between May and July 2009, BAASS reported substantial progress in procuring a highly secure underground vault. It said the vault was needed to "pursue and acquire recovered hardware and biological samples from the corporate world and/or from private individuals and groups". The design called for 5,000 square feet of space 15 to 20 feet below ground, with walls, ceilings and floors 3 feet thick, several tunnels, security doors and at least one thick steel vault door. It was to be operational by late 2009 or early 2010.

Gerb argues that the vault was built for Lockheed's material. His video on Lockheed plays a clip in which an AAWSAP insider says the company's executives had to deal with CIA official [[Glenn Gaffney]], who was "very antagonistic" about the company releasing its crash retrieval materials to BAASS and [[EarthTech]]. Lockheed vice president [[James T. Ryder]] proposed the transfer. A second attempt went through [[Kona Blue]], a prospective DHS program. Both attempts were blocked.
