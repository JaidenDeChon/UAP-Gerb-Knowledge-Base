Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies (BAASS) was the Las Vegas company, set up by [[Robert Bigelow]] as an offshoot of his Bigelow Aerospace, that held the Defense Intelligence Agency's contract for the [[AAWSAP|Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program]]. It is central to the alleged attempts by [[Lockheed Martin]] between 2008 and 2011 to transfer recovered non-human material to the government, which BAASS was preparing to receive.

## The AAWSAP contract

AAWSAP was started by Senator [[Harry Reid]] between 2007 and 2008 in the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]], with about $22 million over five years. According to BAASS's ten-month report of 30 July 2009, contract HHM402-08-C-0072, awarded on 22 September 2008, required BAASS to acquire buildings that met the rules for top secret work and to hire staff to research twelve designated areas: lift, propulsion, control, power generation, spatial and temporal translation, materials, configuration and structure, signature reduction, human interface, human effects, armament, and other areas supporting the first eleven. The same report proposed "Project Forum", to "raise the level of awareness of the importance of ET and the potential disclosure in North America", starting in California.

## The underground vault

By May to July 2009, BAASS reported substantial progress in procuring a highly secure underground vault, which it said was needed to "pursue and acquire recovered hardware and biological samples from the corporate world and/or from private individuals and groups". The design called for 5,000 square feet 15 to 20 feet below ground, with 3-foot-thick walls, ceilings and floors, several tunnels, security doors and at least one thick steel vault door, operational by late 2009 or early 2010.

UAP Gerb argues the vault was built for Lockheed's material. In a clip his Lockheed video plays, an AAWSAP insider says the executives of the aerospace firm had to deal with CIA official [[Glenn Gaffney]], who was "very antagonistic" about the company releasing its crash retrieval materials to BAASS and [[EarthTech]]. Lockheed vice president [[James T. Ryder]] proposed the transfer, the second attempt through the prospective DHS program [[Kona Blue]], and both attempts were blocked.
