The Navy Special Program (NSP) is a highly compartmented US Navy program, administered by the Navy Special Program Office (later referenced as the Navy Special Program Central Office). It serves as an umbrella control system for several of the Navy's clandestine undersea operations. It appears in declassified National Security Council and CIA records of the 1970s and 1980s alongside the National Reconnaissance Program. UAP Gerb argues that it is the likely administrative home of the [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] (NURO) and of any Navy program that retrieves anomalous craft from the sea.

## Documentary trail

UAP Gerb assembles the following references:

- **1974 NSC directive.** The earliest mention the host finds is a 1974 omnibus National Security Council intelligence directive, which lists the NSP beside the National Reconnaissance Program, the funding mechanism for the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]]. Because the NRO was not declassified until 1992, the host reads the pairing as deliberate obfuscation.
- **29 October 1974 CIA memo.** A memo on clearances held by NSC Intelligence Committee working-group members discusses programs that overlap "compartmented areas" of the NSP. It requires read-on requests for a redacted individual and CIA officer Richard Ober.
- **11 December 1975 memo.** A memo for the Deputy Director of Central Intelligence shows the NSP designated to run parallel to the National Reconnaissance Program under the Executive Committee for the Director of Central Intelligence and the Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence. It serves as an intelligence-collection program comparable to the NRO, DIA or CIA.
- **14 December 1984 memo.** A memo from the Director of Central Intelligence's staff to Director of Naval Intelligence Rear Admiral John Butts refers to a letter from Secretary of the Navy John Lehman concerning the NSP. It suggests including a representative of [[Everett Heinman]]'s CIA department on a redacted ad hoc requirements committee.
- **26 May 2023.** Requests for approval of special access program facilities by the Navy Special Program Central Office show the office still active.

A similarly named "Special Navy Control Program", which author Jeffrey T. Richelson identifies as a NURO submarine imagery and SIGINT effort, is treated by the host as closely related.

## Relationship to NURO and undersea retrieval

NURO was created in 1969 by agreement between the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology and the Navy, and its activity grew through the 1970s, the same period as the NSP memos. The host therefore argues that NURO was likely rolled under the NSP. By extension, he argues, a program that recovers craft from the seafloor alongside NURO and delivers them to the [[Office of Naval Research]] would be compartmented within the NSP. He suggests this is why Rear Admiral [[Rear Admiral Timothy Gallaudet|Timothy Gallaudet]] was never briefed on the naval programs whose existence he has heard testified to.

## Personnel

- **Samuel Eugene "Gene" Poteat**, a senior CIA scientific intelligence officer, served as technical director of the Navy's Special Programs Office. He led the CIA's [[Project Palladium]], which used naval ships and submarines to feed false radar targets to Soviet air defenses.
- UAP Gerb reports contacting several former NSP personnel whose identities he withholds: a diver who served three years with Mobile Diving and Salvage Unit One under the NSP, a project lead of eight years for the NSP office and SOCOM's acquisition program office, and a former Office of Naval Intelligence chief information security officer for the NSP.
