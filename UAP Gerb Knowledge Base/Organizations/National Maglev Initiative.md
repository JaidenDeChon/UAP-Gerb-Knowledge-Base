The National Maglev Initiative (NMI) was a US federal research program of the early 1990s that studied whether magnetic levitation (maglev) trains could play a role in the national transportation system. It was a multi-agency effort in which the [[US Army Corps of Engineers]] and the Department of Energy took part, and it commissioned competing system concept definition studies from industry teams. UAP Gerb, who calls it the "National Maglev Institute", treats it as the acknowledged, aboveboard face of maglev research that he believes began much earlier in secret for trains linking [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)|deep underground military bases]].

## Mandate and Reports

In the words UAP Gerb quotes, the NMI sought to "define the role maglev could play in US transportation", to assess the domestic market, to recommend a strategy for introducing maglev technology, and to identify the most appropriate federal role. Its published *Compendium of Executive Summaries from the Maglev System Concept Definition Final Reports* aimed at a nationwide maglev system by the year 2000.

## Participating Contractors

The concept definition teams named in the compendium include:

- **Bechtel**, with Hughes Aircraft and MIT (which runs a University Affiliated Research Center for the Army)
- **Boeing Aerospace and General Dynamics**, studying superconducting levitation
- **Grumman Corporation** (before its merger into [[Northrop Grumman]]), [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] and [[Honeywell]], studying superconducting magnets

UAP Gerb says he strongly suspects all of these contractors of attempting the reverse engineering and material exploitation of non-human craft for decades.

## Significance in DUMB Research

UAP Gerb's proposed order of operations runs: underground bases built from 1961, connective tunnels from around 1972–1974, and train systems about a decade after that. He reads the NMI's 1990s aboveboard work by Grumman, General Dynamics and Battelle as implying clandestine electromagnetic research long before, noting that researchers [[Richard Sauder]] and [[Bill Hamilton]] first heard of secret underground rail systems in the 1980s.
