The National Maglev Initiative (NMI) was a US federal research program of the early 1990s that studied whether magnetic levitation (maglev) trains could play a role in the national transportation system. Several agencies took part, among them the [[US Army Corps of Engineers]] and the Department of Energy, and the program commissioned competing system concept definition studies from industry teams. Gerb, who calls it the "National Maglev Institute", treats it as the acknowledged, aboveboard face of maglev research. He believes that research began much earlier, in secret, for trains linking [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)|deep underground military bases]].

## Mandate and Reports

In the words Gerb quotes, the NMI sought to "define the role maglev could play in US transportation", to assess the domestic market, to recommend a strategy for introducing maglev technology, and to identify the most appropriate federal role. Its published *Compendium of Executive Summaries from the Maglev System Concept Definition Final Reports* aimed at a nationwide maglev system by the year 2000.

## Participating Contractors

The concept definition teams named in the compendium include:

- Bechtel, with Hughes Aircraft and MIT (which runs a University Affiliated Research Center for the Army)
- Boeing Aerospace and General Dynamics, studying superconducting levitation
- Grumman Corporation (before its merger into [[Northrop Grumman]]), [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] and [[Honeywell]], studying superconducting magnets

Gerb says he strongly suspects that all of these contractors have spent decades attempting the reverse engineering and material exploitation of non-human craft.

## Significance in DUMB Research

Gerb proposes this order of construction: underground bases from 1961, connective tunnels from around 1972–1974, and train systems about a decade after that. He reads the aboveboard work that Grumman, General Dynamics and Battelle did for the NMI in the 1990s as implying clandestine electromagnetic research long before. He notes that researchers [[Richard Sauder]] and [[Bill Hamilton]] first heard of secret underground rail systems in the 1980s.
