Air Combat Command (ACC) is a major command of the U.S. Air Force headquartered at [[Langley Air Force Base]], Virginia. It serves as the Air Force component command of U.S. Strategic Command and U.S. Northern Command, managing the service's combat air forces including fighter, bomber, reconnaissance, and command and control aircraft. In the context of alleged UAP legacy programs, ACC's position at Langley AFB — where witness [[Dylan Borland]] observed an apparent electro-optic cloaking triangular craft hovering over a NASA hangar around 2012 — has led researchers to theorize the command may play a role in test and evaluation activities involving reverse-engineered non-human airframes.

## Role in the Air Force

ACC organizes, trains, equips, and maintains combat-ready forces for rapid deployment across the globe. Its headquarters at Langley AFB places it in proximity to the Atlantic Command and joint operational planning infrastructure. The command oversees major combat air wings including those operating F-22 Raptors, F-35s, B-52 Stratofortresses, and the E-3 Sentry AWACS fleet.
