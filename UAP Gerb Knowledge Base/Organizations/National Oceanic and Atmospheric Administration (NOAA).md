The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) is a US federal agency in the Department of Commerce. It is responsible for ocean and atmospheric science, weather forecasting, fisheries management, and environmental observation. NOAA matters to UAP research mainly because of [[Rear Admiral Timothy Gallaudet]], who was the agency's acting administrator. He later became the most prominent institutional advocate for treating maritime UAP, specifically [[Unidentified Submerged Object (USO)|Unidentified Submerged Objects (USOs)]], as a national research priority.

## Timothy Gallaudet's Role

Gallaudet served as Assistant Secretary of Commerce for Oceans and Atmosphere (the Senate-confirmed civilian head of NOAA) and as acting NOAA Administrator. He is an ocean scientist by background and spent three and a half decades in ocean research and policy before he began advocating on UAP. Most UAP advocates lack that kind of direct professional link to USO research.

After leaving government service, Gallaudet drew on his NOAA experience and scientific credibility in his 2024 white paper *Beneath the Surface*, which argued that the study of USOs should be raised to the level of a national research priority. In his view, NOAA's ocean observation equipment, such as its satellites, buoys and research vessels, could be redirected or supplemented to investigate maritime UAP systematically, instead of such encounters being treated as isolated incidents to be suppressed.

## Relevance to USO Observation

NOAA runs a large network of ocean monitoring equipment, including:

- The National Data Buoy Center, which maintains sensor networks across the world's oceans
- NOAA research vessels capable of deep-sea observation
- Ocean satellite monitoring systems

Gallaudet's argument implies that these existing federal assets could help detect and study USOs if there were the political will and institutional direction to use them that way.
