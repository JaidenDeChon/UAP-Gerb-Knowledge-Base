Air Materiel Command (AMC) was the major command, headquartered at Wright Field (later [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]), Ohio, that handled research and development, systems acquisition, supply, procurement and maintenance for the Army Air Forces and the young U.S. Air Force from its establishment in 1946. In 1950-1951 its research and development functions, together with [[Edwards Air Force Base]], passed to the new [[Air Research and Development Command (ARDC)]]. AMC kept logistics and in 1961 was redesignated [[Air Force Logistics Command]], which merged with [[Air Force Systems Command]] in 1992 to form today's [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)]]. It is distinct from the modern Air Force Materiel Command despite the similar name.

## Alleged role in UFO legacy programs

UAP Gerb's *The Hidden Wing* argues that AMC is "not only a safe bet, but highly likely" to have been the Wright-Patterson unit given custody of the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]] wreckage. The argument rests on a claim, relayed by [[Tom DeLonge]], that retired AFRL commander [[Neil McCasland]] knew the Roswell material went to the Wright-Patterson laboratory. Because AFRL and AFMC did not exist in 1947, Gerb assigns that custody to AMC.

Gerb also believes AMC worked with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers to build the first [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)|deep underground facilities]] for legacy programs. In his view, these were the start of a network that lies beneath the [[Antelope Valley]], Edwards, China Lake, the [[Nevada Test and Training Range (NTR)]] and [[Dugway Proving Ground]]. As evidence he cites two declassified 1947 Operation Paperclip documents. In them AMC requested four German technicians to consult on a planned "underground plant program", among them Xavier Dorsch, head of the Nazi Organisation Todt, whose wartime work included huge underground factories. Gerb asks why AMC wanted such expertise before the Cold War gave it a reason to hide facilities from the Soviets. His answer is that the program was partly meant to house recovered craft and their occupants, and he suggests SS weapons chief Hans Kammler may also have been involved.
