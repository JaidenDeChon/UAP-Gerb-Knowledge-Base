The 5412 Committee, later called the Special Group, was the small [[National Security Council]] body that reviewed and approved covert action under President [[Dwight D. Eisenhower]]. It takes its name from NSC directive 5412, which Eisenhower approved on 15 March 1954. The directive defined covert operations, confirmed the [[CIA]] as the agency that carried them out, and required the Director of Central Intelligence to clear major programs in advance with representatives of the Secretaries of State and Defense. A follow-up directive, NSC 5412/2 of 28 December 1955, gave this coordinating role to representatives of the Secretary of State, the Secretary of Defense and the President. The group was known as the 5412 Committee and, from 1957, as the Special Group. National Security Action Memorandum 303 of 2 June 1964 renamed it the 303 Committee without changing its membership or duties.

## In Gerb's research

Gerb treats the 5412 Committee as the place where Eisenhower hid the control group of the UFO crash retrieval programs, the group he calls [[Majestic 12]]. In his timeline, a UFO steering group was first set up inside the covert-activities structure of NSC 10/2 in 1948 and was folded into the 5412 Committee's Special Group in 1954. He dates the committee's creation to 15 March 1954 and says it ran the program from 1954 to 1964, after which the 303 Committee took over until 1970. After that, in his words, the line of control is "a bit of an unknown" until 1994.

Gerb argues that Eisenhower built the committee on the model of the Manhattan Project's Top Policy Group: a small read-in group, a thin paper trail, and presidential control through a trusted intermediary. "With the stroke of his pen," he says, "Eisenhower created the impenetrable UFO control group that would ultimately usurp our system of checks and balances." He lists the committee's seats as the Deputy Under Secretary of State, the Deputy Secretary of Defense, the President's Special Assistant for National Security Affairs and the Director of Central Intelligence. He points out that Gordon Gray, named as a member in the Majestic 12 documents, held the Special Assistant's seat from 1958 to 1961. He also sets the group's size beside [[David Grusch]]'s statement that five people on the NSC were involved at one time.

From this he draws the argument he calls "the most important part" of *Special Access Required*: because crash retrieval, storage and exploitation began under a body created for covert action, the work was covert action from the start (see [[Covert Action Program (50 U.S. Code § 3093)]]). In *The Majestic-12 Documents With Ryan S. Wood*, Gerb relays the claim of [[Thomas Cantwheel]], the pseudonymous source who wrote to researcher [[Tim Cooper]], that he had dealt with the "Majestic community" and "the 5412 group". In the MITRE video Gerb describes the 5412 Committee and the 303 Committee as the NSC structure that once led the effort, later held together by [[Dick Cheney]] until 2009.
