The Galileo Project is an academic research initiative founded by Harvard University astrophysicist [[Avi Loeb]] in July 2021 to search systematically for scientific evidence of extraterrestrial technological civilizations. It is one of the most prominent mainstream academic efforts to study UAP phenomena and to look for technosignatures from non-human intelligence.

## Mission and Objectives

The Galileo Project aims to bring rigorous scientific method and peer-reviewed research standards to the study of possible extraterrestrial technology. Its main objectives include:

1. Deploying telescope and sensor networks to detect and analyze anomalous aerial phenomena systematically
2. Studying interstellar objects such as 'Oumuamua for signs of artificial origin
3. Collecting high-quality multisensor data on unidentified aerial phenomena
4. Researching technosignatures, meaning evidence of extraterrestrial technological activity

The project explicitly presents UAP research as legitimate science that deserves academic resources and attention.

## Academic Leadership

As founder and director, [[Avi Loeb]] gives UFO research considerable mainstream credibility. He is the Frank B. Baird Jr. Professor of Science at Harvard and a former chair of Harvard's Department of Astronomy. His prominence, and his willingness to discuss extraterrestrial technology in public, help reduce the stigma around academic work on UAP.

[[Kevin Knuth]], a physics professor at the [[University of Albany]] and vice president of [[UAP-X]], is a research affiliate of the Galileo Project. The role links Knuth's quantitative analysis of UAP physics, including his 2019 paper on estimating UAP flight characteristics, to the wider academic search for extraterrestrial technology.

## Methodology

The Galileo Project favors data-driven scientific investigation over speculation. Its methods are:

- Detection with purpose-built sensors and telescopes, instead of reliance on chance sightings
- Peer review, with findings published in academic journals under standard review procedures
- Open data, with a commitment to transparency in methods and findings
- Theoretical physics, applying established physics to analyze possible non-human technology

This approach sets the project apart from government UAP programs, which put national security ahead of scientific publication, and from enthusiast-driven research, which may lack rigorous methods.

## Cultural Impact

The founding of the Galileo Project at Harvard University is a sign of a significant shift in academic attitudes toward UAP research and the search for extraterrestrial intelligence. Because it treats non-human technology as a legitimate scientific problem, the project reduces the stigma around academic UAP research and attracts funding for systematic observation programs. It also encourages credentialed scientists who might otherwise avoid the topic to take part, and it gives questions about extraterrestrial technology institutional legitimacy.

The project's affiliation with Harvard gives participating researchers institutional backing, which protects them from the career damage historically associated with UFO research.

## Relationship to Other UAP Research

Other academic and private UAP research efforts run alongside the Galileo Project:

- [[Sol Foundation]], an academic UAP research organization whose presentations have included key researchers such as Knuth
- [[UAP-X]], a nonprofit field research group where Knuth is vice president
- Former government UAP researchers from AATIP and AAWSAP, such as [[Luis Elizondo]] and [[Hal Puthoff]]

Together, these efforts make up an emerging network of credentialed researchers who engage publicly with UAP phenomena using scientific methods.
