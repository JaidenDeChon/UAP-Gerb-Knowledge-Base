The U.S. National Committee on Tunneling Technology (USNC/TT) was a committee of the National Research Council, the operating arm of the US National Academies, formed in 1972 as the national focal point for tunneling technology. It published studies and workshop reports on underground construction for civil and defence purposes, and represented the United States in international tunneling bodies. Gerb treats its formation as a marker for when connective tunnels between [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)|deep underground military bases]] were likely started.

## Mandate

The committee was "the United States focal agency in the field of tunneling technology". Its job was to assess and stimulate improvements in tunneling technology and its application, and to coordinate US tunneling activities with those of other nations. By 1977 it had six major subcommittees, including ones on deep cavities and tunnel support systems. It worked with many corporations, agencies and academics, among them [[Sandia National Laboratories]] and [[Battelle Memorial Institute]].

## Defence Studies

The committee held a 1981–1982 workshop with the Army, Air Force, Navy, Department of Energy and National Science Foundation, among others. The workshop's 1982 report described a need for 400 miles of tunnels 2,500 to 3,500 feet deep and up to 16 feet in diameter, with "interconnecting passageways and additional horizontal passageways for storage, living quarters, and other needs". Those needs included housing nuclear missiles. According to the *New York Times*, the network would be "probably in the desert of the western United States". It was to be powered by fuel cells or nuclear reactors.

In 1988 the committee was publishing reports on tunneling and underground space technology that discussed underground missile systems 3,000 to 8,000 feet deep.

## In UAP Gerb's Research

In *Deep Underground Military Bases (D.U.M.Bs.) - UFO Legacy Programs*, Gerb groups the committee's formation with the [[Bechtel Tunneling Report (1974)|1974 Bechtel report]], RAND's [[Very High Speed Transit System (VHST)|VHST]] study and the [[Nuclear Subterrene|nuclear subterrene]] patents. From this cluster he argues that between 1972 and 1974 "a whole host of tunneling committees and institutes were set up to investigate massive tunneling systems", and that if connective tunnels exist, their construction likely began around 1974.
