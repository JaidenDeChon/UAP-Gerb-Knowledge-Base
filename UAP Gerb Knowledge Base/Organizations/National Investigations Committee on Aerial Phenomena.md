The National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP) was a civilian UFO research organization active primarily from 1956 to 1980. During the 1960s it was the largest and most influential UFO research organization in the United States. It investigated UFO reports independently and lobbied the federal government for greater transparency. The group stressed rigorous documentation, credible witnesses and evidence-based research, which set it apart from more sensationalist or fringe UFO groups.

## Formation and Leadership

NICAP was founded in 1956 by physicist Townsend Brown and aeronautical engineer Thomas R. Dolan, though it is best known for the tenure of retired Marine Corps Major Donald Keyhoe, who was director from 1957 to 1969. Keyhoe had already written extensively on UFOs and was a prominent advocate of the view that UFOs were extraterrestrial craft and that the US government was deliberately suppressing evidence.

## Objectives and Methods

NICAP sought to:
- Collect and analyze UFO reports from credible witnesses, particularly pilots, military personnel, and law enforcement
- Document patterns and characteristics of UFO sightings
- Lobby Congress and federal agencies for open hearings and declassification of UFO files
- Publish regular bulletins and case reports (*The UFO Investigator*)

During the 1960s NICAP's membership grew to tens of thousands, among them many scientists, engineers, military officers and pilots. It had regional subcommittees across the United States.

## Role in the Betty and Barney Hill Case

[[Barney Hill]] and [[Betty Hill]] contacted NICAP after their encounter near Lancaster, New Hampshire, on September 19, 1961. Barney Hill's report to NICAP, a foundational document in the early abduction literature, included his explicit statement that the beings he encountered aboard the craft were "somehow not human".

NICAP investigated the case and referred the Hills to Boston psychiatrist Dr. Benjamin Simon for hypnotic regression. The organization treated the Hills' account as one of the most significant and credible encounters on record.

## Decline and Dissolution

NICAP's influence waned in the 1970s after internal disputes, financial difficulties, and Keyhoe's ouster. The organization was effectively defunct by 1980.

## Legacy

NICAP's emphasis on credible witnesses and documentary rigor set a standard for civilian UFO research and influenced later organizations. Its archives have been absorbed into UFO research collections and remain an important historical resource.
