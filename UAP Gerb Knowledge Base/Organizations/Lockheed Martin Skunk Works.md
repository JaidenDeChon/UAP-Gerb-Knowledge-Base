Lockheed Martin's Advanced Development Programs (ADP) division, universally known as the **Skunk Works**, is the most secretive and storied aerospace research and development unit in the United States. Engineer [[Kelly Johnson]] founded it in 1943. Its landmark classified aircraft include the U-2 reconnaissance plane, the SR-71 Blackbird, the F-117A Nighthawk stealth aircraft and the F-22 Raptor. The Skunk Works is central to allegations that Lockheed Martin is the lead private contractor for US government UAP crash retrieval, material exploitation and reverse-engineering programs.

## Leadership and UAP Allegations

[[Kelly Johnson]] directed the Skunk Works from its founding through 1975. His successor, [[Ben Rich]], was director from 1975 to 1991. Rich allegedly made statements suggesting that the division had achieved the capability for interstellar travel and had knowledge of extraterrestrial technology. At a 1993 UCLA lecture he reportedly said: "We already have the means to travel among the stars, but these technologies are locked up in black projects."

[[Eric Schrock]], a Skunk Works official, allegedly advised author [[Tom DeLonge]] and gave him classified UAP-related information while he wrote the *Secret Machines* novel series. Gerb cites this claim as evidence of deliberate structured disclosure through fiction.

## UAP-Related Programs and Facilities

The Skunk Works is alleged to have designed and built alien reproduction vehicles at several facilities, among them:

- [[Air Force Plant 42]] in Palmdale, California. In November 1988, three disc-shaped craft were allegedly displayed there alongside conventional aircraft at a classified investor briefing witnessed by [[Brad Sorenson]].
- [[Helendale]], a Lockheed radar cross-section (RCS) measurement range in the Mojave Desert. Multiple sources allege that it is connected to UAP testing.
- The [[Advanced Technology Center (ATC)|Lockheed Martin Advanced Technology Center (ATC)]], where researchers including [[Bernard Haisch]] and [[Hal Puthoff]] published work on Zero Point Energy propulsion.

The Skunk Works compartmentalizes its work, with extreme need-to-know access controls, off-the-books funding arrangements, and separation from normal corporate chains of command. This structure is cited as the way UAP-related programs could operate inside Lockheed while staying invisible to most employees and to conventional oversight.

## The 1986 Burbank Audit

The Skunk Works began at Lockheed's plant in Burbank, California. In 1986 that plant was the subject of General Accounting Office (GAO) reports on the control of classified documents in a major Lockheed special access program. One of them, an August 1986 report to the Secretary of Defense, was titled "Need for DoD inspections of special access contracts". The GAO found that Lockheed had not been doing the required inventories at most of its 53 document control substations. Lockheed's own counts found 1,460 discrepancies among some 40,000 secret and top secret items, and some files were reported destroyed. By 1992 Lockheed had moved all aerospace work out of Burbank, and the division now works mainly from [[Air Force Plant 42]]. UAP Gerb's *Lockheed Martin - UFO Reverse Engineering, Material Exploitation, & Legacy Programs* asks whether this audit nearly exposed Lockheed's legacy programs and began the chain of events that led to the 1994 restructuring of special access program oversight described in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo|Wilson-Davis notes]].
