The Air Force Institute of Technology (AFIT) is a graduate school and research institution of the United States Air Force at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] in Ohio. It offers advanced education and research in science, engineering, technology, and management to meet the needs of the Air Force and the Department of Defense. AFIT is the Air Force's premier graduate institution and conducts both classified and unclassified research on advanced aerospace technologies, space systems, and defense applications.

## Fast Walker Research

AFIT has been involved in research on [[Fast Walkers]], objects that space-based sensors detect entering or leaving Earth's atmosphere at extreme speeds. In 2008, USAF Captain [[Bradley R. Townsend]] wrote a study at AFIT titled "Space-Based Satellite Tracking and Characterization Utilizing Non-Imaging Passive Sensors," which analyzed how [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites detect Fast Walkers.

Townsend's paper stated: "Satellite based sensors looking down at the Earth's surface occasionally observe reflected light from an object passing through the image which is moving too fast relative to the background of the image to be located within the atmosphere. These objects are commonly called Fast Walkers."

The study confirmed that DSP satellites routinely detect these objects and that ground-based sensors cannot track them: only space-based infrared systems can detect Fast Walkers.

## Wright-Patterson Connection

AFIT sits at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], a site of historical importance to UAP research. Testimony has repeatedly named Wright-Patterson as a place where recovered UAP materials are kept and as a center for analyzing anomalous technologies. Because AFIT has access to classified research facilities and educates the Air Force's technical personnel, its work lies where advanced aerospace research meets potential UAP-related analysis.

## Significance

AFIT's publication of Fast Walker research is a rare official Air Force acknowledgment, made through a military academic institution, that satellites routinely detect objects that cannot be explained as atmospheric phenomena, meteors, or known spacecraft. That the research is conducted at Wright-Patterson, a base with deep connections to UAP history, adds context to the nature of the work.
