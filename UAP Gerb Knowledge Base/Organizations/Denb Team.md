The **DENB Team** is a mysterious group, or an internal US government investigation body, named as the recipients of the [[Denb Report]]. The report is an anonymously distributed document describing the [[1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval]]. The team's identity, organizational affiliation and purpose are unknown. One hypothesis is that it was an investigation group inside the intelligence community that communicated through early internet message boards during the so-called "UAP Dark Ages" (1969–2007), the years between the closure of [[Project Blue Book]] and the creation of [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]].

## Evidence and Hypotheses

The Denb Report is addressed "to all Denb team members" from [[JS (Denb Report Author)|JS]] and dated March 23, 1992. The document is labelled "File UFO 3263," which suggests it is one of many UAP cases the DENB Team studied. The report states that the facts were "gathered from two eyewitness accounts, documentation illegally copied, and a partially destroyed document" by a now-deceased person in 1978. The DENB group received the notes and documents in February 1992.

Researchers [[Noe Torres]] and [[Ruben Uriarte]] suggest that "DENB" may be the name of a department within the US government. They also suggest that "JS" may be an acronym such as Joint Staff or Joint Services, or possibly an individual's initials. [[Leonard Stringfield]], who reviewed the Denb Report, concluded it was "authoritatively written using correct military terminology" and that it drew clear distinctions between hard evidence and speculation. In his view, both traits were inconsistent with a hoax.

## UAP Task Force Analogy

It has been revealed that the [[UAP Task Force]] used Top Secret online forums to discuss UAP cases. The DENB Team may have been a similar group of intelligence community professionals who used early internet message boards in the 1990s to discuss compartmented data on crash retrieval cases. The Denb Report's history supports this idea: it first surfaced on an electronic bulletin board in 1992 and was later mailed anonymously to UFO researchers, including [[Elaine Douglas]] and [[Nick Redfern]].

If the DENB Team was an internal investigation group working during the "dark ages", when no officially acknowledged UAP programs existed, that would explain why the report was distributed in secret, why JS stayed anonymous and why its documentation was obtained illicitly.

## The Name "Deneb"

Deneb is a highly luminous blue supergiant star in the constellation Cygnus, one of the oldest constellations recognized by early civilizations. The Milky Way's Great Rift, the junction where the Milky Way splits into two long sides, begins near Deneb in Cygnus. This area was especially significant to ancient stargazers, including shamans in southern Mexico who called the rift "Xibalba Be," the birth of all life. Whether this astronomical reference holds symbolic meaning for the DENB Team is unclear.
