**Company E, 52nd Infantry (LRP)** was the long-range patrol company of the US Army's 1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile) in the Vietnam War. It was redesignated Company E, 52nd Infantry (LRP) on 20 December 1967, fought in the Tet Offensive and at Khe Sanh, and in late 1968 moved to Camp Gorvad at Phuoc Vinh, northwest of Saigon, with an area of operations along the Cambodian border. On 1 February 1969, when the Army folded its long-range patrol units into the Rangers, it became Company H, 75th Infantry (Ranger).

## UAP Significance

UAP Gerb identified this company as the unit of the [[Infographic Show Whistleblower]], whose long-range reconnaissance patrol (LRRP) was marking targets for a B-52 raid "the following year" when it saw a craft crash (see [[1968 Vietnam UAP Crash Retrieval]]). The host linked the raid to [[Operation Menu]], which began in March 1969, and dated the crash to between October and December 1968.

The witness says five of his original twelve platoon mates were later killed in combat. Using a memorial site's list of the roughly 1,000 men who served in the unit, 45 of whom were killed, UAP Gerb narrowed the deaths between the crash and the witness's induction to 16, then to 8 killed by hostile action, of whom he judged 5 likely to be the witness's platoon mates.
