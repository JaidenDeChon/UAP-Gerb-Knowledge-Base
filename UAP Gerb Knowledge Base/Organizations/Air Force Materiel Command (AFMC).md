Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC) is a Major Command (MAJCOM) of the United States Air Force, headquartered at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio. It is responsible for research, development, test and evaluation, acquisition, logistics, and sustainment for Air Force weapons systems and equipment. With a budget portfolio of approximately $82.9 billion in FY2025, AFMC is the largest single Air Force command by budget. Its organizations include the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]], the [[Air Force Test Center]] (which includes the [[412th Test Wing|Edwards 412th Test Wing]]), the Air Force Life Cycle Management Center, the Air Force Sustainment Center, and the Air Force Installation and Mission Support Center. AFMC also oversees the network of [[Major Range and Test Facility Bases (MRTFBs)]].

## Alleged UAP Involvement

In "The Hidden Wing", Gerb identifies AFMC as the single most critical target of investigation within the Air Force for alleged UAP legacy program activity. The [[412th Test Wing|Edwards 412th Test Wing]], part of AFMC, allegedly houses derivative technology and [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicle]] test and evaluation programs. The [[Air Force Test and Evaluation (AF-TE)]] within AFMC's purview is argued to oversee reverse engineering of nonhuman technologies. Gerb traces the command's lineage through the [[Air Force Special Weapons Center (AFSWC)]], which was dissolved in 1976; its Office of Primary Responsibility passed to what became the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]]. He argues that this chain represents continuous institutional management of non-human technology exploitation programs.

## Formation and Institutional Lineage

AFMC was formally established in July 1992 through the merger of [[Air Force Logistics Command]] (AFLC) and [[Air Force Systems Command]] (AFSC). The UAP Gerb video *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* traces AFMC's full institutional lineage, which it treats as the reason the command's formation matters to alleged legacy program history. Air Materiel Command (AMC) held custody responsibilities associated with the 1947 Roswell crash in the late 1940s and early 1950s. In 1951, [[Air Research and Development Command (ARDC)]] took over research and development responsibilities from AMC, and it allegedly helped establish the [[MITRE Corporation]]. In 1961, ARDC became Air Force Systems Command, the research and development half of the eventual AFMC merger. The other half, AFLC (1961–1992), handled procurement, supply, maintenance, and transportation. The video argues that every precursor command of modern-day AFMC (AMC, ARDC, AFSC, and AFLC) was heavily involved in alleged UFO-related activity. It also notes that AMC, ARDC, AFSC, and AFMC (via its [[Air Force Test Center]] and [[Air Force Research Laboratory]]) have all maintained a major presence at Edwards Air Force Base's 412th Test Wing.

*The Hidden Wing* traces a second line of inheritance. The [[Air Force Special Weapons Center (AFSWC)]] at Kirtland, where Dr. [[Eric Henry Wang]] worked in the research directorate, came under ARDC on 1 April 1952 and later under AFSC. When it was disestablished in 1976, its office-of-primary-responsibility role passed to the Air Force Weapons Laboratory, which was later folded into AFRL. Gerb concludes that AFMC is the most deeply rooted MAJCOM in prior UFO legacy-program discussion, and that the [[Air Force Test Center]], the [[Air Force Test Pilot School]] and the [[412th Test Wing]], together with AFRL, form the operating core of his [[Hidden Wing]].

As of fiscal year 2025, AFMC's budget portfolio of approximately $82.9 billion makes it the Air Force's largest major command by funding. AFMC has six core mission centers, and the video names three of them as the most critical to modern-day alleged legacy program activity: the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]] (discovery and development, Wright-Patterson), the Air Force Test Center (test and evaluation, Edwards AFB), and the [[Air Force Life Cycle Management Center (AFLCMC)|Air Force Life Cycle Management Center]] (life-cycle management, Wright-Patterson).
