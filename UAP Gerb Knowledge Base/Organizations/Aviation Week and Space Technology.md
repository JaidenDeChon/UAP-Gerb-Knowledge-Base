Aviation Week and Space Technology (commonly abbreviated AW&ST) is a long-running trade publication covering military and civilian aviation, space, and defense technology, and one of the most respected in the aerospace industry. It has a record of carefully sourced reporting on classified aerospace programs. Some of its stories were the first public disclosures of advanced black project aircraft that were later acknowledged.

## Connection to Mark McCandlish and Brad Sorenson

[[William B. Scott]], a senior engineering editor at AW&ST, was directly involved in documenting the story of the ARV (Alien Reproduction Vehicle) disclosures. In December 1990, Scott published some of [[Mark McCandlish]]'s technical sketches relating to the Aurora craft, which drew attention to McCandlish's claims about advanced classified aircraft. Scott then contacted [[Brad Sorenson]], McCandlish's primary source for the [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|ARV Flux Liner]] description, for direct comment.

On December 3, 1990, Scott interviewed Sorenson at length. The interview stayed out of public circulation for decades and was first discussed publicly by UAP Gerb. In that interview, Sorenson confirmed the existence of the classified aerospace exhibit at [[Air Force Plant 42]], the Aurora craft, the losing competitor to the B-2 bomber, and the three ARV Flux Liners. Scott chose not to publish the parts of the interview about the ARV. He later indicated that he had difficulty believing claims about flying saucers capable of faster-than-light travel derived from recovered non-human technology.
