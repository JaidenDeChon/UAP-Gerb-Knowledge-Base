The **UFO Working Group** is an alleged small, secret interagency body of US intelligence and military officials said to have been set up in February 1987 to study UFO reports, chaired by Colonel Harold E. Phillips of the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]]. Its existence was first publicly reported by journalist Howard Blum in his 1990 book *Out There*. Retired Army Lieutenant Colonel [[Philip J. Corso]] described the same group in his manuscript *Dawn of a New Age*, drawing on information he said he gathered long after leaving the Army, and wrote that it was still active when he finished the manuscript in 1993.

## Composition

According to Corso, as presented in UAP Gerb's video on him, the group originally had 17 members:

- one Army general and three Air Force generals
- scientists from the Defense Intelligence Agency
- an Army colonel
- three officials from the [[NSA]]
- a supervisor from the [[CIA]]'s Domestic Collection Division
- a technical team from the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]]

Corso also claimed the group received funding through the US Army's Intelligence and Security Command ([[Army INSCOM]]), founded by Major General [[Albert Stubblebine]].

## Significance in UAP research

UAP Gerb singles out the presence of the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology, which several researchers, among them [[Eric Davis]] and journalist [[Christopher Sharp]], describe as the place where the government's "UFO portfolio" is held. The host links the group to later developments he attributes to the same institutions: former DS&T deputy director [[Doug Wolfe]]'s role in creating the [[CIA Office of Global Access]] in 2003, which Sharp has accused of acting as a logistics coordinator for crash retrievals with [[JSOC (Joint Special Operations Command)|JSOC]], and INSCOM's Intelligence Support Activity passing to JSOC the same year. He likens the working group's cross-agency make-up to the structure attributed to [[Majestic 12]].

The host also notes what the list leaves out. Although he considers the Navy deeply involved in UFO legacy programs, Corso's account of the group names no Navy members.
