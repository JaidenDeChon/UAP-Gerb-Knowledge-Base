The Condon Committee (officially the "UFO Study" or "Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects") was a government-funded UFO research project at the University of Colorado Boulder, run from 1966 to 1968 by physicist Dr. Edward U. Condon. The US Air Force commissioned the project and funded it through the Air Force Office of Scientific Research. Its task was an objective scientific evaluation of UFO reports. The committee's final report, known as the Condon Report, concluded that further UFO study was unlikely to advance scientific knowledge and recommended that the Air Force end its UFO investigation programs.

## Formation and Mandate

The US Air Force asked for the committee after mounting public criticism of [[Project Blue Book]], the Air Force's in-house UFO investigation program. The committee received $500,000 in funding and access to Blue Book case files. Its formal mandate was to assess whether UFOs posed a national security threat and whether continued study had scientific value.

## Findings

The Condon Report analyzed over 90 UFO cases. Its summary concluded that UFOs were not a scientific problem worth further investigation, but the case-by-case analyses in the full report acknowledged that some cases remained unexplained. Astronomer [[William H. Hartman]], for example, analyzed the [[McMinnville UFO Photographs]] and concluded that they were consistent with a genuine anomalous flying craft. The report's final conclusion still recommended ending further official study.

## Criticism and Controversy

The Condon Committee has been widely criticized for bias and predetermined conclusions. Internal memos leaked to Look magazine in 1968 showed project coordinator Robert Low stating before the study began that the project's goal was to reassure the public that UFOs posed no threat, regardless of the evidence. Critics argue that the summary conclusions contradicted the detailed case studies, several of which acknowledged genuinely unexplained phenomena.

In UAP research, the committee is cited alongside the [[Robertson Panel]] as part of a wider institutional framework for suppressing serious scientific engagement with UFOs. Gerb describes the committee as "allegedly directed to explain UFOs away as swamp gas and weather balloons" rather than conducting objective research.

## Legacy

After the Condon Report was published, the US Air Force terminated [[Project Blue Book]] in 1969, which effectively ended official government UFO investigation for decades. The report's findings added to the stigma around UFO research in mainstream science.
