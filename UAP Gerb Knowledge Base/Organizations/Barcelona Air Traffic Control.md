During the [[Manises UFO Incident]] of November 11, 1979, Barcelona Air Traffic Control confirmed to [[TAE (Trabajos Aéreos y Enlaces)|TAE]] flight JK 297 that no other aircraft were operating on or near the airliner's flight path. At the time, the crew of the commercial flight was watching two powerful red lights over the Mediterranean Sea.

## Role in the Manises Incident

At approximately 23:05 hours on November 11, 1979, Captain [[Francisco Javier Lerdo de Tejada]] radioed Barcelona Air Traffic Control after flight mechanic [[Francisco Javier Rodriguez]] alerted the crew to two unidentified red lights approaching their Super Caravelle aircraft at 23,000 feet. The controllers' confirmation that no conventional aircraft were nearby was a major reason Captain Lerdo de Tejada abandoned his flight path and made an emergency landing at [[Manises Airport, Valencia, Spain|Manises Airport]]. It was the first recorded case of a commercial flight being grounded because of a UFO encounter.

Their answer ruled out ordinary air traffic as an explanation for the lights. It also supported the crew's assessment that they were seeing genuinely anomalous objects that posed a safety risk to the 109 passengers aboard.
