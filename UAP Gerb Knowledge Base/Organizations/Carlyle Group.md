The Carlyle Group is a major American private equity and asset management corporation, founded in Washington, D.C. in 1987 by David Rubenstein. It manages over $400 billion in assets and has historically invested heavily in defense, national security and aerospace. UAP researchers have identified the Carlyle Group as a likely node in the private infrastructure around UAP legacy programs. They point to the senior figures among its members, its purchase of companies that feature heavily in UAP lore, and the ties between its leaders and the classified aerospace world.

## Senior Membership and Advisers

Many former senior statesmen have served the Carlyle Group as board members and advisers, among them:
- [[Frank Carlucci]], the 16th US Secretary of Defense, who was Carlyle's chairman from 1992 to 2003
- George H.W. Bush, former US President
- James Baker, former US Secretary of State
- John Major, former UK Prime Minister

UAP researchers see a private investment firm with large aerospace holdings and this many senior defense and intelligence officials as fitting the structure they would expect of privatized management of classified legacy programs.

## EG&G Ownership (1999–2001)

From 1999 to 2001, the Carlyle Group owned [[EG&G]], a defense contractor that comes up repeatedly in UAP lore. EG&G is the alleged operator of the mysterious airline that flies workers to classified test sites. It employed physicist [[Bob Lazar]] at the S-4 site near Area 51 where he claims to have worked. The alleged meeting between Admiral Thomas Wilson and Eric Davis, recorded in the Wilson-Davis memo, took place in an EG&G parking lot. Owning EG&G in those years put the Carlyle Group directly next to several of these claimed links to UAP programs.

## Other Defense Investments

Carlyle also bought the electronics division of [[General Dynamics]] and invested in [[Northrop Grumman]]. UAP researchers have directly accused Northrop Grumman of building classified anti-gravity craft, including the [[TR-3B]] Black Manta.

## UAP Program Connections

UAP Gerb considers the Carlyle Group "highly suspect" of deep involvement in UAP programs. [[Ronald S. Moultrie]] was Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security, overseeing [[AARO]] and its director [[Sean Kirkpatrick]], and he played a central role in publicly denying UAP at the 2022 Congressional hearings. He sat on the board of iCapital, a group Carlyle has invested in, and on the board of the Better Angels Society, which is funded by Carlyle founder David Rubenstein.
