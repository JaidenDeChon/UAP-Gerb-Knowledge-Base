The Air Force Office of Special Investigations (AFOSI) is the U.S. Air Force's primary federal law enforcement and counterintelligence agency, responsible for criminal investigations, counterintelligence, and counterespionage across Air Force and Space Force personnel, programs, and facilities. Within AFOSI, the Office of Special Projects — internally designated AFOSI/PJ — provides dedicated security and counterintelligence support for the Air Force's most sensitive Special Access Programs. In UAP research, AFOSI/PJ is repeatedly identified as a program-protection and counterintelligence element embedded within the alleged UFO legacy program apparatus, both as a security enforcer against whistleblowers and as a formal counterintelligence partner to outside agencies.

## Office of Special Projects (PJ)

AFOSI/PJ was historically headed by [[Terry Phillips]], a nearly three-decade veteran of Air Force and AFOSI service who reached the Senior Executive Service in 2020 who also directed the Air Force's department-wide Special Access Program security enterprise before retiring from federal service around January 2022 and being succeeded by [[Lee M. Russ]]. AFOSI/PJ maintains permanent offices both in [[Crystal City, Virginia]] and at [[Hanscom Air Force Base, Massachusetts|Hanscom Air Force Base]] — the base where [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] opened its first program office.

## Role in Alleged UFO Legacy Programs

UAP Gerb alleges that AFOSI/PJ has historically provided counterintelligence support to MITRE's alleged UFO technology reverse-engineering and USO-monitoring programs, working alongside the [[Office of Naval Intelligence]] and [[Naval Criminal Investigative Service]] on the Navy side of that alleged effort. Separately, UAP Gerb's *Special Access Required Vol.2* alleges that Phillips, prior to his 2021 retirement, acted as "an enforcer for the UFO activity administrative structure, leveraging administrative terrorism and much worse against prospective whistleblowers," and that he continued similar work in a private security role at [[Northrop Grumman]] following his federal retirement.

## In *The Hidden Wing*

UAP Gerb's *The Hidden Wing* devotes a full chapter to AFOSI, which it describes as a federal law enforcement agency of more than 2,600 active-duty, reserve and civilian personnel, about 2,000 of them credentialed special agents, with more than 230 acknowledged sites worldwide. The host notes that Air Force Chief of Staff [[Hoyt Vandenberg]], an alleged original member of [[Majestic 12]], signed General Order No. 29 on 15 July 1948, which empowered OSI to conduct counterintelligence. That was the same month as the classified executive orders that former New Mexico state representative [[Andrew Kissner]] said created the first legacy programs. Of AFOSI's eight field investigation regions, seven align with major commands. The eighth, Region 7, provides counterintelligence and security program management for special access programs under the Office of the Secretary of the Air Force.

The host is clear that he does not accuse AFOSI as a whole. He believes a specialized office, the Office of Special Projects (PJ), protects Air Force UFO SAPs and unacknowledged SAPs. By its own description, PJ's most critical mission is preserving the most classified and sensitive technologies, and its agents are "trained and equipped to secure, recover, and protect" them. It performs program security, inspection, cybersecurity, investigative and counterintelligence work for all Air and Space Force SAPs, and has protected programs such as Tacit Blue, the B-2, the B-21 and the F-117A. The video lists PJ's acknowledged permanent locations as the Pentagon, [[Crystal City, Virginia]], Hanscom AFB, Palmdale and El Segundo in California, [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], Las Vegas, Hill AFB, [[Kirtland Air Force Base, New Mexico|Kirtland AFB]] and [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Dahlgren|Naval Support Activity Dahlgren]], and ties each to earlier investigations. It quotes a former SAP security specialist: "The answers to everything you are looking for lie in Crystal City." The host also recounts the 1979 [[Guardian Angel Program]] with the FBI. He discusses former AFOSI agent [[Richard Doty]] but sets Doty's testimony aside as likely disinformation.
