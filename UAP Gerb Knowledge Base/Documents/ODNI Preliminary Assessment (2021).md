*Preliminary Assessment: Unidentified Aerial Phenomena* is a nine-page unclassified report released on June 25, 2021, by the [[Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI)|Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] to the congressional intelligence and armed services committees. Prepared with the Navy-led [[UAP Task Force]], it covered 144 reports of unidentified aerial phenomena made by U.S. government sources between 2004 and 2021, and it was the first such assessment the intelligence community delivered to Congress in public.

## What it says

- Of the 144 reports, the task force could explain only one with high confidence: a large, deflating balloon. For the rest, the report says the dataset lacked enough information to attribute the incidents to specific explanations.
- In 18 incidents, described in 21 reports, observers reported unusual movement patterns or flight characteristics.
- It proposes five categories of possible explanation: airborne clutter, natural atmospheric phenomena, U.S. government or industry developmental programs, foreign adversary systems, and an "other" bin for whatever fits none of them.
- It says UAP "clearly pose a safety of flight issue" and may pose a challenge to U.S. national security, and it calls for standardized reporting and more data collection, including machine learning to pre-assess reports.
