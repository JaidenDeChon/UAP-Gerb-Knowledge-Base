OPNAV 3820 is the Office of the Chief of Naval Operations' series of instructions on reporting UFOs. According to A.F. Rullán's study of Blue Book's ship reports, the Navy first issued it on 25 September 1952, ordering naval installations to report sightings to ATIC and/or the Pentagon and Air Defense Command. Gerb describes a version issued on January 23, 1954 that required US Navy personnel to report UAP sightings, which it called "flying object reports", to the directors of the Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC), USAF Intelligence and Naval Intelligence, and says it directly cites [[JANAP 146c]]. That citation cannot have been to the "C" edition as dated, since JANAP 146(C) was issued on 10 March 1954, seven weeks later; a January 1954 instruction would have fallen under JANAP 146(B) of September 1951. With these instructions, the US Navy formally took part in the centralized, classified management of military UAP data.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions stated as fact that OPNAV 3820 was issued on 23 January 1954 and cited JANAP 146c. Both details are Gerb's, from the copy in Keyhoe's book; JANAP 146(C) is dated 10 March 1954 (see the transcription), so the two do not fit, and Rullán dates the first OPNAV 3820 instruction to 25 September 1952.

## Content and Requirements

The directive required every UAP sighting by Navy personnel or platforms to be sent to three intelligence recipients at once: the director of ATIC (the Air Force's main technical intelligence body), the director of USAF Intelligence, and the Director of Naval Intelligence. Naval UAP data therefore reached Air Force intelligence immediately. ATIC also ran Project Blue Book; researchers argue that routing Navy reports straight to the intelligence directors kept credible sightings out of any publicly accountable investigation.

> [!edit] Corrected 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the reports went to ATIC "rather than to Project Blue Book". Blue Book was an ATIC project, so the contrast doesn't hold; the claim about accountability is the researchers'.

The directive used the term "flying object reports" instead of "UFO" or a similar name. This added to the mix of terms that has historically made FOIA searches for military UAP records harder.

## Documentation

According to Gerb, a copy of OPNAV 3820 appears in Donald Keyhoe's book *The Flying Saucer Conspiracy* and cites JANAP 146c directly. Rullán, searching Blue Book's ship files, found references to OPNAV INST 3820.9 and to fleet-level instructions (CINCPACFLT INST 3820.3 and CINCLANTFLT INST 03360.2C), but reports that no copies of the OPNAV instructions turned up despite several FOIA requests. A Potomac River Naval Command instruction of 23 July 1954, PRNC 3820.1, was based on an OPNAV Notice 3820. These records establish that the Navy took part in centralized UAP data management as a matter of formal institutional policy.

> [!edit] Clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said Keyhoe's copy documents the directive's "explicit citation of JANAP 146c". That is Gerb's description, and a January 1954 instruction predates JANAP 146(C) of 10 March 1954; Rullán's study adds what Blue Book's files show of the 3820 series.

## Regulatory Context

OPNAV 3820 was one of several regulations that shaped military UAP reporting from 1953 to 1969 and beyond:

- [[USAF Regulation 200-2]] (1953): made ATIC, which ran Project Blue Book, responsible for analysing Air Force UFO reports.
- [[JANAP 146c]] (1954): prohibited public discussion of officially reported sightings, under criminal penalties.
- OPNAV 3820 (first issued 25 September 1952; Gerb cites a January 1954 version): set Navy reporting requirements for UFO sightings.

> [!edit] Corrected 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions said AFR 200-2 sent reports to ATIC "rather than Blue Book"; Blue Book was ATIC's project (AFR 200-2, 26 August 1953, paragraph 4a).

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said OPNAV 3820 (January 1954) set Navy requirements under the authority of JANAP 146c, which was not issued until 10 March 1954. Rullán dates the first OPNAV 3820 instruction to 25 September 1952.

Together, these regulations created a closed system that sent every credible military UAP report, including maritime [[Unidentified Submerged Object (USO)|USO]] sightings, into classified channels managed by ATIC. The system had no mechanism for public disclosure.
