*Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico* (GAO/NSIAD-95-187, file B-262046) is a 20-page report that the General Accounting Office's National Security and International Affairs Division sent to Representative [[Steven Schiff|Steven H. Schiff]] of New Mexico on July 28, 1995. It is signed by Richard Davis, Director, National Security Analysis, and names Gary K. Weeter, Assistant Director, as its major contributor. Schiff had asked GAO, out of concern that the Department of Defense had not given him all it had, to find out what the rules were in 1947 for reporting an air accident like the one near Roswell and to identify any government records of the [[Roswell Crash]]. GAO did the work from March 1994 to June 1995.

## What it says

- **Air accident reports.** Army regulations in 1947 required air accident reports to be kept permanently. GAO found four air accidents reported by the Army Air Forces in New Mexico in July 1947, at or near Hobbs, Albuquerque, Carrizozo and Alamogordo. All involved military fighter or cargo aircraft, and all came after July 8, 1947, the day the [[Roswell Army Airfield|Roswell Army Air Field]] (RAAF) public information office announced the recovery of a "flying disc". The Navy reported none. Air Force officials told GAO there was no requirement at the time to report the crash of a weather balloon.
- **Destroyed records.** Some RAAF records survived and others did not. RAAF administrative records from March 1945 through December 1949 and RAAF outgoing messages from October 1946 through December 1949 had been destroyed. The disposition form doesn't say what organization or person destroyed them, when, or under what authority. The National Personnel Records Center's chief archivist told GAO the form did not properly cite the authority for the disposal, and that in his experience many Air Force records of the period were destroyed without a citation of the governing disposition authority; GAO's own review of other records-control forms, including those for the RAAF's outgoing messages of 1950, supported him.
- **Two 1947 records.** The search turned up two records from 1947. The combined July 1947 history of the 509th Bomb Group and RAAF says the public information office "was kept quite busy . . . answering inquiries on the 'flying disc,'" and that "the object turned out to be a radar tracking balloon." An FBI teletype from the Dallas office, dated July 8, 1947, reports that an Eighth Air Force official had described a hexagonal disc hanging from a large balloon by cable, which resembled a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector and was being sent to [[Wright Field]] for examination. GAO found no regulation for handling such debris, and no [[Air Materiel Command]] record from 1947 to 1950 mentioning the crash or any examination of its debris.
- **Agency replies.** The National Security Council, the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy and the [[Department of Energy]] said they had no records on the crash. The FBI pointed to the material it had already released under FOIA. The Defense Department said the Air Force's July 1994 *Report of Air Force Research Regarding the Roswell Incident*, which named a project MOGUL balloon train as the most likely source of the wreckage, was the extent of its records. The CIA searched all its databases for "Roswell, New Mexico" and found nothing related to the crash; its letter in appendix VIII says it searched for "Project Mogul" too.
- **What else was searched.** None of the other records GAO reviewed mentioned the crash, including classified ones and [[Project Blue Book Special Report No. 14]]. Table 1 lists the archives and offices visited, from the National Archives and the National Personnel Records Center to the Air Force Safety Agency at Kirtland and the CIA's copy of the [[Robertson Panel Report]]. GAO added that missing records, often without explanation, and unclear or changing records rules had complicated the search.

The appendices reproduce the July 1947 history excerpts, the FBI teletype and the letters from the six agencies.

## In Gerb's videos

Gerb tells the story twice in nearly the same words, in [[Videos/Sandia National Laboratories - UFO Reverse Engineering, Material Exploitation, & Legacy Programs/summary|his Sandia National Laboratories video]] and in [[Videos/80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering - A Timeline/summary|his 80-year timeline]]. In his account, former New Mexico state representative [[Andrew Kissner]] shared his findings with Schiff, who "would immediately initiate a General Accounting Office probe" to "determine if the US Air Force or any other US government agency possessed information on the alleged crash and recovery of an extraterrestrial vehicle and its alien occupants near Roswell, New Mexico in July 1947." He says Schiff released the report on 28 July 1995, and quotes Schiff's own statement on it: "The GAO report states the outgoing messages from Roswell Army Airfield for this period of time were destroyed without proper authority," and "It is my understanding that these outgoing messages were permanent records, which never should have been destroyed. The GAO could not identify who destroyed the messages or why." Gerb adds that the GAO estimated the messages were destroyed more than 40 years earlier.

The report itself is more guarded. It says the disposition form didn't record who destroyed the outgoing messages, when, or under what authority, and it gives no date for the destruction; "without proper authority", the 40-year estimate and the point about permanent records come from Schiff's statement. In the timeline Gerb also places Kissner's prompting in 1995 and calls Schiff a "New Mexico State Representative". Schiff was a member of the US House, and GAO's review had begun in March 1994.

Gerb links the destroyed messages to [[David Grusch]]'s account of a 1994 "shred party" held to keep records from President Clinton. He says the inquiry led to the Air Force's 1997 *The Roswell Report: Case Closed*, which he describes as a poor attempt to explain Roswell away as a Project Mogul balloon, and he notes that its sponsor was SAF/AAZ, the Air Force's component-level special access program central office. The Air Force report the GAO cites is the earlier one, of July 1994; *Case Closed* followed it in 1997. In [[Videos/The Manhattan Project 2.0 - the Secrecy of UFO Crash Retrieval Programs Vol.1/summary|The Manhattan Project 2.0]] and [[Videos/The Hidden Wing - US Air Force UFO Reverse Engineering Programs/summary|The Hidden Wing]] he mentions Schiff's inquiry only in passing, as the outcome of Kissner's interviews.
