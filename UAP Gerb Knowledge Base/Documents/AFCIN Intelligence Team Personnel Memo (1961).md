"(U) AFCIN Intelligence Team Personnel" is a US Air Force intelligence staff paper dated 3 November 1961. It was written in the office AFCIN-1E-O at Headquarters USAF in Washington, under the Assistant Chief of Staff, Intelligence (AFCIN), with replies to the attention of Colonel Betz, and it is signed by Lieutenant Colonel Norman M. Rosner. UFO researchers often call it the "Betz memo". Its subject is staffing: how to keep enough qualified people in AFCIN's field intelligence teams, which belonged to the [[USAF 1127th Field Activities Group]]. On the way, it defines three peacetime projects the teams supported: UFO investigation, [[Project Moon Dust]] and [[Operation Bluefly|Operation Blue Fly]]. That makes it the best-known early official record of Moon Dust and Blue Fly. Gerb reads it as the "AFC 1e draft policy".

## What it says

The paper is laid out as a staff study, under the headings Problem, Factors Bearing on the Problem, Criteria, Definitions and Discussion. It is addressed to AFCIN-1E and AFCIN-1, "in turn". Parts of the released copy are blacked out, among them the security markings of several paragraph numbers and the end of paragraph 6f.

- **The problem** (paragraph 1) is "to provide qualified personnel for AFCIN intelligence teams".
- **Factors** (paragraph 2). Besides their staff duties, team members "have peacetime duty functions in support of such Air Force projects as Moondust, Bluefly, and UFO, and other AFCIN directed quick reaction projects which require intelligence team operational capabilities". Normal attrition had already brought the number of qualified team members below the minimum, and losses expected over the next ten months would halve the current manning. Personnel actions within the authority of AFFMP, AFCIN and AFCIN-1E could reverse the decline.
- **Definitions** (paragraph 5). The teams' three roles were the linguist (interrogation, and translation from Russian and Bloc languages), the tech man (field examination and analysis of foreign materiel, with emphasis on the Markings Program and technical photography) and the ops man (team chief). Airborne personnel were military-trained, rated parachutists. The paper then defines the three projects:
  - *Unidentified Flying Objects (UFO)*: "Headquarters USAF has established a program for investigation of reliably reported unidentified flying objects within the United States. AFR 200-2 delineates 1127th collection responsibilities." (See [[USAF Regulation 200-2]].)
  - *Blue Fly*: "Operation Blue Fly has been established to facilitate expeditious delivery to FTD of Moon Dust or other items of great technical intelligence interest." An AFCIN standing operating procedure for Blue Fly operations, dated February 1960, provided for the 1127th's participation.
  - *Moon Dust*: "As a specialized aspect of its over-all materiel exploitation program, Headquarters USAF has established Project Moon Dust to locate, recover and deliver descended foreign space vehicles." Collection responsibilities were set out in ICGL #4 of 25 April 1961.
- **Discussion** (paragraph 6). AFCIN kept its intelligence teams as a function of AFCIN-1E (the 1127th). Each team had three men, a linguist, a tech man and an ops man, all airborne-qualified. Moon Dust and Blue Fly are described as projects of the [[Air Force Foreign Technology Division]] (FTD) of Air Force Systems Command. All three peacetime projects, the paper says, "involve a potential for employment of qualified field intelligence personnel on a quick reaction basis to recover or perform field exploitation of unidentified flying objects, or known Soviet/Bloc aerospace vehicles, weapons systems, and/or residual components of such equipment". The teams were "the only such collection capability available to AFCIN". The paper traces the capability back to the 4602d Air Intelligence Service Squadron, set up by Air Defense Command in 1953 to exploit downed enemy "people, paper, and hardware". The 4602d's spaces passed to AFCIN in July 1957 and to the 1006th AISS, which was redesignated the 1127th USAF Field Activities Group (AFCIN Policy Letter 205-13, 12 April 1960).

So the paper describes Moon Dust, Blue Fly and the UFO program as already in place in November 1961. It does not set any of them up, and it cites earlier papers for each: the Blue Fly procedure of February 1960, ICGL #4 of April 1961 for Moon Dust, and AFR 200-2 for UFOs.

## Draft status and the two copies

On the copy Gerb shows, someone has written across the first page: "This draft proposal was not approved and was not forwarded for action", followed by initials. The same copy has other handwritten marks: arrows, ticks, and "No" written beside the Blue Fly and Moon Dust definitions.

Two versions of the paper circulate among UFO researchers, according to [[Kevin Randle]]. The copy found by researcher Robert Todd, who got it from the Air Force, carries the note. A copy that Clifford Stone said he had from military sources does not. Stone argued that the note covers only the paper's "Action Recommended" section, and that the background it gives describes arrangements that already existed. [[Barry J. Greenwood]] thought the two were otherwise identical and came from one source, and that someone in the UFO community had removed the note before passing the paper on. Either way, the note calls the paper a draft proposal that went no further. It is not evidence that the projects it describes were never real.

## Source

No scan of the paper from an official source is known online. Searches of the National Archives catalog, the Air Force FOIA reading room and The Black Vault's Moon Dust pages turned up no copy. A retyped text has circulated since at least October 1986, when the Computer UFO Network (CUFON) posted it in its FOIA document files, with the draft note included. The copy Gerb shows is a scan marked "Copy enhanced for readability, B. Greenwood 2016". Neither is the Air Force's own release, so the page links no source and hosts no file. If the Air Force or NARA copy turns up, it can be hosted, since it is a federal work.

## In Gerb's videos

In [[Videos/MOON DUST - The Pentagon's Secret UFO Programs/summary|MOON DUST - The Pentagon's Secret UFO Programs]], Gerb calls the paper the first mention of Moon Dust and reads its title as "AFC 1e draft policy". He shows the letterhead with the draft note, the "Moondust, Bluefly, and UFO" passage in paragraph 2 and the definitions in paragraph 5. He says it proposes that the 1127th "receive collection responsibility duties" for the Air Force's established program for investigating reliably reported UFOs in the United States. It also lists one project that had historically held that duty, he says: Moon Dust, with authority to "locate, recover and deliver descended foreign space vehicles". In the paper itself, the UFO definition says AFR 200-2 already sets out the 1127th's collection responsibilities, and Moon Dust is one of three projects the teams already supported. The paper's own proposal concerns staffing the teams, and Gerb does not mention the draft note he shows. The paper is also not the earliest Moon Dust record: it cites ICGL #4 of 25 April 1961.

In [[Videos/US Special Forces Confession - I Recovered Crashed UFOs - Fact or Fiction/summary|US Special Forces Confession - I Recovered Crashed UFOs - Fact or Fiction]], Gerb recommends the "AFC 1e draft policy" again. He says it shows that Project UFO and Moon Dust, both run by the Air Force, held collection duties for UAP and descended foreign space vehicles.

In [[Videos/The Majestic-12 Documents With Ryan S. Wood/summary|The Majestic-12 Documents With Ryan S. Wood]], Gerb describes Moon Dust as "an official group proposed in 1961 by the US Air Force alongside projects bluefly and UFO". He quotes the paper's definitions of Moon Dust ("locate, recover and deliver descended foreign space vehicles") and Blue Fly ("facilitate expeditious delivery to foreign technology division of moondust or other items of great technological intelligence interest"). The paper's word is "technical", not "technological".
