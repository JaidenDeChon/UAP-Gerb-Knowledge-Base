The Twining Memo is a three-page SECRET letter of 23 September 1947 from Lieutenant General [[Nathan Twining]], commanding the [[Air Materiel Command]] (AMC) at [[Wright Field]], Dayton, Ohio. Its subject line is "AMC Opinion Concerning 'Flying Discs'". It is addressed to the Commanding General, Army Air Forces, for the attention of Brigadier General George Schulgen of AC/AS-2, the air intelligence office that had asked for AMC's view. Its best-known line is the opinion that "the phenomenon reported is something real and not visionary or fictitious." It is one of the earliest documents in which a senior US military command told headquarters that the flying discs reported that summer were real objects. Its recommendation of a classified, code-named study led to [[Project Sign]].

## What it says

The letter begins by saying that its opinion rests on interrogation report data supplied by AC/AS-2 and on preliminary studies by AMC's intelligence division (T-2) and the Aircraft Laboratory of its Engineering Division (T-3). The opinion itself was reached at a conference of staff from the Air Institute of Technology, T-2, the office of the Chief of the Engineering Division, and T-3's aircraft, power plant and propeller laboratories.

Its opinion, in paragraph 2, is that:

- the phenomenon is "something real and not visionary or fictitious";
- there are objects "probably approximating the shape of a disc", apparently as large as man-made aircraft;
- some incidents may be natural phenomena such as meteors;
- the reported extreme rates of climb, maneuverability "particularly in roll", and action that "must be considered evasive" when the objects are sighted or contacted by friendly aircraft and radar "lend belief to the possibility" that some are controlled "either manually, automatically or remotely".

It gives the objects' apparent common description in six points: a metallic or light-reflecting surface; no trail, except in a few instances when an object seemed to be operating at high performance; circular or elliptical, flat on the bottom and domed on top; several reports of well-kept formations of three to nine objects; normally no sound, except for a "substantial rumbling roar" in three instances; and level-flight speeds estimated at normally above 300 knots.

The letter says the United States could, with extensive development, build a piloted aircraft of that general description with a range of about 7,000 miles at subsonic speeds, but that doing so would be very expensive and should be set up apart from existing projects. It asks that three possibilities be kept in mind: that the objects are of domestic origin, the product of a high-security project unknown to AC/AS-2 or AMC; that there is no physical evidence, "in the shape of crash recovered exhibits", that would undeniably prove they exist; and that a foreign nation has a form of propulsion, "possibly nuclear", beyond US knowledge.

It recommends that Army Air Forces headquarters issue a directive assigning a priority, a security classification and a code name for a detailed study, with complete sets of the data shared with the Army, the Navy, the [[Atomic Energy Commission]], the JRDB (the Joint Research and Development Board), the Air Force Scientific Advisory Group, NACA, and the [[RAND Corporation|RAND]] and NEPA projects. A preliminary report was to follow within 15 days and a detailed report every 30 days after that. Until such a directive came, AMC would continue investigating within its existing resources.

Its remark on the lack of "crash recovered exhibits" is often quoted in arguments over the [[Roswell Crash]], two months earlier.

## What followed

The study the letter asked for began four months later. According to the Project Sign technical report of February 1949, the project was set up by AMC's Technical Intelligence Division on 22 January 1948 as Project XS-304, under the authority of a 30 December 1947 letter on "Flying Disks" from the Deputy Chief of Staff, Materiel, USAF. That report reproduces a copy of the Twining letter among its attachments. The project was code-named "Sign"; it is often said to have been called Project Saucer first.

## How Gerb uses it

In [[Videos/The Origin of the UFO Stigma/summary|The Origin of the UFO Stigma]], Gerb begins the government's paper trail with Project Sign and shows the letter on screen. He says Twining established Sign and that it was first named Project Saucer "due to" Twining's assertion that flying discs are "real and not visionary or fictitious". In [[Videos/Global Air Force UFO Encounters You've Probably Never Heard of/summary|Global Air Force UFO Encounters You've Probably Never Heard of]], he gives the same origin for Sign before turning to the [[Gorman Dogfight]]. In both videos he adds that all Sign reports had to go to the [[Army and Navy Research and Development Board]], the [[USAF Scientific Advisory Group]] and the Atomic Energy Commission, the link to the AEC that he follows through later programs.

Gerb's account differs from the letter in a few details:

- **The addressee.** Gerb calls the recipient the Air Force (or AAF) commanding general, "George Shugen" or "George Lugan" in the captions. The letter is addressed to the Commanding General, Army Air Forces, for the attention of Brigadier General George Schulgen of AC/AS-2. Schulgen was an intelligence officer, not the commanding general.
- **Who set up Sign.** The letter recommends a study but sets nothing up. Sign was established in January 1948 under an Air Force headquarters directive, by which time Twining had left AMC to command the Alaskan Air Command.
- **The distribution list.** The list Gerb gives matches the letter's paragraph 3a, which recommends sharing the data with the AEC, the JRDB, the Air Force Scientific Advisory Group and others. It is a recommendation in the letter of September 1947, not a rule for Sign's later reports.

The [[MJ-12 Documents|Majestic-12 documents]] include a different paper with Twining's name on it, the Cutler–Twining memo of 14 July 1954, which Gerb mentions in [[Videos/The Majestic-12 Documents With Ryan S. Wood/summary|The Majestic-12 Documents With Ryan S. Wood]]. That memo is disputed; the 1947 letter is not.

## Copies

The copy hosted here is NICAP's three-page scan of the signed original, a National Archives reproduction stamped "COPY from THE NATIONAL ARCHIVES", from Record Group 18 (Records of the Army Air Forces), file AAG 000 General "C". It carries a National Archives declassification number (NND 76016[8?]). NICAP is an aggregator, not the issuer. The scan has been rearranged, so that its second page begins with the foot of the letter's first page.

The National Archives' own online copy is a carbon file copy marked "COPY", attached to the Project Sign technical report in the Project Blue Book Administrative Files (Record Group 341, microfilm T1206, roll 85). It is [catalog item 461456722](https://catalog.archives.gov/id/461456722), images 67 to 69. It is a faded microfilm image, published only as single-page images. The letter's text was also printed in the [[Condon Report]] as its Appendix R.
