The FBI's file on [[Silas Newton]] is the Bureau's release, on its online reading room the FBI Vault, of records it kept on the Denver oilman who was the source of [[Frank Scully]]'s 1950 book *Behind the Flying Saucers* and of the story of the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|1948 Aztec crash]]. The Vault release runs to six parts and 77 pages, from 1940 to 1971. Most of it has nothing to do with saucers: it covers a wartime espionage inquiry that touched Newton, his arrest record and a 1970–71 confidence-game investigation in New Mexico. A few pages from 1950 and 1951 deal with the Aztec story and with Scully's book, and those are the ones Gerb uses in his video on Aztec.

The site hosts Part 2 (seven pages), the April–May 1951 correspondence with the California Attorney General's office, which is the part Gerb discusses at most length. It has a [[FBI File on Silas Newton (Transcription)|transcription]]. The other five parts are on the FBI Vault only.

Every part opens with an FBI FOIA cover sheet, and several end with a deleted-page sheet listing pages withheld in full under exemption (b)(7)(C) and others.

## The 1950 and 1951 documents

**The New Orleans memo, 31 March 1950.** A New Orleans special agent's brother, at an advertising agency, had been contacted about flying discs by a man in Colorado. That man was said to have been told in January 1950 by someone (the name is redacted) that he knew "a prominent Denver oilman named Silas Newton, also known as a mysterious Mr. X", an official of the Newton Oil Co. Newton claimed he had leased land in the Mojave Desert in California and that a flying disc had been found intact on it, with eighteen three-foot-tall human-like occupants, all dead but not burned, the disc being of very hard metal and nearly indestructible. The informant was said to have shown a radio set as a souvenir of the disc, to have been telling the story for three months, and to have claimed calls from Washington and the FBI asking him to keep it to himself. The memo was filed in the Bureau's flying discs file (62-83894) and copied to Denver and Los Angeles.

**The California inquiry, April–May 1951.** On 25 April 1951 the Office of the Attorney General of California, Edmund G. Brown, wrote to [[J. Edgar Hoover]] about Newton (FBI number 835861). The letter was signed for Brown by Arthur H. Sherry, chief assistant attorney general for criminal law and enforcement. It called *Behind the Flying Saucers* "a highly fanciful book" whose gist was that flying saucers "are really manifestations of secret and sinister scientific research by the government", described Newton as the owner of the Newton Oil Company who called himself a Yale graduate and "a geophysicist engaged in secret governmental research", and said a person whose name is redacted claimed the FBI had made "a complete investigation" of him. "Other circumstances coming to our attention in the light of his criminal record," it said, "indicate to us that Newton is engaged in the perpetration of a hoax or swindle of large proportions."

Hoover replied on 3 May 1951 that "the FBI has conducted no investigation of Newton pertinent to your inquiry." An internal note on the file copy asked the San Francisco office to contact the Attorney General's office "to determine if Newton is violating any Federal Statutes in claiming to be 'engaged in governmental research of a secret nature', or in the 'perpetration of a hoax or swindle of large proportions'", and summarised what the Bureau had: Newton's arrest record, last for grand larceny in New York on 25 September 1936; the espionage inquiry, whose redacted subject was reported to have married Newton on 6 July 1939 and in which no derogatory information about him turned up; and a memo of 17 October 1950 from "Mr. Ladd" to Hoover, which reported that OSI in Washington called the assertions in Scully's book "hogwash".

On 15 May 1951 H. G. Robinson, deputy director of the California Department of Justice, thanked Hoover and said a San Francisco special agent had been given all the office's information on Newton's current activities. The August 1951 memo in Part 1 notes that Robinson was a former FBI special agent (1934–1941).

**The 1941 Denver report.** The report of 18 February 1941, from the espionage case in Part 5, found Newton "highly regarded" at the Brown Palace Hotel in Denver, where he had lived since January 1938, and quoted credit records that he had a "very substantial income, good character, and habits", no lawsuits, collections or bankruptcy, and "No indication of Espionage activities." The same report set out his arrest record from 1931 to 1936.

## How Gerb uses it

Gerb's 2025 video on Aztec cites the file to show that the FBI was following Newton within weeks of his March 1950 lecture at the University of Denver, and he quotes the 1941 report against the later picture of Newton as a career con man. He also says [[Scott and Suzanne Ramsey]] obtained 289 pages on Newton from the FBI, with more than 100 pages still withheld. The Vault release is much smaller, 77 pages.

Gerb presents the April–May 1951 letters as written by journalist [[J. P. Cahn]] of the *San Francisco Chronicle*, and finds it odd that Hoover would answer a newspaper writer within a month. In the file the 25 April letter comes from the California Attorney General's office, signed by Arthur H. Sherry, and the 15 May letter from H. G. Robinson of the state Department of Justice. Cahn is not named in Part 2. The letters do mention people whose names are redacted (the source who said the FBI had investigated Newton, and the person given the state's information), but nothing in the file identifies them. The words Gerb quotes as the FBI's purpose, "to determine if Newton is violating Federal Statutes", come from the Bureau's internal note to its San Francisco office, not from the reply, and the reply itself told the state that the FBI had made no investigation of Newton. Gerb calls the 31 March 1950 item a letter to Hoover; it is an office memorandum from the New Orleans field office.
