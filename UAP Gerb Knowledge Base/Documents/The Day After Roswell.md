*The Day After Roswell* is a 1997 book credited to retired Army Lieutenant Colonel [[Philip J. Corso]] and written with [[Bill Burns|William J. Birnes]], published by Pocket Books. It claims that the US government recovered extraterrestrial technology from the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]] and fed it into American industry through the Army's research and development program. It became a bestseller, appearing in the year of Roswell's 50th anniversary, and drew criticism for errors of basic fact.

## Gerb's view of the book

Gerb sets the book aside and works from Corso's own manuscript, *Dawn of a New Age*, which Corso's son [[Philip Corso Jr.]] released to correct it. He describes *The Day After Roswell* as ghost-written and full of "added fluff" by Birnes: it is about 130 pages longer than the manuscript, adds scenes Corso never wrote, and gives a too-perfect account of Roswell, [[Roscoe H. Hillenkoetter]] and [[Majestic 12]], with a CIA intimidation scene and a self-aggrandizing tone. He singles out its claim that the transistor, integrated circuits, fiber optics and night vision came straight from Roswell. "Unlike the Day After Roswell, we as humans didn't magically invent transistors, integrated circuits, night vision, etc. from Roswell," he says; the manuscript makes the more modest claim that recovered technology helped mature human developments. "Let's not listen to the Day After Roswell book," he says elsewhere. "Let's actually read Corso's manuscript."

Gerb notes the book's troubled reception. Senator [[Strom Thurmond]] withdrew his foreword after learning the book was about UFOs, and [[Stanton Friedman]] said a colonel with a PhD had sent him a list of 92 false claims in it. Gerb points out that these objections are aimed mainly at the book and not at the manuscript. He also finds it strange that the manuscript never mentions the body Corso said he saw at [[Fort Riley, Kansas]], although both the book and Corso's 1998 affidavit describe it. [[John B. Alexander]] was among those shown the manuscript around the time the book came out.

Gerb still draws on the book for a few details. In his video on the 1974 Coyame crash he says that according to the book, debris and bodies from Roswell were shipped to [[Fort Bliss]]. In the book the crates Corso saw at Fort Riley had come from Fort Bliss and were bound for Wright Field, so the base was their origin, not their destination.

> [!edit] Corrected 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions credited the book with saying that Roswell debris and bodies were shipped to Fort Bliss. In the book they were shipped from Fort Bliss, bound for Wright Field ([archived 1997 review of the book](https://www.geocities.ws/server2/homestead/area51/Shadowlands/6583/roswell014.html)).
