The **Immaculate Constellation Report** is an undated, unsigned 12-page whistleblower report describing [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)|Immaculate Constellation]], an alleged unacknowledged special access program (USAP) that collects imagery and other intelligence on UAP and on what it calls "ARV/RV (Reproduction Vehicles)". Journalist [[Michael Shellenberger]] first reported on it in October 2024, and on 13 November 2024 it was entered into the record of the House Oversight hearing "Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena: Exposing the Truth". Gerb's videos name its author as the whistleblower [[Matt Brown|Matthew Brown]].

## Provenance and Release

The report presents itself as "the result of a multi-year, internal investigation" into UAP, technologies of unknown origin (TUO) and non-human intelligence (NHI), provided to Congress through the whistleblower mechanisms of the FY23 National Defense Authorization Act and the FY23 Intelligence Authorization Act. It says its author reached the underlying data "while pursuing their lawful duties as an employee of the Department of Defense", and that "this public version of the author's report was reviewed and approved for public release by the Department of State, Bureau of Global Public Affairs." The report gives no author's name and no date.

Shellenberger's article on the report appeared on his outlet Public in October 2024. At the 13 November joint hearing of two Oversight subcommittees, Representative Nancy Mace, chairing, called it "the original document from the Pentagon about Immaculate Constellation that Michael Shellenberger delivered to Congress today", had a copy handed to every member on the dais and, "without objection", entered "this 12-page document" into the record. The printed hearing (Serial No. 118–135) lists it as "Report, Pentagon, 'Immaculate Constellation'; submitted by Rep. Mace" and refers readers to the committee repository at docs.house.gov for its text. The copy linked here is that repository file, a 12-page scan whose last page is blank.

Despite the hearing's label, the document is not an official Pentagon publication. No agency has acknowledged the program, and Mace described it at the hearing as a program "that your government says does not exist." The report itself says that DoD representatives denied the program's existence to congressional members and staff. Because the author's identity and the status of the work are not settled, the knowledge base links the report rather than hosting it.

## Contents

The report sorts its material into seven categories of evidence, each a numbered section:

1. **Unacknowledged Special Access Program: Immaculate Constellation.** The USAP was "established following the public disclosure of the AATIP/AAWSAP programs by Luis Elizondo in 2017", and Congress was not lawfully told of it. Its "primary mission is collecting imagery intelligence" on UAP and ARV/RV "utilizing tasked and untasked U.S. military-intelligence resources". It is described as a "parent" USAP with "child" SAPs beneath it, holding imagery intelligence (IMINT) and measurement and signature intelligence (MASINT) from platforms in low Earth orbit, the upper atmosphere, aviation altitudes and maritime environments. It also "enforces compartmentalization by detecting, quarantining, and transferring UAP and RV imagery collection incidents" before they circulate in the Military Intelligence Enterprise. The report says UAP and ARV/RVs operate "around the globe, often in close proximity to sensitive foreign assets and locations."
2. **USG Imagery Intelligence.** Nine examples of footage said to exist "on USG networks", grouped by combatant command: a "cuboid" formation of about 12 metallic orbs (CENTCOM); a fast oval over a coastal facility (CENTCOM); a large equilateral triangle hovering and slowly rotating 500 to 1,000 meters above SIGINT and MASINT collection vessels, which intelligence analysis "specifies" is a reproduction vehicle (INDOPACOM); a fighter-sized equilateral triangle, called an "ARV/RV of unknown origin", less than 200 meters above an unaware vessel (INDOPACOM); a saucer "between 200-400 meters in circumference" rising out of cloud cover and dropping back into it, seen in OPIR footage (INDOPACOM); a boomerang that emits a sphere of light (INDOPACOM); a jellyfish-shaped UAP crossing the southern border (NORTHCOM); a supersonic UAP making a head-on pass at a Navy fighter (NORTHCOM); and a tic-tac seen by OPIR and MASINT over the Atlantic near a vessel, whose timing "suggests foreknowledge of the UAP event by intelligence" (SOUTHCOM).
3. **Defense Human Intelligence Reporting.** A review of "over 400 defense HUMINT reports" by US personnel spanning 1991 to 2022. Spheres were the most reported shape, then discs; triangles, boomerangs and arrowheads were "by far the rarest". Six tables set out size, speed, signatures, colours, behaviour and effects for each shape class. Three examples follow: an orange-red sphere hovering 100 to 200 yards above an aircraft carrier's flight deck in the Pacific, a formation of metallic orbs that boxed in an F-22 and forced it out of its patrol area in the Eastern Air Defense Sector, and a small "dark sphere" that crossed the runway of an Eastern Seaboard military airfield.
4. **DoD Bureaucratic Records.** Records of AARO and DoD dealings with Congress are said to show "trivialization, obfuscation, and outright denial of UAP data", including denials of UAP-related compartmented access programs and of Immaculate Constellation itself. Other internal records are said to confirm [[Luis Elizondo]]'s account of AATIP/AAWSAP.
5. **Restricted USG Historical Records.** A 1978 NSA report, serial G/00/162-78, on Soviet research into parapsychology, "psi" and the biological effects of UAP.
6. **Signals Intelligence.** General conclusions only: peer and near-peer states have observed UAP over sensitive military and nuclear sites, treat them as security threats, have tried to shoot them down, and run internal organizations to study them.
7. **Sensitive Sources.** The whole section reads "From mouth to ear."

A conclusion headed "VI. Conclusion" calls the disclosure of NHI "a pivotal moment in human history", ends "Be not afraid", and is followed by the motto *Scientia Igne Probata; Veritas Per Fidem*.

The report does not mention the NRO or [[NRO Sentient]] by name. Neither does it give dates for any of the imagery examples. Its only date range is the 1991 to 2022 span of the HUMINT dataset.

## How Gerb Uses It

Gerb first came to the report through Shellenberger's article. In [[Videos/Alien Reproduction Vehicle - the Testimony of Mark McCandlish/summary|his video on Mark McCandlish]] he fixes on the passage about detecting, quarantining and transferring collection incidents, which Shellenberger quoted as "UAP and ARV" (the report itself says "UAP and RV"), because it put the term [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|ARV]] into a government-adjacent document. His [[Videos/Alien Reproduction Vehicle - TR-3B and the Flying Triangles/summary|video on black triangles]] opens with the hearing and the two INDOPACOM triangles the report labels reproduction vehicles. There he says the report's "historical data tracked back to 2009", a date the report itself does not give.

In [[Videos/National Reconnaissance Office - UFO Crash Retrievals, Surveillance, and Legacy Program Gatekeepers/summary|his NRO video]] Gerb says Brown "delivered the Immaculate Constellation report to Congress through Jeremy Corbell"; the hearing record has Shellenberger bringing it to the committee. He reads several of the imagery cases, the cloud-hopping saucer and the SOUTHCOM tic-tac, as overhead infrared collection, and argues that the NRO and NRO Sentient are likely sources for the program. Those links are Gerb's inference, not the report's. His figure of a saucer "roughly 210 to 420 ft in diameter" is the report's 200 to 400 meters of circumference converted to a diameter. He also cites the report's references to IMINT databases "scattered across various organizations" as evidence of compartmentalization inside the NRO.

Across his legacy-program videos Gerb stresses that IMCON, as the report describes it, is a reactive surveillance program set up in 2017, not one of the legacy crash-retrieval programs, and he suspects it originated in the [[National Security Council]].
