The **Condon Report**, formally titled *Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects*, was a comprehensive UFO investigation commissioned by the United States Air Force. The University of Colorado carried it out from 1966 to 1968 under the direction of physicist Dr. Edward Condon. Many in UAP research circles believe the report was designed to give scientific legitimacy to dismissing and stigmatizing UFO phenomena, even though it documents extraordinary military encounters that remain unexplained.

## Overview

The Condon Report was conducted under contract to the Air Force, with the explicit goal of giving an independent scientific assessment of UFO phenomena. The final report, published in January 1969, concluded that UFO investigations were not scientifically valuable and recommended that the Air Force terminate [[Project Blue Book]]. The Air Force acted on this quickly and officially disbanded Project Blue Book in December 1969. Gerb's video on [[The Origin of the UFO Stigma]] treats the Condon Report as a central instrument in the institutional suppression of serious UFO inquiry.

## Case 5: The 1957 RB-47 Encounter

Case 5 of the Condon Report ("South Central, Fall 1957"; investigator: Craig) describes an encounter in the early morning, sometime in the fall of 1957, involving "a B-47" with six crew on a combined navigation, gunnery and electronic counter-measures training mission over the Gulf of Mexico and the South Central United States. The report names no base and no crew; it calls the pilot only "a Major". Gerb dates the case to 19 September 1957 and says it was "also from [[Carswell Air Force Base]]" in Fort Worth. Later accounts identify the aircraft as an RB-47 of the 55th Strategic Reconnaissance Wing flying from Forbes Air Force Base in Topeka, Kansas, and date the flight to 17 July 1957, from Blue Book records that James McDonald found after the study. The Condon study itself found no Blue Book or Air Defense Command record and worked entirely from the crew's recollections a decade later. The case stands out because several different sensors corroborated it. Gerb cites it in his discussion of the [[Del Rio, Texas]] crash cases as evidence of a pattern of high-credibility UAP encounters originating from the same airbase.

## Relevance to Del Rio Cases

Gerb sees the RB-47 encounter as paralleling [[Robert Willingham]]'s 1955 account in both structure and geography. In his telling both originated from Carswell AFB, though later accounts place the RB-47 at Forbes AFB in Kansas, and the report names no base; both involved high-speed luminous objects detected by military radar, and in both, intelligence personnel stepped in to suppress or confiscate evidence. UAP researchers interpret the Condon Report's inclusion of this thoroughly documented case, together with its dismissive overall conclusions in spite of such evidence, as a sign of institutional bad faith in UFO investigation.
