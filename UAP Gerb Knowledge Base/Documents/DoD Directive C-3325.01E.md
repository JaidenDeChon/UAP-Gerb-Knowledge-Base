DoD Directive C-3325.01E, "Foreign Materiel Program (FMP) (U)", is an eight-page Department of Defense directive of 10 October 2006 that set out how the US military acquires and exploits foreign weapons, equipment and technology. Signed by Deputy Secretary of Defense Gordon England, with the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (USD(I)) as its office of primary responsibility, and classified CONFIDENTIAL by Under Secretary Stephen A. Cambone, it reissued a February 1998 directive (C-3325.1) and named the Director of the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]] as the DoD Executive Agent for the [[Foreign Material Program (FMP)|Foreign Materiel Program]]. A redacted copy was released under the Freedom of Information Act (case 08-F-1748) and posted in the DoD FOIA reading room. Its successor, DoD Directive S-3325.01 of 30 December 2011, remains classified.

## What it says

The directive defines three terms that recur across Gerb's work on the program:

- **Foreign materiel**: any item of foreign origin, including technical and operational documentation, with military or potential military application.
- **Foreign materiel acquisition (FMA)**: activities that gain physical possession of, or access to, an item of foreign materiel or technology.
- **Foreign materiel exploitation (FME)**: the analysis, testing, evaluation and documentation of the scientific and technical intelligence (S&TI) characteristics of such an item.

Its policy is that the Department of Defense "shall acquire and exploit foreign weapons systems and components, foreign commercial items with potential military application, and related technologies, documents, and software to enhance national security", and that information from exploitation should get "immediate and widest possible dissemination". Components carrying out FMA or FME follow guidance from the USD(I), advised by an FMP Steering Committee, and coordinate with the DIA director.

The responsibilities section gives the USD(I) oversight of the whole program and of the executive agent, including the duty to "control special access programs applicable to the FMP using required compartmented procedures" and to run an OSD fund for acquisition and exploitation. The DIA director coordinates all FMP activity, keeps a Foreign Materiel Management System database, decides which organizations acquire and exploit each item "based on overall intelligence requirements, operational requirements, and test and evaluation requirements", and makes sure the military departments' S&TI centers (for the Air Force, [[National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)|NASIC]]) are funded for the work. The [[NSA|National Security Agency]] oversees foreign cryptologic materiel; the acquisition under secretary and the Director, Operational Test and Evaluation tie the program to test and evaluation; and the military departments must report every acquisition opportunity to the DIA. Four sub-paragraphs (5.2.11, 5.4.1, 5.5.6 and 5.8.6) are withheld under FOIA exemption (b)(1). A [[DoD Directive C-3325.01E (Transcription)|transcription]] gives the full released text.

## How Gerb uses it

In [[Videos/NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering/summary|NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering]], Gerb notes that the directive now governing the program, S-3325.01, is classified, and turns to what he calls the "declassified 2006 DoD directive C/3325.01" to show that, at least around 2006, the program was overseen by the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence, today the [[Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USDINS)|Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security]], with the DIA as executive agent. He argues that an "off-books" part of the Foreign Materiel Program is where part of the UFO legacy programs hides. The released copy bears its original declassification date of 1 August 2031, but the CONFIDENTIAL marking at the top and foot of every page is struck through, and four sub-paragraphs are blacked out under exemption (b)(1). It was declassified early, in part, for the FOIA release.
