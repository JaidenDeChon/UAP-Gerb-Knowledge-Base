The Twining White Sands Air Accident Report is an alleged four-page document, dated 10 July 1947 and bearing the signature of General [[Nathan Twining]], then commanding the [[Air Materiel Command]], that summarizes the investigation of a disc recovered at [[White Sands Missile Range|White Sands Proving Ground]], New Mexico. [[Leonard Stringfield]] published it in 1994 in [[Stringfield Status Reports|Status Report VII]], saying he had received it from what he called a "solid source". Gerb gives its title as "Air Accident Report of Flying Saucer Aircraft over White Sands Proving Ground, New Mexico". Its authenticity has never been established, and Stringfield himself said he could neither prove nor disprove it. It should not be confused with the authentic [[Twining Memo]] of 23 September 1947.

## What it is said to contain

As Gerb summarizes it in [[Videos/NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering/summary|NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering]]:

- The data came from T-2, Air Materiel Command's intelligence department and an ancestor of [[National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)|NASIC]]. Gerb notes that by July 1947 the unit was formally the Air Intelligence Division, but that Air Force documents of the time still called it T-2, so the label is period-accurate.
- The disc was round and domed on top, with no canopy, portholes or windows, so investigators concluded it was piloted remotely or by "remote viewing".
- It had no weld marks, rivets or solder joints, no external propulsion, power plant, intake or exhaust, and its components appeared molded and pressed into a perfect fit.
- German scientists from Fort Bliss and White Sands concluded it was not a secret German weapon.
- A compartment held a "possible atomic engine", according to Dr. von Kármán (Gerb's "Dr. Bon Carman") and Dr. J. Robert Oppenheimer.

## Provenance

Stringfield received the document around the time of the Majestic documents, but insisted that it had nothing to do with the packages sent to [[Bill Moore]], Jaime Shandera and others. Gerb notes that it reads like the executive summary of the 50-page report that Stringfield's pseudonymous witness "Bob", interviewed in 1991 with [[Tim Cooper|Timothy Cooper]], said he had copied for Colonel Paul Helmick (see [[Stringfield Status Reports]]). He sets out four possibilities: Bob and the report are both accurate; Bob's story is true and the report was planted on Stringfield as "passage material"; the report is genuine and Bob invented his part; or both are false or passage material. Since neither can be proven, Gerb keeps the story as "an interesting yet unprovable footnote". The one part he treats as fact is Twining's involvement in the legacy effort as commander of Air Materiel Command, including in the Roswell crash and in crashes at White Sands in the late 1940s and early 1950s.
