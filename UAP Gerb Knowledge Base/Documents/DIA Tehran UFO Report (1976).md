"Reported UFO Sighting (U)" is a confidential intelligence report, IR 6 846 0139 76, that the US Defense Attaché Office in Tehran sent to the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]] (DIA) in Washington on 23 September 1976. It describes the night of 18 to 19 September 1976, when the Imperial Iranian Air Force sent two F-4 Phantom IIs after a brilliant light over Tehran: the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]]. It is the main official record of that incident. The DIA rated the report highly, and a copy reached the [[NSA]], whose release of its UFO files is the source of the copy hosted here.

## What it says

The message is dated 230630Z September 1976 and comes from USDAO Tehran. The originator is Colonel Frank B. McKenzie, USAF, the defense attaché. It went to the DIA, with information copies to the Secretary of Defense, Commander Middle East Force, US Air Forces in Europe and US European Command. The Joint Chiefs' message center also distributed it to the State Department, the CIA, the NSA, the White House and the chiefs of the Air Force, Navy, Army and Marine Corps. Its subject line is "Reported UFO sighting (U)", for 19 and 20 September 1976.

- **The calls.** At about 12:30 am on 19 September the Imperial Iranian Air Force (IIAF) command post received four telephone calls from people in the Shemiran area of Tehran who had seen strange objects in the sky, "a kind of bird-like object" or a helicopter with a light on. No helicopters were airborne. Brigadier General Yousefi, assistant deputy commander of operations, told one caller it was only stars, then looked for himself. He saw an object "similar to a star bigger and brighter" and scrambled an F-4 from Shahrokhi Air Base.
- **The first F-4.** It took off at 0130 and headed for a point about 40 nautical miles north of Tehran. The object was so bright it was "easily visible from 70 miles away". At 25 nautical miles the jet lost all instrumentation and communications (UHF and intercom), broke off the intercept and headed back to Shahrokhi. Once it had turned away, "and apparently was no longer a threat to it", its instruments and radio came back.
- **The second F-4.** It was launched at 0140. Its backseater got a radar lock at 27 nautical miles, closing at 150 knots. At 25 nautical miles the object moved away, at a speed visible on the radar scope, and held that distance. The radar return was comparable in size to a 707 tanker's. Visually the object was too bright to size: flashing strobe lights in a rectangular pattern, alternating blue, green, red and orange so fast that all the colours could be seen at once.
- **The second object.** South of Tehran, a second bright object about half to a third the apparent size of the moon "came out of the original object" and headed straight for the F-4. The pilot tried to fire an AIM-9 missile at it, but at that instant his weapons control panel went off and he lost all communications. He turned away in a negative-G dive. The object fell in trail at about 3 to 4 nautical miles, went to the inside of his turn, then returned to the primary object "for a perfect rejoin".
- **The third object.** Another object then came out of the other side of the primary, going straight down. With their panel and radio restored, the crew watched it, expecting an explosion, but it "appeared to come to rest gently on the earth" and lit an area about 2 to 3 kilometres across. The crew descended from 26,000 to 15,000 feet to mark its position.
- **The landing.** Each time the F-4 passed through a magnetic bearing of 150 degrees from Mehrabad it lost UHF and interphone, and its inertial navigation system fluctuated between 30 and 50 degrees. The one civil airliner approaching Mehrabad lost communications in the same area but reported seeing nothing. On final approach the crew saw a cylinder-shaped object, about the size of a T-33 ("T-Bird") at 10,000 feet, with steady lights at each end and a flasher in the middle. The tower had no other traffic, and saw the object only after the pilot told it where to look.
- **The next day.** In daylight a helicopter took the crew to the spot where the object seemed to have landed, a dry lake bed. They found nothing there, but to the west picked up "a very noticeable beeper signal", loudest at a small house with a garden. The people inside spoke of a loud noise and "a very bright light like lightening [sic]". The aircraft and the landing area were being checked for radiation.

The reporting officer's comment says the information came from "source in conversation with a sub-source, and IIAF pilot of one of the F-4s", and promises more when available. The report names none of the aircrew. The pilot of the second F-4 is generally identified as [[Parviz Jafari]], who later told his story publicly.

### The DIA's evaluation

The copy also carries the DIA's intelligence report evaluation form. It rates the report's value "High (Unique, Timely, and of Major Significance)", its reliability "Confirmed by other sources", and its use "Potentially Useful" as current intelligence. The remarks are partly struck through and overtyped and can't all be read, but they call it a case meeting the criteria "necessary for a valid study of a UFO phenomenon", seen by witnesses at Shemiran, Mehrabad and the dry lake bed, and then list:

- the high credibility of many of the witnesses ("an air force general, qualified aircrews, and experienced tower operators");
- visual sightings "confirmed by radar";
- similar electromagnetic effects "reported by three separate aircraft";
- physiological effects on some crew members, a loss of night vision from the object's brightness;
- "an inordinate amount of maneuver-ability" by the UFOs.

The form looks to be dated 12 October 1976. Secondary accounts credit the evaluation to Colonel Roland Evans of the DIA, but on this copy the evaluator's name and organisation can't be read, and the approving signature is illegible.

## Release

The NSA's copy is six pages: a DIA routing and transmittal slip dated 8 December 1978 from Louis E. Foster (RDS-3A, DIA), with the addressee redacted and the remark "Per telecon."; the evaluation form; and the four-page message, received at the DIA on 23 September 1976. The evaluation form was scheduled for declassification on 31 December 1982 and is stamped as changed to unclassified. Some routing lines and the classification markings on each page of the message are redacted. The NSA posted the file, scanned in October 1997, with the other UFO documents in its declassified records. The scan's own text layer was unreadable, so the hosted copy has been OCR'd afresh, and a checked [[DIA Tehran UFO Report (1976) (Transcription)|transcription]] gives the full text.

## In Gerb's videos

Gerb never shows or names the report, but his account of the incident follows it. In [[Videos/The First Commercial Flight Grounded Due to UFOs/summary|The First Commercial Flight Grounded Due to UFOs]] he tells it as one of his favourite cases: the first of two F-4 Phantom IIs lost all instruments and communications and turned back, the second was scrambled ten minutes later (the report has the take-offs at 0130 and 0140), a smaller object separated from the main one and came at the jet, and its targeting and radio failed when the pilot tried to engage it. His other details are not in the report, and match Jafari's later accounts: flying at Mach 1, seeing the object jump 10 degrees to his right from 70 miles away, a diamond shape, and his decision to eject if the sphere came within two miles. The report gives 70 miles only as the distance from which the first jet could see the object. In [[Videos/FASTWALKERS – UFOs Outside Earth/summary|FASTWALKERS – UFOs Outside Earth]] he plays a clip of Jafari describing the jump, and, relaying [[Brad Sparks]]'s question of whether a DSP satellite caught the jets rather than the object, calls them "Mirage f4s". The report's jets are F-4 Phantoms; no Mirage took part. It says nothing about satellites.
