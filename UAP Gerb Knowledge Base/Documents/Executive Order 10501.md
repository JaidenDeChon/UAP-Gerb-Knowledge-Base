Executive Order 10501, "Safeguarding Official Information in the Interests of the Defense of the United States," is the classification order President [[Dwight D. Eisenhower]] issued on 5 November 1953. It replaced Truman's Executive Order 10290 and took effect on 15 December 1953. It set three levels of classified defense information, Top Secret, Secret and Confidential, and dropped the "Restricted" level that had been in use before. It also cut back the number of agencies allowed to classify information. Section 2 gives no original classification authority to agencies with no direct responsibility for national defense, and lets agencies with partial responsibility classify only through their head, leaving it mainly with agencies directly responsible for national defense. Accounts of the order put the result at 28 agencies stripped of the authority and 17 limited to their heads; those figures are not in the order itself.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions gave the figures of 28 and 17 agencies as the order's own. The order (18 FR 7049, section 2) sets out three tiers of agencies without counting them; the figures come from accounts of the order.

## In Gerb's research

Gerb treats the order as the start of what he calls the "wild wild west" days of the UFO [[Legacy Programs|legacy programs]]. In *The Manhattan Project 2.0* he says that program managers, "quite peeved" at losing the fourth level, answered by layering ad hoc protections on top of Top Secret, above all Top Secret codeword access, as seen in the early [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]]. In that period, he says, the programs could put themselves anywhere, and the same approach produced black programs such as the 1958 congressional bunker at the Greenbrier. He believes Eisenhower used the same ad hoc approach to hide the program's leadership in the [[NSC 5412 Committee|5412 Special Group]]. He separates the "restricted" level the order abolished from the Department of Energy's [[Restricted Data]], a different category created by statute.

In *Special Access Required* Gerb calls the order the start of the idea of special access. In *The Manhattan Project 2.0* he traces the line from it to [[Richard Nixon]]'s Executive Order 11652 of March 1972, which formally set up a special access framework, and on to the [[Special Access Programs (SAPs)|special access programs]] of today. *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval* makes the same argument: by giving the power to classify to fewer agencies, the order pushed UFO-related compartments toward informal special-access controls of their own.
