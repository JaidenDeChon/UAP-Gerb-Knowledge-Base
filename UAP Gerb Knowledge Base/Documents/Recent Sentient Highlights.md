"Recent Sentient Highlights" is a ten-page [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|National Reconnaissance Office]] briefing dated 6 May 2021. It reports that [[NRO Sentient]], the office's automated research and development system for satellite collection, picked out a possible small airborne object in overhead imagery that day. The NRO approved it for release on 2 June 2022, under FOIA case F-2021-00154, as document C05136334. Only the title slide and one summary slide were released; the other eight pages are withheld in full.

## What it says

The title slide reads "(S/REL) Recent Sentient Highlights", dated 6 May 2021, and is marked "R&D Data Only – Not finished intelligence." Its overall classification, struck through on release, was TOP SECRET//SI/TK/REL, and its releasable information was marked for the United States and the Five Eyes partners.

The second slide, headed "(U) BLUF" (bottom line up front), carries the substance:

- At 0038:17Z on 6 May 2021, Sentient's image processing "detected a possible airborne object ~78km southeast of" a redacted place.
- The object was "small (<10m)" and "did not match the visual signature of typical aircraft detections."
- It did "vaguely resemble similar detections of airborne objects by US Navy aircraft and surface vessels" in a redacted set of operating areas, glossed as "Unidentified Aerial Phenomena". There was "a rough similarity to the previously-reported 'tic tac' shape."
- It was "likely not a sensor artifact or focal plane anomaly (although more in-depth imagery analysis is warranted)."
- No correlating tracks or ELINT/SIGINT turned up in the redacted reporting, despite time-coincident collection. Sentient did detect something in the same imagery about 25 km to the west, which the slide goes on to call a "high-interest vessel". Its identity is redacted. The slide says it has been associated with command-and-control activities as well as "more traditional telemetry and space functions", and that its simultaneous presence is "notable, although possible [sic] merely coincidental."
- "Confidence is relatively low in this detection, but the potential linkage to similar phenomenon off of" a redacted area "may warrant further investigation."

Pages 3 to 8 are marked "Page Denied" under FOIA exemptions (b)(1) and (b)(3). Pages 9 and 10 are marked "Page Denied" and "Non-Responsive Record".

## Release and copies

The NRO posted the file in its FOIA reading room in a batch dated 31 October 2022, together with other records from the same case: a twenty-page "Sentient Operations Highlight" on the same detection (C05136331) and a fifteen-page email chain on Sentient research and development support to the UAP Task Force (C05136347). [[John Greenewald]] of [[The Black Vault]] wrote the records up on 27 February 2023, and his site hosts its own copy of the briefing. The copy hosted here is the NRO's, retrieved through the Wayback Machine because the NRO site refuses scripts.

## How Gerb uses it

In his [[Videos/National Reconnaissance Office - UFO Crash Retrievals, Surveillance, and Legacy Program Gatekeepers/summary|video on the NRO]], Gerb reads out the second slide as proof that Sentient has "provable instances of detecting UFOs". He says the NRO released the document on 2 June 2022, which matches its release stamp, and quotes the object's size, its vague resemblance to the Navy's detections and the sensor-artifact line. He says the NRO "publicly concluded the image was tic tac shaped". The slide claims less than that: it notes "a rough similarity" to the tic-tac shape and calls its own confidence "relatively low".

Gerb sets the briefing against the NRO's 2015 statement that UFOs were "outside of the scope of NRO responsibilities" and that it held no records. He also asks whether this detection could be the tic-tac over US Southern Command described in the [[Immaculate Constellation Report]]. Neither document dates or places its object in a way that settles the question: this slide redacts the location, and the Immaculate Constellation report gives no date for its tic-tac.
