The **Stringfield Status Reports** are seven reports on alleged crash retrievals of non-human craft and bodies that the Cincinnati researcher [[Leonard Stringfield]] wrote between 1978 and 1994. The first was a paper read at the 1978 [[MUFON]] symposium in Dayton, Ohio. Stringfield published the later ones himself, as numbered *Status Reports*, until shortly before his death in December 1994. Each gathers letters, interviews and tips from military and civilian informants, most of them identified only by initials or pseudonyms, case by case. Gerb calls them "a Bible of sorts" and his "magnum opus", draws on them in more than twenty videos, and rates them "the best crash retrieval work done by any investigator".

The 1978 paper is counted as the first report, although Wikipedia's list calls it only "unofficially" Status Report I, and some sources say the second was distributed through MUFON. Before the series he had written a book for a general publisher, *Situation Red: The UFO Siege* (1977), which is not part of it. His unpublished files, which went to MUFON after his death, are also not part of it, though Gerb often cites them alongside the reports: [[Michael Schratt]] found the 5 April 1980 letter on [[Project Redlight]] and the RB correspondence there.

## Who attests what

The reports are Stringfield's records of what his informants told him, often at second or third hand. They attest that the accounts were given to him, in the form he reports them. They do not, by themselves, show that any of the recoveries happened. Stringfield kept most sources anonymous at their request, reported accounts that cut against cases as well as those that supported them ("Uncle" told him Kecksburg was a crashed spy plane), and noted when witnesses changed their stories, as when Dr. Epigoni cut the body count at Edwards from twelve to four.

Gerb's own reading goes further. He takes Stringfield's judgement of a witness as weighty evidence in itself ("the word of Stringfield carries much weight for myself and others"), and he treats a case's appearance in the reports years before other sources as a mark in its favour, as with Coyame.

## Why one page for the series

The seven reports get one page between them, not one each, for three reasons:

- **Gerb uses them as one body of work.** He cites "status reports 1 through 7" as a unit far more often than any single report, and in several videos he cites them without saying which one he means. When he does name a report, it is to locate a case, and the case has its own page.
- **They are one series by one author on one subject**, numbered by Stringfield himself, and a case in one report is often taken up again in a later one, as Aztec is in reports V and VI.
- **None has a public copy to host or link**, so separate pages would hold little beyond the title and year that the table above already gives.

If a video takes up one report as a document in its own right, it can be split out then.

## Copies and rights

All seven are copyrighted works by a private author, and Stringfield's death in 1994 puts their US copyright term past 2060. They have been reprinted in paperback and sold as ebooks in the 2010s, and are in print. No copy is linked here: no copy of the original reports put online by Stringfield, his estate or MUFON was found, and the reprints are commercial editions, not copies of the originals. The page therefore has no `source_url`, and nothing is hosted.

## In Gerb's videos

Gerb draws on the reports throughout his crash retrieval videos, among them [[Videos/The 1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania UFO Crash/summary|The 1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania UFO Crash]], [[Videos/The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval/summary|The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval]], [[Videos/The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash/summary|The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash]], [[Videos/US Navy UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering Programs/summary|US Navy UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering Programs]], [[Videos/UAP Reverse Engineering at Edwards Air Force Base Redacted List Vol.2/summary|UAP Reverse Engineering at Edwards Air Force Base Redacted List Vol.2]] and [[Videos/1997 Peru UFO Crash Retrieval - the Story of Jonathan Weygandt/summary|1997 Peru UFO Crash Retrieval]]. In [[Videos/UFO Whistleblowers Vol.2/summary|UFO Whistleblowers Vol.2]] he names them, with [[Ryan S. Wood]]'s *Majic Eyes Only*, as his handbooks for crash retrieval research.

Where Gerb's account differs from the reports:

- In the Kecksburg video he dates *Status Report III: Amassing the Evidence* to 1993. It appeared in 1982.
- He gives the first report's title as "Crash Retrievals of the Third Kind", and in *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* he calls the whole series "crash retrievals of the third kind status reports 1 through 7". The 1978 paper is *Retrievals of the Third Kind*, and the later reports have their own titles.
- In the Del Rio video he notes that the reports file [[William Drager]]'s testimony under "Del Rio, Texas, 1950", although Drager describes [[Robert Willingham]]'s case, which Gerb dates to 1955. That is a discrepancy in the reports, which Gerb points out rather than makes.
