The Project Grudge Report, "Unidentified Flying Objects – Project GRUDGE", is the report of [[Project Grudge]], the US Air Force's second UFO investigation. It is Technical Report No. 102-AC 49/15-100, Project No. XS-304, dated August 1949. Its title page names Lt H. W. Smith and G. W. Towles. It was approved by Lt Col A. J. Hemstreet Jr., chief of the Technical Analysis Division, and, for the Commanding General, by Col Harold E. Watson, chief of the Intelligence Department, at the [[Air Materiel Command]]. It was issued as a classified report. Stamps on the title page show its classification cancelled on 1 August 1952 over the signature of Captain [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], by then head of Project Blue Book. The National Archives holds it in the [[Project Blue Book]] administrative files, on microfilm publication T1206, roll 85, and its scan runs to 412 pages.

## What it contains

- **Scope.** The report says it "may be considered as final for the period reported upon". Of about 375 incidents on record, it covers numbers 1 to 244, reported up to January 1949. It sets aside the "green fireballs" seen around Albuquerque, New Mexico since 5 December 1948, on the grounds that they have "little or nothing in common" with the other incidents, and notes that Dr. Lincoln La Paz was convinced they were not ordinary meteors. (Those sightings were later the subject of [[Project Twinkle]].)
- **Results.** After vague and duplicate reports are dropped, 228 incidents remain. The consultants offered plausible explanations for 164, 30 could not be explained for lack of evidence, and the remaining 34 are treated in detail in Appendix I, the Air Materiel Command's evaluation.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the 34 remaining incidents were treated in the body of the report. The summary says only "in this report", and the main text sends the reader to Appendix "I", where they are evaluated one by one (see the transcription).

- **The consultants.** The foreword lists [[J. Allen Hynek]] of Ohio State University (under contract from December 1948 to April 1949), the Air Weather Service, Dr. G. E. Valley of the Scientific Advisory Committee (the main text and Valley's own paper say Scientific Advisory Board), the [[RAND Corporation]], the 3160th Electronics Laboratory, Dr. Paul M. Fitts of the Air Materiel Command's Aero-Medical Laboratory, and the US Weather Bureau. The summary gives each one's finding: Hynek thought 32% of cases astronomical (53% allowing for errors of observation), 35% balloons, aircraft, rockets or birds, and 33% without enough evidence or explanation; the Air Weather Service found 12% were weather balloons; RAND found nothing to "seriously controvert simple rational explanations"; Fitts found the errors came chiefly from "inability to estimate speed, distance, and size". The main text gives Hynek's figures differently, for the 228 incidents it treats: about 33% astronomical, 37% suggestive of other explanations and 30% unexplained.

> [!edit] Clarified 17 October 2026
> Added that the main text of the report puts Hynek's figures at about 33%, 37% and 30%, against the summary's 32%, 35% and 33%, and that Valley's paper and the main text name the Scientific Advisory Board where the foreword says Committee.

- **Conclusions.** The reports pose "no direct threat to the national security of the United States". They are the result of misinterpreted conventional objects, "a mild form of mass hysteria or 'war nerves'", hoaxers and publicity seekers, and "psychopathological persons". A planned release of unusual aerial objects with related propaganda "could cause mass hysteria", by or against an enemy.
- **Recommendations.** That the investigation be "reduced in scope", with collection limited to reports showing "realistic technical applications"; that its first two conclusions be declassified and released to the press; and that the Psychological Warfare Division and other agencies interested in psychological warfare be told of the results.
- **Appendices.** Two Air Intelligence information reports, Hynek's final report (Project 364), the Air Weather Service's reports, Valley's paper, a RAND letter and J. E. Lipp's "Space Ship Considerations", an analysis by the 3610th Electronics Station (so numbered in the appendix list and on the appendix's title page; the letter itself is headed 3160 Electronics Station, and signed by Capt. A. C. Trakowski, Jr.), Fitts's psychological analysis, a Weather Bureau note on ball lightning, and a summary of the Air Materiel Command's evaluation of the remaining reports.

> [!edit] Clarified 17 October 2026
> Added that the 3610th of the appendix list is headed 3160 on the letter itself, as in the foreword, and who signed it.

## In Gerb's videos

In [[The Origin of the UFO Stigma]], Gerb says Grudge was officially terminated in 1949, "but Grudge records were updated up until 1999 featuring a full published report in 1960", which he describes as "707 pages of case analysis". He leaves a review of the report for a later video and says at the end that he read "the whole Grudge and Blue Book reports" while preparing it. The report's own date is August 1949. The National Archives' scan runs to 412 pages, and no 1960 edition turned up in the search for a copy. The list Gerb gives of what Grudge was tasked to explain sightings away as (balloons, conventional aircraft, optical illusions, planets, mass hallucinations) is close to RAND's comment, quoted in the report's summary.

His example of Grudge at work, the [[Lubbock Lights]] of August 1951, came two years after the report and isn't in it.

## The copy

The source is the National Archives' scan of the report, from the Project Blue Book administrative files (Record Group 341), catalog entry 461456800. The archives serve it only as 412 separate page images, not as a PDF. The copy hosted here binds those images, in the archives' order, into one PDF, with each page re-encoded as a smaller JPEG (the archives' JPEG 2000 masters come to 654 MB) and a text layer added by OCR. The PDFs of the report found online are aggregators' copies, from other scans:

- Wikimedia Commons, "Project GRUDGE Report 1949.pdf", 413 pages, 75.6 MB, which credits NICAP's scans: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Project_GRUDGE_Report_1949.pdf
- Wikimedia Commons, "Project Grudge Report, 1949.pdf", 404 pages, 126.5 MB, which credits majesticdocuments.com: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Project_Grudge_Report,_1949.pdf
- NICAP's copy, split into ten PDFs of 7 to 10 MB: http://www.nicap.org/grudge/grudge_dir.htm

The Air Force Declassification Office released a separate 29-page file of Grudge records in 2011 (AFD-110719-005). It opens with a letter of 4 January 1950 recommending that special project action on "flying saucer" reports be discontinued; the letter refers to the "Project Grudge Technical Report" but isn't the report.
