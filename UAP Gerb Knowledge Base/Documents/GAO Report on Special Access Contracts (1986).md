*Information Security: Need for DOD Inspections of Special Access Contracts* (GAO/NSIAD-86-191) is a 16-page report that the General Accounting Office sent to Secretary of Defense Caspar Weinberger on August 7, 1986, over the signature of [[Frank Conahan|Frank C. Conahan]]. It grew out of GAO's audit of how [[Lockheed Martin|Lockheed]] kept the classified documents of a special access program at its Burbank plant, and it repeats GAO's 1983 recommendation that the Defense Investigative Service, rather than each program's own office, inspect special access ("carve-out") contracts.

## What it says

- Lockheed's document-control system for the special access program was poor, and the Defense Department program office that oversaw it had not kept watch on document accountability. Its on-site security representative was busy with other duties and inexperienced in information security.
- GAO could not say whether Lockheed was an exception or a sign of how little attention defense components paid to document accountability, but it argued the problems might not have happened had the Defense Investigative Service been allowed to inspect the contract twice a year, as it already did Lockheed's other classified contracts.
- Appendix I reproduces GAO's July 24, 1986, testimony on the audit to the Subcommittee on Oversight and Investigations of the House Committee on Energy and Commerce. It reports that Lockheed had just finished inventorying all 53 of the plant's document control substations and had found 1,460 discrepancies among about 40,000 secret and top secret items, a figure GAO had not yet had the chance to test.
