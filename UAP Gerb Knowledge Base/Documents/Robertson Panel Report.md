The Robertson Panel Report is the CIA's record of the [[Robertson Panel]], the scientific advisory panel on unidentified flying objects that met at CIA headquarters in mid-January 1953 under physicist [[Howard P. Robertson]]. Its cover dates the meetings January 14 to 18, 1953, though the attached tabs give 14 to 17 January, and the memo's own account of the sessions runs from Wednesday 14 January to Saturday 17 January. The copy archived here is the "Durant Report", a memorandum of February 16, 1953, from Frederick C. Durant, one of the panel's associate members, to the CIA's Assistant Director for Scientific Intelligence. It summarizes the meetings, the evidence the panel was shown and the comments of its members, and it attaches the panel's own short report as Tab A. The CIA released this copy through its CREST database, stamped "Approved For Release 2013/06/26".

> [!edit] Clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions gave only the cover's dates of January 14 to 18. Tabs B and C and the memo's session-by-session account show the panel met on four days, 14 to 17 January.

## What it contains

- **The panel's report (Tab A).** The formal two-page report signed by the five panel members: the evidence showed "no indication that these phenomena constitute a direct physical threat to national security", but the continued emphasis on reporting them threatened "the orderly functioning of the protective organs of the body politic". It recommends that the national security agencies "strip the Unidentified Flying Objects of the special status they have been given and the aura of mystery they have unfortunately acquired", and set policies on intelligence, training and public education.
- **Durant's Part II.** An "unofficial supplement" to the formal report, setting out comments and suggestions the members "believed were inappropriate for inclusion in the formal report". The proposals that later made the panel notorious are here, not in Tab A: an educational program with two aims, "training and 'debunking'", carried out through television, motion pictures and popular articles, and the view that civilian groups such as the Civilian Flying Saucer Investigators and the Aerial Phenomena Research Organization "should be watched".
- **The evidence reviewed.** Status reports of [[Project Grudge]] and [[Project Blue Book]], the Tremonton, Utah and Great Falls, Montana films, and work by [[Project Stork]], the [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] study (Tab B).
- **The participants.** Robertson, with panel members including [[Thornton Page]], and [[J. Allen Hynek]] among the associate members.
- **Later pages.** The CREST copy ends (pages 33 to 38) with excerpts from [[Project Blue Book Special Report No. 14|Blue Book Special Report No. 14]], dated 5 May 1955, filed with the memorandum.

> [!edit] Corrected 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the panel reviewed case files and films from Grudge and Blue Book. Tab B lists Grudge and Blue Book status reports and, separately, the Tremonton and Great Falls films. The note on the copy's last pages is new, from the transcription.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> In the Tab A item above, earlier versions said the formal report (Tab A) recommended reducing public interest in UFOs through "debunking" and keeping watch on civilian UFO groups. Both proposals are in Part II of Durant's memorandum, his unofficial record of comments the members kept out of the formal report; Tab A recommends stripping UFOs of their special status and public education.

## In Gerb's videos

In his [[National Reconnaissance Office - UFO Crash Retrievals, Surveillance, and Legacy Program Gatekeepers|National Reconnaissance Office video]], Gerb shows the report's list of the evidence presented to the panel on screen: material from Grudge, Blue Book and Stork. He mentions the panel in about a dozen other videos, most fully in [[The Origin of the UFO Stigma]], where he treats it as the start of the official campaign to make UFOs a subject for ridicule.
