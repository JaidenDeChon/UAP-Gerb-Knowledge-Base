The Estimate of the Situation is a Top Secret intelligence estimate that [[Project Sign]], the US Air Force's first UFO project, is said to have written in the late summer or early autumn of 1948. According to [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], who saw a surviving copy, it concluded that UFOs were interplanetary. General [[Hoyt Vandenberg]], the Air Force Chief of Staff, is said to have rejected it for lack of proof. No copy has ever been made public. Its existence rests on Ruppelt's 1956 book, *The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects*, and on accounts that came later. The Air Force has never released it or confirmed it, and in 2024 the [[AARO|All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office]] reported that it could not find it.

Gerb doesn't use the name. In *Special Access Required* Vol. 2 he calls it a 1948 Project Sign "memo" that Ruppelt saw. He also uses "estimate of the situation" as a term of his own, for a statement by a credible witness that he uses to check one era against another (see [[Andrew Kissner]], [[Wilbert B. Smith]], [[Bob Oechsler]]). Those are not this document.

## Ruppelt's Account

Almost everything known about the Estimate comes from Chapter Three of Ruppelt's book. Ruppelt headed Sign's successors, [[Project Grudge]] and [[Project Blue Book]], from late 1951 to 1953, and wrote about Sign from its files and from talking to people who had worked on it.

- **Why it was written.** On 24 July 1948 an Eastern Airlines DC-3 crew reported a near-collision with a UFO near Montgomery, Alabama (the Chiles-Whitted sighting). Ruppelt writes that "a few days after the DC-3 was buzzed, the people at ATIC decided that the time had arrived to make an Estimate of the Situation. The situation was the UFO's; the estimate was that they were interplanetary!"
- **What it looked like.** "It was a rather thick document with a black cover and it was printed on legal-sized paper. Stamped across the front were the words TOP SECRET."
- **What it contained.** The Air Force's analysis of many incidents, all from "scientists, pilots, and other equally credible observers", each one an unknown. It argued that the reports hadn't started with Kenneth Arnold's sighting of June 1947, citing earlier reports that came in late: a weather observer in Richmond, Virginia who watched a "silver disk" through his theodolite, an F-47 pilot and three others in his formation who saw a "silver flying wing", and the "ghost airplanes" picked up on English radar early in 1947.
- **When it went up.** It was "completed, typed, and approved" and sent up through channels, and "a matter of days after" it was signed the [[Gorman Dogfight]] took place, on 1 October 1948.
- **Its rejection.** "It got to the late General Hoyt S. Vandenberg, then Chief of Staff, before it was batted back down. The general wouldn't buy interplanetary vehicles. The report lacked proof." A group from ATIC went to the Pentagon to argue for it but couldn't convince him.
- **Its end.** "The estimate died a quick death. Some months later it was completely declassified and relegated to the incinerator. A few copies, one of which I saw, were kept as mementos of the golden days of the UFO's."

Ruppelt says the rejection, which he calls "the kickback", gradually took the heart out of Sign. By the end of 1948 enthusiasm for the interplanetary idea was cooling, and an order of 11 February 1949 renamed the project [[Project Grudge]]. In Chapter Five he ties Grudge's debunking stance to the Estimate: intelligence "had tried one answer, the then Top Secret Estimate of the Situation that 'proved' that UFO's were real, but it was kicked back", so the project tried a new hypothesis, that UFOs don't exist.

Ruppelt doesn't say who ordered the copies burned. He doesn't say Vandenberg did, and he doesn't date the destruction beyond "some months later". He also gives no date for when he saw his copy.

## Other Accounts

- **Michael Swords.** In "Project Sign and the Estimate of the Situation" (*Journal of UFO Studies*, new series 7, 2000, pp. 27–64), the historian Michael D. Swords rebuilt the story from Sign's surviving correspondence and from Ruppelt's private papers, including the unedited manuscript of his book. Swords names Sign's project officer, Captain Robert Sneider, as its probable main author. He dates the writing to September 1948, as the latest case known to be in it is from 23 September at Los Alamos, and has it sent near the end of that month. He says it was addressed to Vandenberg but meant for the Director of Intelligence, General Charles Cabell, who passed it upstairs. He also says that Ruppelt and Major [[Dewey J. Fournet]], the Pentagon's UFO officer in 1952, both saw it in 1952, and that "Sign's Estimate was ordered destroyed", without saying who ordered it. Swords adds that "no one can give a detailed description of the contents", as it hasn't been available to anyone studying it since.
- **Dewey Fournet.** Later secondhand accounts say Fournet told researchers that he had seen the Estimate and that a copy survived in the intelligence files he looked after. No such copy has come to light.
- **AARO.** The [[AARO Historical Report Volume 1]] (2024) treats the Estimate as "unsubstantiated and derived from only one source". It says Sign staff "allegedly drafted, signed, and sent" it in late July 1948, that it "allegedly concluded that UFOs were 'interplanetary' in origin", and that Vandenberg rejected it "as lacking proof". It adds that "AARO has been unable to verify his claim or locate the document" (pages 14 and 27–28 of the report; see its [[AARO Historical Report Volume 1 (Transcription)|transcription]]).
- **Related documents that are public.** Two Air Force papers from the same months survive and are sometimes confused with the Estimate. One is Sign's own final report, *Unidentified Aerial Objects: Project "Sign"* (Technical Report F-TR-2274-IA, February 1949), which concluded that some reports describe real objects but that their nature could not be determined. The other is the Pentagon's Top Secret *Analysis of Flying Object Incidents in the U.S.* (Air Intelligence Report 100-203-79, 10 December 1948), which Swords calls "the Assassin of the Estimate": it treated UFOs as possibly real and possibly Soviet, and gave the extraterrestrial idea no weight.

## How Gerb Uses It

- In *[[Videos/Special Access Required - the Secrecy of UFO Crash Retrieval Programs Vol.2/summary|Special Access Required - the Secrecy of UFO Crash Retrieval Programs Vol.2]]*, Gerb says that Ruppelt's book describes a 1948 Project Sign "memo" stating that UFOs were of "interplanetary origin", and that "this memo, according to Ruppelt, was ordered for destruction by General Hoyt Vandenberg, one of the original accused members of [[Majestic 12]]". He uses it to argue that before the [[Robertson Panel]] of 1953 there was "some sort of transparency" on the subject. Ruppelt's book has Vandenberg rejecting the Estimate, but doesn't say he ordered it destroyed.
- In *[[Videos/The Origin of the UFO Stigma/summary|The Origin of the UFO Stigma]]*, Gerb says Ruppelt "claimed Sign endorsed the interplanetary explanation" for some unexplained sightings, and that Vandenberg shut the program down in 1949 for "lack of proof". In Ruppelt's account the "lack of proof" is Vandenberg's reason for rejecting the Estimate in the autumn of 1948. Ruppelt says Sign was renamed Grudge by an order of February 1949, and doesn't credit that order to Vandenberg.
- In *[[Videos/MOON DUST - The Pentagon's Secret UFO Programs/summary|MOON DUST - The Pentagon's Secret UFO Programs]]*, Gerb says that Ruppelt himself, as director, concluded that UFOs had an "interplanetary explanation". In Ruppelt's book the interplanetary conclusion is the 1948 Estimate's, written three years before he joined the project.
