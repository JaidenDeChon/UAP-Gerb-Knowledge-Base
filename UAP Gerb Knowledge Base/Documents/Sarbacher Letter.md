The **Sarbacher Letter** is a two-page private letter dated 29 November 1983 from physicist [[Robert Sarbacher]] to UFO researcher [[William Steinman]] of La Mirada, California. It is typed on the letterhead of the Washington Institute of Technology, Oceanographic and Physical Sciences, which Sarbacher headed as president and chairman of the board from 500 Brazilian Avenue, Palm Beach, Florida. In it Sarbacher answers questions Steinman had sent him that autumn about recovered flying saucers. He says he had no part in the recoveries himself, names John von Neumann and Vannevar Bush as "definitely involved" and Robert Oppenheimer as probably involved, and recalls reports that crash materials were "extremely light and very tough" and that the operators were built "like certain insects". Gerb reads the letter aloud in three videos and treats it as Sarbacher's own written confirmation, three decades later, of what [[Wilbert B. Smith]] wrote down after meeting him in 1950.

## What it says

The letter is addressed to "Mr. William Steinman, 15043 Rosalita Drive, La Mirada, California 90638". A handwritten note at the top right, signed by Steinman, reads "Answer from Dr. Sarbacher Received 12-5-83".

Sarbacher apologises for the delay: he had moved his office and made "a number of extended trips". Answering the last question in Steinman's letter of 14 October 1983, he says there is "no particular reason I feel I shouldn't or couldn't answer any and all of your questions." He then answers, in the order Steinman listed them, the questions from Steinman's letter of 12 September:

1. On his own experience of recovered flying saucers: "I had no association with any of the people involved in the recovery and have no knowledge regarding the dates of the recoveries. If I had I would send it to you."
2. On whether the people Steinman listed were involved: "John von Neuman was definitely involved. Dr. Vannever Bush was definitely involved, and I think Dr. Robert Oppenheimer also." He adds that his association with "the Research and Development Board under Doctor Compton during the Eisenhower administration was rather limited so that although I had been invited to participate in several discussions associated with the reported recoveries, I could not personally attend the meetings." He supposes Dr. von Braun was asked too, and says the others Steinman listed "were probably asked and may or may not have attended. This is all I know for sure."
3. He received "some official reports" in his office at the Pentagon, but left them there, since "we were never supposed to take them out of the office."
4. He does not recall receiving any photographs of the kind Steinman asked about.
5. He gives the same answer as to question 4.

The rest of the letter is unnumbered:

- **The Canadian interview.** "I recall the interview with Dr. Brenner of the Canadian Embassy. I think the answers I gave him were the ones you listed." He says he would have been able to give more specific answers had he attended the meetings, and that he took the assignment as a private contribution: "We were called 'dollar-a-year men'. My first responsibility was the maintenance of my own business activity so that my participation was limited."
- **The materials.** "About the only thing I remember at this time is that certain materials reported to have come from flying saucer crashes were extremely light and very tough. I am sure our laboratories analyzed them very carefully."
- **The operators.** "There were reports that instruments or people operating these machines were also of very light weight, sufficient to withstand the tremendous deceleration and acceleration associated with their machinery." From talking with people at the office, he "got the impression these 'aliens' were constructed like certain insects we have observed on earth, wherein because of the low mass the inertial forces involved in operation of these instruments would be quite low."
- **The secrecy.** "I still do not know why the high order of classification has been given and why the denial of the existence of these devices."
- **The close.** He suggests Steinman get in touch with "the others who may be directly involved in this program", and signs as Dr. Robert I. Sarbacher. A postscript reads: "It occurs to me that Dr. Bush's name is incorrect as you have it. Please check the spelling."

## Reading it carefully

The letter is Sarbacher's own account, written from memory at 76, more than thirty years after the events. It attests to what he says he was told and shown, not to any recovery he saw. A few points in it are worth setting beside the record:

- **The interviewer's name.** The letter never names Wilbert Smith. Sarbacher remembers the interview as one "with Dr. Brenner of the Canadian Embassy". Researchers take it to be the meeting Smith recorded in his notes of 15 September 1950, which Smith's [[Wilbert Smith Memo|memo]] says came about through "discreet enquiries through the Canadian Embassy staff in Washington". Smith himself was a Department of Transport engineer.
- **The confirmation is of Steinman's list.** Sarbacher does not restate his 1950 answers. He says he thinks the answers he gave were "the ones you listed", so his confirmation depends on what Steinman put to him, and Steinman's letters of 12 September and 14 October 1983 are not on the copy found online.
- **The Board and the administration.** The Research and Development Board was chaired by Karl T. Compton in 1948 and 1949, under Truman, and was abolished in 1953, months into Eisenhower's first term. "Under Doctor Compton during the Eisenhower administration" cannot both be right, and the letter does not date his service.
- **No place or date for the meetings.** The letter does not say where or when the discussions about the recoveries were held. The meeting at [[Wright Field]] comes from Sarbacher's recorded telephone conversation with [[Stanton Friedman]] the same year.
- **Spelling.** The letter spells the names "von Neuman" and "Vannever Bush", in the letter whose postscript tells Steinman he has Bush's name wrong.

## Copies and rights

The letter was a private one, and Steinman, its recipient, made it public. The copy linked here is on the Majestic Documents website of Robert and [[Ryan S. Wood]], an aggregator: a four-page PDF holding scans of the two signed pages, with Steinman's note of receipt, followed by a retyped transcription. The transcription differs from the scan in small ways (it drops the "at" in "as at the time" and adds "more" before "directly involved"), so the scan is the text to quote. No archive is known to hold the original.

The letter is not hosted here. It is not a government record. As an unpublished private letter, its copyright belongs to Sarbacher's heirs, and under US law it runs for 70 years after his death in 1986, to the end of 2056. Publication by its recipient would not have changed that. It is marked `copyrighted` and linked.

## In Gerb's videos

- In [[Videos/Dr. Robert Sarbacher & the US Government's Secret UFO Crash Retrieval Group/summary|Dr. Robert Sarbacher & the US Government's Secret UFO Crash Retrieval Group]] he calls it "a truly startling letter", sets out what Sarbacher said he did and did not know, and reads the passages on the materials and the insect-like operators in full. He notes that Sarbacher put "aliens" in quotation marks, and links the light, tough materials to the Roswell wreckage and [[Battelle Memorial Institute|Battelle]]'s 1949 work on lightweight shape-memory alloys.
- In [[Videos/The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval/summary|The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval]] he reads the same passages again in the section on Sarbacher and [[Eric A. Walker]], and compares the materials with those described at Roswell and Aztec.
- In [[Videos/80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering - A Timeline/summary|80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering]] he reads them once more, as part of the evidence for the years 1947 to 1950.
- In [[Videos/The Manhattan Project 2.0 - the Secrecy of UFO Crash Retrieval Programs Vol.1/summary|The Manhattan Project 2.0]] he cites the 1983 communications for von Neumann's and Oppenheimer's involvement.

Where Gerb's account differs from the letter:

- He says Sarbacher answered "a series of 12 questions". The letter answers five numbered questions, taken from Steinman's letter of 12 September 1983, and one more from his letter of 14 October.
- In the Sarbacher video he says Sarbacher "did restate that Vannevar Bush was definitely involved alongside John von Neumann and Dr. J. Robert Oppenheimer". The letter is less sure about Oppenheimer ("I think Dr. Robert Oppenheimer also"). His wording in *The Manhattan Project 2.0*, that Sarbacher strongly suspected Oppenheimer was involved, is closer.
- He says Sarbacher "was not given access to personally attend" the discussions. The letter says he could not attend because his association with the Board was limited and his own business came first, not that he was refused.
- In the Aztec and 80 Years videos he says Sarbacher attested that the meetings were held at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base. The letter names no place. The Wright Field meeting is from the Friedman call.
- He says Sarbacher saw official reports "whilst in his Pentagon office" but did not receive photographs. That matches the letter, which says only that he does not recall receiving any.
