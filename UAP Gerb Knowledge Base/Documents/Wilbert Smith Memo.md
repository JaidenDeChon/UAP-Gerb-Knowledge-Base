The **Wilbert Smith Memo** is a three-page Canadian Department of Transport memorandum, subject "Geo-Magnetics", written in Ottawa on 21 November 1950 by senior radio engineer [[Wilbert B. Smith]] and addressed to the department's Controller of Telecommunications. It was classified top secret. Smith asked for a small, part-time project within his section to study how energy might be drawn from the Earth's magnetic field, and he tied that work to flying saucers. In support, he passed on what "discreet enquiries through the Canadian Embassy staff in Washington" had told him: that the subject was "the most highly classified subject in the United States Government, rating higher even than the H-bomb", that flying saucers exist, and that a small group headed by [[Vannevar Bush]] was studying them. The project was approved on 2 December 1950 and became [[Project Magnet]]. Gerb treats the memo as his first "estimate of the situation" for the early crash retrieval program.

## What it says

The memo is typed on Department of Transport intra-departmental correspondence paper, dated "OTTAWA, Ontario, November 21, 1950", with the subject "Geo-Magnetics" and the file reference "(R.ST.)". It is headed "Memorandum to the Controller of Telecommunications" and signed by W.B. Smith, Senior Radio Engineer. It runs as follows:

- **The work so far.** Smith's section had been studying radio wave propagation, which had led it into aurora, cosmic radiation, atmospheric radioactivity and geo-magnetism. "We are on the track of a means whereby the potential energy of the earth's magnetic field may be abstracted and used." A "small and very crude experimental unit" built about a year earlier in the department's Standards Laboratory had drawn enough energy from the Earth's field to run a voltmeter, about 50 milliwatts. A unit meant to be self-sustaining, a "pilot power plant", had now been designed.
- **The saucer connection.** "We believe that we are on the track of something which may well prove to be the introduction to a new technology. The existence of a different technology is borne out by the investigations which are being carried on at the present time in relation to flying saucers." While Smith was in Washington for the NARB conference, [[Frank Scully]]'s *Behind the Flying Saucers* (the memo calls it "Behind the Flying Saucer") and [[Donald Keyhoe]]'s *The Flying Saucers Are Real* came out. Scully claimed that preliminary studies of a saucer that had fallen into US government hands showed it worked on "some hitherto unknown magnetic principles". Smith thought his geo-magnetic work "might well be the linkage between our technology and the technology by which the saucers are designed and operated."
- **The Washington enquiries.** Through the Canadian Embassy staff, Smith learned four things:
  1. "The matter is the most highly classified subject in the United States Government, rating higher even than the H-bomb."
  2. "Flying saucers exist."
  3. "Their modus operandi is unknown but concentrated effort is being made by a small group headed by Doctor Vannevar Bush."
  4. "The entire matter is considered by the United States authorities to be of tremendous significance."

  He was also told that the US authorities were investigating "quite a number of lines which might possibly be related to the saucers such as mental phenomena", that they were "not doing too well", and that they would welcome a discussion with "suitably accredited Canadians" if Canada was doing anything in geo-magnetics.
- **Smith's opinion.** He had not solved "even the first problems in geo-magnetic energy release", but "the correlation between our basic theory and the available information on saucers checks too closely to be mere coincidence."
- **The Defence Research Board.** Mr. Wright, the Defence Research Board's liaison officer at the embassy, urged Smith to see the board's chairman, Dr. Omond Solandt. Smith met Solandt on 20 November. Solandt agreed that the work "should go forward as rapidly as possible" and offered laboratory facilities, equipment and staff, but Smith preferred to keep the project inside the Department of Transport for the time being.
- **The recommendation.** "It is therefore recommended that a PROJECT be set up within the frame work of this Section to study this problem", part time, at an initial cost of "less than a few hundred dollars", paid from the Radio Standards Lab appropriation. Draft terms of reference were attached. They are not on the copies found online.

The memo does not name Smith's Washington source. The man behind its four claims is identified from Smith's separate handwritten notes of his 15 September 1950 interview with [[Robert Sarbacher]], which record much the same statements, and from Sarbacher's own confirmations in 1983, in a call with Stanton Friedman and his [[Sarbacher Letter|letter to William Steinman]].

## Marks on the copies

The copies online carry the memo's paper trail in typed and handwritten notes:

- "TOP SECRET" at the head of the pages, struck through on the Majestic Documents copy, with a "CONFIDENTIAL" stamp beside it, and a handwritten note on page 1 that the memo was downgraded to confidential on 15 September 1969 ("15/9/69").
- At the foot of page 3, beside the signature, a handwritten "Concur" dated 24 November 1950 ("24/11/50") above illegible initials, and the approval: "OK. Go ahead with it & keep me posted from time to time", signed with the name C.P. Edwards and dated 2 December 1950 ("2/12/50"). That is the date the project was approved.
- On one copy of page 1, a handwritten note: "Given by Mr. X to Stanton Friedman 10/22/78 Toronto", with a reminder that "Top Secret" stood at the top of the page.

## Release and copies

The memo stayed top secret until its downgrade in September 1969. Gerb, following the usual account, says [[Arthur Bray]] and [[Stanton Friedman]] obtained copies in 1978, and that Bray found the memo, along with Smith's notes of the Sarbacher interview, in Smith's papers. Those papers are now held in the Arthur Bray fonds at the University of Ottawa's Archives and Special Collections (series 30-003-S1, "Research on Wilbert B. Smith"), acquired by Bray from Smith's widow and son. The university has not put them online.

Library and Archives Canada quotes the memo, as "Smith, Geo-Magnetics, Department of Transport, November 21, 1950", in its online exhibition *Canada's UFOs: The Search for the Unknown*, but the exhibition reproduces only the 1952 Project Magnet report and Project Second Story papers, not the memo itself. No copy from the Canadian government is known to be online.

The source linked on this page is therefore an aggregator: the Majestic Documents website of Robert and [[Ryan S. Wood]], whose PDF holds scans of the memo's three pages (numbered 24 to 26 in some earlier file) followed by a retyped transcription. Grant Cameron's presidentialufo.com once carried a second scan, Friedman's copy with the 1978 note, of which pages 1 and 3 survive in the Wayback Machine.

## Rights

The memo is not hosted here. It is a Canadian government record, and Canadian Crown copyright works differently from the US rule that federal works are public domain. Under section 12 of Canada's Copyright Act, copyright in a work prepared by or under the direction of a government department belongs to the Crown and runs for the rest of the calendar year of first publication plus 50 years. The memo was an internal paper that was never published by the government in the ordinary sense, so its term may not have begun. Even if its release around 1978 counted as publication, the term would run until the end of 2028 at the earliest. The Open Government Licence – Canada covers only what the government itself publishes under it, which this memo is not. Its status can't be settled with confidence, so it is marked `unclear` and linked rather than hosted.

## In Gerb's videos

Gerb reads out the memo's four claims in three videos and cites it in a fourth.

- In [[Videos/Dr. Robert Sarbacher & the US Government's Secret UFO Crash Retrieval Group/summary|Dr. Robert Sarbacher & the US Government's Secret UFO Crash Retrieval Group]] he opens on the claims, identifies the source as Sarbacher, and says the memo "directly led" to [[Project Magnet]] and [[Project Second Story]].
- In [[Videos/The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval/summary|The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval]] he says the memo "was never supposed to be public" and that its classification "was likely accidentally downgraded". There, and in [[Videos/The Manhattan Project 2.0 - the Secrecy of UFO Crash Retrieval Programs Vol.1/summary|The Manhattan Project 2.0]], he says it was top secret until 15 September 1969 and that Bray and Friedman obtained copies in 1978.
- In [[Videos/80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering - A Timeline/summary|80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering]] it is his first "estimate of the situation", the fixed point for the years 1947 to 1950, and he repeats that it "was never supposed to be public" and was "likely accidentally downgraded". He calls it "invaluable" and links its H-bomb comparison to the secrecy that followed Roswell.
- In [[Videos/US Special Forces Confession - I Recovered Crashed UFOs - Fact or Fiction/summary|US Special Forces Confession]] he cites it as evidence that a retrieval program already existed by 1950.

Where Gerb's account differs from the document:

- He describes the memo as asking Canada to begin its first official UFO investigation, "specifically studying complex magnetism as a form of propulsion". The memo asks for a project on drawing energy from the Earth's magnetic field. It links that work to the saucers and mentions the "reaction forces" a larger unit would let Smith study, but it does not mention propulsion.
- He says the claims came from "a source within Washington". The memo credits them to enquiries through the Canadian Embassy staff and names no one. The identification with Sarbacher comes from Smith's notes and from Sarbacher himself.
- In two videos he introduces "three astounding claims" and then reads four.
