The **Willingham Affidavit** is a signed statement that [[Robert Willingham]] gave in 1977 to researcher [[Todd Zechel]], describing the crash of an unidentified object on the Texas–Mexico border and his visit to the wreck. It is the earliest sworn version of the story that later became the [[1955 Langtry UFO Crash]], and one of four tellings that Gerb sets side by side in [[Videos/The 1950s Del Rio, Texas UFO Crashes/summary|The 1950s Del Rio, Texas UFO Crashes]]. No scan of it is known to be online. Its wording is known from a transcription published by researcher [[Kevin Randle]], and this page describes it from that text.

## What it says

As Randle transcribes it, the affidavit is a short first-person account that opens: "Down in Dyess Air Force Base in Texas, we were testing what turned out to be the F-94." In it Willingham says:

- Radar picked up an unidentified object travelling at high speed toward the aircraft's course, and it later came into sight. A radar station on the "DEW Line (NORAD)" confirmed it and kept tracking it, and reported that it had come down between Texas and the Mexican border.
- The object "played around a little bit". He saw it make 90-degree turns at high speed and concluded it was not a missile.
- Headquarters refused permission to follow it.
- He and a co-pilot later flew a light aircraft to the site and landed in a pasture across from where it had come down. People there told them to leave, and armed guards then formed a line around the area.
- The object seemed to have come down between two sand mounds and gone into the ground, leaving marks across the sand for some 300 to 500 yards.
- On the way back he picked up a small piece of metal. The chunks of metal looked honeycombed, as if from an explosion or a disintegration. He compares the metal to magnesium steel with a lot of carbon in it, and says a cutting torch at 3,200 to 3,800 °F could heat it but not melt it.

The affidavit gives no date for any of this, names no other person, and does not say what rank Willingham held or what aircraft he was flying when he saw the object. Randle notes that the date of signing, 1977, "does not seem to be in dispute". Where it was signed, and before whom, is not recorded in the transcription.

## How it fits the other versions

Willingham's story changed a good deal between tellings, and the affidavit sits between the first newspaper story and the 2008 book.

- **The earlier article.** Gerb says the story first appeared in 1965 in a weekly newspaper in Mechanicsburg, Pennsylvania, which asked Civil Air Patrol pilots about their UFO encounters, and that researchers later found its clippings in the files of [[National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena|NICAP]], which led Zechel to Willingham. Randle traces the first public telling to MUFON's *Skylook* in March 1968, which describes Willingham as a Civil Air Patrol colonel with an interest in UFOs "dating back to 1948 when he was leading a squadron of F-94 jets". The year 1948 comes from that article. The affidavit itself gives none.
- **The year.** Zechel dated the crash to December 1950. Willingham later put it in 1954 or 1955, and *The Other Roswell* (2008), by [[Noe Torres]] and [[Ruben Uriarte]], settles on spring 1955.
- **The base and the aircraft.** The affidavit starts at Dyess Air Force Base, testing the F-94. In the book Willingham is flying an F-86 Sabre out of [[Carswell Air Force Base]], escorting B-47 bombers.
- **The site.** The affidavit has an object buried between two sand mounds, with marks running 300 to 500 yards, and armed guards who send Willingham and his co-pilot away. The book has a disc broken into about three sections after skidding some 300 feet, Mexican soldiers who let Willingham in but keep his companion, [[Jack Perkins]], back, and a Lieutenant Martinez.
- **The bodies.** The affidavit mentions no occupants. The book quotes him saying he never saw bodies, and in a 2012 interview with [[Jeff Rense]] he described three.

Randle calls the missing date "a major oversight". The names in the text carry dates of their own: the base was renamed Dyess Air Force Base on 1 December 1956, the DEW Line went into operation in 1957 and NORAD was formed that year. As written, the affidavit uses names that did not yet exist in 1948, 1950 or 1955.

## Copies and rights

No scan or photocopy of the affidavit is known to be online, and no archive is known to hold it. The text quoted here is from Randle's transcription on his blog, *A Different Perspective*. The page therefore has no `source_url`. The affidavit is a private statement, not a government record. Its copyright, if any, would belong to Willingham's estate (he died in 2015), and it is not clear whether Willingham or Zechel drafted its wording or whether it was ever formally published, so it is marked `unclear` and would be linked rather than hosted even if a scan turned up.

## In Gerb's video

Gerb raises the affidavit while setting out his sources in [[Videos/The 1950s Del Rio, Texas UFO Crashes/summary|The 1950s Del Rio, Texas UFO Crashes]]. He says Zechel "made him sign an affidavit", and he holds the changes between it and the book against Willingham: the aircraft and the year "are still very important things to get right", and if they are wrong "the affidavit is pretty much null and void". He notes that Willingham blamed the "small inaccuracies" in his affidavit on a head wound from Korea, an excuse Gerb refuses to accept for the later contradiction over the bodies. He contrasts it with [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]]'s 1973 affidavit on the Kingman crash, whose details, he says, do not change.

Where Gerb's account differs from the affidavit:

- He says that both the newspaper article and the affidavit have Willingham flying an F-94 in 1948. The affidavit does name the F-94, but it gives no year. The 1948 date is in the article.
- He calls the article both the 1965 article and the "article from 67". Randle dates the first published telling to 1968.
