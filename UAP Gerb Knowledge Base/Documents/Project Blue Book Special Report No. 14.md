Project Blue Book Special Report No. 14, subtitled "Analysis of Reports of Unidentified Aerial Objects", is a statistical study of the UFO reports the US Air Force collected from mid-1947 to the end of 1952. It was issued on 5 May 1955 by the [[Air Force Technical Intelligence Center|Air Technical Intelligence Center]] (ATIC) at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], Ohio, as Project No. 10073, and marked "For Official Use Only (AFR 190-16)". Its title page states that "No copyright material is contained in this publication." The report names no contractor and credits its work to "a panel of scientists both in and out of the Air Force". The outside panel was [[Battelle Memorial Institute]], working under the [[Project Stork]] contract. Its central finding is that it is "highly improbable" that any of the reports examined "represent observations of technological developments outside the range of present-day scientific knowledge."

## What it says

- **Scope.** The study began in 1953 and used files up to the end of 1952. Its charts and tables cover 3,201 cases. About 800 more reports were dropped before the statistics were run, most because they were "extremely nebulous" and the rest because their statements conflicted. Official reports on hand at the end of 1954 totalled 4,834.
- **Method.** Each report was reduced to an IBM punched card through a standard questionnaire and coding system. Each sighting was identified twice: first by the person who transcribed it, then by a conference of four, two from ATIC and two from the panel of consultants, who were not shown ATIC's earlier identification. The categories ran from balloons, astronomical bodies and aircraft to "psychological manifestations", "insufficient information" and "unknown".
- **Unknowns.** Sightings rated UNKNOWN were compared with identified ones. The report found apparent similarities between the two groups, but statistical tests (chi-square) gave a low probability that the similarities were significant. A search for a verified "flying saucer" or a model of one failed: even the best reports "failed to reveal even a rough model".
- **Physical evidence.** The report says twice, in its Summary and its Conclusions, that "there was a complete lack of any valid evidence consisting of physical matter in any case of a reported unidentified aerial object." In its account of method it adds that this "was not assumed to be *prima facie* evidence that 'flying saucers' did not exist." The Conclusions open the same way: "It can never be absolutely proven that 'flying saucers' do not exist."
- **Later cases.** The Summary, written in 1955, adds the 1953 and 1954 cases (425 and 429 reports), with 9% left unknown, and the 131 reports received from 1 January to 5 May 1955, with 3% unknown. It credits the drop to faster field investigations by the 4602d Air Intelligence Service Squadron under the August 1954 revision of [[USAF Regulation 200-2|AF Regulation 200-2]], and concludes that all the unidentified objects "could have been explained if more complete observational data had been available."
- **The rest.** Most of the 312-page file is charts and tables: frequency distributions by year, month, colour, shape, duration and observer reliability, an analysis of where the sun stood relative to the observer, the chi-square tests, the "flying saucer" model study (report pages 76 to 93), Appendix A's tabulations (95 to 254) and Appendix B's working-paper forms (255 onward), including the questionnaire used to code each report.

## How Gerb uses it

In [[Videos/UFOs in the Private Sector - Battelle Memorial Institute/summary|UFOs in the Private Sector - Battelle Memorial Institute]], Gerb says that on 5 May 1955 Battelle and ATIC published Special Report 14, "a comprehensive analysis of 3200 UFO sightings", which concluded that UFOs are not craft beyond human scientific knowledge "because no physical matter had ever been recorded from any UFO sighting". He calls that "a massive contradiction": in 1949, six years earlier, Wright-Patterson had contracted Battelle for titanium-alloy research that he reads as work on [[Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium Alloy)|nitinol]], which he ties to the [[Roswell Crash]] debris.

The report itself differs from that account in a few details:

- **Who issued it.** The report is ATIC's. Battelle's name appears nowhere in it; the outside work is credited only to an unnamed panel of scientists and consultants.
- **The number.** Its statistics cover 3,201 cases, which Gerb rounds to 3,200.
- **The reasoning.** The lack of physical evidence is one of the report's points, not the basis of its conclusion. The conclusion rests on the statistical tests and the failed attempt to build a model. The report also says plainly that the missing evidence did not prove "flying saucers" did not exist.
- **What it says was missing.** The report speaks of a lack of "valid evidence consisting of physical matter" in the cases it studied. It does not say that nothing was ever recovered.

## Copies

The copy hosted here is the CIA's file copy, 312 pages, released in 2026 under section 1842 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2024. The Department of War published it on 12 June 2026, in the third release of its UAP records collection, as CIA-UAP-015. Its first two pages are CIA Archives and Records Center cover sheets, one annotated "Reviewed by RJWarsh[?] 11/21/94" and "315 pages". The cover is copy no. 35, with "ASD/A file" written across it. Its existing text layer is legible for the prose; OCR was added to the 25 pages that had none.

The [[Robertson Panel Report|Robertson Panel memorandum]] on CREST carries excerpts from the same report on its last pages.
