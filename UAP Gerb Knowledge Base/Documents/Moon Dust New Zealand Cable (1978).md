"MOONDUST: NEW ZEALAND" is a secret State Department telegram (WELLINGTON 06093) sent in two sections by the US Embassy in Wellington to the Secretary of State in Washington on 7 November 1978, with "immediate" precedence. It reports two "apparent space fragments" found in farm fields south of Ashburton, on New Zealand's South Island, and asks the Department for guidance under the [[Project Moon Dust]] reporting procedure. It is one of the Moon Dust cables the State Department has released under the Freedom of Information Act, and it is where Gerb's pressure-vessel description of the New Zealand fragments comes from.

## What it says

The cable's summary reads: "Two apparent space fragments have been located in New Zealand, the first discovered October 24 and the second November 5. Both are spherical, perhaps pressure vessels, bear no identifying marks, and caused no physical or personal damage upon impact."

- **The finds.** Farmer John Lovett picked up the first "space ball" in his paddock about 20 km south of Ashburton on 24 October. The New Zealand police sent it to the National Radiation Laboratory in Christchurch, which found it was not radioactive, and then to the New Zealand Department of Scientific and Industrial Research (DSIR). A second sphere turned up on 5 November, struck by a mowing machine in an alfalfa field about 240 metres away. It was found by another Ashburton farmer, also named Lovett and apparently his brother.
- **The Moon Dust checklist.** The embassy answers the questions set out in the guidance airgram it cites (73 STATE A-6343): pre-impact sightings (one unconfirmed report by an Ashburton milkman of an object at about 1400 GMT on 23 October, travelling north to south), time of impact, impact area, damage (none), identifying marks (none on the first sphere), size and weight, re-entry effects, a preliminary analysis, photographs and media interest ("minimal").
- **The first sphere.** A sphere with a welded equator, 180 mm across at the equator and 165 mm at the poles, about 3 mm thick and weighing 882 grams. It had 18 holes through its surface and was spattered with magnetic globules, "probably molten steel". DSIR's preliminary analysis found 11.1 per cent silicon, 0.7 per cent iron, 0.02 per cent magnesium and 0.02 per cent copper, the rest aluminium.
- **The embassy's doubts.** Other apparent space debris had been found near Ashburton in April 1972, and the embassy thought it so unlikely that two spacecraft would come down on almost the same spot that the 1978 objects looked suspect. The Mount John Baker-Nunn tracking station had not been alerted to any spacecraft decay on 23–24 October, and DSIR doubted that a silicon-aluminium alloy melting below 600 degrees could survive re-entry so intact. The second find, in a field worked earlier that year, made it more likely that they were space fragments after all and unconnected with the 1972 finds. The first ball was "too clean inside and out" to have lain in the field since 1972, its interior was coated in a fine light-brown dust not yet analysed, and its equatorial weld was external only.
- **Next steps.** New Zealand's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, told of the findings, said it would try to establish who owned the objects, perhaps through the UN Secretariat, as its treaty obligations under the agreement on the return of space objects required. The embassy asked for further guidance, and said DSIR and New Zealand's External Intelligence Bureau would welcome any information NASA or other US agencies could share. The message was coordinated with the defense attaché and is signed SELDEN.

The routing line copies it to, among others, the CIA (CIAE), the NSA (NSAE), the Defense Department (DODE) and [[NASA]].

## Release

The Department of State released the cable in full in August 2011, in FOIA case F-2011-05250, whose subject is "MOONDUST OR PROJECT MOON DUST ON BLUE FLY". It posted the case's nine telegrams, from 1973 to 1979, to its Virtual Reading Room in 2012 (this one is document C17486302). The same set includes the December 1976 to January 1977 Ottawa cables on "possible returned space objects" found at Windsor, Ontario, a 1973 Wellington cable on spheres found in 1972, and cables on a piece of a Centaur rocket recovered in Bermuda. The Department had already released this cable and others in the series in October 2007 to [[John Greenewald]] (case 200703877), whose copy of that 22-page release is on [[The Black Vault]].

## In Gerb's videos

In [[Videos/MOON DUST - The Pentagon's Secret UFO Programs/summary|MOON DUST - The Pentagon's Secret UFO Programs]], Gerb shows the New Zealand case as the fourth of five Moon Dust cases abroad. He says Moon Dust "investigates space fragments found in New Zealand described as spherical or like a pressure vessel", and he compares the shape to Commander [[David Fravor]]'s description of the [[2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter (Tic Tac)|Tic Tac]] as "similar to a propane tank or pressure vessel without any inlets or outlets". The cable's own words are "spherical, perhaps pressure vessels". It also describes two objects about 18 cm across, which the embassy and DSIR were trying to identify as satellite debris.

None of the other documents Gerb shows in that video is in these State Department releases. They include the 1961 Air Force intelligence draft policy he calls "AFC 1e draft policy" (a 3 November 1961 AFCIN-1E-O letter that names Moon Dust and Blue Fly), the 1968 Nepal cables and the 1978 Bolivia cables. The 1976 Canadian request he describes, for photos of interest to the Foreign Technology Division and the CIA's Office of Weapons Intelligence, is not among the Ottawa cables in either State Department release. Those cables only follow up an earlier request (STATE 300075), which has not been released.
