The Kona Blue documents are a 56-page packet of Department of Homeland Security records on [[Kona Blue]], a prospective special access program (PSAP) that DHS Science and Technology set up in July 2011 and that the department terminated before it ever became a full special access program. DHS declassified the records on 18 July 2023, sent them to the Department of Defense with a cover letter dated 5 February 2024, and [[AARO]] posted them on its website in April 2024, a month after its [[AARO Historical Report Volume 1|Historical Record Report, Volume 1]] described the program. The file on AARO's site is titled "AARO DHS Kona Blue".

## What the packet contains

The documents are mostly scans. Their old classification markings, chiefly TOP SECRET//KONA BLUE//NOFORN, are struck through rather than removed, which the cover letter calls "the strike-out method", and names and some passages are blacked out. In order, the packet holds:

## What the documents say

The slide deck and the SAP questionnaire describe a program built around seven centers: data collection, data analysis, advanced technology, experimental sites, consciousness, medical and education. The Advanced Technology Center was to "gain access to and inventory all existing caches of Advanced Aerospace Vehicle (AAV) materials and documentation within CONUS", whether held in archives, in national laboratories, by government organizations or by contractors. An oral history initiative was to gather information from retired officials and contractors on "the location of advanced aerospace technology and biological samples". The consciousness center was to work on remote viewing and on communication and "physical transport across dimensional/space-time barrier". The questionnaire lists facilities ready for the program: a five-building complex and a separate two-story building in Las Vegas, and a 480-acre research property in Utah with "a fifteen year history of intensive anomalous activity" under armed guard. The owners' names are redacted. The planned budget was at least $12 million in fiscal 2012, $25 million in 2013 and $35–50 million in 2014.

The history pages trace the program to the DIA contract with Bigelow Aerospace that began in 2008, which the documents call the Advanced Aerospace Threat and Identification (AATI) Program ([[AAWSAP]] in most other sources), and to Reid's unsuccessful 2009 request for a restricted SAP. In April 2011 a person whose name is redacted briefed Joel Wall and Steven Cover and invited DHS to take the program on. O'Toole was briefed in May 2011 and approved the PSAP on 11 July 2011. The November 2011 SAPOC minutes say the meeting was called urgently because of "the impending transfer of funds to DHS from Senators Reid and Leiberman [sic]", with $10 million on offer for fiscal 2012. They record that the Deputy Secretary (S2) was "still not certain of what the 'It' is in the program or otherwise what exactly we are researching".

On 8 December 2011, Lute, General Counsel Ivan Fong, Under Secretary Caryn Wagner, Cover and an associate general counsel reviewed the proposal. Their questions included whether the justifications for making it a SAP "were adequate" and whether the budget and personnel parts of the proposal were sufficient. Lute directed that the PSAP be terminated immediately. The memo recording her decision carries the handwritten date 2-10-12, and 10 February 2012 is the termination date the 2024 cover letter gives. Cover's 2013 memorandum for the record adds that she and the General Counsel had decided "the Program as proposed did not require extraordinary security measures".

The packet never names [[Lockheed Martin]], [[James T. Ryder]], [[Glenn Gaffney]] or any other contractor or CIA official, and it says nothing about who held the materials the program hoped to inventory. The documents also give no reason tied to the materials' authenticity for the termination.

## How Gerb uses it

In [[Videos/Lockheed Martin - UFO Reverse Engineering, Material Exploitation, & Legacy Programs/summary|Lockheed Martin - UFO Reverse Engineering, Material Exploitation, & Legacy Programs]], Gerb calls the packet AARO's "declassified slide deck" on Kona Blue and sets it beside the DOPSR-cleared document that Representative [[Tim Burchett]] entered into the record of the November 2024 House Oversight hearing. That document says Tara O'Toole was approached to set up the program, and Gerb points out that the declassified Kona Blue documents carry the same information about her. He reports the official explanation as DHS and other agencies shutting the proposal down "due to skepticism regarding the authenticity of the purported materials", which he disputes, arguing that high-level agency backing stonewalled the transfer. That reason doesn't appear in the documents themselves. They record doubts about the SAP justification and the proposal's completeness, and AARO's report says DHS leadership rejected it "for lacking merit". Gerb calls the deck "quite intriguing" and "maybe another topic for another day".

In [[Videos/Special Access Required - the Secrecy of UFO Crash Retrieval Programs Vol.2/summary|Special Access Required - the Secrecy of UFO Crash Retrieval Programs Vol.2]], he shows the Kona Blue marking on screen as an example of a SAP nickname with a NOFORN ("not releasable to foreign nationals") control. The struck-out TOP SECRET//KONA BLUE//NOFORN banner runs across most pages of this packet.
