Second Lieutenant George F. Gorman was an experienced World War II veteran pilot who became a second lieutenant in the [[North Dakota National Guard]] after 1945. On October 1, 1948, Gorman chased an unidentified glowing object over [[Fargo, North Dakota, USA|Fargo, North Dakota]] for 27 minutes, an incident that became known as the [[Gorman Dogfight]]. Captain [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], who later headed [[Project Grudge]] and [[Project Blue Book]], cited the Gorman case as one of three classic UFO incidents in 1948 that "proved to Air Force intelligence specialists that UFOs were real."

## The Gorman Dogfight Encounter

At around 9:00 p.m. on October 1, 1948, Gorman was flying a P-51 Mustang on a National Guard cross-country flight under clear skies. He saw a small Piper Cub aircraft 500 feet below him but no other traffic. Shortly afterward he spotted an object to the west: a small blinking light that, unlike the Piper Cub, had no visible wings.

At 9:07 p.m., Gorman contacted air traffic control at [[Hector Airport]] and was told there was no other traffic in the area. He accelerated his Mustang to 350-400 mph to chase and identify the object, but soon realized it was too fast to intercept in a straight line. Instead he tried to corner it with tight turns.

As Gorman approached the blinking light at 5,000 feet after a right turn, the object flew directly over his aircraft, only 500 feet away. During this near-collision he got a clear look at it: a simple ball of light about 6 to 8 inches in diameter. As the object accelerated, it stopped blinking and grew brighter. It then came at Gorman head-on before making a steep vertical climb. Gorman followed it up, climbing 9,000 feet, but his Mustang stalled while the ball was still 2,000 feet above him.

For his last attempt to intercept it, Gorman waited at 14,000 feet until the object descended to 11,000 feet, then dove at full power. The UFO again made an impossible vertical climb and passed out of sight. At 9:27 p.m., after 27 minutes of pursuit, Gorman gave up the chase and returned to Hector Airport. Jensen watched the object pass overhead through binoculars but could not make out the blinking light that Gorman reported.

## Sworn Testimony

On October 23, 1948, Gorman gave sworn legal testimony about the encounter:

> I am convinced that there was definitely thought behind its maneuvers. I am further convinced that the object was governed by the laws of inertia because its acceleration was rapid but not immediate, and although I was able to turn fairly tight at considerable speed, it still followed a natural curve. When I attempted to turn with the object I blacked out temporarily due to excessive speed. I'm in fairly good physical condition and I do not believe that there are many, if any, pilots who could withstand the turn and speed affected by the object and remain conscious. The object was not only able to outturn and outspeed my aircraft but was able to attain a far steeper climb and was able to maintain a constant rate of climb far in excess of my aircraft.

## Project Sign Investigation

[[Project Sign]] interviewed Gorman and other witnesses and tested his P-51 Mustang for radiation. The aircraft was measurably more radioactive than other fighters, which at first led investigators to conclude that it had flown close to an "atomic powered object." Further analysis attributed the radiation to reduced atmospheric shielding at an altitude of 14,000 feet, not to being near an unusual source.

Project Sign's final conclusion was that Gorman had chased either a lit weather balloon or the planet Jupiter, and that the object's fantastic maneuvers were an illusion created by his own frame of reference inside the moving aircraft. Gerb ranks the dogfight, together with the 1980 [[La Joya Airbase UFO Intercept]], as the most compelling of the four cases in his video.
