Dr. Vannevar Bush was one of the most powerful American science administrators of the twentieth century. He chaired the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA, the predecessor to NASA) from 1939 to 1941, directed the Office of Scientific Research and Development (OSRD) through World War II, and after the war chaired the Joint Research and Development Board and then the [[Research and Development Board]]. Multiple independent sources in the UFO research community name Bush as the leader or a leading member of a small classified US government group tasked with studying recovered UAP craft and non-human materials from the late 1940s into the early 1950s. Gerb goes further: in his reading, Bush built the secrecy, the laboratories and the contracting model on which UFO legacy programs still rest.

## Career

Bush was born on 11 March 1890 in Everett, Massachusetts. An engineer and inventor, he was dean of engineering and vice president at [[MIT]] before leaving in 1939 to become president of the Carnegie Institution of Washington, a post he held until 1955. He joined NACA in 1938 and chaired it from October 1939 to June 1941. In June 1940 President Roosevelt made him chairman of the new National Defense Research Committee, and from June 1941 to the end of 1947 he directed its successor, the OSRD, through which almost all wartime military research ran, including the early Manhattan Project. After the war he chaired the Joint Research and Development Board set up by the Secretaries of War and the Navy in 1946, and from 30 September 1947 to 14 October 1948 its successor, the Research and Development Board of the new National Military Establishment, before leaving full-time government service. He sat on the board of [[AT&T]] from 1947 to 1962. He died in Belmont, Massachusetts, on 28 June 1974.

## Wartime Scientific Leadership

Bush was the chief organizer of the United States' wartime scientific mobilization. The OSRD, which he directed, coordinated the early Manhattan Project, developed radar and oversaw numerous classified weapons programs. His role gave him broad authority over the country's most sensitive scientific programs and direct access to Presidents Roosevelt and Truman.

## The Research and Development Board

After the war, Bush chaired the Joint Research and Development Board, set up in 1946, which the National Security Act of 1947 replaced with the Research and Development Board of the National Military Establishment. He chaired the new board until October 1948. In his Sarbacher video Gerb dates the joint board to 1941 and says it had twelve members, which he suggests may be where the Majestic 12 idea came from. The board's consultants included [[Robert Sarbacher]], and it is consistently cited as the institutional home of early classified UAP research. Sarbacher told both [[Wilbert B. Smith]] (1950) and [[William Steinman]] (1983) that Bush was at the center of the effort: in Smith's memo, a "small group headed by Dr. Vannevar Bush" was making a concentrated effort to understand flying saucers.

## Alleged UAP Involvement

Bush appears in multiple independent lines of UAP testimony and documentation:

- In his November 1950 memo, Smith recorded that his Washington source (Sarbacher) named Bush as the head of the small group studying UFOs within the US government.
- In his 1983 letter to Steinman, Sarbacher wrote that Bush was "definitely involved" in crash retrieval efforts, alongside [[John Von Neumann]] and [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]].
- In 1990, in three recorded calls with Henry Azadehdel, who wrote as Armen Victorian, [[Eric A. Walker]] confirmed Bush's involvement in the UFO program.
- Among the [[Majestic 12]] documents, the [[Eisenhower Briefing Document]] names Bush as a member of the team that President Truman tasked with investigating crashed UAP, and says he directed the Roswell craft and body recovery with General Nathan Twining. Gerb notes that Truman, Forrestal and Bush really did meet on 24 September 1947, the date of its memo authorizing "Operation Majestic 12".
- The [[Bowen Manuscript]], returned through FOIA in 1999, bears marginal notes from about 1962 that Ryan and Robert Wood attribute to Bush, among them "what is Don Menzel doing?" and, beside speculation about American gravity-control craft, "this should not get out".
- In his 1994 statement, which Gerb treats as an "estimate of the situation", former New Mexico state representative [[Andrew Kissner]] said that from 1947 onward, UFOs recovered from Roswell and White Sands were collected, processed and analyzed by teams operating under Bush. Gerb says this independent line of testimony lines up with Sarbacher's disclosures to Smith.
- [[William Steinman]]'s 1986 book *UFO Crash at Aztec* argues that Bush led the scientific team at the 1948 [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|Aztec, New Mexico retrieval]], alongside [[Detlev Bronk]], von Neumann and Oppenheimer. Gerb considers Aztec real but faults Steinman for not documenting his sources. In his Aztec video Gerb also traces the oil-finding "doodlebug" at the heart of the case to Geophysical Service Inc.'s wartime magnetic submarine-detection work under Bush's OSRD.

## National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA)

As chairman of NACA from 1939 to 1941, Bush headed the institution that became NASA in 1958. NACA's mandate was to advance aeronautics, so any anomalous flight characteristics fell squarely within its interest. Kissner's sources name NACA among the bodies that played a major role in the months after the 1947 crashes.

## Assessment

Bush's place in UAP lore comes from the convergent testimony of independent insiders, among them Sarbacher and Walker, who name him as the central scientific authority overseeing the earliest classified US UAP programs, rather than from any direct public statement he made about the subject. He chaired the military's research boards from 1946 to October 1948, the years of the alleged 1947 and 1948 recoveries, and remained a director of AT&T, whose Sandia management Gerb also examines, until 1962.

## Role in the Manhattan Project 2.0 Security Architecture

Gerb's investigation into the [[Manhattan Project 2.0]] identifies Bush as the single most important figure in building the security architecture of early UFO legacy programs. As OSRD director and RDB chairman, Bush built US military-scientific infrastructure while also holding the nation's most sensitive nuclear secrets. He established the OSRD's system of self-censorship and publication control, an informal practice of treating nuclear physics data as inherently secret that the 1946 Atomic Energy Act later formalized as "restricted data". The investigation identifies this system as the direct precursor to the classification system that protects UFO legacy programs.

Bush also invented the FFRDC/GOCO institutional model: government-owned, contractor-operated research labs that attract top scientific talent while the government keeps strict control over materials and deliverables. The investigation describes this model as a core architectural feature of the Manhattan Project 2.0, embodied in Atomic Energy Commission national labs such as [[Sandia National Laboratories]], [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]] and [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]]. Compartmented military GOCO contracts for science and RDT&E exist today because of the groundwork Bush laid. Kissner's sources add that classified Truman executive orders of 1948 vested custody of recovered discs in those AEC laboratories, to be studied under Bush.

Gerb identifies Bush's Research and Development Board (RDB, 1947–1953) as the probable scientific administration hub for crash retrieval and analysis in those years. It was then reorganized into the Assistant Secretary of Defense for Research and Development, the office that now exists as the Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering.

Within the Manhattan Project itself, Bush was a critical member of the Top Policy Group, the tiny civilian-political oversight body made up of Roosevelt (later Truman), Henry Wallace, Henry Stimson, [[George C. Marshall]], Bush and James Conant. Gerb likens it to the UFO control groups established under Truman and Eisenhower within the National Security Council. Bush also helped oversee the wartime [[Alsos Mission|Alsos Missions]], which Gerb calls the direct blueprint for UFO crash retrieval teams.

## Grandfather of MITRE

Gerb's investigation into the [[MITRE Corporation]] describes Bush as MITRE's institutional "grandfather". Bush's National Defense Research Committee established the MIT Radiation Laboratory in 1940. It aided World War II microwave radar development until 1945 and was reborn in 1951 as [[MIT Lincoln Laboratory]], an FFRDC sponsored by the Department of Defense. MIT Lincoln Laboratory in turn spun off MITRE in 1958 to lead systems engineering for the SAGE air defense program, staffing the new organization largely with Lincoln Laboratory research personnel. Gerb further speculates that some of MITRE's alleged reverse-engineering work may trace back to the anti-submarine warfare and sonar detection research done by Bush's OSRD during World War II, and may connect to later Navy-sponsored technology exploitation programs.
