Doug Bower (born 1934) is a British artist and the primary architect of the crop circle phenomenon that dominated UFO discussion in the 1970s and 1980s. With his friend [[Dave Chorley]], he made hundreds of crop circle formations across southern England over about fifteen years, beginning around 1976, before the two confessed publicly in 1991. The confession resolved the central mystery of what had briefly become one of the most discussed alleged paranormal phenomena in the world.

## Background

Bower was inspired by the "saucer nests" reported in Australia in 1966, which were flattened circular patches in crops. Over an evening drink with Chorley, he suggested recreating the effect in English wheat fields as a prank. In the years that followed, the two men refined their technique, moving from simple circles to complex geometric patterns.

## Technique

Bower and Chorley used simple tools: a plank of wood, rope, and a baseball cap fitted with a wire sight for keeping to straight lines. They anchored one end of a rope at a central point and attached the other to the plank, which they used to flatten crops in a controlled arc. They worked at night and were never caught in all the years they were active.

## Confession

In 1991, Bower and Chorley came forward to the British press and demonstrated their technique live. Their confession explained the first wave of formations but did not end the wider phenomenon. Other hoaxers around the world had taken up the practice independently, and a small community of "circlemakers" carried it on as an art form. Even so, the confession effectively ended the credibility of crop circles as possible evidence of extraterrestrial activity.
