Lieutenant Walter Haut (died 2005) was the public relations officer of the [[509th Bomb Group]] at [[Roswell Army Airfield]] in July 1947. On 8 July he issued the press release announcing that the base had recovered a "flying disc," which set off the [[Roswell Crash]] story. He stayed silent for more than four decades. He then signed two affidavits: one in 1993 about the release, and a sealed one in 2002, released in 2007, in which he said he had seen the craft and bodies in [[Hangar 84]]. UAP Gerb ranks him among the best Roswell whistleblowers because he worked directly with Colonel [[William Blanchard]] and General [[General Ramey|Roger Ramey]].

## Background

During the Second World War Haut flew 35 bombing missions over Japan as a bombardier. He took part in the atomic tests at Bikini Atoll and served in the 509th, the unit that dropped the atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

## The press release

According to Haut, Blanchard, the base commander, dictated the release to him almost verbatim from a scratch pad. It said the base had a flying saucer, found on a ranch north-west of Roswell, and that intelligence officer [[Jesse Marcel]] would fly it to General Ramey's office in Fort Worth. Blanchard ordered him to take it to Roswell's two radio stations and its two newspapers, the Daily Record and the Morning Dispatch, "post haste." Haut said the material was loaded onto a C-54, but that while the plane flew, the material did not. Within hours Ramey retracted the story and recast the recovery as a weather balloon. Haut did not believe Blanchard or Marcel could have mistaken the wreckage for one. [[Arthur McQuitty]], managing editor of the Morning Dispatch, signed his own affidavit confirming that Haut delivered the release and that the base later phoned to retract it.

## The 1993 affidavit

Haut signed his first affidavit on 14 May 1993. It covers Blanchard's dictation, the delivery of the release, and his disbelief at the weather balloon explanation. Marcel had admitted the cover-up publicly to [[Stanton Friedman]] in 1978, and privately to Haut in 1980.

## The 2002 affidavit

In 2002 Haut completed a fuller statement, to be released two years after his death. He had sworn to Blanchard never to reveal what he saw in his lifetime. It was released in 2007 and describes:

- **A morning staff meeting on 8 July 1947** with Blanchard, Ramey, Chief of Staff Colonel [[Thomas J. DuBose]] and others. It dealt with the debris field on the [[Foster Ranch]] and a primary crash site about 40 miles north of Roswell. Samples of lightweight, shape-retaining metal with strange markings were passed around.
- **Ramey's diversion plan**: drawing public attention to the debris field to keep it away from the craft site.
- **Hangar 84**: at 9:30 a.m., after dictating the release, Blanchard took Haut to Building 84, a B-29 hangar guarded inside and out. There Haut saw a metallic, egg-shaped craft 12 to 15 feet long and about 6 feet in diameter, with no windows, portholes, wings, landing gear or tail section. UAP Gerb compares it to the craft [[Eric Taber]] described at [[Area 51]]. Under a canvas tarp were a couple of bodies about 4 feet tall with larger-than-normal heads, beside a temporary morgue.
- **Marcel's account**: Marcel told Haut he took wreckage to Ramey's office and came back from a map room to find weather-balloon foil and balsa wood in its place.
