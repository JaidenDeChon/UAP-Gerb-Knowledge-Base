Dr. James T. Ryder (also known as Jim Ryder) was Vice President of [[Lockheed Martin Space Systems|Lockheed Martin Space Systems Company]] and head of its [[Advanced Technology Center (ATC)|Advanced Technology Center]] (ATC), an R&D organization covering advanced aerospace technologies such as optics, electro-optics, nanotechnology, space sciences and guidance systems. He held these positions from 2004 to 2011 and represented about $9 billion a year in program infrastructure, including remote sensing, telecommunications, defensive systems and strategic systems. In research on UFO legacy programs he matters most for his alleged attempts to transfer recovered non-human craft materials, held by [[Lockheed Martin]] since the 1950s, to [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]] under the [[AAWSAP]] program, the second of them through a proposed waived Special Access Program called [[Kona Blue]]. He died in 2018.

## Career and Qualifications

Ryder held a PhD in theoretical and applied mechanics, a master's degree in engineering mechanics, and a bachelor's degree in theoretical and applied mechanics, all from the University of Illinois. He spent almost 40 years at Lockheed and Lockheed Martin. Under his leadership, the Advanced Technology Center (now called STAR) covered phenomenology and sensors, optics and electro-optics, telecommunications and photonics, guidance and navigation, modeling and simulation, materials and structures, thermal sciences, nanotechnology, and space sciences. Gerb says he knows of cases in which Lockheed worked with the Army to apply optical and electro-optical cloaking, derived from metamaterials and non-human technology, to next-generation combat vehicles.

Ryder's early published work in the Defense Technical Information Center archives includes Air Force Wright Aeronautical Laboratories reports on the effect of load history on fatigue life (February 1981) and on stiffness, strength and fatigue life relationships for composite laminates (April 1982), both specialized fields of aerospace engineering.

## Kona Blue Material Transfer Attempts

Between approximately 2008 and 2011, Ryder allegedly led efforts to divest [[Lockheed Martin]] of recovered UFO materials through a technology transfer to [[AAWSAP]], the Defense Intelligence Agency program administered by [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]]. According to documents that Representative [[Tim Burchett]] entered into the record of the November 2024 House Oversight UAP hearing, Ryder proposed a "UAP material divestment plan" to AAWSAP's leadership. The plan described materials from a specific Lockheed Martin facility, including crash retrieval materials from the 1950s and from other historical operations. [[David Grusch]] has described the same episode: Lockheed wanted to divest itself of hull-like pieces recovered "from like the 50s", and the CIA, the other government customer, said no.

The plan proposed a technology transfer agreement into [[Kona Blue]], a prospective waived Special Access Program at the [[DHS (Department of Homeland Security)|Department of Homeland Security]] championed by Senator [[Harry Reid]]; [[Tara O'Toole]] of DHS Science and Technology was approached to set it up. According to investigative reporting by [[Christopher Sharp]], there were two separate attempts to transfer these materials. The documents say the transfer was killed by [[Glenn Gaffney]], Deputy Director of the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]]; Gerb suggests [[Robert Cardillo]], then Deputy Director of National Intelligence for Intelligence Integration, may have been the final authority, and asks whether Lockheed vice president [[Mary K. Sturtevant]] helped block it. A Kona Blue slide deck later declassified by [[AARO]] says the 2011 proposal died because DHS and other agencies doubted the materials were authentic.

Some of Gerb's sources say the materials included craft hull fragments, or possibly the entire recovered craft from the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|1953 Kingman, Arizona crash]]. Gerb believes, with "moderate to high conviction", that the material was part or all of the Kingman craft, that it proved so hard to study that Lockheed had made no headway since the 1950s, and that Ryder may have been "a wild card" who wanted to bring in fresh teams and fresh minds. [[Eric Davis]], who worked with AAWSAP on the transfer, has said that the materials-analysis effort never dealt with propulsion, airframes or biological remains, and that UFO material exploitation programs were shut down in 1989 for lack of progress. Gerb has considered that Ryder fed Davis such lines as counterintelligence or program protection, and reads a 2019 interview, in which Davis describes a vice president with a PhD drawing a researcher in over an informal talk in a SCIF, as Ryder and Lockheed bringing Davis in. In his [[Sandia National Laboratories|Sandia]] video Gerb adds a working theory: the transfer "lit up the CIA's radar" because Lockheed held material that belonged in the custody of an FFRDC, and was trying to pass it to a contractor outside the sanctioned custodial chain.

## Post-Lockheed Work and Esoteric Interests

After retiring from Lockheed Martin, Ryder joined [[Hal Puthoff]] and [[Eric Davis]] on the Starfire project's science review team, which explored the controversial electric Sun hypothesis. Through it he stayed in professional contact with two of the best-known physicists associated with research into UAP legacy programs.

Between 2015 and his death in 2018, Ryder gave a series of talks for the Lucis Trust, an esoteric organization that promotes spiritual principles. The talks touched on UAP phenomena, consciousness, physics and extrasensory perception (ESP). They included:

- "The Rending of the Veils, Part One: Fairies, Devas and Unidentified Aerial Phenomena" (June 2017, London). It discussed UAP as phenomena rather than craft, and referred to cave art, ancient depictions of non-human beings, crop circles, UFO videos and the 1980 Rendlesham Forest incident.
- "The Soul Is Light: What Then Is Light?" (2015). It explored where esotericism meets physics, touching on Big Bang theories, light as both physical and metaphysical, and human consciousness as an interconnected cosmic matrix, and quoted Puthoff.
- "The Garment of God" (2018). It contrasted the suppression of ESP research in the United States with state support for it in Russia. Gerb sets this beside [[Ben Rich]]'s alleged remarks about ESP and interstellar travel, and [[Eric A. Walker]]'s question about access to the programs: "How good is your sixth sense? How much do you know about ESP?"

Gerb sees Ryder's turn toward consciousness and esoteric topics after retirement as part of a pattern among alleged insiders of UAP legacy programs. Army Colonel [[Karl Nell]], for example, spoke about consciousness and engineering at the Archives of the Impossible conference in 2025.

## Named as a Gatekeeper

On 9 September 2025, testifying under oath before the House Oversight Committee, journalist [[George Knapp]] was asked to name gatekeepers "within the root cell of the UAP SAP Federation". He named "Dr. James Ryder at Lockheed", adding that he did not fault the contractors, who are "supposed to lie" and were told to keep quiet primarily by the CIA; Glenn Gaffney of the CIA was named alongside him. Gerb plays the exchange in his Lockheed Martin video. As Vice President of Space Systems and head of the Advanced Technology Center, Ryder would have had oversight authority over Lockheed's most compartmentalized programs, possibly including programs on technologies of unknown origin.

## Death

Ryder died suddenly in 2018, shortly after his last Lucis Trust talks, when he appeared to be actively exploring consciousness, metaphysics and the meeting point of advanced physics and esoteric knowledge. Gerb says he sees nothing suspicious in his death, but notes that it removed a potentially important witness to Lockheed Martin's alleged custody of UAP material.
