Dr. James T. Ryder (also known as Jim Ryder) was Vice President of [[Lockheed Martin]] Space Systems Corporation and head of the Advanced Technology Center (ATC), an R&D organization covering advanced aerospace technologies such as optics, electrooptics, nanotechnology, space sciences and guidance systems. He held these positions from 2004 to 2011 and oversaw approximately $9 billion a year in program infrastructure, including remote sensing, telecommunications, defensive systems and strategic systems. In research on UFO legacy programs he matters most for his alleged attempts to transfer recovered nonhuman craft materials, held by Lockheed Martin since the 1950s, to [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]] under the [[AAWSAP]] program through a proposed waived Special Access Program called [[Kona Blue]].

## Career and Qualifications

Ryder held a PhD in theoretical and applied mechanics, a master's degree in engineering mechanics, and a bachelor's degree in theoretical and applied mechanics, all from the University of Illinois. His nearly 40-year career at Lockheed Martin put him at the center of the corporation's most advanced and classified research programs. Under his leadership, the Advanced Technology Center's research covered phenomenology and sensors, optics and electrooptics, telecommunications and photonics, guidance and navigation, modeling and simulation, materials and structures, thermal sciences, nanotechnology, and space sciences.

Ryder's published work in the Defense Technical Information Center archives includes research on fatigue life relationships for composite laminates and on how load history affects materials. Both are highly specialized fields of aerospace engineering and critical to the design of advanced vehicles.

## Kona Blue Material Transfer Attempts

Between approximately 2008 and 2011, Dr. Ryder allegedly led efforts to divest [[Lockheed Martin]] of recovered UFO materials through a technology transfer to [[AAWSAP]], the Defense Intelligence Agency program administered by [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]]. According to documents that Representative Tim Burchett entered into the record of the November 2024 House Oversight UAP hearing, Ryder proposed a UAP Material Divestment Plan to AAWSAP's leadership. The plan described materials from a specific [[Lockheed Martin]] facility, including crash retrieval materials from the 1950s and from other historical operations.

The plan proposed a technology transfer agreement into the [[Kona Blue]] PAP, a prospective waived Special Access Program championed by Senator [[Harry Reid]]. According to whistleblower testimony and investigative reporting by [[Christopher Sharp]], there were at least two separate attempts to transfer these materials. Both were stonewalled by [[Glenn Gaffney]], Deputy Director of the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]], and possibly by [[Robert Cardillo]], then Deputy Director of National Intelligence for Intelligence Integration.

Some sources suggest the materials included craft hull fragments, or possibly the entire recovered craft from the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona crash]]. [[Eric Davis]] has stated that this batch of materials was so advanced and beyond human comprehension that Lockheed had made no progress in exploiting them since acquiring them in the 1950s. He has also stated that Ryder may have sought fresh minds and the perspectives of new contractors for a reverse-engineering problem Lockheed could not solve.

## Post-Lockheed Work and Esoteric Interests

After retiring from Lockheed Martin, Ryder joined [[Hal Puthoff]] and [[Eric Davis]] on the Starfire Project science review team, which explored the controversial electric sun hypothesis. The work kept Ryder in professional contact with two of the most prominent physicists associated with research into UAP legacy programs.

Between 2015 and his death in 2018, Ryder gave a series of talks at conferences of the Lucis Trust, an esoteric organization that promotes spiritual principles. The talks touched on UAP phenomena, consciousness, physics and extrasensory perception (ESP). They included:

- "The Rendering of the Veils Part One: Fairies, Devas, and Unidentified Aerial Phenomena" (June 2017, London). It discussed UAP as phenomena rather than craft, and referred to cave art, ancient depictions of nonhuman beings, crop circles, UFO videos and the [[1980 Rendlesham Forest incident]].
- "The Soul is Light: What Then is Light?" (2015). It explored where esotericism meets physics, touching on Big Bang theories, light as both physical and metaphysical, and human consciousness as an interconnected cosmic matrix.
- "The Garment of God" (2018). It contrasted the suppression of ESP research in the United States with state support for it in Russia, themes similar to those [[Ben Rich]] allegedly raised about ESP and interstellar travel.

Ryder's turn toward consciousness and esoteric topics after retirement mirrors a pattern seen in other alleged insiders of UAP legacy programs. [[Carl Nell]], for example, has recently explored consciousness at the Archives of the Impossible conference.

## Connection to Wilson-Davis Notes and Program Gatekeeping

In testimony and interviews, [[David Grusch]] publicly named Dr. James T. Ryder as a specific gatekeeper within the UAP SAP Federation at Lockheed Martin. As Vice President of Space Systems and head of the Advanced Technology Center, Ryder would have had oversight authority over Lockheed's most compartmentalized programs, potentially including those related to technologies of unknown origin.

## Death

Dr. James T. Ryder died suddenly in 2018. His death came after his 2017-2018 Lucis Trust lectures, at a time when he appeared to be actively exploring consciousness, metaphysics, and the meeting point of advanced physics and esoteric knowledge. UAP researchers do not suspect foul play, but they note that his death meant the loss of a potential key witness to Lockheed Martin's alleged custody of UAP material.
