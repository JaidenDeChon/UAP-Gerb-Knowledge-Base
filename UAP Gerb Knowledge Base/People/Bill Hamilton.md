William F. "Bill" Hamilton III (born 1943) was an American UFO researcher and the author of *Cosmic Top Secret: America's Secret UFO Program*. Gerb credits him as the first person to publicly use the term "Alien Reproduction Vehicle" (ARV) and to produce an image of one. He passed witnesses including [[Brad Sorenson]], [[Mark McCandlish]] and [[Bill Uhouse]] to researcher [[Steven Greer]], and he compiled some of the earliest records of alleged underground UAP facilities in the American Southwest, above all in California's [[Antelope Valley]]. Gerb's videos refer to him as the late Bill Hamilton.

## ARV Research and *Cosmic Top Secret*

Hamilton produced the first public use of the term ARV and the first public image of the craft, based on what his own informants told him. Both came years before Sorenson and McCandlish described the ARV at Greer's 2001 [[Disclosure Project]] event. Gerb has called *Cosmic Top Secret* "the most underappreciated book in all of UFOlogy" and a book decades ahead of its time. It contains what Gerb calls the first recorded mention of the term "ARV", although Hamilton spelled the acronym out as "alien replicated vehicle", and it discussed the flux liner nearly a decade before Greer's event. Gerb dates the book to 1992; library catalogues list a first edition from Inner Light Publications in 1991.

In the early 1990s Hamilton discussed Sorenson's flux liner testimony with [[William B. Scott]] of [[Aviation Week and Space Technology]]. He also told Greer about the [[Norton Air Force Base Air Show|1988 Norton Air Force Base air show]] incident and passed him both Sorenson and aerospace illustrator Mark McCandlish. "The next time someone tries to say Steven Greer coined the term ARV," Gerb says, "send them this passage." Gerb suggests Hamilton was one of Greer's primary sources of information over the decades, as the Disclosure Project witness [[Arnold House]] may also have been.

## Underground Base Investigation and the Antelope Valley Network

From the late 1980s, Hamilton built up a large network of informants who described alleged underground UFO facilities in the Antelope Valley. Gerb notes that Lockheed's Skunk Works, Rockwell, McDonnell Douglas and Northrop all had plants in [[Palmdale, California|Palmdale]] around [[Air Force Plant 42]]. Since at least 1987 Hamilton had accused the area's radar cross-section facilities, Northrop's [[Tejon Ranch|Tejon]] site, Lockheed's [[Helendale]] site and McDonnell Douglas's, of housing UFO legacy programs. He was most interested in Tejon, in the [[Tehachapi, California|Tehachapi Mountains]], sometimes called the "Anthill" because of its rumoured underground tunnels. His book and an early-1990s VHS lecture, *UFOs and Underground Bases*, gather the accounts. Among them:

- A 1988 sighting of a giant boomerang over the Northrop facility, and a flying saucer seen taking off from a building on the property.
- A local man with property on 170th Street West reported seeing the ground open like a missile silo and a flying saucer take off from it. Edwards [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations|OSI]] later silenced him.
- A contractor named Chuck described digging tunnels beneath the Anthill, with round doorways, few doors, and red and green lights for identification and entry.
- A source who claimed to have worked on "Project Star Talk" at Tejon described lasers in a large underground building, used to "bring in" UFOs by means of scalar technology. He also said he saw a UFO held in an underground hangar at the McDonnell Douglas site.
- An informant known as "Gober" claimed to work at Tejon, Helendale and the Nevada Test and Training Range with alien technology that "deeply frightened him". He said staff rotated every 14 to 16 days, living on site, and that a command center under Haystack Butte connected to the Anthill.
- An unnamed colonel, an aerospace engineer and NASA consultant, said he worked underground at China Lake, [[Edwards Air Force Base]] and the Nevada Test Site, all linked by maglev, and often entered the network through a base beneath White Sands Missile Range.

Several informants described electric ground vehicles and maglev trains inside the bases, linking installations such as Tejon, McDonnell Douglas, Helendale, George AFB, Edwards, the NASA rocket test site and China Lake. From these sources Hamilton concluded that a large complex of underground facilities in the Antelope Valley, all linked by tunnels and transport systems, was engaged in UFO testing, storage and material exploitation. Gerb gives weight to the colonel's account because a witness told him something similar: an Army officer in the 2010s who rode an underground train from a fort in Texas to White Sands.

## The DUMB Network Map

Hamilton's best-known contribution to underground base research is a map of connected underground UFO facilities and transport links across the American West, printed in *Cosmic Top Secret*. A source identified as "TA" gave it to him in 1990, and some of its data comes from Thomas Castello, of the Dulce story. Gerb has overlaid it on the route map in [[Robert M. Salter]]'s 1972 RAND study of a [[Very High Speed Transit System (VHST)|Very High Speed Transit System]] and on a 2019 map of DoD laboratories, and finds that they match to a significant degree, especially along the routes through Arizona and New Mexico. The map marks sites at Edwards, Tejon, Helendale, McDonnell Douglas, George AFB, China Lake, Area 51, Tonopah, Los Alamos, Sandia and Kirtland, Cheyenne Mountain, Fort Huachuca and an installation in Chihuahua, Mexico. Many are labelled R&D sites rather than full underground bases.

[[Richard Sauder]] spoke with Hamilton at length about the map while Hamilton was alive and, at the time of Gerb's DUMBs video, planned to go through it with Gerb in a future project.
