Randy Anderson is a former 18 Bravo US Army Special Forces (Green Beret) weapons sergeant and personal acquaintance of the UAP Gerb host who is cited in connection with alleged off-world technology programs at underground facilities. Anderson's testimony specifically concerns the [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane|Naval Surface Warfare Center (NSWC) Crane]] in Indiana, where he alleged the existence of an "Off-World Technologies Division" housed in an underground facility beneath the base. This testimony is one of the primary sources for UAP Gerb's investigation into the off-world technologies operations at NSWC Crane.

Anderson's earlier characterization as having "seen triangular craft at Area 51" may reflect a separate component of his witness testimony or a misattribution from earlier research — UAP Gerb's DUMBs video places his most significant disclosure at NSWC Crane rather than Area 51.

## NSWC Crane Underground Facility Account

In the early-to-mid 2010s, Anderson was dispatched to NSWC Crane for an advanced weapons training course, where he performed munitions testing involving experimental Russian ballistics and other advanced weaponry. During this assignment, Anderson stated he and another highly cleared Green Beret were taken by an unnamed contractor to an underground location beneath NSWC Crane, to a working site the contractor referred to as the "Off-World Technologies Division."

While underground, Anderson reported observing a spherical object roughly the size of a basketball that was said to interact with consciousness, as well as a gauntlet-type device covered in glyphic writing. Contractor personnel told Anderson the objects had been recovered, and that the gauntlet in particular was considered a weapon due to an energetic discharge that occurred during its recovery. UAP Gerb's host assesses the unnamed contractor was likely [[SAIC]], drawing a connection to SAIC's broader alleged role absorbing legacy reverse-engineering research portfolios.

UAP Gerb has stated that, at the time of conducting its first interview with Anderson, individuals identifying themselves as CIA personnel reached out to warn the host to be wary of discussing NSWC Crane.
