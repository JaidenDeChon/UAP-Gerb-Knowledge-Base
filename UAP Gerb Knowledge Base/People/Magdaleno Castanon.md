Major Magdaleno Castanon is a [[Mexican Air Force]] pilot who took part in the March 5, 2004 [[Mexico UFO Incident]] over the southern state of [[Campeche, Mexico|Campeche]]. During a routine anti-drug trafficking vigilance mission, Castanon and his crew filmed 11 unidentified flying objects with infrared equipment. The incident became one of the most documented military UAP encounters in Mexico.

## The 2004 Campeche Encounter

On March 5, 2004, Castanon's crew was flying routine anti-drug trafficking surveillance over Campeche when they picked up multiple objects on infrared equipment. The lights appeared to fly at altitudes of 3,500 meters and surrounded the military jet during the patrol. The crew filmed the objects for several minutes with the aircraft's infrared systems.

Of the eleven objects detected on infrared, only three appeared on the plane's radar, so a second sensor partly corroborated the infrared detections. Castanon stated that the military jets "chased the lights" during the encounter.

## Assessment of Object Behavior

Castanon commented on the objects' apparent awareness, saying he believed "they could feel we were pursuing them." Gerb quotes the remark in [[Videos/Global Air Force UFO Encounters You've Probably Never Heard of/summary|Global Air Force UFO Encounters You've Probably Never Heard of]]. He says he chooses to believe the military pilots over the skeptics' flare explanation. In his final assessment, though, he concedes that of the four cases he covers, this one has the highest probability of a prosaic explanation.

## Skeptical Explanations

Skeptic [[Michael Shermer]], head of [[Skeptic Magazine]], suggested the lights were burnoff flares from an offshore oil platform in the [[Gulf of Mexico]]. The explanation has been considered but has not been conclusively confirmed. The case remains notable for its multiple infrared detections, the partial radar corroboration, and the Mexican Air Force's willingness to release the footage publicly.
