Timothy S. Cooper is an American UFO researcher, based in Big Bear Lake, California, who received the largest share of the [[MJ-12 Documents]]. Most of the drops Gerb's videos date came in the 1990s, from 1992 to 1999, though the Peru video places them in the 1980s. That made him one of the main channels through which these disputed documents entered the public record. He also recorded one of the last interviews with [[Albert Bruce Collins]], the engineer known as "Barnabas", in 1990. His father, USAF Master Sergeant Harry B. Cooper, received a commendation signed by General [[Curtis LeMay]], which [[Ryan S. Wood]] and Gerb describe as praising his work on the "USAF UFO program".

## The Majestic document leaks

According to [[Ryan S. Wood]], Cooper received Majestic material by several routes: mailings postmarked Sacramento, California; envelopes placed in his mailbox without postage; mailings from the Freedom of Information Act office at Fort Meade, Maryland; and documents marked "-1" and "-2" in the upper corner. From 1992 a source calling himself [[Thomas Cantwheel]] (sometimes written Cantwell) claimed many of the mailbox drops, supplying the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] Report and Field Order, the Hillenkoetter MAJIC memo and the First Annual Report. According to the Aztec video, Cantwheel handed Cooper the IPU report in person in July 1995, while the field order arrived by courier between 1993 and 1996. Cooper investigated Cantwheel and set out his findings in a five-page affidavit shared with [[Stanton Friedman]] and [[Robert Wood]]. A woman calling herself "Selena", said to be Cantwheel's daughter, sent ten further memos and the Twining "White Hot" Report. In June 1999 a third source sent the Burned Memo, which refers to [[John F. Kennedy]] under the code word "Lancer". The same year Cooper obtained the annotated [[Bowen Manuscript]] from the Fort Meade FOIA office.

The dates on which these papers reached Cooper matter to Gerb in later videos. In the Aztec video he notes that the IPU report and field order surfaced long after [[William Steinman]]'s 1986 book, so Steinman cannot have built his Aztec account on them. In the Sandia video he turns the point around: because the IPU papers surfaced in 1995 and [[Andrew Kissner]] finished his research in 1994, the documents may have borrowed from Kissner rather than the other way round.

Cooper's father, Master Sergeant Harry B. Cooper, received a commendation signed by LeMay for his service from September 1957 to June 1963 at [[Ent Air Force Base, Colorado|Ent Air Force Base]]. The citation, as shown in the video, praises his meritorious service as the NCO in charge of printing management and of the camera section of Air Defense Command's publishing division; Wood calls it a commendation for "outstanding work" on the USAF UFO program. Wood believes Harry Cooper photographed UFOs and printed classified material there, and that friends from that work later chose to pass documents to his son.

## Interview with Albert Bruce Collins

On November 20, 1990, Cooper interviewed [[Albert Bruce Collins]] (pseudonym "Barnabas"), a self-described metallurgical engineer who claimed government involvement in UAP technology research. It proved to be one of Collins's final interviews: he died on December 30, 1990, just weeks later. Collins told Cooper he had watched an egg-shaped craft taken into a warehouse at the [[University of California, Berkeley]] in 1947 and his knowledge of multiple alleged joint UAP research programs, including Project Archangel between the CIA, RAND, and Vatican. He also claimed that [[Los Alamos National Laboratory|Los Alamos National Labs]] had been studying green fireballs and strange debris. The crash retrieval researcher [[Leonard Stringfield]] later referenced Cooper's documentation of Collins's testimony in *UFO Crash Retrievals: The Inner Sanctum, Status Report 6* (1991).
