Timothy S. Cooper is an American UFO researcher, based in Big Bear Lake, California, who received the largest share of the [[MJ-12 Documents]]. Where Gerb's videos give dates for the drops, most fall in the 1990s, from 1992 to 1999, though the Peru video places them in the 1980s. He was one of the main routes by which these disputed documents became public. He also recorded one of the last interviews with [[Albert Bruce Collins]], the engineer known as "Barnabas", in 1990. His father, USAF Master Sergeant Harry B. Cooper, received a commendation signed by General [[Curtis LeMay]], which [[Ryan S. Wood]] describes as praising his work on the "USAF UFO program"; the citation itself, as shown in Gerb's video, praises his service in charge of printing management and a camera section at Air Defense Command headquarters and does not mention UFOs.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said Wood and Gerb describe the commendation as praising work on the "USAF UFO program". The phrase is Wood's alone, in Gerb's Ryan Wood video; the citation describes printing-management and camera-section work.

## The Majestic document leaks

According to [[Ryan S. Wood]], Cooper received Majestic material by several routes: mailings postmarked Sacramento, California; envelopes placed in his mailbox without postage; mailings from the Freedom of Information Act office at Fort Meade, Maryland; and documents marked "-1" and "-2" in the upper corner. From 1992 a source calling himself [[Thomas Cantwheel]] (sometimes written Cantwell) claimed many of the mailbox drops, supplying the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] Report and Field Order, the Hillenkoetter MAJIC memo and the First Annual Report.[^gerb-cooper-1980s] According to the Aztec video, Cantwheel handed Cooper the IPU report in person in July 1995, while the field order arrived by courier between 1993 and 1996. Cooper investigated Cantwheel and set out his findings in a five-page affidavit shared with [[Stanton Friedman]] and [[Robert Wood]]. A woman calling herself "Selena", said to be Cantwheel's daughter, sent ten further memos and the Twining "White Hot" Report. In June 1999 a third source sent the Burned Memo, which refers to [[John F. Kennedy]] under the code word "Lancer". The same year Cooper obtained the annotated [[Bowen Manuscript]] from the Fort Meade FOIA office.

The dates on which these papers reached Cooper matter to Gerb in later videos. In the Aztec video he notes that the IPU report and field order surfaced long after [[William Steinman]]'s 1986 book, so Steinman cannot have built his Aztec account on them. In the Sandia video he turns the point around: because the IPU papers surfaced in 1995 and [[Andrew Kissner]] finished his research in 1994, the documents may have borrowed from Kissner rather than the other way round.

Cooper's father, Master Sergeant Harry B. Cooper, received a commendation signed by LeMay for his service from September 1957 to June 1963 at [[Ent Air Force Base, Colorado|Ent Air Force Base]]. The citation, as shown in the video, praises his meritorious service as the NCO in charge of printing management and of the camera section of Air Defense Command's publishing division; Wood calls it a commendation for "outstanding work" on the USAF UFO program. Wood believes Harry Cooper photographed UFOs and printed classified material there, and that friends from that work later chose to pass documents to his son.

## Interview with Albert Bruce Collins

On November 20, 1990, Cooper interviewed [[Albert Bruce Collins]] (pseudonym "Barnabas"), a self-described metallurgical engineer who claimed government involvement in UAP technology research. It was one of Collins's last interviews, as he died weeks later, on December 30, 1990. Collins told Cooper he had watched an egg-shaped craft being taken into a warehouse at the [[University of California, Berkeley]] in 1947, and described what he knew of several alleged joint UAP research programs, including Project Archangel between the CIA, RAND, and Vatican. He also claimed that [[Los Alamos National Laboratory|Los Alamos National Labs]] had been studying green fireballs and strange debris. The crash retrieval researcher [[Leonard Stringfield]] later cited Cooper's record of Collins's testimony in *[[Stringfield Status Reports|UFO Crash Retrievals: The Inner Sanctum, Status Report 6]]* (1991).

## In the NASIC video

In [[Videos/NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering/summary|NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering]], Gerb draws on two Cooper threads. In [[Stringfield Status Reports|Status Report VI]], [[Leonard Stringfield]] describes a 6 April 1991 interview he and Cooper held with "Bob", a pseudonymous retired Air Force master sergeant who said he copied a 50-page report, prepared for Air Materiel Command and the [[Air Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC)|Air Technical Intelligence Center]], on a disc tracked and recovered at White Sands in July 1947 (see [[Twining White Sands Air Accident Report (1947)]]). Gerb then asks why informants approached Cooper, and points to his father. In this video he reads Harry B. Cooper's commendation for 1957 to 1963 as praising film processing and printing that "provided necessary aids in photographic production for intelligence evaluation of gun camera and still photographs requested on call by the Foreign Technology Division and the National Photographic Interpretation Center in their contribution to the Air Force UFO Program". That wording does not appear in the citation as shown in Gerb's Ryan Wood video, described above. Gerb wonders whether Harry Cooper photographed recovered UFOs, and whether people close to him later approached his son and his son's colleagues, such as Stringfield.
