Tim Cooper is an American UFO researcher who received the largest share of the [[MJ-12 Documents]] during the 1990s. That made him one of the main channels through which these disputed but historically significant documents entered the public record. His father, USAF Master Sergeant Harry B. Cooper, received a special citation from General Curtis LeMay praising his work on the "USAF UFO program," and the citation lends indirect credibility to the Cooper family's connection to official UFO investigation programs.

## The Majestic document leaks

According to [[Ryan S. Wood]], Cooper received Majestic material by several routes: mailings postmarked Sacramento, California; envelopes placed in his mailbox without postage; mailings from the Freedom of Information Act office at Fort Meade, Maryland; and documents marked "-1" and "-2" in the upper corner. From 1992 a source calling himself [[Thomas Cantwheel]] claimed many of the mailbox drops, supplying the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] Report and Field Order, the Hillenkoetter MAJIC memo and the First Annual Report. Cooper investigated Cantwheel and set out his findings in a five-page affidavit shared with [[Stanton Friedman]] and [[Robert Wood]]. A woman calling herself "Selena", said to be Cantwheel's daughter, sent ten further memos and the Twining "White Hot" Report. In June 1999 a third source sent the Burned Memo, which refers to [[John F. Kennedy]] under the code word "Lancer". The same year Cooper obtained the annotated [[Bowen Manuscript]] from the Fort Meade FOIA office.

Cooper's father, Master Sergeant Harry B. Cooper, received a commendation signed by General [[Curtis LeMay]] for his service from September 1957 to June 1963 at [[Ent Air Force Base, Colorado|Ent Air Force Base]]. There he was the NCO in charge of printing management and of the camera section of Air Defense Command's publishing division. Wood believes Harry Cooper photographed UFOs and printed classified material there, and that friends from that work later chose to pass documents to his son.

## Interview with Albert Bruce Collins

On November 20, 1990, Cooper interviewed [[Albert Bruce Collins]] (pseudonym "Barnabas"), a self-described metallurgical engineer who claimed government involvement in UAP technology research. It proved to be one of Collins's final interviews: he died on December 30, 1990, just weeks later. Collins disclosed to Cooper observations of a UAP at UC Berkeley in 1947 and his knowledge of multiple alleged joint UAP research programs, including Project Archangel between the CIA, RAND, and Vatican. He also claimed that [[Los Alamos National Labs]] had been studying green fireballs and strange debris. The crash retrieval researcher [[Leonard Stringfield]] later referenced Cooper's documentation of Collins's testimony in *UFO Crash Retrievals: The Inner Sanctum, Status Report 6* (1991).
