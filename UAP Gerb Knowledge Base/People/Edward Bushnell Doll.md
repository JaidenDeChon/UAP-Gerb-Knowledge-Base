Edward Bushnell Doll (1912–1998), known as Ed Doll, held a doctorate in electrical engineering and served on the [[Manhattan Project]]'s [[Project Alberta]], which was responsible for weaponizing and delivering the atomic bombs dropped on Japan in 1945. After the war, Doll chaired the physics department at Stanford University and was a senior member of the Stanford Research Institute (SRI, later SRI International) before joining [[Space Technology Laboratories (STL)]] in 1955. STL became TRW Systems Group, and Doll stayed with [[TRW]] until he retired in 1977 as executive vice president of TRW Systems Group. His career covered the early decades of America's covert aerospace and weapons programs, from nuclear weapons science and early rocketry to, according to UAP researchers, the clandestine exploitation of non-human intelligence.

## Career

Doll was born in San Diego, California, on 24 September 1912. He took an M.S. (1935) and a Ph.D. (1938) at Caltech. In March 1945 he joined Project Alberta as leader of its fusing team. Gerb's video *Northrop Grumman & TRW* pieces together the rest of his career from newspaper clippings and trade articles:

- After the war: chairs the physics department at Stanford University and is a senior member of SRI.
- 27 March 1953: SRI supplies scientific help for the spring atomic tests, and Doll directs a military effects group for [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]], a joint DoD and Atomic Energy Commission series.
- 1955: joins Space Technology Laboratories, the TRW subsidiary (later division) that led the first US ICBM and Titan programs.
- 1959: elected STL vice president; also serves as technical director for two major special weapons programs of the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)]].
- 1966: senior vice president of TRW Systems Group, the renamed STL, which ran TRW's entire electronics and defense sector. Its units included Special Projects, which built signals-intelligence satellites for NRO Program B, operated with the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]] from [[Pine Gap, Australia]].
- 1971-1977: group assistant general manager on TRW's corporate side, retiring as executive vice president of TRW Systems Group. (In the video's outro Gerb gives the retirement year as 1979.)

He died on 17 February 1998.

## Role in UAP Programs

Doll allegedly coordinated the personnel sent to the site of a crashed unknown vehicle near [[Kingman, Arizona]] on 21 May 1953. [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], an engineer summoned to the crash site, named Doll as the man who recruited him for the assignment. Stansel gave his account to researcher [[Raymond Fowler]] in 1973 under the pseudonym Fritz Werner. Stansel was an engineer with the [[Air Research and Development Command (ARDC)|Air Research and Development Command]], under which Gerb thinks crash retrieval was partly housed. Gerb's 2024 video on the case calls Doll Upshot-Knothole's project director and a physicist at the Nevada Test Site, and notes that he died long ago without ever saying anything about the alleged retrieval. The recovered materials and program responsibilities allegedly passed through TRW and eventually, after the 2002 acquisition, to [[Northrop Grumman]].

Gerb quotes Stansel's diary entry for 20 May 1953: "Spent most of the day on Frenchman's Flat surveying cubicles and supervising welding of plate rider bridge sensors which cracked after last shot. Drank brew in eve, read, got funny call from Dr. Doll about 1000. I'm going on a special job tomorrow." The entry itself records only the call. In his 2026 timeline video Gerb goes further in his own words, saying that Doll "was directly in charge of the UFO crash retrieval". (That video's auto-captions render Doll's surname as "Dah".) SRI, where Doll was a senior member, separately supplied scientific assistance to the spring 1953 atomic test series that included Operation Upshot-Knothole.

## From Kingman to Northrop Grumman

The *Northrop Grumman & TRW* video argues that Doll carried crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering expertise from joint Atomic Energy Commission and DoD task forces into TRW Systems Group, whose units now form [[Northrop Grumman]]'s Mission Systems and Space Systems sectors. "Whatever secret legacy information, materials, and projects Dr. Doll took to TRW Systems Group," Gerb says, is now owned by Northrop Grumman. He notes that Doll drew little public attention compared with [[Eric Henry Wang]], who died in 1960, while Doll "lived a long and fruitful life". He says researcher [[Raymond Fowler]] tried and failed to find Doll while [[Bill Moore]] ran interference. At the end of the video he calls Doll "a perfect example" of how 1950s crash retrieval figures can lead to actionable study of present-day organizations.

## AFSWP Connection

In 1953 Doll was sent to work on the nuclear tests of Operation Upshot-Knothole under a joint DoD and Atomic Energy Commission task force overseen by the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] (AFSWP). Gerb's *Manhattan Project 2.0* describes him there as chairman of the Stanford Research Institute; his Northrop video calls him a senior member of SRI and director of a military effects group. The AFSWP was the joint Army-Navy successor to the Manhattan Project's military nuclear functions, established by Secretary of War Patterson and Secretary of the Navy [[James Forrestal]] in 1947. The Kingman retrieval allegedly took place while the Upshot-Knothole series was under way, and Gerb wonders whether AFSWP also held compartmented crash retrieval roles and people like Doll. His presence there is cited as evidence that the Manhattan Project's security apparatus and UFO crash retrieval activities were joined at the organizational level.
