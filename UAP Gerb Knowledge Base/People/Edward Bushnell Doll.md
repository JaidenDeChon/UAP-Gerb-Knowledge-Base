Edward Bushnell Doll (1912–1998) held a doctorate in electrical engineering and served on the [[Manhattan Project]]'s Project Alberta, which was responsible for the weaponization and delivery of the atomic bombs dropped on Japan in 1945. Following the war, Doll served as chairman of the physics department at Stanford University and as a senior member of the Stanford Research Institute (SRI, later SRI International) before joining [[TRW]] Systems Group (via its predecessor, Space Technology Laboratories), where he worked from approximately 1955 until the late 1970s, ultimately becoming senior vice president of TRW Systems Group. His career spanned the critical early decades of America's covert aerospace and weapons programs, placing him at the intersection of nuclear weapons science, early rocketry, and — according to UAP researchers — clandestine non-human intelligence exploitation.

## Role in UAP Programs

Doll is alleged to have coordinated personnel to the site of a crashed unknown vehicle near [[Kingman, Arizona]] on 21 May 1953, under the authority of the Air Research and Development Command. [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], an engineer summoned to the crash site, named Doll as the individual who recruited him for the assignment. According to Stansel's account — disclosed under the pseudonym Fritz Werner to researcher [[Raymond Fowler]] in 1973 — Doll directed a team of specialists to evaluate the recovered craft, with the recovered materials and program responsibilities allegedly passing through TRW and eventually to [[Northrop Grumman]] following the 2002 acquisition.

Stansel's personal diary from the period, quoted by UAP Gerb (whose auto-captions render Doll's surname as "Dah"), records a more direct claim of Doll's authority over the retrieval: "Dr. Dah was directly in charge of the UFO crash retrieval." The same entry describes Doll placing a call to Stansel about an upcoming "special job" — consistent with Stansel's affidavit account of being summoned to the Kingman site the following day. SRI, where Doll served as a senior member, separately supplied scientific assistance to the Spring 1953 atomic test series that included Operation Upshot-Knothole.

## AFSWP Connection

In 1953, Doll was sent as chairman of the Stanford Research Institute (SRI International) to operate on Operation Upshot Knothole's nuclear tests under a joint DoD and Atomic Energy Commission task force overseen by the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] (AFSWP). The AFSWP was the joint Army-Navy successor to the Manhattan Project's military nuclear functions, established by Secretary of War Patterson and Secretary of the Navy [[James Forrestal]] in 1947. Doll's presence on an AFSWP-overseen task force at Operation Upshot Knothole — where the Kingman crash retrieval allegedly occurred — is cited as evidence of the organizational bridge between the Manhattan Project security apparatus and UFO crash retrieval activities.
