Edward Bushnell Doll (1912–1998) held a doctorate in electrical engineering and served on the [[Manhattan Project]]'s Project Alberta, which was responsible for weaponizing and delivering the atomic bombs dropped on Japan in 1945. After the war, Doll chaired the physics department at Stanford University and was a senior member of the Stanford Research Institute (SRI, later SRI International) before joining [[Space Technology Laboratories (STL)]] in 1955. STL became TRW Systems Group, and Doll stayed with [[TRW]] until he retired in 1977 as executive vice president of TRW Systems Group. His career covered the early decades of America's covert aerospace and weapons programs. It took in nuclear weapons science and early rocketry and, according to UAP researchers, the clandestine exploitation of non-human intelligence.

## Role in UAP Programs

Doll allegedly coordinated the personnel sent to the site of a crashed unknown vehicle near [[Kingman, Arizona]] on 21 May 1953, under the authority of the Air Research and Development Command. [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], an engineer summoned to the crash site, named Doll as the man who recruited him for the assignment. Stansel gave his account to researcher [[Raymond Fowler]] in 1973 under the pseudonym Fritz Werner. According to it, Doll directed a team of specialists to evaluate the recovered craft. The recovered materials and program responsibilities allegedly passed through TRW and eventually, after the 2002 acquisition, to [[Northrop Grumman]].

Gerb quotes Stansel's personal diary from the period, which makes a more direct claim about Doll's authority over the retrieval: "Dr. Dah was directly in charge of the UFO crash retrieval." (The video's auto-captions render Doll's surname as "Dah".) The same entry describes Doll calling Stansel about an upcoming "special job", which is consistent with Stansel's affidavit account of being summoned to the Kingman site the following day. SRI, where Doll was a senior member, separately supplied scientific assistance to the Spring 1953 atomic test series that included Operation Upshot-Knothole.

## Career at STL and TRW

Gerb's video *Northrop Grumman & TRW* pieces together Doll's corporate career from newspaper clippings and trade articles:

- 1955: joins Space Technology Laboratories, the TRW subsidiary (later division) that led the first US ICBM and Titan programs.
- 1959: elected STL vice president; also serves as technical director for two major special weapons programs of the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)]].
- 1966: senior vice president of TRW Systems Group, the renamed STL, which ran TRW's entire electronics and defense sector. Its units included Special Projects, which built signals-intelligence satellites for NRO Program B, operated with the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]] from [[Pine Gap, Australia]].
- 1971-1977: group assistant general manager on TRW's corporate side, retiring as executive vice president of TRW Systems Group.

The video argues that Doll carried crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering expertise from joint Atomic Energy Commission and DoD task forces into TRW Systems Group, whose units now form [[Northrop Grumman]]'s Mission Systems and Space Systems sectors. Gerb notes that Doll drew little public attention compared with [[Eric Henry Wang]]. He says researcher [[Raymond Fowler]] tried and failed to find Doll while Bill Moore ran interference. At the end of the video he calls Doll "a perfect example" of how 1950s crash retrieval figures can lead to actionable study of present-day organizations.

## AFSWP Connection

In 1953, Doll was sent as chairman of the Stanford Research Institute (SRI International) to work on the nuclear tests of Operation Upshot Knothole, under a joint DoD and Atomic Energy Commission task force overseen by the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] (AFSWP). The AFSWP was the joint Army-Navy successor to the Manhattan Project's military nuclear functions, established by Secretary of War Patterson and Secretary of the Navy [[James Forrestal]] in 1947. Doll served on a task force overseen by AFSWP at Operation Upshot Knothole, where the Kingman crash retrieval allegedly occurred. His presence there is cited as evidence of the organizational bridge between the Manhattan Project security apparatus and UFO crash retrieval activities.
