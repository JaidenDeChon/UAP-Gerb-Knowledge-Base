Dr. Eric Davis is an American astrophysicist and UAP researcher best known for his role in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]]. The memo purports to record an October 16, 2002 conversation between Davis and Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] about a classified UFO crash retrieval and reverse engineering program, which Wilson discovered in 1997 and was then denied access to. It has become one of the most significant documents in the modern debate over UAP disclosure.

## Career

Davis took a BSc in physics and mathematics in 1983 and a PhD in astrophysics in 1991, both at the University of Arizona, where he worked on data from the Voyager missions to Jupiter. According to his published biography he was the staff aerospace and astrophysics researcher at Robert Bigelow's National Institute for Discovery Science in Las Vegas from 1996 to 2002. He has since worked as a research physicist at [[EarthTech|EarthTech International]], [[Hal Puthoff]]'s company in Austin (Gerb's first video calls him its chief science officer), ran his own firm, Warp Drive Metrics, under which he wrote the Air Force Research Laboratory's 2004 *Teleportation Physics Study*, and later worked at the [[Aerospace Corporation]]. He was a science adviser to the Pentagon's [[AAWSAP]] and [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]] programs and to the [[UAP Task Force]].

## Studies for the Pentagon

In 2018 journalist [[George Knapp]] leaked reports of 38 studies commissioned under the Pentagon's UAP program (AAWSAP, which Gerb's video calls AATIP). [[John Greenewald]] of The Black Vault later confirmed them through a 2019 Freedom of Information Act request. Among the studies were research proposals by Eric Davis titled "Traversible Wormholes, Stargates and Negative Energy," "Anti-Gravity for Aerospace Applications," and "Concepts for Extracting Energy from the Quantum Vacuum". These papers show that Davis has worked at the cutting edge of advanced science, on topics with potential applications to UAP propulsion and physics.

## Investigation of Crash Retrieval Programs

In 2002 Davis was under contract to the Department of Defense and had been investigating a UFO crash retrieval program embedded within the United States government. The investigation led him to seek a meeting with Vice Admiral Thomas Wilson, who had recently retired as Deputy Director and Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency.

[[Oak Shannon]], a former manager of special projects at [[Los Alamos National Laboratories]] and a former Department of Energy nuclear physicist, arranged the meeting. Shannon asked Wilson to speak to Davis about what Wilson had told [[Will Miller]] about crashed or retrieved UFO craft.

Before the meeting with Wilson, Davis had received a letter from Commander Will Miller offering to research crash retrieval programs under contract. Miller claimed to know of special crash retrieval teams (possibly projects code-named [[Project Moon Dust|Moon Dust]], [[Blue Fly]], or [[Zodiac]]), a senior officer with firsthand knowledge of [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicles]], and civilian contractors who knew about "alien-derived technologies, crashes, landings, and associated events."

## The October 16, 2002 Wilson Meeting

On October 16, 2002, Davis met Wilson in Wilson's car outside the defense contractor EG&G. There Wilson allegedly described how in 1997 he had investigated special access programs, found a crash retrieval program run by an aerospace contractor, and met the program's [[Watch Committee]], and how he was then denied access even though he was Deputy Director of the DIA.

According to the 15-page memo documenting the meeting, Wilson told Davis the program had "a craft, an intact craft they believed could fly" that was "not of this Earth, not made by man, not by human hands." Wilson said the information was extremely sensitive and told Davis that if Davis ever violated his trust, Wilson would "deny the meeting, deny everything said in the meeting, and would never seek to know more or talk more on this topic without clearance."

Davis ended the meeting by promising to keep it private, for his personal use only, and never to discuss it without clearance.

## Public Statements on the Memo

In a segment of The Basement Office with [[Steven Greenstreet]] that has since been deleted, Davis cited security clearances and refused to comment directly on whether the meeting took place:

"I can't discuss that... I'm not at Liberty to discuss it... They were leaked out of Ed Mitchell's estate. There's nothing I can say about it... I can't address that at all. I won't answer any questions on the Admiral Wilson notes... When you have security clearances that's something you don't want to violate because the Department of Justice under the Obama Administration and continued under the Trump Administration policies is they will vigorously prosecute anybody with security clearances who will go out of the way to discuss any classified information that got leaked or released into the public illegally."

Davis would not confirm or deny the meeting itself, but he did confirm that the notes were leaked from [[Edgar Mitchell]]'s estate. Many researchers read his refusal to deny the meeting, on the grounds of his security clearances, as implicit confirmation that the meeting was authentic. Their reasoning is that if the meeting never occurred, Davis could simply say so without violating any clearances.

## Del Rio Endorsement

In June 2018 journalist George Knapp interviewed Davis on *Coast to Coast AM*. Speaking about UFO crash retrievals, Davis said: "Yeah, they've got—I would say, you know, if you're going to throw your bets on Roswell, your bets really good. Del Rio, Texas—that was a 1950s case. That was another one. And the other ones I won't bring up because those are still classified." Gerb cites this endorsement of the [[Del Rio, Texas]] region as a high-credibility crash retrieval case as context for the 1950 and 1955 incidents.

## Comments on the Kona Blue / James Ryder Materials Program

Davis has separately discussed the compartmented [[Lockheed Martin]] program associated with the late [[James T. Ryder]] and the attempted [[Kona Blue]] material transfer. He said that the locked program under Ryder dealt strictly with materials and metallurgy, reverse-engineering only specific metals, alloys, and metamaterials, and not intact craft or propulsion and power systems. He added that its attempts to replicate them at scale had been largely fruitless. According to Gerb, the engineers in this compartmented program were reportedly not told where the materials they were studying really came from.

Davis has also said that, to his knowledge, nothing has ever been successfully derived from recovered UFO materials, let alone a functioning alien reproduction vehicle (ARV). Gerb has publicly disputed this claim as inconsistent with his own research into decades of alleged reverse-engineering efforts.

## Aerospace Corporation Research

Davis, together with [[Jacques Vallee]], [[Hal Puthoff]], [[Kit Green]], and Kristen B. Zimmerman, identified the [[Aerospace Corporation]] as one of the legacy private corporations suspected of covert UAP research. The group's analysis, recorded in Vallee's journals for 2000 to 2009, *Forbidden Science 5* (Gerb's video calls it *Hidden Science 5*), adds context to the corporation's documented work with [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites and [[Fast Walkers]] detection systems.

## Gerb's Disputes with Davis

Gerb still treats the core of the Wilson-Davis notes as true, but by 2026 he describes his trust in Davis as "wafer-thin", while saying he still listens to everything Davis says. The friction is over Davis's later statements. At the 2024 [[Sol Foundation]] conference, and again in an interview with Gerb, Davis said that much of his concrete information came from the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]], that no reproduction vehicles have ever been built, and that progress was limited to advanced materials. In the same interview he told Gerb that UFO material exploitation programs were shut down in 1989 for lack of progress. Gerb reads these statements as deliberate program protection, possibly mandated by AFRL, and as inconsistent with Lockheed Martin's attempt to divest itself of recovered materials between 2008 and 2011.

Davis has also pointed Gerb's research in several directions. He described an unnamed 1960 spin-off of [[TRW]], which Gerb identifies as the Aerospace Corporation, as involved in UFO programs, with TRW as "principal investigator" for about 29 years; he later said one of his main sources was a woman at TRW. Vallee's journals record him describing the rumored [[Operation Zodiac|Zodiac]] program and a ten-volume binder at Wright-Patterson. He has said that UFO legacy programs use sole-source contracts, and, according to Vallee, he heard George H. W. Bush say that [[Philip J. Corso]] could not have mistaken Nazi hardware for recovered technology.
