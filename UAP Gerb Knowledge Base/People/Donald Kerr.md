Donald M. Kerr (d. 2021) was a senior U.S. intelligence and national laboratory official who held an unusually long sequence of top-tier science, technology, and intelligence leadership roles across four decades. Holding a Ph.D. in plasma physics and microwave electronics (1966), Kerr served as director of Los Alamos National Laboratory (1979–1985), president of EG&G (1989–2002), executive vice president and director of SAIC (1993–1997), deputy director of the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology (2001–2005), director of the National Reconnaissance Office (NRO, 2005–2007), and Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence (2007–2009) under Director of National Intelligence Mike McConnell. UAP Gerb identifies Kerr as one of the most senior alleged UFO legacy program figures of his era — describing him, using Majestic 12 terminology, as an "MJ-1" equivalent — and notes that, before his death, Kerr contributed to public transparency on the topic.

## MITRE Corporation Chairmanship

Following his federal service, Kerr served as trustee and chairman of the [[MITRE Corporation]] board from 2009 until his death in 2021. UAP Gerb highlights the significance of a career trajectory running from Los Alamos, through the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology and the NRO directorship, to the Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence position, and finally to the MITRE board chairmanship — arguing this pattern of movement from the most technically sensitive positions in U.S. intelligence into senior leadership at MITRE is consistent with the channel's broader thesis that MITRE has served for decades as the R&D lead on classified UFO reverse-engineering and USO-monitoring programs administered by the U.S. Navy.
