Tara O'Toole is an American physician and biosecurity specialist who served as Under Secretary for Science and Technology at the [[DHS (Department of Homeland Security)|Department of Homeland Security]] from 2009 to 2013. In that role she was approached to establish [[Kona Blue]], the prospective special access program meant to receive recovered non-human material from [[Lockheed Martin]].

## Kona Blue

According to the declassified [[AARO]] history of Kona Blue, O'Toole approved the proposal as a DHS prospective special access program in 2011, and it was disapproved about six months later. DHS's own records, the [[Kona Blue Documents]] that AARO posted in April 2024, give the months: she was presented with the program in May 2011 and established it on 11 July 2011, and it was terminated in February 2012. A document cleared by the Defense Office of Prepublication and Security Review and entered into the record of the November 2024 House Oversight hearing by Representative [[Tim Burchett]] likewise says she was approached to set up the program, into which Lockheed vice president [[James T. Ryder]] proposed transferring 1950s crash retrieval material. AARO's report says DHS leadership rejected the proposal "for lacking merit", and DHS's own termination memo records that Deputy Secretary Jane Holl Lute ended it after a December 2011 review that asked whether its justifications as a SAP, and its budget and personnel requirements, were adequate. Nothing was transferred. Gerb summarizes the official reason as skepticism about the materials' authenticity, and his Lockheed video attributes the program's end to the CIA's [[Glenn Gaffney]].

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions gave May 2011, July 2011 and February 2012 as AARO's own dates and said AARO blamed doubts about the materials' authenticity. The months come from DHS's records in the Kona Blue Documents (Joel Wall's 11 July 2011 memo bears her approval), and the authenticity point is Gerb's summary: AARO's report (page 35) says the proposal lacked merit, and the DHS memos cite its justification, budget and personnel requirements.
