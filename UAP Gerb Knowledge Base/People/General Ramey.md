Brigadier General Roger M. Ramey, commander of the Eighth Air Force in 1947, was a United States Army Air Forces officer (the Air Force became a separate service that September) who played a central role in the public suppression of the 1947 Roswell incident. He appeared in photographs alongside debris that the military said was a weather balloon, and at a press conference in Fort Worth he gave the weather-balloon identification that replaced Roswell Army Air Field's "flying disc" press release.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions called him a US Air Force officer who issued the official press release. In July 1947 he served in the Army Air Forces, and the "flying disc" press release came from Roswell Army Air Field; Ramey's contribution was the balloon identification.

## Roswell Cover-Up

Ramey is best known for personally managing the public account of events after the initial Roswell Army Air Field press release in July 1947. He identified the recovered material as a weather balloon, held a press conference in Fort Worth, and was photographed holding what appeared to be foil and debris. Researchers widely believe this debris was substituted for the actual material. In one photograph he holds a message that has been partially analyzed and interpreted as referring to "victims of the wreck" and a shipment to "Fort Worth."

## December 6, 1950 UFO Alert

On December 6, 1950, at 10:30 AM Eastern Time, a nationwide US military alert was called, warning of a "large number of unidentified aircraft approaching northeastern US at high altitude." Ramey cancelled the alert at 1:16 PM. The alleged [[Majestic 12]] Eisenhower Briefing Document gives the same date for a UAP crash at [[El Indio, Texas]], and Gerb notes the coincidence as contextual evidence supporting the El Indio case.

## 1952 Washington DC UFO Sightings

After the major 1952 UFO sightings over Washington, DC, Ramey and General Samford jointly issued a press release that attributed the radar returns and visual sightings to atmospheric temperature inversions. Pilots and radar operators who witnessed the events widely criticized this explanation.
