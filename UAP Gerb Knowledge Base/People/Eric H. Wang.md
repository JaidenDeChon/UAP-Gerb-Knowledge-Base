Eric H. Wang (also referred to as Eric Henry Wang) is a historical figure in UFO legacy program research discussions, described by the presenter of "The Hidden Wing" as a key yet relatively obscure character in the early organizational history of Air Force UAP-related programs. Wang had documented ties to the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]] at [[Wright Patterson Air Force Base]] and [[Kirtland Air Force Base]], and is alleged to have had involvement with the research and development directorate of the [[Air Force Special Weapons Center (AFSWC)]]. He is considered notable in UAP research circles for his possible connection to early material exploitation efforts following the recovery of non-human craft. The files for "Eric H. Wang" and "Eric Henry Wang" refer to the same individual.

## Role in UAP Programs

Wang is cited by prior witnesses — most notably in accounts connected to the 1953 Kingman, Arizona crash retrieval — as a key figure involved in overseeing or participating in reverse engineering and material exploitation efforts on recovered non-human vehicles. The presenter contextualizes Wang within the organizational lineage running from the [[Air Force Special Weapons Center (AFSWC)]] through its 1976 dissolution and transfer of responsibilities to the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]], arguing that Wang represents an early node in a continuous institutional chain for managing UAP legacy programs.
