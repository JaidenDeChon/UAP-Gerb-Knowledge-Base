Ellen M. Lord is a former US defense official and industry executive. From 2018 to 2021 she was the first [[Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment (USD A&S)|Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment]], a post that followed the split of the Under Secretary for Acquisition, Technology and Logistics into two offices. Before entering government she was chief executive of Textron Systems. Gerb's interest in her rests on two things: the Corporate Portfolio Program she set up in 2020, and her move to an advisory board at [[SAIC]] the year after she left the Pentagon.

## Career

Lord studied chemistry at Connecticut College and took a master's degree in chemistry at the University of New Hampshire. She spent more than thirty years in the defense industry, much of it at Textron, where she ran AAI Corporation and Textron Defense Systems before serving as president and chief executive of Textron Systems from October 2012 to June 2017. She was sworn in on 10 August 2017 as Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition, Technology and Logistics. When that office was split on 1 February 2018 she became the first Under Secretary for Acquisition and Sustainment, and she held the post until the change of administration on 20 January 2021. In April 2021 she was named a senior fellow at the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory, and in July 2021 SAIC named her one of the three founding members of its new Strategic Advisory Board, with David Norquist and Dana Deasy.

## Alleged Role in UAP Programs

Gerb credits Lord with establishing the Department of Defense's corporate portfolio program in 2020. He says it gives defense-industrial-base corporations access to DoD executives, scientists, [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)|FFRDCs]] and management with broad access to special access program portfolios. He calls it a major milestone in the evolution of the UFO legacy programs because it tightened the bond between program administrators, FFRDCs and contractors. Gerb also lists her among thirteen alleged gatekeepers with NRO-adjacent ties, because of the office she held. That office descends from the acquisition under secretary's office named in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] and is today the [[Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment (USD A&S)]], which shares milestone authority over acquisitions by the NRO (National Reconnaissance Office).

## SAIC Strategic Advisory Board

After leaving the Pentagon in 2021, Lord joined the Strategic Advisory Board of [[SAIC]]. In his SAIC video Gerb dates the move to July 2021 and calls it "an almost comical sequence of events"; in his [[Lockheed Martin]] video he sums it up as Lord expanding defense contractors' access to SAP scientists, SAP security and DoD assets before she "ran off" to SAIC. In his video *[[Off-World Technologies Division – UAP Technology Reverse Engineering]]*, Gerb links this move to the Corporate Portfolio Program she created. SAIC is eligible for the program, and Gerb argues that it gives the company access to DoD special access program portfolios. He places that access alongside SAIC's large contracts at [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane]], the alleged home of the [[Off-World Technologies Division]]. They include a 2020 award of $133 million that reaches Crane's special capabilities branch.
