Ellen M. Lord is a former US defense official and industry executive. From 2018 to 2021 she was the first Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment, a post that followed the split of the Under Secretary for Acquisition, Technology and Logistics into two offices. Before entering government she was chief executive of Textron Systems.

## Alleged Role in UAP Programs

Gerb credits Lord with establishing the Department of Defense's corporate portfolio program in 2020. He says it gives defense-industrial-base corporations access to DoD executives, scientists, [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)|FFRDCs]] and management with broad access to special access program portfolios. He calls it a major milestone in the evolution of the UFO legacy programs because it tightened the bond between program administrators, FFRDCs and contractors. Gerb also lists her among thirteen alleged gatekeepers with NRO-adjacent ties, because of the office she held. That office descends from the acquisition under secretary's office named in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] and is today the [[Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment (USD A&S)]], which shares milestone authority over acquisitions by the NRO (National Reconnaissance Office).

## SAIC Strategic Advisory Board

After leaving the Pentagon in 2021, Lord joined the Strategic Advisory Board of [[SAIC]]. In his video *[[Off-World Technologies Division – UAP Technology Reverse Engineering]]*, Gerb links this move to the Corporate Portfolio Program she created. SAIC is eligible for the program, and Gerb argues that it gives the company access to DoD special access program portfolios. He places that access alongside SAIC's large contracts at [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane]], the alleged home of the [[Off-World Technologies Division]]. They include a 2020 award of $133 million that reaches Crane's special capabilities branch.
