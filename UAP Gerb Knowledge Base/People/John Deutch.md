John M. Deutch is an American physical chemist and former senior U.S. national security official. In quick succession in the 1990s he was Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology (1993–1994), Deputy Secretary of Defense (1994–1995) and Director of Central Intelligence (1995–1996). Gerb's videos, and their captions, spell his name "Deutsch". Gerb cites him for two reasons. As Deputy Secretary he chaired the [[SAPOC (Special Access Program Oversight Committee)|SAPOC]] and its Senior Review Group at the Pentagon, and he had ties to [[SAIC]], [[TRW]], [[Los Alamos National Laboratory|Los Alamos]] and the [[MITRE Corporation]]. Gerb uses those ties to argue that the movement of people between contractor leadership and top government oversight roles is a recurring feature of alleged legacy-program gatekeeping. According to the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], Deutch threatened Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]]'s career when Wilson complained in 1997 about being denied access to a crash retrieval program.

## Career

Deutch was born in Brussels, Belgium, on 27 July 1938. He took a B.A. in history and economics at Amherst College, then a B.S. in chemical engineering and a Ph.D. in physical chemistry at [[MIT]], where he has taught since 1970 and served as chairman of the chemistry department, dean of science and provost. From 1977 to 1980 he held a series of posts at the [[Department of Energy]]: director of energy research, acting assistant secretary for energy technology, and under secretary. In the Clinton administration he was Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology from April 1993, Deputy Secretary of Defense from March 1994, and Director of Central Intelligence at the [[CIA]] from May 1995 to December 1996. He has also sat on the boards of several companies, SAIC among them.

## Threats Against Wilson

At the end of May 1997, the program's [[Watch Committee]] refused to read in Thomas Wilson, whom the notes describe as deputy director of the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]] (DIA), to a crash retrieval and reverse-engineering program. Back in Washington, Wilson complained to the SAPOC Senior Review Group (SRG) at the Pentagon.

The review group sustained the contractor and its denial of access, and told Wilson to drop the matter immediately and let it go. According to the notes, as Gerb reads them, the SRG chairman, Deutch, told Wilson that if he did not follow their suggestion "he would not see the director of the DIA promotion, he would get an early retirement and lose one to two stars."

The notes date this to 1997. By then Deutch had left the Pentagon (May 1995) and the CIA (December 1996).

## Significance

Gerb treats the threat as significant for several reasons:

1. The oversight body was protecting the program, and it threatened an officer whose post, Gerb stresses, ordinarily carried statutory oversight of every DoD special access program.

2. The threat was specific: Wilson would lose his expected promotion to DIA director, be forced into early retirement, and potentially lose one or two of his three stars.

3. Deutch's career ran from the acquisition office that allegedly held legacy files, to the SAPOC chair, to the CIA (1995–1996). That last post fits researchers' claims that the [[CIA]] Directorate of Science and Technology has played a leading role in crash retrievals.

4. It shows how institutions shielded unacknowledged programs, even from senior military intelligence officials who held the right clearances.

Wilson did not give up at once. According to the notes, in January 1998 he spoke to the new Under Secretary, [[Jacques Gansler]], and learned that Gansler had been read into the program. Wilson went on to become Director of the DIA (1999–2002).

## SAIC Connection

In his video on [[SAIC]], Gerb introduces Deutch among the senior officials the company recruited, and says that before 1995, possibly while he was Deputy Secretary and in the acquisition office, Deutch was an SAIC director who even worked in its La Jolla office. Gerb also says he consulted for TRW, Los Alamos and MITRE before his time on SAPOC: "three institutions I directly accuse of being involved in UFO programs." Since Bill Perry, who reorganized SAP oversight, had also served on SAIC's board, Gerb concludes "with a high degree of certainty" that Deutch served as a gatekeeper for UFO legacy programs, and he wagers that SAIC was the unnamed contractor in the Wilson-Davis notes. He lists Deutch among the living gatekeepers Congress should subpoena.

## SAP Governance Role

Gerb's *Special Access Required Vol.2* names Deutch, alongside [[Bill Perry]], as a chair of the [[SAPOC (Special Access Program Oversight Committee)|SAPOC]], established in 1994. Its Senior Review Group is the top level of access to the most sensitive DoD special access programs, including those Gerb alleges belong to the UFO legacy program portfolio. Gerb ties Perry's SAP reforms, and Deutch's role, to the 1994 move of program control out of the National Security Council, which he says was driven by fear of President Clinton's disclosures.

## MITRE Board Membership

Gerb's investigation into the [[MITRE Corporation]] states that Deutch served on MITRE's board before and during his time as Under Secretary for Acquisition and Technology and as Deputy Secretary of Defense (Gerb says "secretary of defense"), the period in which he chaired SAPOC. Gerb cites the overlap as another case of an official with formal SAP gatekeeping authority holding a leadership position at MITRE at the same time.

## NRO Carryover Funds

As Director of Central Intelligence, Deutch removed the leadership of the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|National Reconnaissance Office]] after the agency's mid-1990s scandal over billions of dollars in unreported carryover funds. Within months of becoming DCI he forced out the NRO's director and deputy director, saying he had been unaware of the surplus. In Gerb's investigation of the NRO, Deutch is one of thirteen officials he names as likely current or former members or gatekeepers of the UFO legacy programs. Gerb sees a possible conflict of interest in the same official overseeing both the SAP reorganization and the NRO fallout.
