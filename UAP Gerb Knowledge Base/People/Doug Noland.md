**Doug Noland** was a 19-year-old oil-field worker in 1948 whose account, given to [[Scott and Suzanne Ramsey]], is the fullest civilian description of the craft at the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|Aztec crash]] site. Gerb's 2025 video treats it as important corroboration that does not depend on [[Silas Newton]] or [[Frank Scully]].

## His Account

Noland worked for the El Paso oil company. Early one morning, on his way to pick up his supervisor Bill Ferguson for work in the field south of [[Aztec, New Mexico|Aztec]], he learned that a brush fire had been reported near the company's oil drip tanks on Hart Canyon Road, 12 miles northeast of town. When the two arrived, they found other oil workers already there, who told them something strange was sitting on the hill.

Noland described a disc about 100 feet across but only 18 to 20 feet high and very thin at the edges. It was "smooth with no noticeable seams, rivets, bolts, or weld marks," like brushed rather than polished aluminium, and had three gold-coloured rings around its rim. The portholes on its dome "looked like mirrors until you would get closer, then you could see right through them," and one had a hole about the size of a quarter. Through it he saw two small bodies slumped over what looked like a control panel. When local law enforcement arrived, Ferguson, ignoring orders to stay away, was poking at the craft with a fire pole and triggered "a door or walkway." The bodies were charred dark brown, but the interior was undamaged.

Ranchers Mr. and Mrs. Knight warned the workers off, a helicopter circled overhead, and policeman [[Manuel Sandoval]] arrived. The military came "in a very big way": seasoned, assertive, well-trained men in Army-looking vehicles, whom [[William Steinman]] would identify as the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]]. They split the civilians up for interviews and told Noland the event "was extremely important to the United States" and never to be spoken of.
