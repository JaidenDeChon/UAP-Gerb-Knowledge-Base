**Carl Mets** was a Pennsylvania State Police Fire Marshal who responded to the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]] site on the evening of December 9, 1965. Mets went into the crash area in the woods with another investigator. When he came back out, he made a cryptic remark to radio journalist [[John Murphy]] that has come to stand for the way officials deflected questions about the incident.

## Response to the Kecksburg Crash

On December 9, 1965, reports began to spread of a fiery object crashing into the woods near [[Kecksburg, Pennsylvania]], and Mets arrived at the scene as part of the state police response. John Murphy, news director at WHJB radio in Greensburg, was already there. He had come to investigate after a distressed witness called him.

Mets and another investigator went into the woods where the object had come down. When Mets came out, Murphy asked him what they had found. His answer was evasive but pointed:

> **"You better get your information from the Army."**

The remark indicated that Mets had seen something unusual and that the military had already taken charge of the scene. He said nothing more and refused to discuss what he had seen in the woods.

## Significance

Mets' cryptic remark matters to the case in three ways:

1. By sending Murphy to the Army, Mets acknowledged that whatever was in the woods was under military jurisdiction. That contradicts the later official claims that nothing was found.

2. It suggests that Army or Air Force personnel had reached the scene very quickly, within hours of the crash, and had taken control of the site before civilian authorities could investigate it thoroughly.

3. His refusal to give details fits the evasion and deflection documented throughout the Kecksburg case, from the intimidation of witnesses to [[Project Blue Book]]'s dismissive explanation that the object was a meteor.

Mets never publicly elaborated on what he saw in the Kecksburg woods, and no record exists of him being interviewed by UFO researchers in later years.

## Later Observations at the State Police Barracks

John Murphy later visited the state police barracks in Greensburg and found them swarming with Army and Air Force personnel. Murphy was an experienced journalist who knew how the police normally worked. He said the scene was entirely unprecedented and that it convinced him something of major military significance had been recovered from the woods.
