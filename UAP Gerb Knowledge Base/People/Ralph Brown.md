Ralph Brown was a Master Sergeant in the U.S. Air Force. On April 27, 1953, Captain [[Plandowski]] of the [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations]] (AFOSI) interviewed him, and the document recording that interview has since entered the UAP research record. In the interview, Brown passed on what a redacted individual had told him: that [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] had three flying saucers in its possession, one damaged and two functional, as well as the bodies of their pilots.

The AFOSI document is significant because it shows that rumors of recovered non-human craft and bodies at Wright-Patterson were circulating within the Air Force's institutional structure as early as 1953. That was decades before the public acknowledgment of the [[Roswell Incident]] and before Senator [[Barry Goldwater]]'s attempts to access the alleged "[[Blue Room]]" at the same facility. The document establishes an institutional paper trail that corroborates the broader pattern of Wright-Patterson serving as a classified repository for UAP-related materials.
