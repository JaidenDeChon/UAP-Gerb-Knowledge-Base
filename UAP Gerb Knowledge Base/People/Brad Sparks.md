Brad Sparks is a UFO researcher and an early co-founder of Citizens Against UFO Secrecy (CAUS), an organization dedicated to forcing government disclosure of UFO information through legal and legislative means. He has done detailed technical analysis of NASA and military satellite data on UFO incidents.

## 1976 Tehran UFO Analysis

Sparks analyzed the alleged [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellite detection data from the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]]. In an email exchange dating back to 1998 and preserved on the now-defunct website virtuallystrange.net, Sparks asked whether DSP-1 actually detected the UFO during the Tehran incident, or instead tracked the Iranian Mirage F-4 jets scrambled to pursue it.

### Technical Critique

Sparks pointed to an "out of focus graphic image" that showed only the date of the detection, not the time. He cited data showing 238 scans marked "possible SR" (possibly Strategic Reconnaissance), and noted that because DSP scans once every 10 seconds, this amounted to 39.7 minutes of continuous tracking.

Sparks asked whether such a long tracking time meant the satellite had detected the jets rather than the faster-moving UAP. His analysis is an example of the rigorous technical scrutiny needed to interpret satellite detection data correctly.

## CAUS and Government Transparency

As a co-founder of Citizens Against UFO Secrecy, Sparks took part in an early effort to use FOIA requests and legal pressure to force disclosure of classified UFO information. CAUS was one of the earliest organized civilian efforts to challenge government secrecy on the UFO issue systematically.

## Significance

Sparks' work shows how hard it is to interpret satellite detection data without complete records. His technical questions about the Tehran case show why proper scientific analysis of [[Fast Walkers|Fast Walker]] events needs full disclosure of DSP detection records rather than grainy partial printouts.
