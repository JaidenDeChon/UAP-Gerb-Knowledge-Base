Major General Albert N. "Bert" Stubblebine III (1930–2017) was a United States Army officer who was commanding general of the US Army Intelligence and Security Command ([[Army INSCOM|INSCOM]]) from 1981 to 1984, at the end of a 32-year career. He then became a vice president of [[BDM International]]. He is known for his interest in parapsychology and psychic phenomena, and he was one of the main sponsors of the Army's remote-viewing work. Gerb calls him "one of the primary characters of this channel": Gerb, [[Philip J. Corso]] and [[Steven Greer]] accuse him of taking part in UFO crash retrieval, craft storage and material exploitation, and Gerb ties him to a classified 1985 physics conference at BDM and to an alleged Men in Black unit called [[TREAT]].

## Career

Stubblebine was born at Fort Sill, Oklahoma, and graduated from the US Military Academy at West Point in 1952. Over 32 years in the [[US Army]] he commanded the Army's Electronic Research and Development Command and its Intelligence Center and School, and from 1981 to 1984 he led INSCOM, the Army's signals intelligence, human intelligence and counterintelligence command. In 1984 he was replaced at INSCOM, retired, and went on to serve as a vice president of BDM, the defense contractor later bought by [[TRW]] and then [[Northrop Grumman]].

Gerb repeatedly calls Stubblebine the founder of INSCOM. The command was in fact organized on 1 January 1977 at Arlington Hall Station, Virginia, under its first commander, Major General William Rolya; Stubblebine took command four years later.

## INSCOM and Psychic Research

As commanding general of INSCOM from 1981 to 1984, Stubblebine oversaw the Army's main signals intelligence, human intelligence and counterintelligence operations. INSCOM then also commanded the Intelligence Support Activity, the secretive special missions unit sometimes called "Task Force Orange", which was handed to [[JSOC (Joint Special Operations Command)|JSOC]] in 2003. Gerb and journalist [[Christopher Sharp]] accuse JSOC elements of taking part in foreign UFO crash retrievals alongside the CIA's [[CIA Office of Global Access|Office of Global Access]], set up the same year, and Gerb traces that line back through the Intelligence Support Activity to Stubblebine's INSCOM.

Stubblebine was one of the main sponsors of the Army's research into psychic phenomena for military use. The acknowledged US work on anomalous cognition began in 1977 as a joint program of the Army, DIA, CIA and SRI International, run from Fort Meade, Maryland. It went through a series of code names (Grill Flame was one of the names for INSCOM's part) and was consolidated as [[Project Stargate]] in 1991 and moved to SAIC. Jon Ronson's book *The Men Who Stare at Goats*, and the film made from it, describe Stubblebine's interest in remote viewing, spoon-bending and related subjects while he led INSCOM. In Gerb's words he was "obsessed with what you could call psychic phenomena for military application."

[[Philip J. Corso]] claimed that in February 1987 a [[UFO Working Group]], chaired by Colonel Harold E. Phillips of the Defense Intelligence Agency, was set up and saw ample funding from Army INSCOM under General Stubblebine.

## BDM International and the 1985 Physics Conference

After leaving INSCOM in 1984, Stubblebine became a vice president of [[BDM International]]. From 20 to 25 May 1985, BDM held the classified "Advanced Theoretical Physics" conference at its secure facility in McLean, Virginia, under Department of Energy classification controls. [[Oak Shannon|Oke Shannon]], formerly manager for special projects at Los Alamos National Laboratory, attended and later released his notes. According to Gerb, they cover UFOs, legacy programs, a "major engineering project under [[Bobby Ray Inman]]", underwater UFO hotspots off Argentina, an organization that "collects, manages, and destroys" UFO information, and a plan to approach [[Donald M. Kerr]] for "influence and money".

The working group behind the conference was led by Army Colonel [[John B. Alexander]], who had reported directly to Stubblebine from 1982 to 1983. Gerb wonders whether the conference, Stubblebine, a 1985 BDM contract for an ICBM deep-basing communication study, and the working group Corso described are connected. Rear Admiral [[Sumner Shapiro]], who described disassembled craft to [[Bob Oechsler]], also sat on BDM's board, and Commander [[Will Miller]] named BDM among the civilian contractors he said kept "the secrets".

## UFO Crash Retrieval and Storage Allegations

Gerb, Philip J. Corso and Steven Greer accuse Stubblebine of taking part in UFO craft storage and material exploitation while he headed Army intelligence at [[Fort Huachuca]], Arizona. Greer's witness log lists Stubblebine as witness 10658, with the description: "Has been involved since the 1960s, former head of Fort Huachuca Army intelligence where ET craft and bodies are stored." Gerb estimates that the fort is heavily involved in UFO programs. It hosts a Defense Agency major range and test facility base, the Joint Interoperability Test Command, and [[Bill Hamilton]]'s 1990 map of deep underground military bases marks it as a standalone underground site.

Greer also says Stubblebine tried to buy him off. In Greer's account, Stubblebine intercepted him in 1992 after a contact event near Pensacola, Florida, and about a month later personally offered him $2 billion "to not pursue what I was doing and become part of his team."

## TREAT Connection

[[Edgar Fouche|Edgar Fouché]] said a man he called Gerald belonged to [[TREAT]], the "Tactical Reconnaissance Engineering Assessment Team", which he likened to the Men in Black. The only other public mention Gerb could find is an anonymous letter to Steven Greer dated 31 August 1997. It accuses Prince [[Hans Adam II]] of Liechtenstein of funding counterintelligence operations on UFOs, and says: "Adam's funding should be focused on his support for TREAT and its GRD, who is wed to a former US Army General, ex-head of Army Intelligence and Security Command." The letter does not name the general; Gerb identifies him as Stubblebine, and reads TREAT as possibly "the real life Men In Black housed within US Army INSCOM".
