Major General Albert Stubblebine III (1930–2017) was a United States Army officer who was commanding general of the US Army Intelligence and Security Command (INSCOM) from 1981 to 1984. He is widely regarded as the founder of INSCOM. In UAP research he is known for his alleged connections to UFO crash retrieval and storage programs, his role in classified weapons and intelligence projects, and his documented interest in parapsychology and psychic phenomena.

## Role at INSCOM

As commanding general of INSCOM from 1981 to 1984, Stubblebine oversaw the Army's primary signals intelligence, human intelligence, and counterintelligence operations. In those years INSCOM's mandate included highly classified special access programs. His command coincided with significant alleged UAP-related activity within the Army intelligence community.

[[Philip J. Corso]] claimed that a UFO working group was set up in the mid-1980s with substantial funding from Army INSCOM under General Stubblebine's direction. If Corso's claim is true, Stubblebine was a central figure in making UAP research a formal part of the Army.

## BDM Advanced Theoretical Physics Conference

From 20 to 25 May 1985, BDM International hosted the classified "Advanced Theoretical Physics Conference" at a secure facility in McLean, Virginia. Stubblebine was Vice President of BDM International at the time. Oke Shannon, a former manager for special projects at Los Alamos National Laboratory, attended the conference and released its notes. The conference discussed several aspects of UFOs, including legacy programs and a "major engineering project under Bobby Ray Inman." The conference used Department of Energy security controls, which suggests that the nuclear materials classification framework was involved.

[[John B. Alexander]], who reported directly to Stubblebine from 1982 to 1983, is also connected to the BDM conference. Gerb credits Alexander with leading the Advanced Theoretical Physics Working Group that held it.

## UFO Crash Retrieval and Storage Allegations

Gerb and other researchers have accused Stubblebine of taking part in UAP craft storage and material exploitation while he was head of Army intelligence, possibly including operations at Fort Huachuca, Arizona. Fort Huachuca has a Defense Agency major range and test facility base, and [[Bill Hamilton]]'s 1990 DUMB (deep underground military base) network map marks it as a standalone underground facility site. The Joint Interoperability Center is also at Fort Huachuca, which gives the base a further institutional link.

## TREAT Connection

A 1997 letter to [[Steven Greer]] accused Crown Prince [[Hans Adam II]] of Liechtenstein of funding TREAT (Tactical Reconnaissance Engineering Assessment Team). The letter also linked Stubblebine to the organization, stating that TREAT's "GRD" was "wed to a former US Army General, ex-head of Army Intelligence and Security Command," a direct reference to Stubblebine.

## Parapsychology and Men Who Stare at Goats

Jon Ronson's book *The Men Who Stare at Goats* and the film that followed it describe Stubblebine's documented interest in parapsychology and psychic phenomena while he led INSCOM. His interest in remote viewing, spoon-bending, and related research was part of a broader INSCOM program that tried to develop psychic intelligence capabilities. Combining conventional intelligence command authority with investment in paranormal programs puts Stubblebine in an unusual position within the military intelligence community.
