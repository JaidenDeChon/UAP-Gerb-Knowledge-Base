Richard Geldreich Jr. is an American software engineer, known in graphics programming for his work on texture compression, who also researches UFO crash-retrieval cases and publishes his findings on Medium. In UAP research he is best known for archival work establishing the real identities and service records of the two main witnesses to the alleged [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]]: [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]] and [[Bill Uhouse]]. The auto-generated captions of Gerb's Kingman video render his name as "Richard Gild Jr.", a spelling that has also circulated in the vault.

## The Kingman research

In a Medium article on the Kingman case, Geldreich profiled Arthur Glenn Stansel Jr., the engineer behind the pseudonym "Fritz Werner" on the 1973 affidavit describing the crashed craft. Gerb uses it as the source for the parts of Stansel's career he presents as verifiable: D-Day service, Air Force engineering work at Wright Field from 1949, blast-effects measurement at the Nevada Proving Ground during [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]], work on aspects of the [[Manhattan Project]], and 16 years on the Apollo program. Gerb credits him with "doing the digging" on Stansel and links the article from the video.

A second article, "William (Bill) G. Uhouse's Marine Corps Service Verified", assembled newspaper evidence for Uhouse's military career: a 3 March 1949 clipping recording his re-enlistment in the Marine Corps, a 30 March 1953 *Evening Times* article describing eight years of service, tours in Korea and a demonstration by the Second Engineers Battalion, and a service company report confirming his role in that battalion. Gerb presents this as the answer to the objection that a witness who disclosed through [[Steven Greer]]'s [[Disclosure Project]] cannot be taken seriously.
