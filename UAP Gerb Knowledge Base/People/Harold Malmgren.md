Harald Malmgren (1935–2025), spelled Harold in Gerb's videos and on this site, was an American economist who spent many years as a senior government adviser, working closely with several US administrations and intelligence figures. He was associated with the Institute for Defense Analysis, a Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC) connected to the wider national security apparatus. His name entered UAP research mainly through claims he made about conversations with [[Richard Bissell Jr.]], the first co-director of the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)]].

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions spelled his first name Harold and described him in the present tense. His Wikipedia entry and his Fauquier County obituary give the name as Harald Bernard Malmgren and record his death on 13 February 2025.

## Role in UAP Programs

Malmgren has claimed that [[Richard Bissell Jr.]] confirmed to him that a UFO crashed in Magenta, Italy, in 1933, and briefed him on otherworldly technologies associated with that event. UAP researchers cite Malmgren as evidence that knowledge of UAP crash recovery programs reached senior advisers and FFRDC staff connected to the NRO's founding leadership. They treat his account as corroborating two assertions: that the NRO was connected to UFO legacy programs from the start, and that people who held both government advisory roles and positions at intelligence community FFRDCs were part of the network of people who knew about the legacy programs.

## Bluegill Triple Prime recovery

Malmgren is also linked to the alleged [[Bluegill Triple Prime UFO Recovery]]. The claim is that the October 1962 Bluegill Triple Prime high-altitude nuclear test of Operation Fishbowl disabled a nearby UFO, which fell into the Pacific. In a late-life interview, Malmgren said he had handled debris from a UFO brought down by that test. The argument from Navy ship logs, that the salvage ship USS Safeguard and others searched the area and recovered a "pod", comes from Australian intelligence officer Geoff Cruickshank, whose work was aired by journalist Ross Coulthart, not from Malmgren. Gerb presents the incident as discussed by Malmgren and cites it as acknowledged Navy participation in at least one undersea retrieval.

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said that "by his account" the USS Safeguard recovered a "pod", making Malmgren the source for the ship-log details. Those details come from Geoff Cruickshank's analysis of the recovery-fleet logs (covered by Metabunk, DECUR and others); Malmgren's own claim was that he handled debris from the downed craft.
