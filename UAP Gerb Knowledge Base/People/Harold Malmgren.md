Harold Malmgren is an American economist who spent many years as a senior government adviser, working closely with several US administrations and intelligence figures. He is associated with the [[Institute for Defense Analysis]], a Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC) connected to the wider national security apparatus. His name entered UAP research mainly through claims he made about conversations with [[Richard Bissell Jr.]], the first co-director of the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)]].

## Role in UAP Programs

Malmgren has claimed that [[Richard Bissell Jr.]] confirmed to him that a UFO crashed in Magenta, Italy, in 1933, and briefed him on otherworldly technologies associated with that event. UAP researchers cite Malmgren as evidence that knowledge of UAP crash recovery programs reached senior advisers and FFRDC staff connected to the NRO's founding leadership. They treat his account as corroborating two assertions: that the NRO was connected to UFO legacy programs from the start, and that people who held both government advisory roles and positions at intelligence community FFRDCs were part of the network of people who knew about the legacy programs.

## Bluegill Triple Prime recovery

Malmgren is also the source for the alleged [[Bluegill Triple Prime UFO Recovery]]. By his account, the October 1962 Bluegill Triple Prime high-altitude nuclear test of Operation Fishbowl disabled a nearby UFO, which fell into the Pacific. The Navy searched the area, and the salvage ship USS Safeguard recovered a "pod". Gerb cites the incident as acknowledged Navy participation in at least one undersea retrieval.
