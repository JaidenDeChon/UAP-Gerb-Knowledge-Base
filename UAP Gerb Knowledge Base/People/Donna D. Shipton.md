Donna D. Shipton is a retired U.S. Air Force lieutenant general and acquisition officer. From January 2024 to June 2026 she commanded the [[Air Force Life Cycle Management Center (AFLCMC)|Air Force Life Cycle Management Center]] (AFLCMC) at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], its first female commander. Before that she was the military deputy in [[SAFAQ|SAF/AQ]], the Office of the Assistant Secretary of the Air Force for Acquisition, Technology and Logistics, responsible, in the words of her official biography, for "research and development, test, production, production support, and modernization of Air Force programs worth more than 60 billion annually". Earlier still she was deputy director of the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|National Reconnaissance Office]]. In his [[Hidden Wing]] video Gerb names her as a senior Air Force officer he alleges is involved in UFO legacy program operations, and urges the administration to serve her with a "friendly subpoena".

## Career

Shipton graduated from Clemson University with a degree in electrical engineering in 1991 and was commissioned through Air Force ROTC, beginning active service in 1992. Her career ran through acquisition and space programs: squadron and group command, senior materiel leader, program executive officer, center vice commander, and director of strategic plans, programs, requirements and analyses at [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)|Air Force Materiel Command]]. In August 2021 she became deputy director of the NRO and commander of its Space Force element. In 2022 she was nominated for promotion to lieutenant general and for the military deputy post in SAF/AQ, which she held from August 2022 to December 2023. She took command of AFLCMC, one of six centers under AFMC, on 17 January 2024, with additional duties as Air Force Program Executive Officer for Combat Readiness and Integrating PEO for Mission Systems. She handed over command on 30 June 2026 and retired in July 2026 after almost 34 years in uniform.

## Alleged Legacy Program Significance

Gerb's Hidden Wing investigation traces how the Air Force's acquisition chain could run UFO legacy programs, and SAF/AQ sits at the top of it. Its portfolio includes the most deeply classified special access programs, managed under [[SAFAQL (Special Programs)]], and research programs under [[SAFAQR (Science, Technology and Engineering)]], with the [[Rapid Capabilities Office (RCO)]], AFMC and its program executive offices beneath them. The programs under this oversight, worth more than $60 billion a year, include classified work at the RCO, the AFMC laboratories and the [[412th Test Wing|Edwards 412th Test Wing]]. Gerb found that eight of fourteen former assistant secretaries had worked for contractors he alleges are involved in legacy programs, including [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Northrop Grumman]], [[TRW]] and [[General Dynamics]], and he presents that revolving door as one way the programs carry on from one administration to the next.

At the end of that section Gerb turns to Shipton, the office's most recent military deputy: "I would be remiss if I didn't mention a senior Air Force general officer connected to SAF/AQ that I allege is involved within UFO legacy program operations." He admits he has no trail to follow further, but asks viewers to keep her in mind and the administration to "pass on a friendly subpoena". In his closing remarks he lists her among the gatekeepers he wants brought into the conversation, alongside [[Terry Phillips]], [[William E. MacLure]], [[Randall G. Walden]] and [[Russell E. Wyler]].

## Program Protection and MITRE

In *Special Access Required Vol.2* Gerb profiles AFLCMC's Information Protection Directorate as one of four "program protection" offices he alleges guard the legacy portfolio, and calls Shipton, then its commanding general, "a senior individual within Air Force UFO legacy activities". In his MITRE video he notes that AFLCMC is the contracting agent for MITRE's National Security Engineering Center, and describes Shipton as having "a very long history within Air Force legacy program portfolio". He adds that MITRE's own legacy involvement, as he knows it, centres on the US Navy.
