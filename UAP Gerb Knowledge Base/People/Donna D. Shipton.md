Donna D. Shipton is a U.S. Air Force Lieutenant General. Since early 2024 she has been Commander of the [[Air Force Life Cycle Management Center (AFLCMC)|Air Force Life Cycle Management Center]] (AFLCMC), its first female commander. From 2022 to 2023 she was Military Deputy in the Office of the Assistant Secretary of the Air Force for Acquisition, Technology and Logistics (SAF/AQ, also rendered SAF/ATNL). That senior acquisition role is responsible for the research and development, testing, production and modernization of Air Force programs worth over $60 billion a year. Shipton has also held senior positions in [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)]] and, earlier in her career, with the Space Force element at the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)]]. Researchers of UAP legacy programs theorize that she is among the senior Air Force officials who may have been read in to classified programs involving non-human technology. Their reasoning is that both the SAF/AQ military deputy role and the AFLCMC commander role sit directly in the acquisition chain that, they argue, oversees the [[Hidden Wing]] portfolio.

## Role and Responsibilities

The SAF/ATNL Military Deputy is one of the most senior uniformed acquisition roles in the Air Force. It is subordinate to the civilian Assistant Secretary, who is the Air Force Acquisition Executive. The role covers the whole lifecycle of Air Force advanced technology development, including classified [[Special Access Programs (SAPs)]] and Acquisition Category I programs across the [[Rapid Capabilities Office (RCO)]], [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)]] and the program executive offices beneath them. The programs under this oversight, consistently cited at over $60 billion a year, include classified programs at the RCO, AFMC laboratories and the [[412th Test Wing|Edwards 412th Test Wing]].

## Alleged Legacy Program Significance

Gerb's "Hidden Wing" investigation named the SAF/AQ (now SAF/ATNL) Military Deputy as one of the Air Force's senior acquisition roles through which knowledge of, or access to, UAP legacy programs would in theory pass. The investigation noted that eight of fourteen former SECAF ATNL officials had worked for contractors alleged to be involved in UAP legacy programs, including [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Northrop Grumman]], [[TRW]] and [[General Dynamics]]. This revolving door between the senior acquisition office and the program contractors is cited as one way the programs keep operating continuously from one administration to the next.

The SAF/ATNL Military Deputy's portfolio includes the most deeply classified SAP advanced technology development programs, managed under [[SAFAQL (Special Programs)]], and research programs under [[SAFAQR (Science, Technology and Engineering)]]. For that reason, researchers theorize that a four-star or three-star officer in this role would at minimum know what capabilities exist in the Air Force's most classified technology development programs, whether or not the officer is formally read into specific UAP-related subprograms.
