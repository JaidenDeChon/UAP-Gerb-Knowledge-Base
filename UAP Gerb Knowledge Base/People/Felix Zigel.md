Felix Zigel (1920–1988) was a Soviet academic and one of Russia's most respected and prolific UFO researchers. An astrophysicist and lecturer at the Moscow Aviation Institute, he was one of the few Soviet scientists willing to argue publicly for serious study of unidentified aerial phenomena at a time when official Soviet policy was deeply hostile to such inquiry. His manuscripts and books are cited as primary sources for Soviet cases of unidentified submerged objects (USOs).

## UAP Research Under Soviet Constraints

The stigma attached to UFOs in the Soviet Union was at least as oppressive as American institutional culture, and in some respects more so. Zigel nonetheless compiled detailed logs of Soviet UFO and USO encounters reported by military, naval and civilian sources. Gerb cites Zigel's manuscripts as the source for the 1977 [[Soviet Ship Nooget USO Sighting]], in which the Soviet ship Nooget, sailing in the Gulf of Guayaquil off Ecuador, observed a white luminescent sphere rise from the water, circle the ship, hover 20 meters above the vessel, and dive back into the ocean.

Zigel's work is considered foundational in Russian UFO research. It is also one of the main collections of Soviet-era records of anomalous phenomena, a body of material that remains partially inaccessible to Western researchers.
