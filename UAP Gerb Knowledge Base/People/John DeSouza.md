John DeSouza is a former FBI special agent and paranormal author who says he served more than 25 years with the Bureau, working counter-terrorism and violent-crime cases while collecting what he calls the real-life "X-Files". He claims that cases he investigated inspired the first season of the television series *The X-Files*.

## The X-Files claim

In a 2023 YouTube video, DeSouza said the show used his stories during its first season and turned to other true cases afterwards. The most remarkable, which he said he was still not supposed to discuss, took him to a military base where several disappearances had coincided with UFO appearances and the commander was at his wit's end. DeSouza and his partner joined a group that regularly watched the craft at the edge of a forest near the base. He described them as so full of light that they no longer looked metallic. One craft singled out DeSouza and his partner, came straight at them and appeared to land on them before vanishing. Base personnel then took him in against his will and interrogated him about what he knew of the UFOs.

In *Alien Reproduction Vehicle - TR-3B and the Flying Triangles*, UAP Gerb sets this account beside the first regular episode of *The X-Files*, "Deep Throat", in which Agent Mulder investigates missing Air Force test pilots, sees a triangular craft said to be reverse-engineered from the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]], and is seized by base personnel and made to forget. The episode's craft was a Fox render of [[Bill McDonald]]'s "Tehachapi Triangle", his drawing of the alleged [[XF-131 Super Sentinel]]. Gerb says he strongly disagrees with the "fake alien invasion" (Project Blue Beam) narrative DeSouza's video promotes, but finds the X-Files remarks worth noting alongside the rumour that series creator [[Chris Carter]] was once questioned by military officials about how much he knew.
