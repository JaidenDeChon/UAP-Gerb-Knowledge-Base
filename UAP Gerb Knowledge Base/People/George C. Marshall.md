George Catlett Marshall (1880–1959) was one of the most consequential military and diplomatic figures in American history. As Army Chief of Staff throughout World War II, he built the US Army from a peacetime force into a global fighting organization, and he sat on the [[Manhattan Project]]'s Top Policy Group, the very small body that oversaw the atomic bomb program and controlled access to it. As Secretary of State he gave his name to the Marshall Plan, and he later served as Secretary of Defense. In Gerb's videos, Marshall and [[Vannevar Bush]] are the two men who "constructed the very skeleton" of the secrecy around early UFO crash retrieval, the [[Manhattan Project 2.0]]: through the Alsos Missions, which Gerb calls the blueprint for crash retrieval teams, and through his alleged leadership of the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] (IPU).

## Career

Marshall graduated from the Virginia Military Institute in 1901 and was commissioned in the [[US Army]] in February 1902. He became Army Chief of Staff on 1 September 1939 and held the post until the end of the war in 1945. President Truman made him Secretary of State in January 1947, and he served until January 1949. He returned as Secretary of [[Department of Defense|Defense]] from September 1950 to September 1951, and in 1953 he received the Nobel Peace Prize for the Marshall Plan. He never served in Eisenhower's administration, a point Gerb makes in weighing how long Marshall could have shaped the programs.

## Military Career and Manhattan Project Role

As Army Chief of Staff, Marshall oversaw the transformation of the US Army and coordinated Allied strategy at the highest levels. In the Manhattan Project he sat on the Top Policy Group, the highest civilian-political oversight body for the atomic program. Its initial members were President Roosevelt (later replaced by Truman), Vice President Henry Wallace, Secretary of War Henry Stimson, Marshall, [[Vannevar Bush]] of the Office of Scientific Research and Development, and Bush's deputy James Conant. Through it, the president kept control of the Manhattan Project while bypassing normal government channels.

Marshall also sat at the top of the extremely short chain of command to which Manhattan Project director [[Leslie Groves]] reported, directly to the Secretary of War and the Army Chief of Staff. This narrow oversight let Groves bypass standard acquisition and bureaucratic channels. Gerb identifies it as the model that was copied into the organization of UFO legacy programs.

## The Alsos Missions

The secrecy around the Manhattan Project was so tight that military intelligence could not be briefed on atomic matters, which left it with no means of assessing German progress on atomic weapons. In 1943 Marshall proposed a separate intelligence operation under the Manhattan Engineer District, the [[Alsos Mission|Alsos Missions]], to run completely outside standard military intelligence channels. Gerb says the missions were overseen by Groves, Marshall, Colonel Boris Pash, whom he describes as Marshall's G-2 at the time, and Bush, and notes that Marshall "was fully prepared to utilize his G-2 for covert and unacknowledged missions." Alsos ran in three phases, in Italy, France and Germany, from an original detachment of 13 military personnel, including interpreters, and five scientists.

Alsos relied on T-Forces, an operational arm of the Supreme Headquarters Allied Expeditionary Force, to secure German scientists, technology, laboratories and materials on the front lines. Their methods were described as sometimes resembling "kidnapping at night by state officials who offered no evidence of identity," and they captured atomic processing plants inside Soviet-controlled territory. In the end Alsos captured Werner Heisenberg and other leading German atomic scientists, along with stores of uranium ore, heavy water and research documents.

Gerb identifies the Alsos Missions as the direct historical blueprint for modern UFO crash retrieval rapid-reaction teams. In this model, a highly mobile, lightly armed direct-action team secures a crash site so that specialized scientific personnel can move in and exploit it. Those personnel are now theorized to include [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|DOE NNSA NEST]] teams. A compartmented intelligence apparatus, "often compartmented or hid within offices like Marshall's G-2," supports the operation and answers to legacy program elements rather than to standard DoD and intelligence channels.

## Alleged Leadership of the Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit

The Army has confirmed, through institutional memory alone, that an Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit existed as an in-house project for an unnamed Army Assistant Chief of Staff for Intelligence (G-2), and that it was disestablished in the late 1950s. Researchers [[Ryan S. Wood]] and the late [[Robert Wood]] argue that the G-2 was a compartmented office and that the unit's real leader was Marshall. Placing its leadership in a subordinate office would have kept Marshall out of the chain of command, just as the Top Policy Group kept the bomb out of normal channels. In the Aztec video Gerb adds that Timothy Good, too, suggests the IPU answered not to MacArthur but to someone of higher rank, his superior Marshall.

Marshall appears throughout the disputed Majestic documents. An alleged 5 March 1942 memo from Marshall to President Roosevelt discusses the recovery of an unidentified airplane after the 1942 [[Battle of Los Angeles]], says it was likely of interplanetary origin, and suggests creating a special intelligence unit to investigate the phenomenon. The alleged IPU report dated 22 July 1947, written while Marshall was Secretary of State, says he was the only cabinet member who knew of the crashed UFOs and that [[James Forrestal]] would be briefed on certain aspects.

## The Aztec Account

According to [[William Steinman]]'s sources, the impact area of the craft in the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|1948 Aztec, New Mexico crash]] was relayed to Marshall, who contacted the IPU, said to operate out of Camp Hale, Colorado, and Vannevar Bush. A plastic "book" of glyph-like writing found in the craft was, allegedly, passed by Marshall to the cryptologists William F. Friedman and Lambros D. Callimahos, who could not decipher it. Gerb finds it striking that Steinman's sources put Marshall and the IPU at the head of the Aztec recovery years before the IPU documents surfaced, but he faults Steinman for stating this as fact without sources or corroboration.

## Secretary of State and Secretary of Defense

Marshall was Secretary of State from 1947 to 1949 and Secretary of Defense from 1950 to 1951. His time at State came right after the alleged Roswell crash, when, in Gerb's account, the Manhattan Project 2.0 was being organized. The two posts put him at the top of the civilian defense and foreign policy establishment during the formative years of the alleged UFO legacy program. When Truman presented him with a Distinguished Service Medal in 1945, he said that although millions gave America extraordinary service, Marshall gave it victory.
