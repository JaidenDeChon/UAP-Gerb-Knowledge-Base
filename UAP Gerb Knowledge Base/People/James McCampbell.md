James McCampbell is a UAP researcher known for documenting the electromagnetic effects UFOs produce when they come close to vehicles and witnesses. His research on the electric fields of UAPs has informed the understanding of how craft can disable vehicle electronics and engines.

## Research on Electromagnetic Effects

McCampbell's work, cited in UAP Gerb's video as "the 1983 McCampbell case", documented the electromagnetic field strengths a UFO would need to disable a vehicle's electrical system. His analysis established that an electric field of approximately 3×10⁶ volts per meter is required to ionize air and short out the spark plugs and distributor cams of a gasoline engine. A small UFO generating such a field would need several coulombs of charge, and that output equates to approximately 10⁹ joules of energy.

These figures put numbers on one of the most commonly reported UAP effects, the failure of vehicle engines and electronics near a craft.

## Connection to Broader UAP Physics

[[Kevin Knuth]] refers to McCampbell's work on electromagnetic effects in his analysis of the physical characteristics of UAPs. The electric field calculations complement Knuth's wider analysis, which covers the power needed for extreme UAP acceleration, the magnetic fields that produce the Faraday effect in photographs, and energy systems that might explain propulsion and electromagnetic effects as parts of one phenomenon.

The research suggests that electromagnetic interference may be an unintended byproduct of UAP propulsion or power generation systems.

## Pattern Across Cases

The electromagnetic effects McCampbell documented match what has been reported in many other UAP cases:

- [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]]: the avionics of [[Parviz Jafari|Major Jafari]]'s F-4 failed as he approached the craft.
- [[1987 HMNZS Southland USO Incident]]: the ship lost all power when an unidentified submerged object (USO) passed beneath its hull.
- Vehicle interference cases: there are widespread reports of car engines and radios failing near UFOs.

Because McCampbell calculated the field strengths required, his work helps distinguish psychological effects from genuine electromagnetic phenomena.
