**Dr. Eric Arthur Walker** (1910–1995) was an engineer who was president of Pennsylvania State University from 1956 to 1970, founded the Navy laboratory at Penn State that became its [[Applied Research Laboratory]], and sat on many high-level government science boards, among them the Pentagon's [[Research and Development Board]] under [[Vannevar Bush]]. Researchers believe he knew about classified UFO crash retrieval programs and may have been a member of [[Majestic 12]]. In the 1980s and 1990 he gave cryptic interviews in which he said he had attended meetings on recovered saucers, had known of MJ-12 for 40 years, and had been at the site of the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg crash]]. Gerb calls him one of the most crucial names in the crash retrieval programs of the 1950s and 1960s, and has brought him into fourteen videos.

## Career

Walker was born in Long Eaton, England, in 1910 and studied at Harvard, where he took a bachelor's degree in electrical engineering, an MBA and, in 1935, a doctorate in science and engineering. He taught electrical engineering at Tufts and the University of Connecticut before returning to Harvard in 1942 as associate director of its wartime Underwater Sound Laboratory. In 1945, when Harvard closed the laboratory, he moved part of its Navy-supported work to Penn State, where it was re-created as the Ordnance Research Laboratory, today the [[Applied Research Laboratory]], a Navy University Affiliated Research Center (UARC). At Penn State he became head of electrical engineering, dean of engineering and architecture in 1951, and president in 1956, serving until 1970. He died in State College, Pennsylvania, in 1995.

Walker was executive secretary of the Pentagon's [[Research and Development Board]] from 1950, at the very time the earliest classified discussions of UFO recoveries are alleged to have taken place. (Gerb's videos give his term as 1950 to 1951 in one place and 1950 to 1952 in another.) He also chaired the Institute for Defense Analyses, and served on the National Science Board and the Defense Science Board. Gerb also describes him as a former chairman of [[TRW]], a contractor Richard Dolan and others tie to a crash retrieval program called Zodiac. Stanton Friedman found an early-1950s Army document on "indoctrination for special intelligence for Mr. Eric Arthur Walker", and Gerb notes that Walker went on to assist [[Project Azorian]], the 1970s effort to raise a sunken Soviet submarine.

## Identification by Robert Sarbacher

When [[Stanton Friedman]] spoke with [[Robert Sarbacher]] in 1983, Sarbacher mentioned a man from Philadelphia who attended the [[Wright Field Crash Retrieval Meeting|Wright Field crash retrieval meetings]] and was "acting very smug about it." That man was later identified as Eric A. Walker, whose Research and Development Board role would have given him access to such meetings. Sarbacher himself did not name Walker; in his 1983 letter to [[William Steinman]] he named [[Vannevar Bush]], [[John Von Neumann]] and [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] as involved. Once Walker's name became public, a wave of researchers began contacting him, starting in the mid-1980s.

## Conversations with William Steinman

Steinman first wrote to Walker in March 1984. Walker typed his reply on the lower part of Steinman's own letter, cut off Steinman's return address and stuck it on the front of his envelope. It read: "Stop. Don't try and find me. I came on that machine. I will leave May 15. Ergo, quiet, quiet, quiet." (Gerb's video gives the date of Steinman's letter as 19 March "1948", evidently a slip for 1984.) A further attempt on 31 May 1984 got no response. On 30 August 1987, weeks after the MJ-12 documents were made public, Steinman reached Walker by telephone to ask about the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery]], [[Majestic 12]] and his relationship with Sarbacher. Walker's answers were cryptic, but he did not deny the central claims. Transcripts of the call published by researchers include these exchanges, which Gerb's videos do not quote.

On Sarbacher:
> "Steinman: I wanted to know if you knew Dr. Robert I. Sarbacher—he was a consultant to the Research and Development Board back in the early 1950s. Did you know him?
> 
> Walker: Yes.
> 
> Steinman: Do you remember him?
> 
> Walker: Slightly, yeah."

On MJ-12:
> "Steinman: I wanted to know if you ever heard of a group called 'MJ-12' or 'Majestic 12'?
> 
> Walker: Yes, I have.
> 
> Steinman: Was there such a group?
> 
> Walker: I can't answer that question."

Walker confirmed that he attended meetings around 1949–1950 at which crash retrieval and body recovery efforts were discussed: "Yes, I attended meetings concerning that subject matter. Why do you want to know about that?" He said he would try to find his notes of the meetings and consider sharing them. When Steinman raised Majestic 12, Walker replied, "Yes, I know of MJ-12. I have known of them for 40 years." He added, "I believe that you're chasing after and fighting with windmills", an allusion to Don Quixote, and warned Steinman that he was "delving into an area that you can do absolutely nothing about."

On crashed flying saucers:
> "Steinman: Do you know if there were any discs that were recovered in the late 1940s and early 1950s?
> 
> Walker: I don't have any idea about that."

Steinman's account also has Walker saying: "One of the documents was shown to me in which my name appeared and I said it was a phony. I won't tell you any more than that." Walker never denied that crash retrievals or MJ-12 were real, but he always refused to confirm details. Researchers read his tone as that of a man bound by a lifetime security oath.

## Interviews with Henry Azadehdel (1990)

In 1990 Walker took part in three recorded phone calls with researcher Henry Azadehdel, who also published under the pen name "Armen Victorian." Gerb's videos relay these admissions and statements:

- In the first interview they discussed the [[1978 Bolivia UFO Crash]] and the possibility that insect-like occupants had been recovered, which is consistent with Sarbacher's description.
- In the second interview Walker confirmed that MJ-12 exists but said the documents are likely forgeries. He suggested that few people could make headway on the recovered craft: "A couple of people are capable of handling this issue. Unless your mind ability is like Einstein's or likewise, I do not think you can achieve anything." Asked about his own involvement, he answered with questions: "How good is your Sixth Sense? How much do you know about ESP? Unless you know about it and how to use it, you would not be taken in. Only a few know about it." Gerb returns to this remark in later videos as a hint that access to the programs involved psychic or ESP research.
- In the third interview Walker kept discussing UAP topics, more cryptically, and confirmed that [[Vannevar Bush]] was involved in the program.

## Kecksburg Connection

Walker was president of Pennsylvania State University when the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]] took place on 9 December 1965, about 100 miles from Penn State's campus. In conversation with Armen Victorian, Walker said he had been at the crash site:

> "We went there with two from the military but not on duty."

He also went with a colleague. Asked whether a UFO was recovered, he replied, "I cannot comment on that. I cannot tell you." He would not say how long he stayed, but said he and the other men prepared no reports. [[Stan Gordon]] had a report of someone fitting Walker's description at the crash site.

In *The 1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania UFO Crash*, Gerb reasons that two off-duty military men cannot have been the three-man [[Project Blue Book]] team sent from the 662nd Radar Squadron. Gerb asks whether Walker's party were the men in civilian clothes witnesses saw giving orders, or even the trench-coated men who ordered the volunteer firemen away from the object. He also asks whether Walker and his Penn State colleague went on behalf of a program based at the [[Applied Research Laboratory]]. A witness called "TB" told [[Steven Greer]] that such programs are embedded within University Affiliated Research Centers. In a later video Gerb adds that [[AARO]], under its director Jon Kosloski, has acknowledged working alongside UARCs and FFRDCs, the kinds of institutions witnesses allege house UAP legacy programs.

## Relationship with Lyndon B. Johnson

The same video traces Walker's closeness to President [[Lyndon B. Johnson]]. Walker attended a White House luncheon with Johnson and other university presidents on 3 August 1964, and was the only university president invited to the 1967 Clean Air Act signing. On 10 September 1964 Johnson wrote to thank him for his service on the Board of Visitors to the US Military Academy, and on 24 September 1964 he asked Walker to recommend a student to be honoured as a young leader. On 18 May 1966, five months after Kecksburg, Johnson thanked Walker for his service on the National Science Board and, cryptically, "for the unstinting manner in which you served your government in other areas", including the Defense Science Board. Gerb connects this relationship to Johnson's day of meetings with the Joint Chiefs and senior science officials on 10 December 1965, the day after the crash.

## Assessment

Walker confirmed to researchers that MJ-12 existed and that he had attended the meetings, while discouraging investigation and speaking in metaphors. Gerb and other researchers read this as the behavior of an insider who acknowledged that UAP programs were real but worked under constraints that prevented full disclosure. In their view, his refusal to deny key claims, Sarbacher's description of him and his documented position on the [[Research and Development Board]] point to direct knowledge of classified UFO crash retrieval programs, and possibly to membership, at the fringe or in full, of the [[Majestic 12]] group. His statement that he went to Kecksburg in 1965 would mean he stayed involved in UAP matters well into his Penn State presidency.
