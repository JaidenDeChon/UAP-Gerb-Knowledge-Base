George Smithson was a United States Air Force pilot who took part in a spring 1955 Cold War simulation training exercise from [[Carswell Air Force Base]], during which he and other F-86 aviators escorted B-47 and B-52 bombers. In the debrief after the incident, Smithson confirmed seeing the same unidentified craft as [[Robert Willingham]], which corroborates at least the first part of Willingham's account, the UAP sighting itself.

## The 1955 Sighting

On the spring 1955 mission, the squadron received intelligence by radio that Distant Early Warning (DEW) radar installations in Canada had detected fast-moving unidentified signatures heading south toward Texas. A B-47 pilot then radioed that radar showed an unknown aircraft approaching from the northwest. Willingham described seeing a brightly lit object "like a star" approaching at an estimated 2,000 mph, roughly four times the F-86's maximum speed of 685 mph. Smithson and other pilots confirmed seeing the craft in the post-incident debrief.

The object made a sharp 90-degree turn to the southwest, toward the Mexican border. It was then seen giving off "a lot of sparks" while tilted at about 45 degrees before it fell out of view, which suggests the abrupt maneuver damaged its structure. Willingham later received authorization to investigate the estimated crash site near [[Langtry, Texas]].
