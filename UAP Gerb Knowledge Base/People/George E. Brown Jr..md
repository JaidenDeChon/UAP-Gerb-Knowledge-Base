George Edward Brown Jr. (1920–1999) was a Democratic US Representative from California who served in the House from 1963 to 1971 and again from 1973 until his death in 1999. A long-time member of the House science committee, he chaired the House Committee on Science, Space and Technology from 1991 to 1995 and was known as one of Congress's leading advocates for science and space research. In UFO literature he is named by industrial designer [[Brad Sorenson]] as one of the senior figures flown from the [[Norton Air Force Base Air Show]] to the classified 1988 exhibit at [[Air Force Plant 42]] where three [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicles]] were allegedly displayed.

## The 1988 Exhibit

According to Sorenson's account, as relayed by [[Mark McCandlish]], Brown was among the military and civilian guests who boarded a military Boeing 727 at Norton on 12 November 1988 and flew to Palmdale, alongside Senator [[Alan Cranston]]. UAP Gerb's video describes Brown as then chairing a congressional space science committee. The video's cold open also includes a claim that a male staff member in Congressman Brown's office confirmed both the exhibit and the fact that three discs, hovering without visible support and referred to as alien reproduction vehicles, were displayed there.

The exhibit was reportedly a private briefing for about 30 senior figures, intended to win billions of dollars in black-budget appropriations for new craft, which gives the alleged presence of a senior member of the House science committee particular significance in the story.
