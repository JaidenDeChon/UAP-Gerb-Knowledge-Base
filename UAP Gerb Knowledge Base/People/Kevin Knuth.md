Kevin H. Knuth is a physics professor at the [[University of Albany|University at Albany]] (SUNY) and a former NASA research scientist, and one of the best-known academics working on UAP. He earned his PhD in physics at the [[University of Minnesota]] and works on information physics, the foundations of quantum mechanics and Bayesian data analysis. He is vice president of [[UAP-X]], a nonprofit UFO field research group, and a research affiliate of [[Avi Loeb]]'s [[Galileo Project]]. Gerb calls him the one accomplished academic who publicly takes the physics of UFOs seriously, at a time when the rest of academia scoffs at the subject.

## Career

Knuth was born in 1965 in Fond du Lac, Wisconsin. He took a bachelor's degree in physics and mathematics at the University of Wisconsin–Oshkosh in 1988, a master's in physics at Montana State University in 1990 and a PhD in physics at the University of Minnesota in 1995. From 2001 to 2005 he was a research scientist in the Intelligent Systems Division at [[NASA]]'s Ames Research Center, designing machine-learning algorithms for astrophysical data. In 2005 he joined the University at Albany as an assistant professor of physics and informatics, and he is now a full professor there. He is also editor-in-chief of the journal *Entropy*. He was a founding member of UAPx when former Navy personnel Kevin Day and Gary Voorhis set it up in 2019, first leading its science and technology work and then, from October 2021, serving as its vice president.

## "Estimating Flight Characteristics of Anomalous Unidentified Aerial Vehicles" (2019)

On 25 September 2019 Knuth, [[Robert M. Powell]] and [[Peter Reali]] published "Estimating Flight Characteristics of Anomalous Unidentified Aerial Vehicles" in the peer-reviewed journal *Entropy*. The paper estimates lower bounds on the accelerations, speeds and power shown by the objects in a handful of well-documented encounters. Among them are the 1951 Bethune encounter over the North Atlantic, the [[1986 Japan Airlines UFO Incident|1986 Japan Airlines Flight 1628]] incident over Alaska and the [[2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter (Tic Tac)|2004 Nimitz encounters]] off Southern California, for which the authors drew on Commander [[David Fravor]]'s testimony, the ship's radar and the FLIR footage. Gerb says the paper has inspired him since it appeared.

Gerb returns to the paper in other videos. In his Obscure Oddities iceberg he cites its figures for the Japan Airlines object, which by Knuth's estimate pulled 68 ± 7 G crossing the diameter of its orbit around the 747, or 84 ± 8 G counting only the circular motion, sustained for the 31 minutes of the encounter. (Gerb introduces Knuth there as a New York University professor; he teaches at the University at Albany.) In his video on [[Mark McCandlish]]'s testimony, Gerb cites the paper's estimate of more than 5,000 G for the Tic Tac, far beyond what a missile can survive, alongside [[Karl Nell]]'s remarks on faster-than-light travel.

## Sol Foundation talk (2023)

In November 2023 Knuth gave a talk on UAP physics at the [[Sol Foundation]]'s symposium, alongside [[Karl Nell]], [[David Grusch]] and [[Gary Nolan]]. The UAP Gerb video "The Physics of UFOs– Dr. Kevin Knuth" goes through it (see [[Sol Foundation 2023 Presentation by Kevin Knuth]]). Knuth opened by saying he is skeptical of people who assume they have witnessed an alien spacecraft, and that unfamiliar engineering can look a lot like anomalous physics. At the same time he questioned academia's confidence that "we know our physics". He then quantified the [[Five UAP Characteristics (AATIP)|observables]] associated with [[Luis Elizondo]]:

- Acceleration and power: the Nimitz "Tic Tac" dropped from 28,000 feet to sea level in about 78 seconds, which Gerb gives as an acceleration of about 5,400 G. Assuming a mass of 1,000 kg, a tenth of a similarly sized F-18, the manoeuvre needed about 1,100 gigawatts, which Gerb puts at ten times the total nuclear output of the United States. Stopping should have released as much energy as 250 Tomahawk cruise missiles, yet no explosion was seen.
- Other cases: [[Hermann Oberth]]'s 1954 lecture on more than 50 radar measurements at about 19 km/s, the 1968 B-52 encounter at [[Minot Air Force Base, North Dakota|Minot Air Force Base]] (209 G, Mach 12), and the Japan Airlines object, which Knuth calculates reached about 269,000 mph.
- Light and fields: luminosities of 2,000 to 30,000 megacandelas, electric fields strong enough to stop car engines, magnetic fields large enough to show a Faraday effect through a polarizing filter, and avionics failures such as [[Parviz Jafari]]'s in the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]].
- Transmedium objects: the [[Aguadilla Puerto Rico Trans-Medium UAP Incident|Aguadilla footage]], the [[1987 HMNZS Southland USO Incident]] and eight early cases of objects moving between air and water. The phenomenon is also covered on the [[Unidentified Submerged Object (USO)]] and [[Trans-Medium Vehicle]] pages.

The talk also used seven photographs, including [[Almiro Baraúna]]'s [[1958 Trindade Island UFO Photographs]], to present UAP as a class of well-observed phenomena.

In February 2024 the Sol Foundation released a set of interviews and panels with Knuth, [[David Grusch]], [[Chris Mellon]] and [[Hal Puthoff]] ([[Sol Foundation Interview Release]]), which Gerb announced at the end of his METAPOD video. In his video on [[Karl Nell]], Gerb lists Knuth among the Sol Foundation's figures and plans follow-ups on the symposium talks.

## Other appearances in UAP Gerb's coverage

When covering [[Rodrik Castle]]'s account of electronic effects during his 1997 encounter at [[Marine Corps Air Ground Combat Center 29 Palms|29 Palms]], Gerb cites Knuth's Sol Foundation discussion of vehicle malfunctions near UAP: an electric field of about 3 × 10⁶ volts per metre around a craft would ionise the air and short out spark plugs and distributors. If that is what stopped the Marines' Humvee, Gerb reasons, the operators' own trucks must have been shielded against it. Gerb's USO coverage cites Knuth's treatment of the HMNZS Southland case, in which the object submerged without disturbing the water, and connects it to the [[Frasier Island USO Sighting]] from the same broad region of the south-west Pacific. It also links the engine failure in the [[Kamchatka Lake USO Sighting (1970)|1970 Kamchatka lake sighting]] to the vehicle interference Knuth has described.
