Frank Charles Carlucci III (1930–2018) was an American diplomat, politician and businessman. He was the 16th United States Secretary of Defense (1987–1989) under President Ronald Reagan, and Deputy Director of the Central Intelligence Agency (1978–1981) under President Jimmy Carter. He was also National Security Advisor (1986–1987) and held senior posts in the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare and the Office of Economic Opportunity. After leaving government he became chairman of the [[Carlyle Group]], a major private equity firm with extensive defense industry investments.

## Wackenhut Board

Carlucci served on the board of directors of [[Wackenhut Corporation]], the private security firm associated with classified government installations including [[Area 51|Groom Lake (Area 51)]] and the [[Nevada Test Site]]. UAP researchers cite his seat on the Wackenhut board, alongside figures such as [[Bobby Ray Inman]] and [[William F. Raborn]], as evidence of deep connections between senior defense and intelligence officials and the private security infrastructure around UFO legacy program facilities.

## 1988 Norton Air Force Base Connection

While Carlucci was Secretary of Defense (1987–1989), a classified aerospace exhibit was held on November 12, 1988 at [[Air Force Plant 42]] in Palmdale, California. At this event [[Brad Sorenson]] reportedly got into a restricted section of a hangar displaying three disc-shaped [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|Alien Reproduction Vehicles (Flux Liners)]]. [[Mark McCandlish]] identified Carlucci, with high confidence, as the "tall white man with an Italian-sounding name" who took Sorenson on a Boeing 727 flight from Norton Air Force Base to the Palmdale facility, passed him off as his aide to gain access, and told him to say nothing about the classified aircraft on display.

The Carlyle Group owned [[EG&G]] from 1999 to 2001. EG&G is the company central to Bob Lazar's claims about Area 51 and the location of the Wilson-Davis meeting, and UAP researchers have noted the ownership as further evidence of Carlucci's proximity to UAP legacy program infrastructure.
