Hermann Oberth (1894–1989) was a Romanian-born German physicist and engineer considered one of the founding fathers of rocketry and astronautics. He was the mentor of [[Wernher von Braun]], and UAP researchers note that he studied UFOs extensively. His interest in unidentified flying objects lent the subject credibility at a time when mainstream science largely dismissed it. Because he took part in both the early rocket programs and UFO research, his career links mid-20th century aerospace development with the alleged reverse engineering programs discussed by [[Edgar Fouché]] and others.

## Famous Quote

Oberth once said: "We cannot take the credit for our record advancements in certain scientific fields alone. We have been helped by people of other worlds." UAP researchers often cite this quote as evidence that early rocket scientists may have known about, or had access to, non-human technology.

## 1954 Flying Saucer Lecture

In 1954, Oberth gave a lecture on flying saucers in which he presented analysis of UFO speeds based on radar data. [[Kevin Knuth]] references this lecture in his academic presentations on UAP physics.

Oberth cited more than 50 UFO speed measurements from [[US Air Force]] and [[US Navy]] radar data. From those 50-plus readings, he calculated UFO speeds of 19 kilometers per second, approximately 42,000 mph or Mach 55. On how far the data could be trusted, he said: "If there would only be three or four measurements I would not rely upon them and would wait for further measurements, but there is existing more than 50 such measurements"

The 1954 analysis established that military radar had recorded extreme UFO performance decades before modern UAP disclosure efforts.

## 1954 American Weekly Article

In the same year, Oberth wrote in *American Weekly*: "It is my thesis that flying saucers are real and that they are spaceships from another solar system. I think that they are possibly manned by intelligent observers who are members of a race that may have been investigating our Earth for centuries."

He proposed that UFOs might fly by "distorting the gravitational field". This theoretical propulsion idea anticipated modern physics discussions of spacetime manipulation, and present-day UAP researchers who analyze craft capabilities have referred to it.

## Relationship to Werner Von Braun

Oberth mentored [[Wernher von Braun]], who became one of the leading figures in both German and American rocket programs. [[Philip J. Corso]]'s testimony cites their relationship as part of a lineage of scientists and engineers who may have known about, or been involved with, [[Technologies of Unknown Origin]] derived from UAP crash retrievals.

## Significance to UAP Research

As both a founding father of rocketry and a public advocate for the reality of flying saucers, Oberth has a unique place in UAP history. His readiness to say publicly that UFOs were real spaceships, backed by his analysis of military radar data, lent mainstream scientific credibility to UFO research in the 1950s.

[[Kevin Knuth]] draws extensively on Oberth's 1954 radar analysis in his academic presentations. He notes that the extreme speeds Oberth calculated (Mach 55) are in line with modern multisensor UAP data, such as the [[1986 Japan Airlines UFO Incident]], for which Knuth calculated speeds of approximately 269,000 mph.
