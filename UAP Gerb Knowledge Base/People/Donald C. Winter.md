Donald C. Winter is a physicist and former defense executive who served as the 74th United States Secretary of the Navy from 2006 to 2009. He joined [[TRW]] in 1972 to work on laser physics and, apart from a 1980-1982 appointment at [[DARPA]], stayed there for three decades, becoming president and CEO of TRW Systems. When [[Northrop Grumman]] acquired TRW in December 2002, Winter became the first head of the new Northrop Grumman Mission Systems sector, until President George W. Bush nominated him to lead the Department of the Navy.

## DARPA and the Strategic Defense Initiative

At DARPA, Winter managed space acquisition, tracking and pointing programs. His main responsibility was Talon Gold, an experiment to demonstrate acquiring and tracking targets from space with a low-energy laser and precisely pointing a narrow beam, a prerequisite for the high-energy lasers of the [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)]]. UAP Gerb notes that metallurgical engineer [[Albert Bruce Collins]] told [[Leonard Stringfield]] that Talon Gold and Teal Ruby were programs meant to track, and possibly down, UFOs.

## Alleged Legacy Program Relevance

UAP Gerb finds it notable that a man with Winter's SDI background was the first to run Mission Systems, the TRW-derived sector that a Northrop security director called "the coolest and most dynamic portfolio in the company". The video lists Winter among the TRW executives who carried TRW's culture, and in the host's view its legacy-program work, into Northrop Grumman.
