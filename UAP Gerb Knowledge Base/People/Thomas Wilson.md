Vice Admiral Thomas R. Wilson is a retired U.S. Navy intelligence officer who was Director for Intelligence (J-2) of the Joint Staff and then, from 1999 to 2002, Director of the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]] (DIA). In UAP discussion he is best known as the central figure in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], notes of a 2002 conversation in which he allegedly described finding, in 1997, an unacknowledged special access program doing crash retrieval and reverse engineering of non-human technology, and being refused access to it. Wilson has denied the meeting the notes record. Gerb has treated the notes as genuine since his first video, and has returned to them in a dozen videos since.

## Career

Wilson was born in Columbus, Ohio, in 1946 and graduated from Ohio State University in 1968. He served in the [[US Navy]] from 1969 to 2002 as an intelligence officer. His flag posts included Director of Intelligence for U.S. Atlantic Command, Vice Director of Intelligence on the Joint Staff, Associate Director of Central Intelligence for Military Support at the CIA, and Director for Intelligence (J-2) of the Joint Staff, the post he held as a rear admiral in 1997. On 27 July 1999 he became the 13th Director of the DIA, with the rank of vice admiral, and he served until July 2002, retiring from the Navy that year. His awards include the Defense Distinguished Service Medal and the Navy Distinguished Service Medal.

The Wilson-Davis notes, and Gerb in reading them, describe his 1997 post as deputy director of the DIA and assistant to the Joint Chiefs of Staff for intelligence (J-2). Gerb stresses what the post meant: in any ordinary case it carried statutory oversight and regulatory authority over every special access program in the Department of Defense, acknowledged or unacknowledged.

## The 1997 Pentagon Briefing and Investigation

In April 1997, at the request of retired Navy Commander [[Will Miller]], Wilson attended a briefing in a Pentagon conference room where [[Steven Greer]] presented information about UFOs, Roswell and crashed UFOs to Wilson, Miller and Apollo 14 astronaut [[Edgar Mitchell]] (see [[1997 Meeting (Wilson, Miller, Mitchell, Greer, Hughes)]]). Miller later said that DIA Director [[Patrick M. Hughes]] had also requested the briefing and wanted it expedited, and several sources, including researcher Joe Murgia, place [[Steven Lovekin]] there as well. After the briefing, Miller and Wilson spoke privately, and Wilson came away intrigued enough to investigate further. (In *UFO Whistleblowers Vol.1* Gerb dates the briefing to 1998; it is usually dated 1997.)

Wilson then began a 45-day investigation that ran from April to June 1997. General [[Marshal Ward]] and Secretary of Defense [[Bill Perry]] advised him to examine records in OUSD(AT), the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology (today the [[Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment (USD A&S)|USD(A&S)]]). Both told him about a "special project records group not belonging to usual special access programs": a special subset of unacknowledged carve-outs and waived programs that, according to the notes, Perry himself had organized in 1994. In *Special Access Required Vol.2* Gerb singles out one detail as the most important in the whole document: the record Wilson found held zero budget information.

## Discovery of the Program

According to the Wilson-Davis memo, Wilson found the unusual records group in the index abstracts and called seven program managers. Four of them referred him to the same program, run by three people who called themselves the "[[Watch Committee]]": a security director (formerly of the NSA), a program director and a corporate attorney. Wilson would not give Davis the compartment's name, calling it a "core secret", but described the project coordinator as an aerospace technology contractor active in defense and intelligence, "the best one of them". In his first video Gerb guessed the contractor was [[Lockheed Martin]]; by 2025 he had come to believe it was [[SAIC]], because two of the SAPOC figures Wilson dealt with were SAIC alumni.

At the end of May 1997, Wilson made three calls to the program manager, including a conference call with the security director and corporate attorney. He told them he had read into their program record and wanted to know about their crashed UFO retrieval program. They reacted with confusion and agitation. Invoking his regulatory authority, Wilson demanded a formal briefing, and the overseers agreed to meet him in person.

## Meeting with the Watch Committee

Wilson flew out to meet the watch committee in its conference room, inside a secure vault. The committee told him it had been formed after an earlier audit investigation nearly exposed the program. To prevent future discovery, a formal agreement had been struck with [[SAPOC (Special Access Program Oversight Committee)|SAPOC]], and special criteria had been set to control access.

The committee showed Wilson pages of a [[Bigot List]] dated 1990 to 1993. Every name he recognized was a civilian (scientists, technicians, engineers and managers) or an official from OUSD(AT), the National Security Council or other Pentagon offices; the list included no politicians, White House officials, presidents or members of Congress. The committee told Wilson the program was a reverse engineering effort focused on recovered technological hardware. The program manager stated they had "a craft, an intact craft they believed could fly" that was "not of this Earth, not made by man, not by human hands."

## Denial of Access and Threats

Despite his position and clearance level, the watch committee denied Wilson access because he was not on the bigot list and did not meet their undisclosed special access criteria. Wilson complained to the SAPOC senior review group (SRG) at the Pentagon, but the SRG upheld the contractor's decision to deny him access. The SRG's chairman, [[John Deutsch]], threatened that if Wilson did not drop the matter, "he would not see the Director of DIA promotion, he would get an early retirement, and lose one to two stars."

In January 1998 Wilson spoke to [[Jacques Gansler]], who had been read into the program. Gansler told him: "UFOs are real, so-called alien abductions not real," and would say nothing more on the topic.

## The Leaked NRO Document

In *UAP Reverse Engineering at Edwards Air Force Base Redacted List Vol.2*, Gerb relays Steven Greer's claim that he gave Wilson an allegedly classified NRO document leaked in July 1991, addressed to need-to-know groups such as Cosmic Ops, Majic Ops, Nellis AFOSI, Area 51 S4 and a [[Blackjack Team]]. According to Greer, Wilson said he recognized one of the entities listed and that it was run by a contractor. Gerb notes that at least the meeting between Greer and Wilson can be confirmed.

## The 2002 Meeting with Eric Davis

On 16 October 2002, shortly after retiring, Wilson allegedly met physicist [[Eric Davis]] in his car outside the defense contractor [[EG&G]]. [[Oak Shannon]], a former manager of special projects at Los Alamos National Laboratory, had asked Wilson to speak with Davis. Davis, who had been investigating crash retrieval programs, wanted to hear Wilson's account. Wilson set his terms at the start: he would not speak to the press, and if Davis ever broke his trust he would deny the meeting and everything said in it. He then recounted the whole 1997 investigation and the denial of access that followed.

## Public Denials

In a 2020 interview with investigative reporter Billy Cox for the Sarasota Herald-Tribune, Wilson flatly denied the events described in the Wilson-Davis memo: "It's all fiction. I wouldn't know Eric Davis if he walked in right now... I'm not saying that sometime somewhere I never met Davis, but I certainly don't know him. I don't remember him, and I definitely did not sit with him in a car for an hour in Las Vegas."

He did admit to meeting Steven Greer and Edgar Mitchell, but said he did not follow up on any investigation. Gerb notes that in the memo itself Wilson stated he would deny the meeting and everything discussed if his trust was broken, and that his career advancement had been threatened if he kept pursuing the matter.

## Confirmations from Other Sources

In 2008, before his death, [[Edgar Mitchell]] described the 1997 episode on CNN's *Larry King Live*: a vice admiral who said he would find out, traced the black-budget funding for a UFO program, and was then told he had no need to know. [[Will Miller]] confirmed the 1997 Pentagon meeting in an interview with Joe Murgia. [[Luis Elizondo|Lou Elizondo]], [[Chris Mellon]] and [[Oak Shannon]] have all vouched for the memo or aspects of it, and it was entered into the Congressional Record in 2022.
