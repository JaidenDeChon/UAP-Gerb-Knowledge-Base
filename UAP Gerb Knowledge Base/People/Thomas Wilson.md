Vice Admiral Thomas R. Wilson was Deputy Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) and later its Director, from 1999 to 2002. In UAP discussion he is best known as the central figure in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]]. The memo documents his alleged discovery, in 1997, of an unacknowledged special access program conducting crash retrieval and reverse engineering of non-human technology, and the alleged denial of his access to it.

## Military Career

Wilson was a decorated U.S. Navy officer whose many awards included the Distinguished Defense Medal and the Distinguished Service Medal. As Deputy Director and later Director of the DIA, he held one of the most senior intelligence positions in the U.S. military. The role carried statutory oversight and regulatory authority over every special access program in the Department of Defense, acknowledged or unacknowledged.

## The 1997 Pentagon Briefing and Investigation

In April 1997, at the request of retired Navy Commander [[Will Miller]], Wilson attended a briefing in a Pentagon conference room where [[Steven Greer]] presented information about UFOs, Roswell, and crashed UFOs to Wilson, Miller, Apollo 14 astronaut [[Edgar Mitchell]], and DIA Director [[Patrick M. Hughes]]. After the conference, Miller and Wilson spoke privately, and Wilson came away intrigued enough to investigate further.

Wilson then began a 45-day investigation that ran from April to June 1997. General [[Marshal Ward]] and Secretary of Defense [[Bill Perry]] advised him to examine records in [[OUSD(AT) (Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology)|OUSD(AT)]], the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology. Both Ward and Perry told Wilson about a "special project records group not belonging to usual special access programs": a special subset of unacknowledged carve-outs and waived programs that Perry himself had organized in 1994.

## Discovery of the Program

According to the Wilson-Davis memo, Wilson found the unusual records group in the index abstracts and called seven program managers. Four of them referred him to the same program, run by three people who called themselves the "[[Watch Committee]]": a security director (former NSA), a program director, and a corporate attorney. Wilson identified the project coordinator as an aerospace technology contractor (likely [[Lockheed Martin]]), described as "the best one of them," active in defense and intelligence.

At the end of May 1997, Wilson made three calls to the program manager, including a conference call with the security director and corporate attorney. He told them he had read into their program record and wanted to know about their crashed UFO retrieval program. They reacted with confusion and agitation. Invoking his regulatory authority as Deputy Director of DIA, Wilson demanded a formal briefing, and the overseers agreed to meet him in person.

## Meeting with the Watch Committee

Wilson flew out to meet the watch committee in its conference room, inside a secure vault. The committee told him it had been formed after an earlier audit investigation nearly exposed the program. To prevent future discovery, a formal agreement had been struck with [[SAPOC (Special Access Program Oversight Committee)|SAPOC]], and special criteria had been set to control access.

The committee showed Wilson pages of a [[Bigot List]] dated 1990 to 1993. Every name on it was a civilian's (scientists, technicians, engineers and managers), and it included no politicians, White House officials, presidents or members of Congress. The committee told Wilson the program was a reverse engineering effort focused on recovered technological hardware. The program manager stated they had "a craft, an intact craft they believed could fly" that was "not of this Earth, not made by man, not by human hands."

## Denial of Access and Threats

Despite his position and clearance level, the watch committee denied Wilson access because he was not on the bigot list and did not meet their undisclosed special access criteria. Wilson complained to the SAPOC senior review group (SRG) at the Pentagon, but the SRG upheld the contractor's decision to deny him access. The SRG's chairman, [[John Deutsch]], threatened that if Wilson did not drop the matter, "he would not see the Director of DIA promotion, he would get an early retirement, and lose one to two stars."

In January 1998 Wilson spoke to [[Jacques Gansler]], who had been read into the program. Gansler told him: "UFOs are real, so-called alien abductions not real," and would say nothing more on the topic.

## The 2002 Meeting with Eric Davis

On October 16, 2002, after retiring from the DIA, Wilson allegedly met physicist [[Eric Davis]] in a car outside the defense contractor EG&G. [[Oak Shannon]], a former manager of special projects at Los Alamos National Laboratories, facilitated the meeting. Davis, who had been investigating crash retrieval programs, wanted to hear Wilson's account. Wilson recounted the whole 1997 investigation and the denial of access that followed, and told Davis to keep the meeting private and never to discuss it without clearance.

## Public Denials

In a 2020 interview with investigative reporter Billy Cox for the Sarasota Herald-Tribune, Wilson flatly denied the events described in the Wilson-Davis memo: "It's all fiction. I wouldn't know Eric Davis if he walked in right now... I'm not saying that sometime somewhere I never met Davis, but I certainly don't know him. I don't remember him, and I definitely did not sit with him in a car for an hour in Las Vegas."

He did admit to meeting Steven Greer and Edgar Mitchell, but claimed he did not follow up on any investigation. Researchers note that in the memo itself Wilson stated he would deny the meeting and everything discussed if his trust was broken. They also note that his career advancement had been threatened if he kept pursuing the matter.

## Confirmations from Other Sources

In 2008, before his death, [[Edgar Mitchell]] confirmed the 1997 meeting on CNN's Larry King Live and described Wilson's discovery of black budget funding for a UFO program and the denial of access that followed. [[Will Miller]] confirmed the 1997 Pentagon meeting in an interview with [[Joe Murgia]]. [[Lou Elizondo]], [[Chris Mellon]], and [[Oak Shannon]] have all confirmed aspects of the Wilson-Davis memo that documents these events, or its authenticity.
