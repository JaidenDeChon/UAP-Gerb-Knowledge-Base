Jeffrey T. Richelson (1949–2017) was an American author and a senior fellow at the National Security Archive at George Washington University. He is widely regarded as one of the foremost historians of the US intelligence community. His large body of work includes authoritative books on the structure, operations and programs of American intelligence agencies, many of them based on extensive use of Freedom of Information Act requests and declassified documents.

## Key Works

- *The US Intelligence Community*, a comprehensive reference work on the organization and operations of American intelligence agencies. Gerb cites it as a source of useful information on the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|National Reconnaissance Office]] and the National Underwater Reconnaissance Office (NURO).
- *Defusing Armageddon: Inside NEST, America's Secret Nuclear Bomb Squad*, a detailed account of the [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|Nuclear Emergency Support Team]]. Its operational details, including deployment capabilities, vehicle fleets, laboratory partnerships and response protocols, proved instrumental in reconstructing the 1997 [[Peru UFO Crash Incident]].

## Relevance to UAP Research

Richelson's work on NEST is not about UAP, but it supplies an essential factual basis for understanding the Department of Energy's emergency response capabilities. Details from *Defusing Armageddon* are central data points in the theoretical reconstruction of how a DOE retrieval team could have reached a crash site in the Peruvian jungle within approximately 8 hours. They include NEST's authority to commandeer DoD aircraft, its fleet of Cessna Citation 2 jets (464 mph max speed), and its 24/7 Joint Technical Operations team, established in February 1997. They also include the 1996 Mars 96 probe incident, in which NEST considered deploying to Bolivia and Colombia based on [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellite intelligence.
