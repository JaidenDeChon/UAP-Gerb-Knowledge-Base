Boyd Bushman (1936–2014) was a Senior Research Scientist at [[Lockheed Martin]] in Department 60-8, the Special Programs Division, and held Top Secret/SCI/SAP clearances throughout his career. He is known mainly for a video recorded shortly before his death. In it he claimed that Lockheed possessed recovered non-human craft materials and conducted anti-gravity research, and he displayed photographs he identified as alien bodies. Those photographs were later identified as images of a commercially available toy figurine.

## Career at Lockheed Martin

Bushman was a verified senior specialist at Lockheed Martin's Special Programs Division, a position that required and confirmed his Top Secret/Sensitive Compartmented Information/Special Access Program clearances. He was an inventor of significant standing and held numerous patents in advanced physics and engineering. Among the best known is a 1997 patent for a device that generates a magnetic monopole beam capable of emitting pulses, levitating objects, disrupting electronics, and separating materials. Magnetic monopoles (isolated north or south poles of a magnet) have never been proven to exist under standard physics, so the patent is an extraordinary technical claim by any measure.

## Deathbed Claims

Shortly before his death in August 2014, Bushman gave a video interview in which he made several specific claims about Lockheed's involvement with non-human technology:

- Debris and materials recovered from crash sites in New Mexico were held by Lockheed. He was referring to Roswell and possibly the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona UFO Crash]].
- Lockheed conducted research on anti-gravity propulsion, consistent with what other Lockheed-connected sources alleged about the company's black programs.
- UAP craft studied by Lockheed were made of three main materials: thoride, germanium, and palladium. Bushman noted that these have genuine material science properties: pure germanium was the first metallic material to become a superconductor in the presence of an electromagnetic field; palladium is essential to capacitors; and thoride alloys have thermoelectric and photovoltaic properties. In theory, the combination could be used to build a thermoelectrical/photovoltaic generator with semiconductor and capacitor elements.
- Bushman displayed photographs that he claimed showed alien bodies, which he described as beings about 4.5 to 5 feet tall with elongated fingers. The photographs were later traced to a toy figurine sold at Kmart as a Halloween decoration, a claim that originated in a 2008 thread on the Above Top Secret forum. No verifiable commercial listing for the toy has been produced, so it remains an open question whether the similarity was coincidental, deliberate disinformation by a third party, or deliberate fabrication by Bushman.

## FBI Investigation (1999)

In 1999, while Bushman was at Lockheed, the FBI investigated him for suspicious foreign contact. The investigation centered on contacts in Prague, Czech Republic. Available evidence suggests his outreach concerned anti-gravity research rather than foreign espionage affecting national security. Lockheed became concerned that sensitive information might have been shared without authorization. The outcome of the investigation is not publicly documented.

## Credibility Assessment

Gerb rates Bushman as the second most credible of the four principal Lockheed-connected insiders discussed in his review, after [[Bernard Haisch]] and ahead of [[Don Phillips]]. Bushman's verified employment at Lockheed and his SAP clearance level, together with the substantive material science reasoning behind the UAP composition he described, lend his testimony partial credibility. The alien body photographs, however, whether a deliberate hoax by Bushman or planted disinformation, are a serious credibility problem that the video acknowledges without resolving. Gerb offers three possible explanations: the government had toy companies make alien-like models to discredit legitimate whistleblowers; the photo was planted to discredit Bushman; or Bushman knowingly included fraudulent material for unexplained reasons.
