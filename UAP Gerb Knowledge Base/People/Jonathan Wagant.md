Jonathan Wagant (also rendered as "Weygandt" in some sources) is a former United States Marine Corps Lance Corporal who testified about encountering a crashed unidentified aerial object during [[Operation Laser Strike]], a classified US counter-narcotics operation conducted in [[Peru]] in late March to early April 1997. His testimony is widely regarded as one of the most detailed and credible firsthand accounts of an alleged UAP crash retrieval event involving US military personnel. Wagant first went public through [[Steven Greer]]'s Disclosure Project in 2000, appeared on a podcast with [[Martin Willis]] in 2023, and sat down for an in-depth in-person interview with [[UAP Gerb]] as the subject of a comprehensive investigative analysis.

## Military Service

Wagant joined the Marine Corps in 1995 and served until 1998. After sustaining a back injury in training, he was assigned to the Second Low-Altitude Air Defense Battalion within the Marine Corps's [[MAC G28|Air Control Group 28]], stationed at [[Cherry Point, North Carolina Marine Corps Air Station|Marine Corps Air Station Cherry Point]]. Wagant served as a low-altitude air defense gunner with training on Stinger man-portable air defense systems (MANPADS) and Avenger surface-to-air missile (SAM) platforms. In 1997, Wagant and MAC G28 deployed to Peru for Operation Laser Strike, operating as perimeter security for mission radar installations and replacing aging Hawk missile batteries with Stinger and Avenger platforms.

## Radar Anomalies Before the Encounter

Prior to his crash encounter, Wagant overheard USAF personnel in the radar installation's command center discussing aircraft flying in and out of the atmosphere at Mach 10-plus speeds — objects that would stop instantaneously and reverse direction. An Air Force colonel entered the command center approximately 30 minutes after these conversations and confiscated the radar log books tracking such flight data. Wagant stated this happened at least three or four times during his deployment.

## The Peru Crash Encounter

One night between March and April 1997, Wagant was approached during guard duty by [[Staff Sergeant Montil]], [[Sergeant Allen]], and [[Sergeant Atkins]] and informed that a downed aircraft — possibly friendly — needed to be secured. By 3–4 a.m., Wagant, along with 8–10 Marines in a caravan of Humvees, departed for the crash site. After a combination of driving and trekking through jungle, they reached the area around 6–7 a.m. at first light.

The crash site was marked by a massive gash in the terrain where something had cut through the jungle and embedded itself into the side of a roughly 200-foot granite ridge. Vegetation was burned clean, not broken — as if an energy source similar to a laser had carved through the foliage. Wagant, Allen, and Atkins formed the vanguard, climbing to the ridge top where they first observed the craft.

### The Craft

The object was a massive metallic egg or teardrop shape measuring approximately 10 meters in height and 20 meters in length. The craft displayed a purplish-green color that fluctuated like gasoline on water — a phenomenon known as the [[Mother of Pearl Effect]]. The shifting colors sometimes made the craft appear nearly translucent or silver. Critically, the craft did not cast a shadow despite direct sunlight, appearing to absorb light rather than reflect it.

The craft appeared organic rather than machined — sculpted rather than assembled from parts. Large gill-like vents ran along the exposed side, progressively decreasing in size, which Wagant speculated were used for propulsion. Three non-flush hatches sat near the top of the craft, one of which was half-open. A sweeping band of light traveled around the craft's circumference, gradually slowing until the vehicle powered down, accompanied by a deep bass hum Wagant compared to a guitar amplifier — a sound nearly identical to testimony from [[Michael Herrera]] describing an octagonal [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)]].

Catastrophic damage was visible on the rear of the craft — an enormous gash Wagant attributed to [[MIM-23 Hawk Missile|Hawk MIM-23]] air burst fragmentation rounds, likely fired by the [[Peruvian Air Force]]. The disabled craft was dripping a clear liquid with the viscosity of maple syrup that poured from the open hatch and covered the surrounding foliage.

### Contact with Non-Human Occupants

Through the half-open hatch, Wagant observed what appeared to be a four-fingered arm hanging limply — a detail [[Steven Greer]] apparently edited out of the published 2000 testimony. Wagant reported feeling a telepathic presence from the craft's occupants: beings he described as tall (8–10 feet), gray-type entities with elongated oblong heads, large dark eyes, small mouths, and no visible ears. The entities projected feelings that they meant no harm and were requesting help. Wagant estimated four to five occupants aboard. The communication led him to believe the craft was controlled via consciousness — a concept now discussed as [[Psionics]].

### Electromagnetic Effects and Physical Exposure

Compasses spun wildly and communications equipment ceased functioning near the craft. Wagant came within 50–100 yards and got the mysterious liquid on his battle dress uniform. The liquid discolored his clothing like acid and caused the hair on his legs to fall out permanently.

## Detention and Interrogation

After approximately 15–20 minutes at the site, two to four US Army CH-47 Chinook helicopters arrived, landing in a nearby clearing. Armed men in all-black fatigues with no insignia, patches, or identification — older men in their late 30s and 40s speaking with American dialects — intercepted the Marines. These operators had arrived before the helicopters and had been monitoring the Marines' approach. Wagant took a swing at one operator, striking him on the head, and was beaten, restrained, and forced face-down into the ground.

A team of approximately 30 then departed the CH-47s, wearing rain jackets marked "DOE" (Delta, Oscar, Echo — [[Department of Energy]]) and biological containment suits with DOE lettering. These [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|NEST]] personnel in varying levels of [[MOPP Gear (Mission-Oriented Protective Posture)|MOPP gear]] stripped Wagant to his underwear, bound his hands and feet, strapped him to a stretcher, and loaded him onto a helicopter.

Wagant was flown to a location with permanent structures — possibly a Peruvian military installation — where he observed Americans alongside Chinese, German, and other nationalities, all armed and in black fatigues. He was taken underground to an interrogation room and held for up to two days. Operators threatened to kill him and his family, demanding his silence. A USAF lieutenant colonel with no name tag forced Wagant to sign multiple confidentiality documents before he was driven back to his original radar installation.

## Aftermath

Wagant was segregated with USAF personnel at his base for three weeks before being sent back to Cherry Point. He had no opportunity to contact Allen, Atkins, or the other Marines. By May 1997, Wagant was tormented by nightmares, missing time, and disturbing dreams — symptoms that had never occurred before witnessing the crash. He maintains there were internal USMC attempts to make him appear unstable. By late 1997, Wagant confessed to using banned substances to secure a discharge, and by March 1998 he left the Marines.

## Parallels with Castle and Herrera

Wagant's encounter shares a distinct pattern with those of [[Rodrik Castle]] (March 1997, 29 Palms, California) and [[Michael Herrera]] (2009, Sumatra, Indonesia). In all three cases, young Marines performing their duties were confronted by clandestine teams in all-black unmarked uniforms, held at gunpoint, and threatened. In Wagant's and Herrera's cases, the Marines were forcibly stripped of their gear, debriefed, and forced to sign extensive non-disclosure agreements under penalty of death by senior Air Force personnel. All three witnesses also reported receiving unsolicited [[Anthrax Vaccine|anthrax boosters]] immediately following their encounters.

## Investigative Analysis

UAP Gerb conducted extensive investigation into the case, including geospatial analysis placing the crash within a 90-mile radius of [[Iquitos, Peru]] (based on travel time reconstruction), identification and attempted contact with [[Sergeant Allen]], [[Sergeant Atkins]], and [[Staff Sergeant Montil]] — all confirmed as real USMC veterans with long careers. One Marine responded, claiming Wagant never deployed to Laser Strike and dismissing his account, though Wagant's detailed knowledge of Operation Laser Strike and MAC G28 deployment contradicts this claim.

UAP Gerb has theorized that the crash retrieval team was likely a [[NEST (Nuclear Emergency Support Team)|DOE NEST]] rapid recovery unit departing from [[Sandia National Laboratories]] via Cessna Citation 2 jets, arriving in approximately 8 hours total, with on-site security provided either by [[US Army 7th Special Forces Group]] Green Berets already deployed to the region or [[DOE Special Response Teams (SRTs)]]. Helicopters were likely [[1st Battalion 228th Aviation Regiment]] CH-47 Chinooks from [[Joint Task Force Bravo]]. Wagant may have been held at [[NAMRU (Naval Medical Research Unit)|NAMRU-6 (NAMRU South)]] in Iquitos for quarantine after exposure to the craft's liquid.
