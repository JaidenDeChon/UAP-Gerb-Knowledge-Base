Dr. Eric Henry Wang (died 1960) was an Austrian-born engineer who headed the Office of Special Studies at the Wright Air Development Center at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], and later worked at [[Kirtland Air Force Base, New Mexico|Kirtland Air Force Base]]. Researchers suspect that he led UFO crash retrieval and reverse-engineering work in the late 1940s and 1950s: [[William Steinman]] tied him to the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|1948 Aztec crash]], and [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]] named him as a lead of the team that examined the craft at the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|1953 Kingman retrieval]].

Very little biographical information about Wang is available, and Steinman believed his career had been deliberately kept off the public record. In *The Hidden Wing* (2026), Gerb found his obituary and the first known photograph of him.

## Career

A graduate of the Vienna Technical Institute, Wang taught structural and metallurgical engineering at the University of Cincinnati from 1943 to 1952. He began work on secret government projects in 1949, when he became head of the Department of Special Studies at the Wright Air Development Center. According to his obituary in the April 1961 issue of *Mechanical Engineering* magazine, he worked as a consultant to the Wright Air Development Center on aerodynamic and metallurgical design for the structures division, and was on the staff of the University of Cincinnati. Gerb's research into his role in the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]] gives a more specific title for 1949–1956: chief of the special studies office at Wright-Patterson. The office came under the [[Air Research and Development Command (ARDC)]], the precursor to Air Force Systems Command, and sat within the research directorate of the [[Air Force Special Weapons Center (AFSWC)]]. AFSWC, formerly an Air Force major command, specialized in atomic, nuclear and other unconventional weaponry.

In 1956, Wang's department was moved to the [[Sandia National Laboratories|Sandia Laboratory complex]] at Kirtland Air Force Base, where he remained until his death on 4 December 1960, at the age of 54.

## Connection to Aztec and Kingman

According to [[William Steinman]]'s 1986 book *UFO Crash at Aztec*, Dr. Wang was called onto the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery]] project in November 1949, more than a year and a half after the craft was recovered. The timing suggests that he was brought in for specialized analysis or reverse-engineering work.

Witness [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]] also named Wang as a lead of the reverse-engineering team for the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|1953 Kingman crash]]. From 1949 to 1960 Stansel worked in the Air Materiel Command's installation division within Wang's office of special studies. There Wang allegedly led the technical examination of a recovered craft, with [[Edward Bushnell Doll]] coordinating the engineers. Wang's role in special studies appears in an unclassified Defense Department document on [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]], the nuclear test series some researchers believe caused the Kingman craft to crash. In UFO lore Wang is also said to have led reverse-engineering efforts "under Kissinger", and to have been a close associate of [[Victor Schauberger]]; Gerb says this needs more investigation.

## Records Suppression

Wang is exceedingly difficult to trace in historical records, as if he had been deliberately erased from public documentation. [[William Steinman]] believed that [[Majestic 12]] wanted to keep their "prize scientist" off the public record.

A person known only as "MW" told Steinman that when Wang died, military intelligence confiscated all of his personal papers and locked them away in a highly secret section of the library at Kirtland Air Force Base. Steinman wrote to MW to follow up but never received a reply. He also noticed that his own mail was being tampered with and that there was a tracking device on his van.

## New findings in *The Hidden Wing*

Gerb's *The Hidden Wing* (2026) returns to Wang as the link between early crash retrievals and today's [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)]]. Gerb reports three findings.

- Records show that [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], who named Wang as the senior lead of the Kingman reverse-engineering team, really did work directly under Wang on [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]]. Wang was the technical and scientific monitor for the Air Force program. In 1953 his official post was chief of the special studies office at Wright-Patterson under the [[Air Research and Development Command (ARDC)]].
- Gerb located the *Mechanical Engineering* obituary that [[William Steinman]] had cited. It gives Wang's birth in Austria on 6 May 1906 and places his department, both at Wright-Patterson and after its move to Kirtland, in the research directorate of the [[Air Force Special Weapons Center (AFSWC)]]. That command later fed into both [[Air Force Systems Command]] and the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]], and so into modern AFMC. Gerb adds that the obituary identifies Steinman's informant "MW" as Maria Wang, Eric Wang's wife. On that reading, it was Wang's wife who confirmed his role in the "flying saucer program" and told Steinman that a "Dr. H.A.K." was completely in charge of it while Wang was alive. Gerb suggests these initials could belong to Henry Kissinger.
- In AFSWC's 1956 Kirtland Air Force Base yearbook, under the research directorates, Gerb found a photograph of Wang, which he presents as the first image of him ever shown publicly.
