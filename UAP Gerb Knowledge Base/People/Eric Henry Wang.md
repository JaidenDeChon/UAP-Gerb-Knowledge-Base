**Dr. Eric Henry Wang** was an Austrian-born scientist who headed the Office of Special Studies at Wright Air Development Center. Researchers suspect that he led UFO crash retrieval and reverse-engineering operations in the late 1940s and 1950s.

Wang's career has been deliberately obscured in public records. Very little biographical information about him is available, despite his alleged involvement in highly classified aerospace programs.

## Professional Background

After earning his degrees and teaching in Cincinnati, Ohio, Wang began work on secret government projects in 1949 at Wright Development Center. According to his April 1961 obituary in *Mechanical Engineering* magazine, Wang worked as a consultant to Wright Air Development Center on aerodynamic metallurgical design for the structure division, and was on the staff of the University of Cincinnati. Gerb's research into Wang's role in the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]] gives a more specific title for 1949–1956: chief of the special studies office at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. The office came under Air Research and Development Command (ARDC), the precursor to Air Force Systems Command, and sat within the research directorate of the Air Force Special Weapons Center (AFSWC). AFSWC, formerly an Air Force Major Command, specialized in atomic, nuclear, and other unconventional weaponry.

In 1956, Wang's department was moved to [[Sandia National Laboratories|Sandia Laboratory Complex]] at [[Kirtland Air Force Base, New Mexico|Kirtland Air Force Base]], where he remained until his death. This page follows Wang's April 1961 *Mechanical Engineering* obituary and dates his death to 1961. UAP Gerb's 2026 timeline video instead says he died in 1960 at age 54. Both accounts agree that he died young, at age 54, soon after moving to Kirtland.

## Connection to Aztec and Kingman

According to [[William Steinman]]'s 1986 book, Dr. Wang was called onto the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery]] project in November 1949, more than a year and a half after the craft was recovered. The timing suggests that he was brought in for specialized analysis or reverse-engineering work.

Witness Arthur Stansel also named Wang as the head of the reverse-engineering team for the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona UFO Crash]]. There Wang allegedly led the Office of Special Studies' technical examination of a recovered craft. Researchers of the Kingman case have speculated that Wang's involvement was connected to Operation Upshot-Knothole, a nuclear test series believed to have caused the Kingman craft to crash.

## Records Suppression

Wang has proven exceedingly difficult to trace in historical records, as if he had been deliberately erased from public documentation. [[William Steinman]] believed that [[Majestic 12]] wanted to keep their "prize scientist" off the public record.

A person known only as "MW" told Steinman that when Wang died, military intelligence confiscated all of his personal papers and locked them away in a highly secret section of the library at Kirtland Air Force Base. Steinman wrote to MW to follow up but never received a reply. He also noticed that his own mail was being tampered with and that there was a tracking device on his van.

## New findings in *The Hidden Wing*

UAP Gerb's *The Hidden Wing* (2026) returns to Wang as the link between early crash retrievals and today's [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)]]. Gerb reports three findings.

- Records show that [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], who named Wang as the senior lead of the Kingman reverse-engineering team, really did work directly under Wang on [[Operation Upshot-Knothole]]. Wang was the technical and scientific monitor for the Air Force program. In 1953 his official post was chief of the special studies office at Wright-Patterson under the [[Air Research and Development Command (ARDC)]].
- Gerb located the *Mechanical Engineering* obituary that [[William Steinman]] had cited. It gives Wang's birth in Austria on 6 May 1906 and places his department, both at Wright-Patterson and after its move to Kirtland, in the research directorate of the [[Air Force Special Weapons Center (AFSWC)]]. That command later fed into both [[Air Force Systems Command]] and the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]], and so into modern AFMC. Gerb adds that the obituary identifies Steinman's informant "MW" as Maria Wang, Eric Wang's wife. On that reading, it was Wang's wife who confirmed his role in the "flying saucer program" and told Steinman that a "Dr. H.A.K." was completely in charge of it while Wang was alive. Gerb suggests these initials could belong to Henry Kissinger.
- In AFSWC's 1956 Kirtland Air Force Base yearbook, under the research directorates, Gerb found a photograph of Wang, which he presents as the first image of him ever shown publicly.

The video, like UAP Gerb's timeline video, dates Wang's death to 1960.
