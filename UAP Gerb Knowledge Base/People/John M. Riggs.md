John M. Riggs is a retired United States Army officer. He enlisted in 1965, rose to lieutenant general, and served until 2004–2005, when he was forced to retire and lost a star, leaving as a major general. The UAP researcher Gerb theorizes that Riggs is the anonymous [[Infographic Show Whistleblower|whistleblower]] whose testimony, analyzed in the video "'US Special Forces Confession - I Recovered Crashed UFOs': Fact or Fiction?", describes taking part in a classified UAP crash retrieval program for approximately 35 years. In separate testimony, a witness identified as "TB" names Riggs as a general who allegedly showed classified UAP-derived cloaking technology to a [[BAE Systems]] employee, an act that led to his investigation, demotion and forced retirement.

## Military Career

Riggs enlisted in the US Army in 1965 and was commissioned as a second lieutenant in 1969 after Infantry Officer Candidate School at Fort Benning. He flew helicopters on a combat tour in Vietnam and earned the Distinguished Flying Cross; Gerb credits him with about 1,000 flight hours. He rose to lieutenant general (three stars), held senior roles that included work with Congress, and was assistant division commander of the 3rd Infantry Division in Germany. In 2004 the Army cited misuse of contractors when it removed him; he lost a star and retired as a major general. Supporters argued the real reason was his public criticism of troop levels for Afghanistan and Iraq. After retiring he worked as an aviation and strategic defense consultant, and in 2006 he joined other retired generals in calling for Defense Secretary Donald Rumsfeld's resignation.

## Alleged UAP Involvement

### TB's Testimony (Off-World Technologies Division Context)

In Gerb's video on the [[Off-World Technologies Division]], a witness identified as "TB" described a US Army general, whom Gerb identifies as Riggs, showing classified UAP-derived cloaking technology to an employee of [[BAE Systems]], a major British-American defense contractor. The technology had been developed through reverse engineering programs. After this classified technology was exposed to an unauthorized party, Riggs was investigated, demoted by one star and forced to retire. The incident is presented as an example of the extreme secrecy around UAP-derived technology and of the consequences for insiders who breach that secrecy.

### The Anonymous Whistleblower Theory

The anonymous whistleblower account published by [[The Infographic Show]] comes from a former US special forces soldier. He describes witnessing a UAP crash in Vietnam in 1968 and being inducted into the crash retrieval program shortly afterward. In his analysis of the account, Gerb lines up the witness's stated timeline against Riggs's documented service record:

| Witness claim | Riggs record |
|---|---|
| Served in Vietnam; program induction 1969 | Enlisted 1965, commissioned 1969 |
| Served in program until approximately 2004 | Forced retirement 2004–2005 |
| Rapid reaction units in theater | Helicopter pilot, about 1,000 flight hours, Distinguished Flying Cross |
| Moved to administrative side of program | Promoted to general, worked with Congress |
| Stationed near European operations | Posted to US Army base in Germany |
| Aviation and defense work after program | Became an aviation and strategic defense consultant after retiring |

Gerb explicitly calls this identification a theory that rests on circumstantial chronological alignment and career trajectory, not direct confirmation. He says, "I don't want to kick off an investigation I can't control", and notes that on its own the date overlap may be coincidence.

## Significance in UAP Research

If either the TB testimony or the whistleblower identification is accurate, Riggs would be one of the highest-ranking US military officials known to have had direct personal contact with UAP-derived technology. That goes far beyond the indirect involvement described for figures such as Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]]. His alleged forced retirement for disclosing classified technology to BAE Systems would also be documented institutional enforcement of UAP program secrecy at the general-officer level.
