Max Flatow (1915-2003) was an American mid-century modernist architect who worked for most of his career in Albuquerque, New Mexico. After serving in the US Army Corps of Engineers during the Second World War, designing military installations, he moved in 1945 to the secret city of Los Alamos, where he was architectural superintendent of construction for the [[Manhattan Project]]'s laboratory and reported to [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]]. In 1947 he opened his own practice in Albuquerque, which, with his university roommate Jason Moore, became Flatow and Moore, one of the largest architectural firms in New Mexico.

## Sandia Connection

In his investigation of [[Sandia National Laboratories]], Gerb traces the firm's modern successor, a small design practice rooted in Albuquerque. Its project list includes technical work on at least eight Sandia facilities, among them an Instrumentation Materials Research Laboratory and a Simulation Technology Laboratory at Sandia National Laboratory, a Nuclear Safeguards Laboratory on Sandia Base, and other laboratories in Albuquerque and at [[Kirtland Air Force Base, New Mexico|Kirtland Air Force Base]], as well as work for [[Honeywell]]'s Sperry Flight Systems.

Gerb's interest is in who designs and builds sites he believes may hold legacy-program material. Citing [[David Grusch]]'s statement that the early UFO programs copied the Manhattan Project's secrecy, he argues that the architect who oversaw construction at Los Alamos, under extreme compartmentalization, would have been an ideal designer for early program facilities, and describes Flatow as having gone on to shape much of Sandia's New Mexico campus. This is Gerb's inference from the firm's record; no program work is attributed to Flatow directly.
