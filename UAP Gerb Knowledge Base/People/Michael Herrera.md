Michael Herrera is a former United States Marine Corps infantryman and SAW (Squad Automatic Weapon) gunner who witnessed a large octagonal craft and armed operators during a humanitarian relief mission in Indonesia in October 2009. The encounter took place about 9-10 days after a September 2009 earthquake. He was on officer's chow detail aboard the USS Denver at the time and volunteered for a security mission. Herrera later became a prominent UAP whistleblower. He testified to AARO in April 2023 and spoke at the National Press Club in June 2023, and he claims that insiders from classified UAP programs have contacted him and given him more context about what he witnessed.

## 2009 Indonesia Encounter

### Mission Context

In October 2009, Herrera was deployed aboard the [[USS Denver]] as part of a humanitarian relief operation after a major earthquake in Indonesia. He was on officer's chow detail with other Marines when a female USMC major briefed volunteers for a security mission in the wardroom. The briefing showed pictures of [[Barack Obama]] as a child, along with references to his time in Indonesia. Herrera and his team were issued M16 rifles (Herrera normally carried a SAW). A CH-53 Super Stallion helicopter flew them first to Tobing Airport, which is described as having distinctive blue roofs, and then on to a hasty landing zone in hilly, heavily vegetated terrain.

The stated purpose of the mission was to guard supply deliveries to remote villages, justified by the region's reputation as the second-largest terrorist capital in the world. Yet Herrera's team was dropped at a landing zone (LZ) with no other personnel present, no radio communications, and no clear extraction timeline, which is unusual for a routine security detail.

### The Craft and Observation

After landing, Herrera's team of six Marines climbed a hill overlooking the LZ. From there they saw what looked like a structure in the distance, partly hidden by vegetation, that was changing color. The team decided to investigate, even though this meant abandoning their security post. As they came down the hill and approached, they saw:

- An octagonal craft about the size of a football field, with a pyramid-like top section
- A surface that seemed to absorb light, described as "the blackest, darkest thing" Herrera had ever seen, with a scale-like pattern across it
- Vents at the corners, along the edges of the craft
- A change of color, from light matte gray to dark matte black
- A raised platform beneath the craft, roughly as long as the craft itself, with pointed features. The platform slowly rose about 10 feet off the ground before the craft departed
- No sound except a low hum like a guitar amplifier or transformer, and no rotor wash or disturbance to the surrounding vegetation
- Red, yellow, green, and blue lights that came on at the craft's corners as it took off

### The Transport Operation

At the same time, Herrera's team witnessed a logistics operation that involved:

- Four black up-armored trucks that looked like Ford F-350s, with aggressive off-road tires and reinforced armor similar to Secret Service vehicles
- Pelican cases (weapons cases) in each truck bed
- Trailers carrying black shipping containers, each with a large black cylinder spanning the full width of the container
- Night vision equipment mounted on the sides of the trucks, which suggested they operated at night
- Trucks driving onto the raised platform beneath the craft

When he first testified to AARO, Herrera said he believed the cylinders were humidity control equipment, possibly for drug trafficking. He later learned from insiders that the cylinders were oxygen supply systems and that the containers held human beings. These were allegedly people recruited from disaster zones under false pretenses to serve as "pink assets" (biological subjects) in classified programs.

### Armed Interception

As Herrera's team broke into the clearing, eight armed operators in unmarked black uniforms immediately intercepted them. The operators used a tactical formation called a "nosbed" (interlocking fields of fire from two flanking groups). They:

- Wore all-black gear with no identifying markings or insignia
- Carried M4-style rifles with ACOGs and PEQ-16 illuminators
- Could not be identified as US Navy SEALs (Herrera had been briefed that SEALs might be in the area) or as any conventional military unit
- Used professional tactics consistent with former military training
- Disarmed the Marines methodically, removing their magazines and slinging the weapons uncomfortably tight on the Marines' backs
- Scanned the Marines' military IDs with thin, advanced devices resembling modern smartphones (more advanced than 2009-era BlackBerrys or Sony Ericsson phones), after first trying a BATS-like biometric system
- Threatened the Marines, saying "we will fucking kill you" and "it's easy to get lost out here"
- Included one operator who stayed back in a standoff position with a radio and appeared to coordinate with the others

The operators knew exactly where the Marines kept their IDs, which suggested they were former US military personnel. They searched the team and confiscated the Marines' camera footage. They then handed back the weapons, deliberately loaded in a way that made them hard to use, and ordered the Marines to leave and not look back.

### Return and Aftermath

Back at the LZ, Herrera's team met a Gunnery Sergeant whom Herrera had never seen before. He was not wearing the red patches that showed assignment to the ship, and where he came from was unclear. The Gunnery Sergeant seemed upset that the Marines' weapons were slung improperly. Shortly after a radio call, a CH-53 helicopter arrived and took the team back to the USS Denver, where they returned their weapons and borrowed magazines to the armory.

Back at Subic Bay, Herrera asked a fellow Marine whether he would tell anyone about the incident. The Marine answered "absolutely not," and the team stayed silent. None of the Marines have come forward publicly. Senate Intelligence Committee representatives, working through Herrera, later asked one of them to give anonymous testimony, and he refused in a text message, published in the Daily Mail, that said "leave me out of that".

## Whistleblower Journey

### Coming Forward

Herrera stayed silent about his encounter for about 14 years, bound by a non-disclosure agreement that allegedly threatened his life. He came forward after newly passed whistleblower protection laws made certain NDAs illegal. He also wanted to help others who may have had similar experiences and to add to public understanding of classified UAP programs. Herrera says he did not believe in UAPs before his experience and understands why others are skeptical.

### AARO Testimony (April 2023)

In late April 2023, Herrera testified to [[AARO]] (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office) in a secure facility (SKIF). He describes the session as being like an interrogation. He says that [[Sean Kirkpatrick]], AARO's director, showed little interest and asked only three questions, over and over, all aimed at identifying the operators Herrera had met. Kirkpatrick said AARO had access to satellite imagery of the location and promised to follow up. Herrera says that more than a year later, no one had contacted him again to review the imagery.

Herrera alleges that AARO's Historical Report Volume 1 deliberately misrepresented his testimony. According to him, it falsely attributes two statements to him: that he saw an "extraterrestrial vehicle" and that the operators were "US Special Forces". He adamantly denies saying either. He asserts that the omission and distortion were deliberate and not an innocent error, and notes that another person who was in the SKIF can corroborate that he never made those statements.

### National Press Club (June 2023)

Herrera gave his account of the 2009 encounter publicly at the National Press Club in June 2023. Before that, at a two-day disclosure event on June 10-11, he presented his hypothesis that the operation involved drug trafficking, based on how he interpreted the cylindrical containers. Dr. [[Steven Greer]] helped arrange Herrera's Daily Mail interview and his National Press Club appearance, and facilitated much of his effort to go public.

### Contact with Insiders

After his public testimony, people claiming to be insiders in classified UAP programs approached Herrera, at first through Dr. Greer's contacts.

An insider at the June 2023 National Press Club event sent Dr. Greer an encrypted text message, over a platform described as more sophisticated than Signal, saying they knew what Herrera had witnessed and wanted to make contact. Greer showed Herrera the message in a green room, with Herrera's wife present.

Herrera received contact information for a CIA Non-Official Cover (NOC) operative who offered to meet him and provide documentation. After thinking it over, Herrera called and arranged to be flown to an undisclosed location to meet in person. The operative:
- Held the meeting under extensive security, including devices for detecting chips
- Gave Herrera a loaded sidearm as a gesture of trust
- Seemed nervous and fidgety, which Herrera took as genuine concern
- Apologized to Herrera on behalf of the organization for the harassment and trauma caused by the 2009 encounter
- Said he would "never kill a patriot" or innocent people, though he acknowledged that people have been murdered to keep the programs secret
- Took Herrera to an underground facility to prove who he was and that the programs exist

Through these insider contacts, Herrera learned:
- The operation he witnessed was human trafficking for classified programs, not drug trafficking
- Individuals are recruited from disaster zones under false pretenses and used as "pink assets" (biological subjects/equipment)
- Participants are heavily drugged and do not fully understand or remember their involvement
- Two groups within the US government study UAP: the "Blue Group" (programs with congressional oversight, such as AARO, AATIP, and Project Blue Book) and the "Black Group" (an organization founded in the 1940s to reverse engineer non-human intelligence technology, which operates without oversight in a way that mirrors the [[Wilson Davis Memo]])
- The Black Group has successfully reverse-engineered non-human intelligence (NHI) craft to an unknown degree and uses these craft in operations. Flying craft are considered "boring tech" compared with its other technologies
- EMP-like weapons have been developed that can target, disrupt, and disable UAP so that they can be retrieved after a crash
- The program recruits people with psionic abilities (called "P3"), often from third-world nations, because such people are needed to interface with and control NHI technology
- Recruits are offered security and safety for their families in return, likely because of permanent or semi-permanent isolation
- The Indonesia operation Herrera witnessed involved this P3 recruitment and transport
- Reverse-engineered craft (ARVs) have become so advanced that insiders cannot tell them apart from genuine non-human intelligence craft
- The programs have internal factions: "good guys" who want disclosure and Congressional oversight, and "bad guys" who want to keep the secrecy, commit crimes against humanity, and hold on to power
- The covert group acts as an enforcement arm for political corruption, with assets embedded in government, law enforcement, and military institutions
- Civilian aircraft that accidentally enter Temporary Flight Restricted Areas (TFRs) near classified installations are allegedly targeted with undisclosed weapon systems
- Non-human intelligence craft are being shot down in desert locations around the world, which risks provoking an advanced species

The Insider also told Herrera he is writing a book titled "Sentinels of Ether" with a ghost writer. The book mixes truth and fiction, similar to Tom DeLonge's "Secret Machines." It describes an event in Mexico in 2004 in which a paramilitary black program team wiped out a JSOC team during an operation that involved transporting humans in shipping containers to a hidden mountain base. The Insider gave Herrera a two-chapter manuscript that is currently under review by DOPSR (Defense Office of Pre-publication and Security Review). The Insider claims to be playing "4D chess on DOPSR": he deliberately includes deep black classified information to test whether the pre-publication reviewers will flag it, and uses the government's response as inverse intelligence. This strategy raised credibility concerns for Joey Is Not My Name, who questioned how a fictional book describing a completely different event could serve as corroborating evidence.

Reddit investigator [[Joey Is Not My Name]] carried out extensive open-source verification of Herrera's account. Joey independently confirmed the main operational details, including the USS Denver's sole assignment to the West Coast of Indonesia, the presence of the 31st Marine Expeditionary Unit, the use of CH-53 Super Stallion helicopters, and the setting up of hasty landing zones in remote terrain. Joey's research corroborated every verifiable detail of the military operation described in Herrera's testimony.

Joey also used objective data, including flight records and other intelligence, to independently verify that Herrera was indeed flown to a secure facility to meet an insider. Before the meeting, Joey recorded a phone call in which Herrera described his apprehension and the meeting details. The recording served as documentary evidence and as a "dead man switch" in case Herrera disappeared. Joey's verification relied on tracking the movements and checking the facility's location objectively, rather than on trusting Herrera's word.

Herrera says he has been in contact with David Grusch and has successfully put insiders in touch with prominent figures in the disclosure movement. He sees the harassment he continues to face as confirmation that the information he is revealing is accurate.

## Parallels with Other Witnesses

Herrera's account is strikingly similar to other UAP encounter testimonies that Gerb has documented:

- Like [[Rodrik Castle]] and [[Jonathan Weygandt]], Herrera encountered personnel in all-black or unmarked gear who could not be identified as conventional military. Gerb's theory is that these were [[Wackenhut Corporation|Wackenhut]]-trained [[DOE Special Response Teams|DOE Special Response Teams (SRTs)]]
- All three witnesses were detained, debriefed, and warned against disclosure
- After his encounter, Herrera received an anthrax booster shot, the same post-encounter medical protocol given to Castle and Weygandt. Gerb theorizes that this may be connected to proximity to nuclear-powered craft or to materials associated with the [[Atomic Energy Act of 1954]]
- All three witnesses were active-duty Marines at the time of their encounters

## Controversy and Credibility

### Nathan's Contradictory Account

Nathan, a fellow Marine who was Herrera's team leader at a different point in the deployment, came forward claiming that Herrera fabricated the UAP encounter. Nathan acknowledged, however, that he was not present on the specific mission during which Herrera claims the UAP sighting took place. Nathan provided a photograph of two Marines on a helicopter, allegedly being flown into Indonesia. He claimed one of them was Herrera and asserted that the Marines in the photo had no rifles, contradicting Herrera's account that his team was armed. Herrera denies being in the photograph and has provided the names of the two people he believes it shows.

The rifles matter because personnel on humanitarian missions are typically unarmed. Joey Is Not My Name's investigation found that the first wave of Marines in the Indonesia relief operation was in fact armed with M16 A4s because of the heavy terrorist presence in the region. Later deployments were ordered to stop carrying rifles after an Air Force Colonel objected to the bad optics. This corroborates Herrera's claim that he was armed, and it leaves unclear which wave Nathan photographed.

Herrera points out a paradox in Nathan's testimony. If Herrera were fabricating the story, he could simply have admitted going on a separate mission with Nathan and kept his UAP account. Instead he denies being in Nathan's photograph, which would be a strange choice for a fabricator trying to cover all bases.

### Documentation and Evidence

- Herrera took photographs and video of the craft and the operation with a camera he kept in his dump pouch
- The operators confiscated these during the encounter
- Herrera gave the names of all the fellow Marines involved to AARO, the Pentagon, and the Senate Intelligence Committee
- None of the fellow Marines have come forward, and one refused when Senate representatives contacted him
- No documentation or confirmation from other witnesses has emerged publicly

### Motivation and Financial Interests

Herrera has said repeatedly that he is not profiting financially from his disclosure and that his main motivations are the truth and helping others who may have been affected by these programs. He runs his own business, separate from his UAP advocacy, and does not accept payment for interviews or appearances.

### Disinformation Concerns

The Insider's disclosures to Herrera remain unverified. Investigators have noted parallels with the CIA's 1989 disinformation campaign against [[Paul Bennewitz]], who was fed false information about extraterrestrials and underground bases until he eventually suffered a psychological breakdown. If The Insider is running a similar psychological operation, that would not invalidate Herrera's 2009 Indonesia UAP encounter, which happened before any insider contact and whose verifiable operational details have been independently corroborated. It would, however, raise questions about whether the insider claims about the Black Group, P3 recruitment, and other program details are true.

## Assessment

Herrera's testimony is highly detailed and internally consistent across multiple interviews and public appearances. His insider contacts give him context that other witnesses lack, although the insiders themselves remain anonymous. The apparent misrepresentation of his testimony in AARO's historical report, if accurate, suggests institutional resistance to his account. His fellow Marines have refused to corroborate his story publicly. That is understandable given the possible risks to their careers and personal lives, but it leaves Herrera as the only public witness to an extraordinary claim.

Gerb was more guarded when he first covered the case, in [[Videos/The Marines Who Got too Close to UFOs/summary|a January 2024 video]] that paired Herrera with [[Jonathan Weygandt]], and described himself as "torn". He accepted that Herrera had testified under oath. He noted, though, that if the account is true, the craft was an [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicle]] flown by shadow US military factions, which is the theory [[Steven Greer]] promotes, and he said he doubted that humans had reverse-engineered any non-human technology.
