Abraham "Avi" Loeb is a theoretical physicist and professor of astronomy at Harvard University. He is one of the most prominent mainstream scientists to investigate publicly the possibility of extraterrestrial technology and non-human intelligence. He founded and directs the [[Galileo Project]], an academic initiative that searches for scientific evidence of technological artifacts made by extraterrestrials.

## Academic Background

Loeb is the Frank B. Baird Jr. Professor of Science at Harvard University and was chair of Harvard's Department of Astronomy (2011–2020). He has published over 1,000 scientific papers on subjects including black holes, cosmology and the early universe. Because his standing in mainstream science is well established, his public interest in the search for extraterrestrial intelligence and anomalous phenomena carries considerable weight.

## The Galileo Project

In July 2021, Loeb founded the [[Galileo Project]], a research initiative that searches systematically for evidence of extraterrestrial technological civilizations. The project aims to:

1. Search for technological signatures of extraterrestrials using networks of telescopes and sensors
2. Analyze UAP data scientifically, using rigorous academic methods
3. Study interstellar objects such as 'Oumuamua for signs of artificial origin
4. Develop systems that detect anomalous objects in the air and in space

The Galileo Project is a major shift in academic attitudes toward UAP research. It brings the resources and credibility of Harvard University to questions that were previously considered outside mainstream science. [[Kevin Knuth]] is a research affiliate of the project, linking academic analysis of UAP physics with Loeb's broader search for extraterrestrial technology.

## 'Oumuamua and Interstellar Objects

Loeb drew wide media attention for his hypothesis that 'Oumuamua might be an artificial probe and not a natural asteroid or comet. 'Oumuamua passed through our solar system in 2017 and was the first interstellar object to be detected. Loeb pointed to its unusually elongated shape, its unexpected acceleration, and the lack of any observable outgassing that would explain its changes in trajectory as natural.

The idea is controversial among astronomers. Even so, Loeb's willingness to consider technological explanations for anomalous observations in public has made room for other scientists to engage seriously with similar questions.

## Cultural Impact

As a Harvard professor who talks openly about extraterrestrial technology, Loeb has helped reduce the stigma around serious academic work on UAP phenomena. His approach puts scientific method ahead of speculation and data-driven analysis ahead of belief-based conclusions. It also stresses academic transparency and peer review, and treats the search for technology as a legitimate scientific endeavor.

His work parallels and complements [[Kevin Knuth]]'s physics-based analysis of UAP. Together, the two are part of a growing willingness among academics to apply rigorous science to questions about non-human intelligence and technology.
