John P. Craraven was the chief scientist of the Special Projects Office of the United States Navy and the founding director of the Navy's Deep Submergence Systems Project (DSSP), launched in 1964. He is best known publicly for his leading role in the design and development of the Polaris submarine-launched ballistic missile program. In UAP research, Craraven is significant for documenting — in his 2002 memoir *The Silent War: The Cold War Battle Beneath the Sea* — the existence of a highly classified Navy program called [[Sand Dollar]] tasked with recovering objects of national security interest from the world's seafloors, and for his candid acknowledgment that the Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicles (DSRVs) he oversaw were designed from the outset to support clandestine intelligence missions beyond submarine rescue.

## Deep Submergence Systems Project

In 1964, Craraven was tasked by the US Navy to establish the Deep Submergence Systems Project, a program intended to dramatically expand the Navy's deep ocean engineering and recovery capabilities. The DSSP was initiated partly in response to the loss of the USS Thresher and partly to satisfy requirements identified by the classified [[Sand Dollar]] program. The DSSP produced the Navy's two publicly acknowledged Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicles — DSRV-1 (Mystic) and DSRV-2 (Avalon) — manufactured by Lockheed Missiles and Space Company in Sunnyvale, California. The DSSP also proposed six DSRV units and a family of Deep Submergence Search Vehicles (DSSVs) with an operational depth of 20,000 feet; only Mystic and Avalon were publicly acknowledged as having been built.

Craraven wrote of the DSRV program's dual purpose in terms that UAP researchers find significant: "A DSRV designed, constructed, and deployed for every conceivable rescue mission would also be available for the intelligence 'mission impossible' that were sure to occur." He also acknowledged that submarines like the USS Halibut and USS Seawolf — NURO assets — "only transported top secret equipment and teams of industry specialists who would carry out unknown missions in unknown parts of the sea."

## Project Sand Dollar Briefing

In 1965, Craraven was given a high-level intelligence briefing by a naval intelligence officer and informed about the existence of an "extremely sensitive program" named Sand Dollar. The program was tasked to retrieve militarily sensitive hardware and other items with national security importance from the seafloor of the continental shelf and in deep marine environments. Sand Dollar was buried within the structure of yet another secret program, which was itself hidden within the Polaris subprogram — a nested compartmentalization structure Craraven compared to that observed in other classified naval programs.

Craraven was shown a classified inventory itemizing objects of interest distributed across seafloors worldwide — not limited to any single region. He noted that objects were spread between continental shelf and deep ocean locations globally, with a skew toward the continental shelf due to shallower water and easier access. The program remained classified as of 2002 when Craraven published *The Silent War*.

Craraven wrote of his tasking regarding Sand Dollar: "Could I organize and manage a program to carry out this mission using the assets of the DSSP? Furthermore, could I carry it out in such a manner that my family and friends and closest professional colleagues would have no idea that I was involved in such a project?"

## Connection to Alleged UFO Retrieval Programs

UAP researchers, including UAP Gerb, argue that Sand Dollar may have served as the Navy's first formalized UFO legacy program — a structure specifically designed to identify, monitor, and retrieve non-human craft from the ocean floor. The timing of the DSSP (1964) and DSRV development (1970–1972) relative to the alleged 1962 Bluegill Triple Prime nuclear test UFO recovery suggests the Navy was systematically expanding undersea retrieval capabilities in response to encounters with non-human craft. The DSSP's stated launch date also coincides closely with the founding of the [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] in 1969.
