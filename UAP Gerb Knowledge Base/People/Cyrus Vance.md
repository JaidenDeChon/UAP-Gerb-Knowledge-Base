Cyrus Roberts Vance (1917–2002) was an American lawyer and government official who served as Deputy Secretary of Defense under President [[Lyndon B. Johnson]] and as the 57th United States Secretary of State under President [[Jimmy Carter]], from 1977 to 1980. He appears in two of Gerb's crash retrieval cases, a dozen years apart. In December 1965, as [[Robert McNamara]]'s deputy, he was among the officials who met Johnson at his Texas ranch the day after the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|Kecksburg crash]]. In 1978, as Secretary of State, he wrote a secret cable about the [[1978 Bolivia UFO Crash]].

## Career

Vance joined the Kennedy administration as General Counsel of the [[Department of Defense]] in January 1961. He was Secretary of the Army from July 1962 and Deputy Secretary of Defense from January 1964 to June 1967. Under Carter he became Secretary of State in January 1977, and he resigned in April 1980 in protest at Operation Eagle Claw, the failed attempt to rescue the American hostages in Iran.

## 1965 Kecksburg Crash: the Meetings at the Texas White House

On 10 December 1965, the day after the object came down at Kecksburg, Pennsylvania, President Johnson held a day of meetings at his Texas ranch. In Gerb's account the morning session ran from 10:10 a.m. to 12:51 p.m. with the [[DOD Joint Chiefs|Joint Chiefs of Staff]], Johnson's assistant Bill Moyers, [[Joseph Califano]], Defense Secretary McNamara and his deputy, Vance. After lunch Johnson met McNamara, Vance and Moyers again, with science adviser [[Donald Hornig]] and Agriculture Secretary [[Orville Freeman]]. None of the meetings names Kecksburg. Gerb argues that the guest list suggests a day of briefings on the crash, and says every one of the names offers "a key piece to the puzzle".

## 1978 Bolivia UFO Crash

On 6 May 1978, thousands of people near El Taire, Bolivia, saw a cylindrical object crash into a mountainside with a sonic boom heard up to 150 miles away. The Bolivian military found a dull metallic cylinder 12 feet long with a few dents and waited for technical commissions to arrive. Two US Air Force officers, [[Colonel Robert Simmons]] and Major [[John Heise]], arrived afterwards.

Secret documents obtained through the Freedom of Information Act show Secretary of State Vance writing that "appropriate government agencies have been contacted regarding this crash." Gerb says the agency was [[Project Moon Dust]], the classified Air Force program that, in his account, received the Bolivian military's film of the object and was tasked with monitoring the situation. Gerb admits that the trail ends there: he does not know whether the object was retrieved.
