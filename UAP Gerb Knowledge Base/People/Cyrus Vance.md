Cyrus Roberts Vance was the 57th United States Secretary of State, under President Jimmy Carter, from 1977 to 1980. He held one of the highest-ranking posts in the US government at a time when several UFO crash retrieval incidents occurred, including the [[1978 Bolivia UFO Crash]]. His part in coordinating the government's response to the Bolivia crash is documentary evidence that the State Department knew about UFO-related events and helped coordinate the response to them.

## 1978 Bolivia UFO Crash Coordination

On May 6, 1978, thousands of people near El Taire, Bolivia, saw a cylindrical object crash into a mountainside with a sonic boom heard up to 150 miles away. The Bolivian military then found a dull metallic cylinder 12 feet long with a few dents and asked for technical assistance.

Secret documents obtained through the Freedom of Information Act show that Secretary of State Vance wrote that "appropriate government agencies have been contacted regarding this crash." The agencies contacted were later confirmed to include [[Project Moon Dust]], the classified Air Force crash retrieval program. Moon Dust received film of the object from the Bolivian military and was tasked with monitoring the situation.

Vance's coordination of the response across agencies shows that UFO crash retrieval operations reached the highest levels of government. When a crash happened on foreign soil, the diplomatic coordination went through the State Department.

## Institutional Role in UAP Operations

As Secretary of State, Vance would have been responsible for the diplomatic side of UFO incidents abroad, including:

- Coordinating with foreign governments regarding crashed objects on their territory
- Facilitating access for US military and intelligence personnel to foreign crash sites
- Managing diplomatic cover stories and information control with allied nations

The Bolivia case establishes that the State Department, as well as the military and intelligence agencies, played an active role in UFO crash retrieval operations during the 1970s.
