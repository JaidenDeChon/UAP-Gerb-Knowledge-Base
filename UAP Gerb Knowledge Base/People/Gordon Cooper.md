Leroy Gordon Cooper Jr. (1927–2004) was a US Air Force test pilot and one of NASA's original seven Mercury astronauts. He flew the final Mercury mission, *Faith 7*, in 1963 and commanded Gemini 5 in 1965. In UFO literature he is best known for his account of a disc that landed on the dry lake bed at [[Edwards Air Force Base]] in 1957 and was filmed by a camera crew under his supervision.

## The 1957 Edwards landing

In 1957 Cooper was a project manager at the Air Force Flight Test Center at Edwards. By his account, he had assigned cameramen to film the installation of a precision landing facility at the edge of the dry lake. A saucer flew over them, lowered three small landing gear and set down on the lake bed. The crew picked up their cameras and moved toward it, filming, and got within about 20 yards before it lifted off, retracted its gear and climbed away at very high speed. They described it as shiny silver and about 30 feet in diameter.

Cooper said that while he searched the regulations for the right number to report the event in Washington, he had the film developed. By the time it came back, he was being passed up the chain to ever more senior officers, and a colonel told him to put the film in a courier pouch; a courier would fly it to Washington on the base aircraft, and no prints were to be made. Cooper never ran the film, but he held it up to a window and saw good close-up shots.

## In UAP Gerb's research

In *UAP Reverse Engineering at Edwards Air Force Base*, UAP Gerb opens his survey of earlier Edwards accounts with Cooper's story, as support for his thesis, drawn from witness [[Ed (Witness 11063)|Ed]], that Edwards is home to a reverse-engineering and test program for non-human craft. He plays a clip of Cooper telling it and says he would like to cover the case in more detail.
