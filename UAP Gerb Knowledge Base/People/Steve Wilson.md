**Colonel Steve Wilson** (d. 1997) was a purported US Air Force officer who, shortly before his death, relayed extensive disclosures about UFO legacy program operations through researcher [[Richard Boylan|Dr. Richard Boylan]], who collected them in 1996 and 1997. Wilson claimed 40 years of military service culminating in work for the Majestic-12 oversight structure, assignment to [[Area 51]]'s S-4 underground facility, and command of an elite Air Force-NRO unit responsible for recovering downed non-human craft. Gerb, who discusses him in three videos, calls his disclosures fascinating but "to be taken with a huge grain of salt", while finding that several of his details point to real programs, companies and facilities.

Whether he was a genuine military officer is still disputed. Researcher [[Kevin Randle]] raised concerns about Wilson's DD-214 (military discharge documentation), including a claimed 40 years of service and what appeared to be misreported medals. No scans or verified images of Wilson's service record have been made public: Boylan reprinted the DD-214 without posting images of it, and he has not answered Gerb's repeated requests for Wilson's documents, photos or medals. Gerb, who says he often questions Boylan's research, recommends reading Randle's critiques alongside Wilson's disclosures.

## Claimed Military Career

Wilson claimed to have served across several elite units over a 40-year career:

- 1963: Assigned as a captain in a tactical fighter squadron at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], recruited into [[Majestic 12|Majestic-12]] and promoted to major. He was briefed on MJ-12's mission: "UFO surveillance and interdictions, retrievals and analysis of records of extraterrestrial spacecraft and occupants, and public access to any information about these matters."
- 9 years: Assigned to [[First Special Forces Air Command]], where he received special training with [[Delta Force]] and the Black Beret. On behalf of MJ-12, he was tasked with making contact with key personnel at nearly every Air Force base in the world.
- 1972: Reassigned as a lieutenant colonel from First Special Forces Air Command at [[Vandenberg Air Force Base]] to [[Area 51]]'s S-4 facility by men presenting CIA credentials.
- After S-4: Claimed to head [[Project Pounce]], an elite Air Force-[[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]] Special Forces unit for retrieving downed UFOs.

## S-4 Assignment and Underground Facility

Wilson said S-4 was near [[Papoose Lake]], south of Area 51, and that he went 30 stories underground there. He claimed to have seen eight different kinds of non-human craft, as well as an extremely tall woman who appeared to be extraterrestrial. According to Wilson, S-4 existed to test reverse-engineered anti-gravity vehicles. He stated that the first successful test flight took place on July 18, 1971, one year before his own assignment, and that [[Bobby Ray Inman|Admiral Bobby Ray Inman]] was present for it. Gerb calls this an "interesting connection" to Inman's later introduction of [[Bob Echler|Bob Oechsler]] to [[Sumar Shapiro|Rear Admiral Sumner Shapiro]], a former Director of Naval Intelligence who reportedly told Oechsler that factions within the US possessed extraterrestrial vehicles and that he had studied one at close quarters.

## Project Pounce, Delta and the NRO

Wilson described Project Pounce as an Air Force and "National Reconnaissance Organization" unit that retrieved downed UFOs and supplied cover stories. Its security team, "Delta", was an arm of the NRO and, he said, flew [[XH-75D]] anti-gravity helicopters. It also fielded "the Equalizer", a truck-mounted EMP cannon for shooting down craft, built by LTV. He also named a "Jason Society" tasked with the "alien question".

Gerb doubts the truck-mounted cannon and finds no evidence or corroborating testimony for the XH-75D, but he follows four threads:

- LTV's electronics business became [[E-Systems]], a contractor to the CIA, NSA and NRO, which Raytheon bought in 1995.
- "National Reconnaissance Organization" is not the NRO's real name, but the term appears in 1960s CIA documents and 1976 newspapers. It either reflects usage of the time or shows that Wilson knew the NRO only from the press.
- A real Project Pounce appears in the evidence reviewed by the 1953 [[Robertson Panel]]: a 1952 Kirtland Air Force Base plan, described by [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], to photograph UFOs with stripped F-94C interceptors. Gerb notes it also appears in the disputed Majestic-12 Project Aquarius document, and that how it could have become a retrieval unit remains unclear.
- The real JASON advisory group, founded in 1960 and run through MITRE, has worked for the NRO.

## Northrop Grumman Claims

Wilson stated that [[Northrop Grumman]] made an anti-gravity disc that glowed an intense orange-gold during flight, at a "secret facility northeast of Lancaster, California". Gerb matches the location to Northrop's underground facility at [[Tejon Ranch]]. Boylan reportedly witnessed the craft, which Gerb calls "Northrop's Great Pumpkin", in test flights over [[Area 51]] and the Northrop complex near [[Edwards Air Force Base]]. Wilson also said that US clandestine operations flew the [[TR-3B]] reverse-engineered triangle.

Wilson also described the XH-75D ("XH Shark"), an alleged anti-gravity helicopter designed by [[Teledyne Ryan]]'s San Diego division, and said such craft were assigned to Delta 4/the NRO for UFO crash retrieval operations. Wilson's 1997 drawing of the XH-75D shows a dual-rotor helicopter. Gerb notes that it looks strikingly like the Raider X, a 2019 concept helicopter from Sikorsky, a Lockheed subsidiary, though he finds no link between them beyond the design. Teledyne Ryan, which [[Edgar Fouche]] also named among the TR-3B's contractors, was bought by Northrop Grumman in 1999.

## The "Star Wars City" Organizational Chart

Shortly before his death, Wilson produced a chart titled "Star Wars City" showing the alleged structure of UAP legacy programs under the [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)|Strategic Defense Initiative]] (SDI) umbrella at the [[Cheyenne Mountain Complex]] in Colorado Springs. The version Gerb shows is a recreation of Wilson's 1997 chart published in 2001, with spelling errors such as "Lockhead". It shows:

- An SDI headquarters under Cheyenne Mountain as the nexus of UAP legacy operations
- The NRO, National Security Council, NSA, CIA, NORAD and [[Majestic 12|Majestic-12]] around it
- Defense contractors including Northrop Grumman, [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Boeing]], McDonnell Douglas, Rockwell, Martin Marietta, IBM, [[Ford Aerospace]] and [[Teledyne Ryan]]
- [[Decision Science Applications, Inc. (DSAI)|Decision Science Applications Inc.]] (DSAI), led according to Wilson by former Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]], as a board of the directors of the contractors' UFO black-budget divisions

The chart supports Wilson's broader testimony that SDI, though publicly presented as a defense against Soviet ICBMs, was the financial and operational cover structure for UAP legacy programs. In his [[SAIC]] video Gerb adds Wilson's claim that DSAI and SAIC were "one and the same", and follows DSAI's assets forward to SAIC and L3Harris.

## Underground Tunnel Network

Wilson claimed that the Northrop, Lockheed and [[McDonnell Douglas]] radar cross-section (RCS) facilities were connected to one another, and to [[Area 51]] and other sensitive locations, by underground train systems. He stated that the tunnels were dug with "nuclear subterrenes", tunnel-boring machines that melt rock using compact nuclear reactors. [[Los Alamos National Laboratory|Los Alamos National Laboratories]] designed patents for these machines in the 1970s.

## Assessment

Gerb ranks Wilson's testimony well below that of David Grusch, Dylan Borland or Edgar Fouche, but finds that it intersects with other accounts in several places: Project Pounce appears in the disputed [[Majestic 12|MJ-12]] documents and the 1953 [[Robertson Panel]] report; Teledyne Ryan was a real Northrop Grumman acquisition; Northrop's underground facility at Tejon Ranch is discussed by forensic artist [[Bill McDonald]] and researchers [[Bill Hamilton|William Hamilton]] and [[Richard Sauder|Dr. Richard Sauder]]; and the SDI-UAP connection is referenced by several other witnesses. Without published military records, Gerb treats Wilson's legitimacy in the Air Force as "still shrouded in mystery", and says the colonel deserves a video of his own.
