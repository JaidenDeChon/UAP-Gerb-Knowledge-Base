Captain Kenju Terauchi was the pilot of Japan Airlines cargo flight 1628. On November 17, 1986, he reported that a massive unidentified craft and two smaller luminous objects shadowed his Boeing 747-200F for 31 consecutive minutes at 35,000 feet over Alaska. Two crew members corroborated his account and FAA radar confirmed it, and the encounter is considered one of the most credible and well-documented aviation UAP incidents on record.

## The Encounter

On the Reykjavik-to-Anchorage leg of a Paris-to-Tokyo cargo route, Terauchi and two crew members first spotted the objects approaching from the left at about 5:00 p.m. He described the main craft as roughly the size of an aircraft carrier, about four Boeing 747s in diameter, with the two smaller luminous objects alongside it. The main craft kept a standoff distance of 7.5 miles from the aircraft. At times it circled the 747 at a constant speed, and at others it darted rapidly in and out of that circling path. The encounter lasted 31 minutes at cruising speed.

An FAA FPS-117 long-range 3D phased array radar independently confirmed an object about 7.5 miles from the aircraft throughout the encounter. The object changed sides between the radar's sweeps, which came every 12 seconds.

## Aftermath

Aviation authorities and researchers found Terauchi's account credible enough to take seriously. Aviation Week and Space Technology promoted the explanation that he had seen the planets Mars and Jupiter. [[J. Allen Hynek]] and other researchers cited that explanation as an example of institutionalized debunking of credible reports. [[Kevin Knuth]] later published an academic analysis estimating that the craft's circular maneuvers would have subjected it to 84 ± 8 Gs sustained over 31 minutes, far beyond any human physiological or known engineering limit.
