Richard L. Haver is a senior U.S. intelligence community veteran whose career moved between defense contractors and the highest levels of intelligence management in the executive branch. He joined [[TRW]] in 1999 as vice president and director for intelligence programs and, after [[Northrop Grumman]]'s 2002 acquisition of TRW, retired from Northrop Grumman in 2010 as vice president for intelligence programs. After the 2000 election, Vice President [[Dick Cheney]] personally chose Haver to lead the administration's intelligence community transition team, which put him at the center of the intelligence restructuring that followed. He then served as Special Assistant to Secretary of Defense Donald Rumsfeld for Intelligence, a post that gave him broad visibility into highly classified programs across the DoD and the Intelligence Community.

## Government Career

Haver rose from active-duty Navy service to civilian intelligence analyst in the Anti-Submarine Warfare Systems Branch of the Naval Intelligence Support Center, and served as deputy director of Naval Intelligence from 1985 to 1989. In 1989 Secretary of Defense Dick Cheney created the post of Assistant to the Secretary of Defense for Intelligence Policy for him, with broad authority to review DoD intelligence, counterintelligence, security and covert action; [[Chris Mellon]] later held the same post (1997-1998). Haver was then executive director for intelligence community affairs (1992-1995) and, from 1998, chief of staff of the National Intelligence Council and deputy to the assistant director of Central Intelligence for analysis and production.
