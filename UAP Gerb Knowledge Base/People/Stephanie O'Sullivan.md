Stephanie O'Sullivan was Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence (PDDNI) from 2011 to 2017, the deputy of Director of National Intelligence [[James Clapper]], who personally recommended her for the post. Before that she spent sixteen years at the [[CIA]], where she headed the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology|Directorate of Science and Technology]] (DS&T) and then served as the agency's Associate Deputy Director. Earlier still she worked at [[TRW]], the defense contractor [[Northrop Grumman]] bought in 2002, and at the [[Office of Naval Intelligence]]. Since leaving government she has sat on many boards, and she chairs the [[Aerospace Corporation]]. In Gerb's videos she is one of the officials [[David Grusch]] named in January 2026 as aware of the crash retrieval issue, and Gerb alleges that she helped Clapper run the National Security Council's UFO crash retrieval portfolio.

## Career

O'Sullivan was born in Cape Girardeau, Missouri, and earned a bachelor's degree in civil engineering from the Missouri University of Science and Technology. She joined TRW around 1982, where her Battelle board biography says she provided oversight for "a series of sensitive projects", and then worked on sensitive research and development programs at the Office of Naval Intelligence. She joined the CIA in 1995. She became deputy director of the DS&T in 2003, headed the directorate for four years, and in December 2009 was named Associate Deputy Director of the CIA. On 18 February 2011 she became the fourth Principal Deputy DNI, and she served until 20 January 2017. In a clip Gerb plays, she sums up her career as "30 years of work at TRW, with the Office of Naval Intelligence and in CIA."

After leaving the ODNI she joined the board of the Aerospace Corporation in 2017 and became its chair in 2021. She also sits or has sat on the boards of [[Battelle Memorial Institute]], Booz Allen Hamilton, Huntington Ingalls Industries and, from about 2018, [[Peraton]], on the advisory board of [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]], and among the trustees of In-Q-Tel, the CIA's venture capital firm.

## Role in UAP Programs

Gerb alleges that O'Sullivan co-managed UFO legacy programs alongside [[James Clapper]], and that her TRW years and her CIA DS&T posts gave her prior need-to-know access. He does not think every DNI and principal deputy serves in legacy programs. He argues instead that Clapper and O'Sullivan arrived with prior exposure, and that Clapper "specifically recruited O'Sullivan as his PDDNI to wield dual-hatted roles together". In Gerb's account, after [[Dick Cheney]] left office in January 2009 a reduced [[National Security Council]] UFO crash retrieval portfolio passed to Clapper, who ran it with deputies including O'Sullivan and [[Michael Dempsey]]. He says *[[The Age of Disclosure]]* left the NSC out of its outline of legacy programs to protect Clapper.

The support Gerb cites is [[David Grusch]]'s appearance on Megyn Kelly's show in January 2026. Responding to Clapper's appearance in *The Age of Disclosure*, Grusch said Clapper "was well aware of the crash retrieval issue, managed the crash retrieval issue", and "placed people in critical roles to manage this issue both publicly and... non-publicly." He went on: "General Clapper, Stephanie O'Sullivan, other folks in the IC that are well aware of this issue, that were in rooms discussing this issue, I ask you to be greater leaders on this." Grusch also referred to Obama-era actions to help a presidential candidate if the subject became normalized. Gerb believes Clapper, O'Sullivan and [[Luis Elizondo]] were involved in that effort, and identifies the candidate as Hillary Clinton.

## TRW Years and Later Boards

Gerb's video *Northrop Grumman & TRW* places O'Sullivan in a line of officials who passed through TRW on their way to the top of the intelligence community. She joined TRW around 1982, and overlapped there for about two years with [[Dawn Meyerriecks]], who later served under her and Clapper. Gerb calls the thin public record of her TRW and naval intelligence years "data masking", meaning classified work obscured on a CV; beyond a 2018 alumni article and her Battelle biography, he says, almost nothing about them can be found. He thinks she likely crossed paths at the Office of Naval Intelligence with [[Richard Haver]], its deputy director from 1985 to 1989, and asks why former ONI official [[Jay Stratton]] has not discussed her. Gerb ranks her CV with those of [[Edward C. Aldridge]] and [[Donald M. Kerr]], and treats the DS&T posts she held as significant in the legacy structure, as with [[Glenn Gaffney]], [[Doug Wolfe|Doug Wolf]] and Meyerriecks.

He also reads her later boards as part of the pattern. Battelle and Booz Allen Hamilton are, in his words, channel regulars; Huntington Ingalls was spun off from Northrop in 2011; Oak Ridge is where [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] went after AARO; at In-Q-Tel she worked alongside Gaffney; and at Peraton she shares the board with former NGA director [[Robert Cardillo]], whom Gerb is certain was involved in blocking Lockheed Martin from passing UFO materials to AAWSAP. Whatever legacy exposure O'Sullivan carried through TRW, he says, is "now owned by Northrop Grumman."

## MITRE Timing Coincidence

Gerb's investigation of [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] notes that O'Sullivan's term as Principal Deputy DNI (2011–2017) lines up exactly with the window of an alleged security clampdown at MITRE. It began with a 2011 restriction on historical briefings for new legacy program personnel, when intelligence-community partners were brought into a Navy-administered reverse-engineering program, and ended with MITRE's alleged loss of control over that program by 2017. "I'm sure those dates are just coincidence," Gerb says. He also observes that the PDDNI post is often filled by an agency insider with strong FFRDC ties: he names O'Sullivan and Kerr as holders who "certainly had legacy ties", and her successor [[Susan Gordon]], now a MITRE trustee, as a less certain case.
