**Guglielmo Giovanni Maria Marconi** (1874–1937) was an Italian inventor, electrical engineer, and Nobel laureate in Physics (1909) credited with pioneering long-distance radio transmission and developing the first practical wireless telegraphy system. His work revolutionized global communication and made him known internationally. He was also an Italian Senator and a public supporter of Mussolini's fascist regime, though he kept his research scientifically independent.

## Extraterrestrial Communication Research

Marconi spoke openly in favor of the possibility of extraterrestrial intelligence and pursued the idea of contacting non-human civilizations by radio. A rare 1937 article by Lella Mario, "Guglielmo Marconi: Magician of the Invisible, Ruler of Spaces", documents this interest. It refers to public claims Marconi made in 1920 and 1932 that he was trying to make radio contact with possible extraterrestrial sources.

## Alleged Role in RS-33

Marconi is believed to have possibly been involved in the [[RS-33 (Gabinetto RS 33)]], the top-secret Italian government group set up in June 1933 to examine the craft recovered near [[Magenta, Italy]]. According to accounts provided to researcher [[Roberto Pinotti]], Marconi initially resisted [[Benito Mussolini]]'s assessment that the craft was of terrestrial origin (French, British, or German). Marconi reportedly believed instead that the object was extraterrestrial, a view consistent with his documented interest in the possibility of non-human intelligence.

Gerb's broader timeline of legacy programs states that RS-33 was "spearheaded" by Marconi, citing confirmation from both his daughter and grandson that he worked on the real RS-33 and was "absolutely convinced that extraterrestrials existed." The same video notes that, according to Marconi's family, it may actually have been [[Enrico Fermi]] who spearheaded RS-33, with Marconi taking part but not leading it.

If Marconi was involved, he would have given RS-33 expertise in electromagnetic phenomena and radio technology, possibly to analyze any signals or propulsion characteristics of the recovered craft. The astronomer [[Gino Cecchini]] is a confirmed member of the group's scientific arm, while political oversight came from Mussolini, Foreign Minister [[Galeazzo Ciano]], and Air Marshal [[Italo Balbo]].

## Death

Marconi died of a series of heart attacks in Rome in July 1937, four years after the alleged Magenta recovery. The fascist government gave him a state funeral.
