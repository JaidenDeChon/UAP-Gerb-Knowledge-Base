Donald Henry Rumsfeld (1932–2021) was an American politician and businessman. He was the 13th and 21st United States Secretary of Defense (1975–1977 under Ford; 2001–2006 under George W. Bush) and White House Chief of Staff under President Gerald Ford (1974–1975). In UAP research he is known mainly for a 1975 incident in which he personally denied [[J. Allen Hynek]], the scientific adviser to [[Project Blue Book]], access to information about any secret UAP programs that may have continued after Blue Book closed.

## The Hynek Denial (April 13, 1975)

On April 13, 1975, at the White House, Rumsfeld told Hynek that he did not have a "need to know" whether a secret UAP study existed after [[Project Blue Book]] ended in 1969. Rumsfeld used "need to know", a phrase from compartmentalized security, with the man who had officially run the Air Force's public UFO investigation for 17 years. Hynek took it as confirmation that classified successor programs existed and that he had never been given access to them.

The exchange shows that whatever UAP-related programs continued after Blue Book closed were compartmentalized so tightly that even Blue Book's own chief scientific consultant was formally kept from knowing they existed.

## Secretary of Defense

Rumsfeld was later Secretary of Defense in the years after 9/11, overseeing the invasion of Afghanistan and the Iraq War. He is also remembered for his cryptic "known unknowns" framework for describing the limits of knowledge, which he set out at a 2002 press conference. It has been cited both critically and approvingly in discussions of how to analyze intelligence under uncertainty.

During his time at the Pentagon, Rumsfeld also chose [[Richard Haver]] as Special Assistant to the Secretary of Defense for Intelligence.
