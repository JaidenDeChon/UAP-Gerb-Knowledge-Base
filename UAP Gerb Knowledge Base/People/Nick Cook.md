Nick Cook is a British aviation journalist and author best known for his book *The Hunt for Zero Point* (2001), an investigation into classified anti-gravity research programs within the US and UK military-industrial complex. Cook was aerospace consultant at *Jane's Defence Weekly* for many years. His research is noted for the first-hand access he gained to defense insiders and facilities.

## The Hunt for Zero Point

The book documents Cook's years-long investigation into evidence that the United States and other nations have secretly developed anti-gravity propulsion technology. He drew on declassified documents, interviews with engineers and visits to facilities. The investigation traces a line of descent from post-World War II research into Nazi Bell-type experiments to the American corporate black programs of the Cold War era.

## Encounter with the Astra Lineage Chart

In the late 1990s Cook visited [[Lockheed Martin]]'s Palmdale facility. As he was leaving with [[Jack Gordon]], then head of the Skunk Works, he saw a large wall chart showing the complete lineage of every [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works]] aircraft since the XP-80. The chart went past the Darkstar reconnaissance aircraft, the one that appears in *Top Gun: Maverick*, to an entry labeled "Astra," drawn as an ultra-high-speed triangular reconnaissance craft. It resembled the triangular objects sighted over Belgium in the early 1990s during the [[Belgian UFO Wave]]. When Cook asked Gordon directly about Astra, Gordon became visibly flustered and dismissed it as "an old airliner". Given the context and Gordon's demeanor, Cook found that explanation unconvincing. His account of the Astra lineage chart is cited as indirect evidence that the triangular-craft [[Aurora Program]] exists within Lockheed's official internal records.
