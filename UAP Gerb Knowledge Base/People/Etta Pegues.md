Etta Pegues was a long-time resident of [[Aurora, Texas]] who, aged 86, told *Time* magazine that the 1897 [[Aurora Texas UFO Crash]] story had been made up. Her statement is the most direct first-hand claim that the case was a hoax, and it sits at the centre of the debate over the story's authenticity. (Some accounts, including earlier versions of this knowledge base, spell her name "Etna Pegue".)

## Claim

Pegues said that S. E. Haydon, the local correspondent whose article ran in the *Dallas Morning News* on 19 April 1897, wrote the story as a joke to bring interest to Aurora. The railroad had bypassed the town, she explained, and Aurora was dying. UAP Gerb's iceberg video dates the interview to 1980.

## Place in the debate

Her account stands against the material gathered by [[MUFON]]'s 1973 investigation, led by [[Bill Case]]: two new eyewitness accounts from [[Mary Evans]] and [[Charlie Stevens]], a grave marker that appeared to show a flying saucer, and metal-detector readings at the alleged pilot's grave. MUFON itself called its findings inconclusive and did not rule out a hoax.
