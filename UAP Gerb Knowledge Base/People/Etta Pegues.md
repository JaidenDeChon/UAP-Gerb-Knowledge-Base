Etta Pegues was a long-time resident of [[Aurora, Texas]] who told *Time* magazine, at the age of 86, that the story of the 1897 [[Aurora Texas UFO Crash]] had been made up. Hers is the most direct first-hand claim that the case was a hoax, and it is central to the debate over whether the story is genuine. (Some accounts, including earlier versions of this knowledge base, spell her name "Etna Pegue".)

## Claim

Pegues said that S. E. Haydon, the local correspondent whose article ran in the *Dallas Morning News* on 19 April 1897, wrote the story as a joke to bring interest to Aurora. She explained that the railroad had bypassed the town and Aurora was dying. Gerb's iceberg video dates the interview to 1980.

## Place in the debate

Her account conflicts with the material gathered by [[MUFON]]'s 1973 investigation, led by [[Bill Case]]: two new eyewitness accounts from [[Mary Evans]] and [[Charlie Stevens]], a grave marker that appeared to show a flying saucer, and metal-detector readings at the alleged pilot's grave. MUFON itself called its findings inconclusive and did not rule out a hoax.
