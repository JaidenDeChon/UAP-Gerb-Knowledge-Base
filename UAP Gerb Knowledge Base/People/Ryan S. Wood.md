Ryan S. Wood is an American author and UAP researcher. With his father [[Robert Wood]], a former McDonnell Douglas aerospace engineer, he has worked extensively on authenticating [[Majestic 12]] documents and documenting UFO crash retrieval cases. He wrote *Majic Eyes Only: Earth's Encounters with Extraterrestrial Technology* (2005), a catalog of 74 alleged UFO crash retrieval incidents that Gerb calls his all-time favorite book and uses as a standard crash retrieval reference. He has also spent decades on forensic analysis of approximately 3,500 pages of alleged Majestic 12 documents, which by his count were leaked between 1984 and 1999 from seven different sources.

## Career

Wood runs the Majestic Documents website with his father, and between 2003 and 2009 the two organized seven UFO Crash Retrieval Conferences, the first of them in Las Vegas in November 2003. Gerb notes that it was at the second of these that [[Linda Moulton Howe]] presented [[Andrew Kissner]]'s 1994 statement. *Majic Eyes Only* followed in 2005. Gerb met Wood while working on his August 2024 video on the [[1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval|Coyame crash]], saying he was learning from Wood how to search service records and personnel archives, and later that month published a long interview with him on the [[MJ-12 Documents]].

Gerb draws on *Majic Eyes Only* across his coverage. It rates the Coyame case as medium to high in authenticity and the 1953 [[1953 Garrison, Utah Crash Retrieval|Garrison, Utah]] case as moderate confidence. It records a 1994 crash landing at the UK's Boscombe Down that one magazine speculated was a TR-3A. It also discusses an alleged 1944 Majestic document titled the Special Committee on Non-Terrestrial Science and Technology, Gerb's only documentary lead for the [[Off-World Technologies Division]].

## MJ-12 Document Authentication Work

Wood has developed a weighted system for rating the authenticity of Majestic documents. It draws on several kinds of evidence, including typography and printing analysis, period-accurate terminology, personnel verification, witness testimony, cross-document corroboration, and forensic ink/paper testing. On the forensic evidence, he gives his highest confidence in authenticity to documents including the [[Special Operations Manual (SOM 1-01)]], the [[Eisenhower Briefing Document]], and [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] reports. Apart from the crash retrievals themselves, he considers the Special Operations Manual the most important of the MJ-12 documents.

### Document Provenance Investigation

To trace the document control initials "EWL" (MJ-01) and "JRT" (MJ-04) on the control pages of SOM 1-01, Wood consulted Albuquerque phone books for 1954-1957 and cross-referenced them with National Personnel Records Center files. He found that a Captain Lewis (EWL, as Wood recalls the name) and a man matching "JRT" lived four or five houses apart on Perimeter Road inside [[Kirtland Air Force Base, New Mexico|Kirtland Air Force Base]] at the relevant time. Wood tracked down the JRT, who agreed to meet but denied being the one named in the document, describing himself vaguely as a psychological counselling officer. A private investigator found EWL's family and learned that he had died and was buried in Florida.

### Sources and Leakers

Wood traces the leaks through a handful of recipients, the largest cache going to researcher [[Tim Cooper]]. Its main source was the pseudonymous "[[Thomas Cantwheel]]," who, near death from cancer, wrote to Cooper describing a career as a Counter Intelligence Corps officer in the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] from 1942 to 1958. In the same video Gerb presents the case made by Reddit researcher "Harry is White Hot" that Cantwheel was Boris Dimitri Tarasoff. Tarasoff was a CIC officer (1942-1945) trained at Camp Ritchie, Maryland, who later worked for the CIA (1956-1968). He served at the Mexico City CIA station in 1963, transcribing surveillance of Soviet compounds including Lee Harvey Oswald traffic, and lived in San Leandro, California, where Cooper believed Cantwheel lived.

## Response to Alexander Disinformation Theory

Wood strongly disputes [[John B. Alexander|Colonel John B. Alexander]]'s claim that [[Special Operations Manual (SOM 1-01)|SOM 1-01]] was created in 1954 as Soviet disinformation. He argues that exposing detailed crash retrieval infrastructure, facility locations, entity descriptions, and protocols merely to deceive the Soviets makes no sense as psychological warfare. If SOM 1-01 were disinformation aimed at the Soviets, he argues, it would not be part of a 19-year leak campaign of 3,500 pages from seven different sources using multiple delivery methods. He notes that the Soviets had their own crashes and had penetrated the Manhattan Project within a year, so they would certainly have intelligence on U.S. UAP activities.

Wood states: "I don't have any information that any document that's listed on the website is fake. I have no credible evidence that it's genuinely faked." He says he has found "no credible objections" that withstand forensic scrutiny. He maintains that some documents remain in his "neutral basket" until there is further evidence, but he believes the core documents show overwhelming signs of authenticity.

## UFO DX Research Tool

Wood created UFO DX (UFO-dx.com), a ChatGPT tool trained on 500-800 UFO books, so that researchers can query the accumulated knowledge of ufology. Users can ask natural language questions on any topic in ufology, and the tool bases its answers on Wood's curated library of research materials. Gerb says he has used it dozens of times in his own research.

## Coyame Case Assessment

In *Majic Eyes Only*, Wood rates the [[1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval]] case as medium to high in authenticity, and Gerb uses the book as one of his four main sources on the case, beside the [[Denb Report]]. Wood's Majestic files also hold an Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit summary report that Gerb quotes on the case's precedents. Wood has no copy of the original Denb Report, which Gerb regrets, since it could otherwise be put through the kind of forensic testing Wood applies to the MJ-12 documents.
