Nathan is a former United States Marine Corps team leader who served during the 2009 Indonesia humanitarian relief mission. He has publicly disputed [[Michael Herrera]]'s UAP encounter testimony, though he acknowledges he was not present during the specific mission where Herrera claims the UAP sighting occurred.

## Contradictory Testimony

Nathan came forward after Herrera's public disclosure to claim that Herrera fabricated the UAP encounter. However, Nathan's testimony contains significant limitations:

**Temporal Disconnect**: Nathan explicitly stated he was not boots-on-the-ground with Herrera during the alleged UAP encounter mission. He was Herrera's team leader at a different point during the broader Indonesia deployment but cannot provide eyewitness refutation of Herrera's specific account.

**Photographic Evidence**: Nathan provided a photograph of two Marines on a CH-53 helicopter allegedly being flown into Indonesia without rifles, claiming one of the individuals is Herrera. This photograph is significant because it contradicts Herrera's claim that his team was armed with M16 A4 rifles during their mission. However:
- Herrera denies being in the photograph and states he can identify the two Marines shown
- Investigator [[Joey Is Not My Name]] discovered that the first wave of Marines in the Indonesia relief operation were armed due to heavy terrorist presence in the region, but subsequent waves were ordered to disarm after an Air Force Colonel objected to the optics
- This creates ambiguity about which deployment wave Nathan's photograph documents

**Paradoxical Logic**: Analysts noted that if Herrera were fabricating his account, he could have simply acknowledged being on a separate unarmed mission with Nathan while maintaining his armed UAP mission story. Instead, Herrera flatly denies being in Nathan's photograph — a strange choice for someone attempting to construct a believable false narrative.

**Character Assessment**: In some accounts, Nathan dismissed Herrera's credibility by citing behaviors like weight lifting and spending time alone as evidence of mental instability, an assessment that lacks professional psychological basis.

## Investigative Context

Multiple Marines from Herrera's platoon were contacted by [[Joey Is Not My Name]] during his investigation. Of those who responded, all except one expressed belief that Herrera was lying. However, when pressed, these Marines acknowledged they were either in different squads, remained on the USS Denver, or were otherwise not present during the specific alleged UAP encounter. None provided firsthand refutation of the events Herrera described.
