C.M. Kosemen (also known as "Nemo Ramjet") is a Turkish author, paleoartist, and speculative biologist. He is best known for *All Tomorrows: A Billion Year Chronicle of the Myriad Species and Varying Fortunes of Man* (2006), a landmark work of Speculative Evolution that imagines a billion years of future evolution in which humanity's descendants split into many post-human forms. Kosemen works as a professional paleoartist and with academic paleontologists, and he is recognized internationally in both scientific illustration and speculative biology.

## All Tomorrows

*All Tomorrows* follows humanity's descendants from the near future to about one billion years from now. Humans from Earth colonize space and are conquered and genetically altered by an alien civilization called the Qu. Over hundreds of millions of years they then diversify into hundreds of radically different post-human species, each adapted to a different planetary environment. The book is notable for:

- Applying real principles of evolutionary biology (convergent evolution, genetic drift, isolation, adaptive radiation) to speculation about the future.
- Exploring the philosophical questions raised when posthumans diverge this far, including sapience, memory, cultural continuity, and what it means to be human across geological time.
- Working both as hard science fiction and as a meditation on extinction, survival, and identity.

The book is often cited as a standout example of the Speculative Evolution genre. Gerb recommends it as a starting point for thinking seriously about what extraterrestrial biology might look like.

## Other Work

Kosemen has worked with paleontologist Darren Naish on books that ask how plausible cryptozoological creatures would be if they existed as real biological organisms (*Hunting Monsters*, 2016, with Naish and John Conway). With Naish and John Conway he also co-wrote *All Yesterdays* (2012), a book that challenged overly conservative conventions in paleoart.
