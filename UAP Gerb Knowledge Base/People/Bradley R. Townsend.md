Captain Bradley R. Townsend, USAF, wrote *Space Based Satellite Tracking and Characterization Utilizing Non-Imaging Passive Sensors* (March 2008, AFIT/GA/ENY/08-M06), a master's thesis at the [[Air Force Institute of Technology]] at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. It revisits the problem of determining the orbit or motion of [[Fast Walkers|fast walkers]] and is the second of the two papers US Northern Command released to [[John Greenewald]] in 2013. The transcript of UAP Gerb's video renders his name "Bradley R. Townson".

## The 2008 thesis

The thesis defines the term directly: satellite-based sensors looking down at Earth "occasionally observe reflected light from an object passing through the image which is moving too fast relative to the background of the image to be located within the atmosphere. These objects are commonly called Fast Walkers." It comments on the 1972 study behind [[Richard P. Osedacz]]'s 1989 thesis and refers to a database drawn from ballistic missile early warning satellites. In [[FASTWALKERS – UFOs Outside Earth]], Gerb notes that its methods reached a similar result, with no way to determine the range to a fast walker target, and that the warning satellites it mentions point again to the [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]].
