Dawn M. Dunlop is a retired U.S. Air Force major general and test pilot who served from 1988 to 2021. She was among the first American women selected to fly combat aircraft, was the first woman to fly the F-22, and was the first woman to command an Air Force test wing. After serving as vice commander of the [[412th Test Wing]] at [[Edwards Air Force Base]], she commanded it from August 2009 to March 2012. She later commanded the NATO Airborne Early Warning and Control Force (2015-2018) and directed the Department of Defense Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO) in 2018-2019. In 2019 she left SAPCO amid inspector general investigations into complaints of a toxic work environment. Her final post was Director of Operational Capability Requirements in the Deputy Chief of Staff for Strategy, Integration and Requirements at Headquarters Air Force, and she retired in November 2021.

## Role in UAP research

In the UAP Gerb video *The Hidden Wing*, Gerb names Dunlop as an official he believes kept UFO legacy programs away from official channels and who may have worked inside them at the test and evaluation level. He compares her SAPCO post with the role he attributes to Air Force general [[Marshal Ward|H. Marshal Ward]]. According to the [[Wilson-Davis Memo|Wilson-Davis notes]], Ward, as SAPCO director, pointed Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] toward legacy-program records. Gerb argues that the SAPCO director still acts as a gatekeeper, possibly answering to the director of the [[National Program Special Management Staff (NPMS)]]. He puts weight on her 412th Test Wing command, which the video dates to 2009-2012, and says witness Ed received letters of commendation signed by Dunlop during his time "in legacy" at the 412th. The video asks the administration to subpoena her. UAP Gerb's *Special Access Required Vol.2* also lists her with Ward as a former SAPCO-level figure.
