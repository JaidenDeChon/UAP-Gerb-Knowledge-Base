William S. Steinman is an American UFO researcher and author, best known for his investigation into the alleged 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO crash and for his contacts with Dr. [[Robert Sarbacher]], who answered his letter in 1983, and Dr. [[Eric A. Walker]], who spoke to him by telephone in 1987. Those contacts produced two of the most significant insider admissions in early UFO research history. His 1986 book *UFO Crash at Aztec: A Well Kept Secret*, written with Wendelle C. Stevens in a printing of only 1,000 copies, revived the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|Aztec case]].

## Investigation of the Aztec Case

Steinman investigated the alleged 1948 Aztec crash at length. He analyzed court records from the fraud trial of [[Silas Newton]] and [[Leo GeBauer]], the two con men whose claims about the Aztec case were later used to discredit it, looking for evidence that a genuine recovery event had occurred independently of their fraudulent claims.

### UFO Crash at Aztec (1986)

Steinman became interested in Aztec after reading about it in [[Leonard Stringfield]]'s *UFO Crash/Retrieval Status Reports*. He took the case as genuine and built it up again from his own sources, and Stringfield wrote the book's foreword. In the book's reconstruction, as summarised in UAP Gerb's 2025 video, three radar sites tracked the craft. General [[George C. Marshall]] was notified and called in the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] from Camp Hale, Colorado, together with [[Vannevar Bush]] and a scientific team including [[Detlev Bronk]], [[John Von Neumann]], [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] and [[Carl A. Heiland]]. The disc was opened through a porthole, dismantled using interlocking keys and taken to Los Alamos, and [[Eric Henry Wang]] joined the project in November 1949. Steinman named Heiland as the original source of the leak to Newton and GeBauer, and as the scientist who refused to testify against GeBauer's devices. He first learned of the rancher [[Valentine Archuleta]]'s sighting in 1982, and filed FOIA requests in 1984 that confirmed the Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit had existed.

Gerb praises the book as the work of a researcher who "admirably cold contacted" key figures. Its account came from sources unconnected to Scully and Newton, so it is not circular reporting. Gerb's chief criticism is that Steinman states much of this as fact without citing sources or corroboration. Steinman said that while he was researching Wang, his mail was tampered with and he found a tracking device on his van.

## The 1983 Sarbacher and Walker Correspondence

Steinman and [[Stanton Friedman]] tracked down Dr. [[Robert Sarbacher]], a physicist and consultant to the DoD Research and Development Board who had been [[Wilbert B. Smith]]'s source in 1950. In 1983 Steinman wrote to him with twelve questions about UFO-related government programs. Sarbacher's November 1983 reply is one of the most detailed written admissions by a credentialed government insider. He said he had no association with the recovery personnel, did not know the dates of any crashes, and had been invited to Research and Development Board discussions he could not attend. He did say:

- That [[Vannevar Bush]], [[John Von Neumann]], and [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] were definitely involved in crash retrieval efforts.
- That recovered materials were extremely light and very tough.
- That recovered occupants were reportedly constructed like insects due to their low mass.
- That he saw reports, though no photographs.

Steinman traced the one man Sarbacher remembered attending every meeting to [[Eric A. Walker]], former president of Penn State University and executive secretary of the Research and Development Board. Walker's first reply was a cut-up letter typed on the bottom of Steinman's own, reading "Stop. Don't try and find me." Steinman wrote again on 31 May 1984 without an answer, but when he telephoned on 30 August 1987, weeks after the [[MJ-12 Documents]] became public, Walker confirmed attending the crash retrieval meetings at [[Wright Field]] that Sarbacher described (circa 1949–1950). When Steinman raised the Majestic 12 documents, Walker responded: "Yes, I know of MJ-12. I have known of them for 40 years." Walker advised Steinman to drop the subject, stating he was "delving into an area that you can do absolutely nothing about" and that he was "chasing after and fighting with windmills."

## Significance

Steinman's contacts with Sarbacher in 1983 and Walker in 1987 were a turning point in UAP research. A civilian researcher had reached two high-ranking, government-connected men independently, and both gave consistent admissions about crash retrieval programs and the involvement of the US government's most senior wartime scientists. The letters and interview notes from these contacts are primary sources and remain central to the evidentiary record.
