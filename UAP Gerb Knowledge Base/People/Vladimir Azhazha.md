Vladimir G. Azhazha was a Soviet naval officer who also worked as an ethologist (UFO researcher). Holding both roles gave him unusual institutional standing to bridge official naval authority and the investigation of anomalous phenomena. He authorized the mandatory UFO reporting directive that [[Fleet Admiral Nikolai Smirnov]] issued after the 1977 [[Soviet Submarine Repair Ship Vulga Sighting]].

## Role in Soviet UFO Documentation

On October 7, 1977, in the Barents Sea, nine discs circled the Soviet submarine repair ship Vulga for 18 minutes while its communications failed. After this encounter, the Soviet Navy formalized UFO reporting requirements. Azhazha authorized the resulting Smirnov directive, which Naval Deputy Chief of Staff P. Noitov also signed. Their involvement reflects how seriously the Soviet naval establishment treated the phenomenon at the institutional level, even if public acknowledgment remained suppressed. The directive required Soviet hydrographic, scientific research and reconnaissance ships to report UFO sightings, which made it a Soviet functional equivalent of American restrictions such as [[OPNAV 3820]].

Because Azhazha also had a career as a civilian UFO researcher, he was a significant link between the Soviet military's classified awareness of the phenomenon and the limited public discourse that was tolerated or emerged in the post-Soviet period.
