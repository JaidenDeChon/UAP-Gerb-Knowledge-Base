HJ is the pseudonym of a former United States Army servicemember who claimed to have witnessed an [[Egg-shaped UAP|egg-shaped craft]] crash-land at [[Camp Polk, Louisiana]] in July 1953 while serving as a 17-year-old private in B Company of the [[US Army 24th Infantry]]. His testimony was documented by pioneering crash retrieval researcher [[Leonard Stringfield]] between 1980 and 1982 through *UFO Crash/Retrieval Status Report 3*.

## The Camp Polk Incident

According to HJ, at approximately 7 p.m. during routine duty maneuvers, his platoon under the command of one Sergeant RS observed an egg-shaped object crash-land in a remote area. Both A and B companies were ordered to the crash site. HJ approached within 15 yards of the object before a Lieutenant Whitting — a verifiable officer at Camp Polk at the time — ordered Company B to withdraw to 100 yards, stating that "peons" like HJ needed to "get out of the way."

## The Craft

The ovoid craft was as wide as a three-bedroom single-story ranch house (estimated 35–70 feet, comparable to [[Jonathan Weygandt]]'s 20-meter estimate). It had no windows or lights and was surrounded by a fin-like protrusion on its equator that was still rotating — a detail strikingly similar to the sweeping band of light Wagant observed circling his [[Egg-shaped UAP|egg-shaped craft]] in Peru in 1997. The ground around the craft was badly burned, reduced to a powdery substance like crushed brick, and HJ could still feel heat emanating from the vehicle.

## The Occupants

A special detachment with medics, an ambulance, and specialized equipment arrived on scene and approached an open hatch on the side of the craft. Two medics carried away a stretcher containing a body. Three living occupants then emerged, aided by medics and apparently injured. HJ described the beings as 3.5 to 4 feet tall, of slight build, walking as if they had no knees — bending only at the hips. They had large heads with indistinguishable features that HJ believed were covered by helmets, and wore tight-fitting, dull metallic-green uniforms. One occupant "kept looking at the one in the litter and made strange noises."

HJ later learned the living occupants were hospitalized and placed in isolation but all died in captivity. The corpses were reportedly sent to a medical center near Washington, D.C. — possibly Fort Detrick, approximately 40 miles from the capital, where [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] manages the National Biodefense Analysis and Countermeasures Center.
