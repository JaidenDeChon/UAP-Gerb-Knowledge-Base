Robert Scott "Bob" Lazar is a controversial figure in UFO research. In 1989 he publicly claimed to have worked on reverse-engineering recovered extraterrestrial craft at a secret facility near [[Area 51]] known as S-4, allegedly built into the mountains near [[Papoose Lake|Papoose dry lake bed]]. Lazar came forward through Las Vegas television journalist [[George Knapp]]. His claims covered nine disc-shaped craft being studied at S-4, an exotic propulsion system using an element he called Element 115, and the organizational structure of the reverse engineering program, for which he said he was hired through the contractor [[EG&G]]. His credentials and background have been disputed, but contemporary UAP analysis still refers to his descriptions of saucer flight dynamics and propulsion systems.

Gerb mentions Lazar in more than a dozen videos but has never covered him in depth. He calls him "controversial as he is," and in his Manhattan Project 2.0 and underground-bases videos he deliberately leaves Lazar's testimony out of the analysis because of that controversy and, he says, his own bias. He nonetheless believes S-4 is a real place.

## Background

Lazar was born on 26 January 1959 in Coral Gables, Florida. Gerb notes that Lazar verifiably attended Pierce College, the same college as the Skunk Works claimant [[Don Phillips]]. His claimed degrees from MIT and Caltech have not been confirmed through institutional records; he has said his records were erased. According to Gerb's Manhattan Project 2.0 video, physicist Edward Teller allegedly got Lazar hired at S-4 under EG&G. Lazar dates that work to 1988 and 1989.

## S-4 Facility and Reverse Engineering Claims

Lazar described S-4 as a facility built into the mountainside near Papoose Lake south of [[Area 51]] (Groom Lake), with aircraft hangars concealed by doors that appeared to be part of the mountain face. He claimed to have worked on nine recovered disc-shaped craft of non-human origin. He said his role was to study the propulsion system of one craft, the "sport model," which he described as working by gravity amplification. It used an element not then synthesized, which he designated Element 115. Element 115 was first synthesized in 2003 and named moscovium in 2016; advocates and skeptics debate whether this validates Lazar's claim. Gerb compares the conical component that the Dugway witness [[MS (Dugway Witness)|MS]] saw generating power in a partly dismantled craft with the reactor Lazar drew for the sport model.

Lazar also described tight compartmentalization at S-4: teams studying the propulsion of recovered craft could not share findings with other teams, often only with their immediate lab partner. Gerb notes that everyone from [[David Grusch]] to Lazar has described the same need-to-know structure that the Manhattan Project made famous. In Lazar's accounts, the technology was so far beyond human comprehension that reverse engineering progress was agonizingly slow. In his video on two Marine witnesses, Gerb sets this beside statements by [[Ross Coulthart]], David Grusch and the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] that recovered-craft programs have made little headway, and cites them all for his doubt that humans have reverse-engineered any non-human technology.

Lazar's story also features in Gerb's account of [[Wackenhut Services]], the contractor that guarded the perimeter of Area 51: its guards famously caught Lazar and his friends camping on public land overlooking the base to watch test flights of the sport model. In his underground-bases video, Gerb dates the rise of underground UFO bases as a subject to 1989 and Lazar's S-4 claims.

## Tonopah's Site 4

Some have argued that Lazar borrowed the name "S-4" from Site 4 at the [[Tonopah Test Range (TTR)|Tonopah Test Range]], an acknowledged facility. Gerb disagrees. In his Lockheed Martin video he shows that Tonopah's Site 4 sits out in the open and does not match Lazar's description of a site built into the mountains by Papoose Lake, and he says he is confident Area 51's S-4 is "a very real site near or exactly where" Lazar placed it.

## Flight Dynamics Claims

Lazar described recovered alien craft as flying at an angle with their "belly towards their destination" rather than flying level. He attributed this to the gravitational propulsion systems he claimed to have studied. Gerb points out the same angled flight in many historical UFO photographs and reports, including the [[McMinnville UFO Photographs]] from 1950 and the disc-shaped craft seen in the [[Flyby Footage]].

## Credibility Assessment

Gerb groups Lazar with [[Mark McCandlish]], [[Bill Uhouse]], and [[Boyd Bushman]] as whistleblowers whose astounding claims have persisted for decades but lack the legal testimony or evidentiary backing of witnesses such as [[David Grusch]], [[Robert Jacobs]], or [[Robert Salas]]. In his Kingman video he contrasts Lazar with [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]], whose career he could check against the record, as a witness of the kind he treats as verifiable.
