Barney Hill (1922–1969) was an American postal worker and civil rights activist, and the male witness in the [[Betty and Barney Hill Abduction Incident]] of September 19, 1961. It is one of the most extensively documented and most influential alien abduction cases in UFO history. His testimony, especially from his hypnotic regression sessions, brought in physiological detail and emotional intensity that later became hallmarks of the abduction narrative.

## Background

Barney Hill worked as a laborer for the US Postal Service in Portsmouth, New Hampshire, and was active in the local NAACP chapter and the US Civil Rights Commission. He was married to [[Betty Hill]], a white social worker. Interracial couples were unusual enough in 1961 that this added complexity and potential social risk when the couple went public. Barney was reportedly skeptical of UFOs at the time of the encounter, and at first dismissed the light in the sky that concerned Betty as likely an aircraft.

## The Encounter

While driving on US Route 3 near [[Lancaster, New Hampshire]], on the way back from a vacation near Niagara Falls, Barney stopped the car to look through binoculars at what Betty identified as an approaching craft. He described the hovering object as resembling a large pancake with illuminated windows. Looking up at it, he saw 8 to 11 humanoid figures peering out at him. All but one of the figures retreated into the craft. The one that remained communicated with him telepathically, telling him to "stay where you are and keep looking." Barney panicked and went back to the car, and the couple drove away. They arrived home some three hours late, with fragmented and disturbed memories of the encounter.

## Hypnotic Regression

Barney's hypnosis sessions with Boston psychiatrist Dr. Benjamin Simon produced some of the most disturbing testimony in the abduction literature. He recalled feeling compelled to pull off the road into the woods, where he encountered six figures on a dirt road. Under hypnosis he described the beings' eyes with particular horror: "Oh those eyes — they're in my brain... I was told to close my eyes because I saw two eyes coming close to mine and I felt like the eyes had pushed into my eyes... All I see are these eyes... they're just up close to me pressing against my eyes." He reported being carried aboard the craft and given a long physical examination. In his report to [[National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena|NICAP]], he explicitly described the beings as "somehow not human".

## Death and Legacy

Barney Hill died of a cerebral hemorrhage on February 25, 1969, at age 46, before the abduction narrative had fully entered mainstream awareness. Journalist John Fuller published his and Betty's story in *The Interrupted Journey* (1966), and it was adapted into a 1975 television film. Barney's testimony remains among the most emotionally detailed and internally consistent in the abduction literature.
