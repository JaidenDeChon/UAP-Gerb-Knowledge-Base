**William Hamilton** is a UFO researcher and author of the 1992 book *Cosmic Top Secret*, which UAP Gerb describes as "the most underappreciated book in all of UFOlogy" and "a truly excellent piece decades ahead of its time." Hamilton was among the first researchers to document the term "ARV" (Alien Reproduction Vehicle), to investigate Northrop Grumman's [[Tejon Ranch]] underground facility, and to serve as a conduit between key UFO witnesses and researcher [[Steven Greer]].

## Cosmic Top Secret (1992)

Hamilton's 1992 book contains what UAP Gerb identifies as the first recorded mention of the term "ARV" (though Hamilton misidentified the acronym as "alien replicated vehicle" rather than "alien reproduction vehicle"). The book predates by nine years the rise to fame of the [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle]] account through Steven Greer's 2001 Disclosure Project. Hamilton spoke with Bill Scott of *Aviation Week & Space Technology* to discuss the [[Brad Sorensen]] fluxliner testimony in the early 1990s. He subsequently passed both primary witness Brad Sorensen and aerospace illustrator [[Bill McDonald]] to Greer, who then featured them at the Disclosure Project event.

## Tejon Ranch Investigation

Hamilton drew serious early research attention to the Antelope Valley region of California near [[Edwards Air Force Base]], noting that Lockheed's Skunk Works, Rockwell, [[McDonnell Douglas]], and [[Northrop Grumman]] all maintained facilities in [[Palmdale, California]] — the nation's then-chief center of aerospace technology — around [[Air Force Plant 42]]. Hamilton specifically highlighted Northrop's secret underground installation in the Tehachapi Mountains at the [[Tejon Ranch]] RCS facility. He documented:

- A 1988 sighting of a "giant boomerang" over the Northrop facility
- Reports of a flying saucer seen taking off from a building on the property
- That the Tejon site recorded a disproportionate number of anomalous sightings compared to nearby Lockheed and McDonnell Douglas RCS facilities
- Engineer testimonies describing the facility as a UAP reverse engineering and material exploitation site, with an underground complex reportedly comprising 42 levels nicknamed "the Anthill"
