Mark F. Moynihan is a retired US intelligence officer whose career spanned the CIA, the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|National Reconnaissance Office]] and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence. He joined the CIA as a career trainee and later served as chief of policy and interim chief of staff of the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]]. At the NRO he rose through engineering posts to become chief systems engineer and director of policy for the Ground Enterprise Directorate. In the early 2010s he was the Assistant Deputy Director of National Intelligence for Acquisition, Technology and Facilities.

## Alleged Role in UAP Programs

According to Gerb, Moynihan also directed the CIA's Office of Advanced Technologies and Programs and the [[CIA Office of Global Access]] between 2000 and 2010. Gerb names him among thirteen alleged gatekeepers of the legacy programs, because his career "checks the boxes" Gerb associates with running them: the Directorate of Science and Technology (DS&T), the Office of Global Access (which Gerb alleges coordinates crash retrievals outside the United States), the NRO, and the acquisition office of the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI), which shares oversight of NRO acquisitions. Gerb also compares Moynihan's career with the nearly identical path of [[Doug Wolfe]].
