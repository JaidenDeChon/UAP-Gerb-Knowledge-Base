Erich von Däniken (born 1935) is a Swiss author and the main popularizer of the [[Ancient Astronaut Theory]], the hypothesis that extraterrestrial beings visited Earth in antiquity and influenced, or directly took part in, the development of ancient human civilizations. His 1968 book *Chariots of the Gods?* (*Erinnerungen an die Zukunft*) became an international bestseller and started a genre of popular speculation that explains archaeological mysteries as extraterrestrial intervention. Von Däniken has since written over 40 books and is a co-founder of the *Ancient Alien* entertainment franchise concept.

## Core Claims

Von Däniken argues that ancient structures, artwork and religious texts around the world contain evidence of extraterrestrial visits. His central claims include:

- Megalithic structures such as the Pyramids of Giza, Easter Island's moai and Stonehenge required engineering knowledge beyond what ancient human civilizations had. Von Däniken attributes their construction to extraterrestrial help or instruction.
- Many ancient carvings, paintings and sculptures, he says, depict astronauts in space suits, spacecraft and advanced technology. One of the pieces of evidence he cites most often is the so-called "Palenque astronaut" carved on a Mayan sarcophagus lid.
- Major religious traditions, he argues, are records of contact with extraterrestrials whom ancient humans took for gods. His readings of the Old Testament include two in particular:
  - He reinterprets the prophet [[Ezekiel]]'s visions of "wheels within wheels" and of four-faced beings ([[Biblically Accurate Angels]]) as encounters with advanced spacecraft.
  - He proposes that the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah by "fire and brimstone" records a nuclear detonation.
- The Nazca Lines, large geoglyphs etched into the Peruvian desert, were in his view landing strips or navigational markers for extraterrestrial aircraft.

## Reception and Criticism

Academic archaeologists, historians and anthropologists broadly reject von Däniken's claims. Critics argue that his ideas imply ancient people were less ingenious than they were, that he reads texts and art selectively and without support from scholarly consensus, and that conventional archaeology has satisfactorily explained much of what he presents as inexplicable.

His personal credibility has also been questioned, partly because of fraud convictions in Switzerland in the early 1970s, before *Chariots of the Gods* had its greatest success.

## Influence

Although academics reject it, von Däniken's work has had a very wide cultural influence. His books sold tens of millions of copies and directly inspired the television series *Ancient Aliens* (History Channel, 2009–present), which made the ancient astronaut hypothesis a staple of popular UFO and conspiracy culture.
