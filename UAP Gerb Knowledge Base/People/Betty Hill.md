Betty Hill (née Barrett, 1919–2004) was an American social worker and one of the two central witnesses in the [[Betty and Barney Hill Abduction Incident]], which is widely regarded as the first extensively documented alien abduction case in the United States. She and her husband [[Barney Hill]] reported being abducted on September 19, 1961, near [[Lancaster, New Hampshire]], while driving home from a vacation near Niagara Falls. Her account was corroborated under hypnotic regression, reported to federal investigators and later serialized in national media, and it established many of the narrative conventions that abduction accounts followed for decades.

## Background

Betty Hill worked as a social worker in New Hampshire. She was already interested in UFO sightings before the incident and had read Donald Keyhoe's book on flying saucers. She and Barney, a postal worker and civil rights activist, were an interracial couple, which was unusual in 1961 and made their public disclosure more socially complicated.

## The Encounter

As they drove south on US Route 3, Betty saw a bright point of light that moved erratically and grew larger. Through binoculars she saw a disc-shaped craft with multicolored flashing lights crossing the face of the Moon. The craft descended rapidly toward their car and hovered silently about 80–100 feet above them. To Barney it looked like a large pancake with lit windows. Both of them saw humanoid figures looking out. Betty reported that one figure stayed at the window as the others retreated, and that [[Barney Hill|Barney]] received a telepathic message to remain still.

The couple got home about three hours later than expected. Their 178-mile drive home took seven hours instead of the usual four, and neither of them could account for the missing time.

## Post-Encounter and Hypnosis

About ten days after the encounter, Betty had vivid dreams on five nights in a row in which she recalled being escorted aboard a craft by small humanoids. She and Barney later went to Boston psychiatrist Dr. Benjamin Simon for hypnotic regression therapy to deal with their anxiety about the experience. Under hypnosis, Betty gave detailed recollections of being brought aboard the craft, talking with its occupants and observing the interior. She described a conversation with a tall "leader" figure about the craft's navigation, and recalled seeing a three-dimensional star map in what she believed was the control room. She drew the map from memory.

## The Star Map Controversy

Betty Hill's hand-drawn star map became one of the most analyzed artifacts of UFO research. In 1968, amateur astronomer Marjorie Fish tried to match its pattern to known star positions and concluded that it showed Sol, Zeta Reticuli 1 and Zeta Reticuli 2 as the origin stars of the craft's occupants. Some researchers said the odds of the pattern being a random match were at least 1,000 to one against. [[Carl Sagan]] challenged this interpretation in his 1980s *Cosmos* series, arguing that the identification depended on picking selectively from many possible star configurations.

## Later Life and Legacy

Betty Hill became a prominent figure in UFO research and lectured on her experience for the rest of her life. She was a member of [[National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena|NICAP]], and her account stayed consistent. She died on October 17, 2004. Journalist John Fuller told the Hills' story in the book *The Interrupted Journey* (1966), which was later adapted into a 1975 television film.
