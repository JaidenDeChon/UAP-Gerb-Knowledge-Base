Major Hector Quintanilla Jr. was head of [[Project Blue Book]] from 1963 to 1969, the program's final period of leadership. He is known for his 1966 testimony before Congress, which included statements that [[J. Allen Hynek]] later identified as deliberate falsehoods. Quintanilla's tenure was the most actively dismissive period of Blue Book's operation and ended with the program's termination in 1969.

## Congressional Hearing (1966)

The House Armed Services Committee hearing on UFOs on April 5, 1966 was the first closed congressional session on the subject. At the hearing, Quintanilla testified that Project Blue Book had "no radar cases that are unexplained," and attributed the vast majority of radar detections to temperature inversions or misidentified phenomena. Hynek sat with Quintanilla on the Blue Book panel at the hearing. He later publicly identified this statement as an irrefutable lie, citing documented radar unknowns in Blue Book's own files, including the 1951 Goose Bay case, the 1956 Lakenheath case, and the 1957 Shreveport case.

Secretary of the Air Force Harold D. Brown was also on the hearing panel with Quintanilla. Hynek had grown dissatisfied with Quintanilla's direction of Blue Book and with his own role, which he described as being "a puppet of the Air Force who only says what the Air Force wants me to say". That dissatisfaction led him to prepare a surprise statement for the hearings, requesting that the USAF commission an independent civilian panel to examine the UFO problem critically.
