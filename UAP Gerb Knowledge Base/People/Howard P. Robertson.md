Dr. Howard Percy Robertson was an American mathematician and physicist at the California Institute of Technology. He was a CIA consultant and chaired the [[Robertson Panel]], the 1953 scientific advisory committee that recommended the US government carry out a public campaign to suppress interest in UFOs. In UAP research, his role on the panel is seen as a turning point in the institutionalization of the [[UFO Stigma]].

## The Robertson Panel

The CIA picked Robertson in January 1953 to chair a scientific review of Air Force UFO case files, during the [[1952 UFOs Over Washington DC|1952 Washington DC UFO sightings wave]] and a period of heightened public interest in UFOs. The panel, named after him, met over four days, 14 to 17 January 1953, in eight sessions, and by Gerb's count reviewed only 23 of the 2,331 available [[Project Blue Book]] case files.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the panel held four formal sessions, as Gerb says in the video. Durant's memo on the panel (the [[Robertson Panel Report]]) records eight sessions over four days, Wednesday 14 to Saturday 17 January. The 23-of-2,331 figure is now credited to Gerb.

Panel signatory [[Thornton Page]] later admitted in a signed statement dated October 3, 1993, that Robertson told committee members before the first meeting to debunk all UFO reports presented to them and to "reduce public concern." Gerb takes this as showing that the panel's conclusions were decided in advance. [[J. Allen Hynek]] said the panel was "handed down" what he called the "unwritten law of the Air Force: don't rock the boat, play it cool, don't get the public excited."

> [!edit] Attribution clarified 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said as fact that Page's statement confirmed the conclusions were decided in advance, and that Robertson passed on the "unwritten law". The first is Gerb's reading of Page; Hynek said the panel was handed the law, without naming Robertson as the one who passed it on.

The panel's final recommendation was that the national security agencies should strip UFOs of "the special status they have been given", and, as part of an education programme, "reassure the public of the total lack of evidence of inimical forces behind the phenomena". Gerb renders this as telling the public there is "no legitimate evidence" of their existence. Watching civilian UFO groups, because of their potential influence on mass thinking and possible use for subversive purposes, was proposed in Durant's unofficial Part II of the memo, not in the formal report.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the panel recommended telling the public there was "no legitimate evidence" for UFOs' existence, and listed monitoring civilian groups among its recommendations. The first is Gerb's paraphrase of the report's "total lack of evidence of inimical forces behind the phenomena", and the monitoring idea is only in Durant's Part II.
