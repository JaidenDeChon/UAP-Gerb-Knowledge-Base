John Greenewald Jr. is an American researcher and Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) activist who founded and runs [[The Black Vault]], one of the largest privately run collections of declassified US government documents. He started it in 1996, at the age of fifteen, and has since filed thousands of FOIA requests about UFO/UAP programs, classified projects and national security operations, publishing the responses and documents. In Gerb's videos he turns up mostly as the person whose requests were refused, or whose releases put a document on the public record.

## Fast Walker FOIA Campaign

Over many years Greenewald pressed for information about [[Fast Walkers|fast walkers]] and slow walkers, the military terms for objects that [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites and other space-based sensors detect entering or leaving Earth's atmosphere. Gerb argues that the separate term lets the government refuse such requests with blanket classification.

### 2013 Request to US Northern Command

In 2013 Greenewald asked [[US Northern Command]] (NORTHCOM), which oversees [[NORAD]], for any reports, information or detailed military documentation on fast walkers and slow walkers. NORTHCOM replied that fast walker documentation is "currently and properly classified" under Executive Order 13526, as material "considered critical to National Defense or foreign policy."

The denial was only partial. NORTHCOM released two unclassified Air Force Institute of Technology theses showing that fast walkers were being detected and analyzed:

1. *Orbit Determination of Sunlight Illuminated Objects Detected by Overhead Platforms* (1989), by USAF Captain [[Richard P. Osedacz]]
2. *Space Based Satellite Tracking and Characterization Utilizing Non-Imaging Passive Sensors* (2008), by USAF Captain [[Bradley R. Townsend]]

The 1989 thesis says fast walker data has been recorded by early warning satellites since 1972, which Gerb takes to mean DSP.

### 2023 Request to US Space Force

In late 2023 Greenewald received what Gerb calls a "decisive and final" answer to his many requests on fast and slow walkers from the [[US Space Force]], which now runs the space surveillance work, DSP included. It was a full denial under FOIA exemption 1, citing classification in the interest of national defense or foreign policy, and released no documents.

## Aurora and AATIP

In 1997 Greenewald inquired into the [[Aurora Program|Aurora program]], a secret aircraft program Gerb links to [[Lockheed Martin]], and was denied any access. In 2019 a FOIA request of his confirmed the 38 studies commissioned under [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]] that journalist [[George Knapp]] had reported in 2018, including papers by [[Eric Davis]] on traversable wormholes, anti-gravity and extracting energy from the quantum vacuum.

## The NRO and the Inspector General

Gerb also draws on Greenewald's releases in his videos on the NRO and on the Air Force's hidden programs. In 2023 he received a FOIA response on [[NRO Sentient]], which the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]] describes as an "ongoing research and development program" to "revolutionize" the intelligence cycle; Gerb argues that Sentient diverts anomalous satellite data away from ordinary analysts. In 2024 Greenewald published documents on the NRO's dealings with the [[UAP Task Force]], including a May 2021 message about the agencies that had consulted it on collection assets.

On 15 July 2025 Greenewald published an article on The Black Vault titled "The DoD Inspector General's Evaluation of the DoD's Actions Regarding Unidentified Aerial Phenomena". The records he obtained show [[Terry Phillips]], then executive director of the Air Force Office of Special Investigations' Office of Special Projects, taking part in numerous classified UAP conversations with the DoD and listed among its UAP points of contact. For Gerb, who casts Phillips as a gatekeeper of the legacy programs, this is "the cherry on top": verifiable instances of Phillips being connected to UFOs. Gerb also mentions that [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] passed his Signal exchanges with [[Chris Mellon]] to Greenewald for a FOIA release, which he places around the summer of 2024.

## USS Trepang Photograph Analysis

Greenewald weighed in on the disputed [[USS Trepang USO Photographs|USS Trepang photographs]] from 1971, said to show triangle, cigar-shaped and possibly egg-shaped UAP interacting with the ocean. Researcher Wim Van Utrecht found signs of tampering in one of the best images of the set. Greenewald argued that the one altered image does not discredit the others: many publications enhance photographs for print, and the original image differs somewhat in aspect from the magazine version.

## Philip J. Corso FBI Documents

In July 2024 Greenewald and The Black Vault published FBI documents on [[Philip J. Corso]]. Part of them concern a 1964–65 FBI background check, run when Corso was being considered for a post with a House subcommittee on immigration and national security. They record claims Corso passed on that the FBI could not validate, and quote another agency calling him "a parasite who has never produced any intelligence through his own efforts". All of the files are cross-references rather than main files, so, as Gerb reads them, the FBI never formally investigated Corso. None of them discusses UFOs or his Army duties, which Gerb finds odd in itself.

## Significance

Greenewald's FOIA requests have put on the public record a consistent pattern of classification and denial around space-based detection of unidentified objects. Through them, the record shows that the US military has tracked objects entering and leaving Earth's atmosphere since at least 1972, and that this data is among the most tightly held of the government's UAP-related information. In Gerb's argument, the term "fast walker" works in practice as a shield against FOIA requests that ask about "UFOs" or "UAP".
