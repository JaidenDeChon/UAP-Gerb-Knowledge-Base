John Greenewald is a researcher and Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) activist who founded and runs [[The Black Vault]], one of the world's largest privately run collections of declassified US government documents. Since the 1990s he has filed thousands of FOIA requests about UFO/UAP programs, classified projects and national security operations, and has made the responses and documents public.

## Fast Walker FOIA Campaign

Over several years, Greenewald ran a persistent campaign to get information about [[Fast Walkers]] and Slow Walkers. These are the military terms for objects that [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellites and other space-based sensors detect entering or leaving Earth's atmosphere.

### 2013 Request to US Northern Command

In 2013, Greenewald sent a FOIA request to [[US Northern Command]] (NORTHCOM), which oversees [[NORAD]], asking for any reports, information or detailed military documentation on Fast Walkers and Slow Walkers.

NORTHCOM replied that Fast Walker documentation is "currently and properly classified" under Executive Order 13526 as material "considered critical to National Defense or foreign policy."

The denial was only partial. NORTHCOM provided two unclassified US Air Force research papers that confirmed Fast Walkers were being detected and analyzed:

1. "Orbit Determination of Sunlit Illuminated Objects Detected by Overhead Platforms" (1989) by USAF Captain [[Richard P. Osedacz]]
2. "Space-Based Satellite Tracking and Characterization Utilizing Non-Imaging Passive Sensors" (2008) by USAF Captain [[Bradley R. Townsend]]

These papers confirmed that DSP satellites had been detecting and cataloging Fast Walkers since at least 1972.

### 2023 Request to US Space Force

In late 2023, Greenewald sent a similar FOIA request to [[US Space Force]], which had taken over oversight of space surveillance operations, including DSP. Space Force denied the request in full under FOIA Exception One, citing classification in the interest of national defense and foreign policy. It provided no documents, and its response was decisive and final.

## USS Trepang Photograph Analysis

Greenewald weighed in on the disputed [[USS Trepang USO Photographs|USS Trepang photographs]] from 1971, a set of images said to show triangle, cigar-shaped and egg-shaped UAP interacting with the Arctic Ocean near Jan Mayen Island. Researcher Wim Van Utrecht identified signs of photographic tampering in one of the best-known images. Greenewald argued that tampering in a single image does not invalidate the other photographs. He noted that publications routinely enhance images for print, and that the differences in aspect between the original image and the magazine version are consistent with standard print enhancement rather than deliberate falsification.

## Philip J. Corso FBI Documents

In July 2024, Greenewald released FBI documents, obtained through The Black Vault, about a 1964–65 background check of [[Philip J. Corso]]. The documents portrayed Corso negatively. One, attributed to another government agency, described Corso as "a parasite who has never produced any intelligence through his own efforts." None of the FBI documents discussed Corso's Foreign Technology Division duties or UAP, and all the files were cross-references rather than primary investigatory files. Gerb cited these documents as counterpoints to Corso's claims, while noting that the FBI's characterization does not directly address the specifics of his military career.

## Significance

Greenewald's systematic FOIA requests have put on public record a consistent pattern of government classification and denial around space-based UFO detection. His work has established on the official record that the US military has been tracking objects entering and leaving Earth's atmosphere since at least 1972, that this data is among the most heavily classified UAP-related information the government holds, and that the term "Fast Walker" has in practice served as a classification shield against FOIA requests that use the terms "UFO" or "UAP."
