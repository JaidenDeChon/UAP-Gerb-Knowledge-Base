Harry S. Truman (1884–1972) was the 33rd President of the United States, serving from 1945 to 1953. In Gerb's coverage he is the president who, with [[Dwight D. Eisenhower]], laid the legal and administrative groundwork of the [[UFO Legacy Program Framework|UFO legacy programs]]. He signed the National Security Act of 1947, issued NSC 10/2 in 1948 and handed [[Sandia National Laboratories|Sandia]] to [[AT&T]] in 1949. The disputed [[MJ-12 Documents|Majestic documents]] say he created the [[Majestic 12]] group by a classified order in September 1947. He also responded personally to the [[1952 UFOs Over Washington DC]] radar incidents, in which unidentified objects flew over the White House and the US Capitol.

## Career

Truman served as a US Senator from Missouri from January 1935 to January 1945. From March 1941 he chaired the Senate committee investigating the national defense program, known as the Truman Committee, until he stepped down in August 1944 to run for vice president. He was Franklin D. Roosevelt's vice president from January 1945 and became president on Roosevelt's death in April 1945. He left office in January 1953 and was succeeded by Eisenhower.

## 1952 Washington DC UFO Incidents

In the summer of 1952, a wave of unidentified radar contacts over Washington DC alarmed the federal government. A National Airlines crew flying into the capital saw erratic lights above their aircraft, and within minutes radar at National Airport and Andrews Air Force Base both tracked objects. The sightings made the front pages, and President Truman made personal calls to his Air Force aides to find out what had happened. That he intervened himself suggests the incidents were treated as a national security concern at the highest level of the executive branch.

The incidents also drew the CIA's interest, and the CIA convened the [[Robertson Panel]] in January 1953 with a brief to "strip" UFOs of their "special status". Gerb identifies this brief as the origin of the decades-long stigma around the subject.

## Majestic-12 Controversy

Documents circulating since the 1980s, known as the [[MJ-12 Documents|Majestic documents]] or MJ-12 papers, purport to show that Truman authorized a secret panel called [[Majestic 12]] to manage information about recovered extraterrestrial craft and biological material after the 1947 [[Roswell Crash|Roswell incident]]. The [[Eisenhower Briefing Document]], dated 18 November 1952, says Operation Majestic 12 was set up by a classified executive order of Truman on 24 September 1947, on the recommendation of [[Vannevar Bush]] and Secretary of Defense [[James Forrestal]]. Truman's appointment records show a meeting with Forrestal and Bush that day, arranged at Forrestal's suggestion. They also show an off-the-record meeting with [[Walter Bedell Smith]] on 1 August 1950, the date the briefing document gives for Smith replacing Forrestal.

The documents remain contested. Some researchers treat them as authentic or partly authentic, while others argue they are fabrications. The alleged Truman memorandum is a central piece of evidence in this debate.

Gerb's wider timeline of the legacy programs ties the alleged Majestic 12 body to Truman's June 1948 National Security Council Directive 10/2, the "National Security Council Directive on Office of Special Projects". That directive gave the CIA authority to conduct covert operations through its Office of Special Projects, which was renamed the Office of Policy Coordination on 1 September 1948. The video alleges that Majestic 12 was set up under the structure NSC 10/2 created to oversee covert activities. It says Truman "laid the groundwork" for the UFO control group apparatus, and credits [[Dwight D. Eisenhower]] with completing the lion's share of forming the programs.

Astrophysicist [[Bernard Haisch]], offering what he called conjecture from sources, said in 2018 that four related but separate unacknowledged programs "tracing back to a 1947 Truman memorandum" still existed and were housed as of the 1990s at aerospace companies such as Lockheed, [[TRW]], [[Raytheon]] and the [[Aerospace Corporation]].

## National Security Act of 1947 and UFO Legacy Program Foundations

Gerb's investigation into the [[Manhattan Project 2.0]] names Truman and Eisenhower as the two presidents who laid the legal and administrative groundwork of UFO legacy program secrecy, through legislation and classified presidential executive orders. The National Security Act of 1947, which Truman signed, created the [[National Security Council]], the [[CIA|Central Intelligence Agency]], the independent [[US Air Force]] and the [[Research and Development Board]]. Gerb is clear that the act was not about UFOs, but he identifies all four as foundational institutions of the legacy programs. In agreement with Kissner, he says coordination of the Manhattan Project 2.0 went to the new National Security Council.

[[Andrew Kissner|J. Andrew Kissner]], a former New Mexico State Representative, interviewed firsthand sources from the legacy program in the early 1990s. According to Kissner, a classified presidential executive order that Truman signed in July 1948 placed custody of recovered technical vehicles with the [[Atomic Energy Commission]] and its national laboratories, with support from Air Force Systems Command and the Naval Research Laboratory. Gerb identifies this alleged order as one of several Presidential Emergency Action Documents (PEADs) that gave the Manhattan Project 2.0 its legal teeth, delegating physical custody of recovered craft to [[Sandia National Laboratories]], [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]], [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]] and similar institutions.

Truman's relationship to the Manhattan Project was unusual. As a senator, he chaired the Truman Committee, which reviewed defense programs, and saw millions of dollars funneled into a project labeled only "expediting production" with no further description. When he asked Secretary of War Henry Stimson for an explanation, he was told the information was classified to protect national security. He was not briefed as Roosevelt's vice president either, and learned of the Manhattan Project from its Top Policy Group only when he became president in 1945. Gerb argues that Truman then carried that model of a small group under presidential control over to the UFO problem.

## Sandia and AT&T

On 13 May 1949 Truman asked LeRoy Wilson, president of [[AT&T]], to run Sandia as an entity separate from Los Alamos' Z Division, telling him that "you have here an opportunity to render an exceptional service in the national interest." From 1 November 1949 Sandia Corporation managed the laboratory for the AEC. In his video on the 1950s Del Rio, Texas crashes, Gerb notes that the laboratory said to have received the debris of the [[1950 El Indio UFO Crash]] was therefore run by a telephone company, one that witnesses on [[Steven Greer]]'s Disclosure Project list tie to the transfer of non-human technology. Truman also set up the Nevada Proving Ground, later the [[Nevada National Security Site (Nevada Test Site)|Nevada Test Site]], under the AEC, and Sandia supported nuclear testing there from its first years.
