Harry S. Truman (1884–1972) was the 33rd President of the United States, serving from 1945 to 1953. UAP research links him to two events: the alleged formation of the Majestic-12 study group following the 1947 Roswell crash, and his personal response to the [[1952 UFOs Over Washington DC]] radar incidents in which unidentified objects flew over the White House and US Capitol.

## 1952 Washington DC UFO Incidents

In the summer of 1952, a wave of unidentified radar contacts over Washington DC caused significant alarm within the federal government. The contacts were tracked on multiple independent radar systems and corroborated by visual witnesses. According to contemporary accounts, President Truman personally called Air Force aides to demand an explanation for the objects tracked in the restricted airspace above the nation's capital. That he intervened himself indicates the incidents were considered a national security concern at the highest levels of the executive branch.

The incidents prompted the CIA to convene the [[Robertson Panel]] in January 1953. This scientific advisory committee was asked to recommend how to manage public interest in UFOs, and the Robertson Panel's later mandate to suppress public engagement with UAP phenomena is widely identified as the origin of the institutional UFO stigma.

## Majestic-12 Controversy

Documents circulating since the 1980s, known as the [[Majestic-12]] documents or MJ-12 papers, purport to show that Truman authorized the formation of a secret panel called "Majestic-12" to manage information about recovered extraterrestrial craft and biological material following the 1947 Roswell incident. The documents remain contested. Some researchers treat them as authentic or partly authentic, while others argue they are fabrications. The alleged Truman authorization memorandum is a central piece of evidence in this debate.

Gerb's wider timeline of the legacy programs ties the alleged Majestic 12 body specifically to Truman's June 1948 National Security Council Directive 10/2 ("Office of Special Projects"). That directive gave the CIA authority to conduct covert operations through the Office of Policy Coordination, which was renamed the CIA's Office of Special Projects that September. The video presents Majestic 12 as having been set up under the structure NSC 10/2 created to oversee covert activities. It credits Truman with having "laid the groundwork" for the UFO control group apparatus, and credits [[Dwight D. Eisenhower]] with completing most of the group's formal organization.

## National Security Act of 1947 and UFO Legacy Program Foundations

Gerb's investigation into the Manhattan Project 2.0 names Truman as one of the two presidents most responsible for setting up the legal and administrative framework of the UFO legacy program. The National Security Act of 1947, which Truman signed, created the National Security Council, the Central Intelligence Agency, the independent US Air Force and the Research and Development Board. All four are identified as foundational institutions of the UFO legacy program.

J. Andrew Kisner, a former New Mexico State Representative, conducted extensive interviews with firsthand sources from the legacy program in the early 1990s. According to Kisner, a classified presidential executive order that Truman signed in July 1948 placed custody of recovered non-human technical vehicles with the [[Atomic Energy Commission]]'s national laboratories. Gerb identifies this alleged 1948 executive order as one of several "Presidential Emergency Action Documents" (PEADs) that gave the Manhattan Project 2.0 its operational legal authority, delegating physical custody of recovered craft to [[Sandia National Laboratories]], [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]], [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]], and similar institutions.

Truman's relationship to the Manhattan Project was unusual. As a senator, he had chaired the Truman Committee, which reviewed defense programs, and had seen millions of dollars funneled into a project labeled only "expediting production" with no further description. When he asked Secretary of War Stimson for an explanation, he was told the information was classified to protect national security. Truman was not briefed on the Manhattan Project until he became president after FDR's death in 1945.
