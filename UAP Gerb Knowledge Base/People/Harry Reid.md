Harry Mason Reid (1939-2021) was a Democratic United States Senator from Nevada from 1987 to 2017 and Senate Majority Leader from 2007 to 2015. As a party leader he was a member of the congressional "Gang of Eight," the small group of leaders briefed on the most sensitive intelligence matters. In UFO research he is best known as the sponsor of the Defense Intelligence Agency's [[AAWSAP]], and for later saying he believed the government held UFO material that he had been denied access to. Gerb returns to Reid's statements whenever he weighs which defense contractors hold recovered material, [[Lockheed Martin]] above all.

## Career

Reid was born in Searchlight, Nevada, on 2 December 1939. He served in the U.S. House of Representatives from 1983 to 1987 and in the Senate from 1987 to 2017, where he was Democratic whip (1999–2005), minority leader (2005–2007 and 2015–2017) and majority leader (2007–2015). He died in Henderson, Nevada, on 28 December 2021, at 82.

## AAWSAP and Kona Blue

Reid started AAWSAP between 2007 and 2008 and secured about $22 million for it over five years. The [[Defense Intelligence Agency]] ran it from 2008 to 2012 through a contract with [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]]. The program is often confused with the informal [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]] associated with [[Luis Elizondo]], which Elizondo describes as funded by Reid. According to [[David Grusch]] and reporting cited by Gerb, one purpose of AAWSAP was to receive recovered material from [[Lockheed Martin]] through [[Kona Blue]], a proposed waived special access program that Reid championed and intelligence officials blocked. Grusch says the CIA objected and "Harry Reid's request to get the material transferred to the [AAWSAP] program was totally killed." In June 2009 Reid wrote to the Deputy Secretary of Defense asking for special access program status for the program, and the Defense Department declined.

## "I was denied access for decades"

In *Special Access Required Vol.2*, Gerb plays David Grusch describing a meeting with Reid in Reid's living room, with other witnesses present, about nine months before Reid's death. Grusch wanted to learn what Reid, as a former Gang of Eight member, actually knew and why he had funded AAWSAP. According to Grusch, Reid told him: "Yeah, I knew we had UFO material. I was denied access for decades." Gerb uses the account to argue that even the Gang of Eight, which is supposed to be told of waived unacknowledged programs, was kept out of the legacy portfolio.

Grusch adds that Reid made his own disclosure "a couple weeks after we met" in a May 2021 *New Yorker* story, naming Lockheed. In his 2024 video on the company, Gerb quotes Reid: "I was told for decades that Lockheed had some of these retrieved materials. I tried to get, as I recall, a classified approval by the Pentagon to have me go look at the stuff. They would not approve that."
