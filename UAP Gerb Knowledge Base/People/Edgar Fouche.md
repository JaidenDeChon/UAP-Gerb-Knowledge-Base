Edgar Albert Fouché (sometimes called Edgar Rothschild Fouché) was a United States Air Force Master Sergeant. He was born in 1948 in South Central Georgia, into a family that had served in military intelligence and classified projects for generations. Fouché made public alleged details of the [[TR-3B]], a classified triangular stealth reconnaissance platform that he described as the most exotic and classified aerospace vehicle ever built. He first set out his claims in his 1998 book *Alien Rapture* and at MUFON and IUFOC conferences. They drew on his own service career and on testimony from five close friends and sources within the US military-industrial complex. He has since died.

## Military Career

During the Vietnam conflict, while attending college, Fouché worked as a machinist making ordnance. In 1968 he was drafted into the USAF for pararescue basic training. After fracturing his ankle at Fort Benning, he completed training in electronics, communication intelligence, and cryptological methods. From 1964 to 1974, Fouché was stationed at several Tactical Air Command and Air Training Command bases, and spent 3.5 years in Vietnam and at nearby Asian installations. He earned degrees in electronics and avionics engineering and a bachelor's in business, and completed roughly 4,000 hours of technical training from the USG and DOD, half of it classified.

Fouché was considered an Air Force expert in classified electronic countermeasure test equipment, cryptological test equipment, and automatic test equipment. He was handpicked as a core member of programs for the F-15 Eagle, A-10 Warthog, B-1 Lancer, and F-117A stealth fighter. A promotion recommendation obtained through FOIA confirms that Fouché was assigned to Detachment 3, Air Force Flight Test Center (AFFTC), the Edwards AFB detachment that runs operations at [[Area 51]] Groom Lake. Late in his career he spent eight years as a defense contractor and engineering manager working on classified black programs.

## Groom Lake and the Defense Advanced Research Center

In 1979, after working at [[Nellis Air Force Base]] with top secret crypto access clearance, Fouché was asked to take a temporary reassignment to an unnamed facility. At 4:30 a.m. he boarded a dark blue USAF bus with blacked-out windows. When he arrived at [[Area 51]], he was given heavy goggles that limited his vision to 30 feet. For ten days in a row, and on later visits, he left Nellis before sunrise and returned after dark.

Fouché claimed Area 51 housed a super-secret underground laboratory he called the Defense Advanced Research Center (DARC), which is not the same as [[DARPA]]. DARC allegedly had 10 underground stories beside a mountain near [[Papoose Lake]], south of Groom Lake, and sat next to a hangar built into a mountainside where TR-3B craft were stored. A holographic generator hid the hangar entrance by projecting the appearance of the mountain surface. A declassified 1973 ARPA historical study mentions a proposed "Defense Advanced Research Center" linked to the Army Ballistic Missile Agency and the Jet Propulsion Laboratory. Fouché was recruited to Groom Lake from that same JPL at Edwards.

DARC allegedly met its objectives in full in the mid-1980s, when it received extra [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)]] funding under the Reagan administration. Fouché claimed his sources estimated that, beginning in 1982, up to 35% of SDI funding was siphoned off to support USAF black programs.

## TR-3B Disclosures

Fouché claimed TR-3B prototype flights began in the early 1970s and the first operational model flew in the early 1990s. By 1994 at least three operational models, each costing over a billion dollars, were flying. The triangles measured approximately 200 feet across for test models and 600 feet for operational models. Fouché himself observed prototypes three times before 1994: in 1975 over [[Edwards Air Force Base]], in 1976 over the southern Nellis range, and in 1979 at Groom Lake. The prototype had different engines on the tips and no rotatable crew compartment.

According to Fouché, the craft's main technology was the [[Magnetic Field Disruptor (MFD)]], a circular plasma field accelerator ring surrounding a rotatable crew compartment. The TR-3B was code-named Astra (Advanced Stealth Technology Reconnaissance Aircraft) and was the most exotic model in the [[Aurora Program]]. [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Boeing]], [[Northrop Grumman]], Teledyne Ryan and others did the engineering, and the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]], [[NSA]] and [[CIA]] managed the program. The MFD propulsion was reverse engineered from a nonhuman crash recovery at [[Sandia National Laboratories]] and [[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory]], both [[FFRDC (Federally Funded Research and Development Centers)|Department of Energy FFRDCs]].

Fouché stated that the TR-3B had a crew of four and was used for logistics support and transport for a secret space command. Its pilots were top graduates of Navy and USAF test pilot schools, mainly from the Edwards 412th Test School. The craft used two nuclear reactors and was built of advanced composite metal materials and titanium. It had variable vectored intake and advanced multi-mode propulsion engines on each tip. Its outer casing was electrochromatic: it reacted to electrical radar stimulation and could change its reflectiveness and radar absorption for daytime stealth operations.

## The Five Disclosure Sources

Fouché based his TR-3B testimony on information from five close friends within DOD and classified programs:

1. Gerald, a former [[NSA]] investigator and [[TREAT]] team member, worked for the [[Department of Energy]] and NSA as cover, but the NSA controlled all his movements. His job was to monitor employees with Top Secret and Q clearances at the Nevada Test Site, Nellis range, Los Alamos, Sandia, and Area 51. He was found dead of a heart attack a year after his last meeting with Fouché.
2. S worked directly with the NSA on electronics intelligence, then became a defense contractor and worked for the company that created the TR-3B's MFD gravity disruption device.
3. Doc was an SR-71 spy pilot and USAF test pilot at [[Edwards Air Force Base]].
4. Dell served with Fouché during Vietnam. Dell's father worked for the NSA for over 25 years and sent Fouché [[Majestic 12]] documents.
5. Bud was a DOD contractor electronics engineer who worked on top secret R&D programs with electronic countermeasures.

## Credibility Controversies

Fouché's disclosures drew wide attention and controversy. [[Jeremy Rys]] (Alien Scientist), who met Fouché in 2009 and at first considered him a legitimate whistleblower, later accused him of fabricating the TR-3B testimony by combining stories about the Nazi Bell device with the [[Belgian UFO Wave]]. Rys also alleged Fouché used a stolen F-117A photograph and faked military documents. YouTuber [[David Hilton]] posted a 2013 video titled "Edgar Fouche Fake Documents" that made similar accusations. Both Rys and Hilton later removed or unlisted their critical videos.

Researcher [[Andrew Johnson]], who interviewed Fouché many times, spoke highly of him. Researcher [[Dan Benkert]], who co-authored work with [[Michael Schratt]], argued against the criticism. In a June 2012 Skype conversation, Hilton made concerning remarks to Dan Benkert: he claimed he worked for a monitoring agency and asserted control over Fouché.

## Significance

The craft that [[Rodrik Castle]] described during the 1997 [[Hunter Warrior Advanced Warfighting Experiment]], a silent black triangle of 200–300 feet, closely matches Fouché's descriptions of the TR-3B. Fouché's military documents obtained through FOIA, including the record of his assignment to Detachment 3 AFFTC at Groom Lake, substantiate the main parts of his service history.
