Dr. Amy Eskridge was a 35-year-old American scientist and co-founder of the Institute for Exotic Science in Huntsville, Alabama, who died in 2022 under circumstances that researchers in the anti-gravity and ARV disclosure communities have found highly suspicious. In the period leading up to her death, Eskridge had been lecturing on behalf of [[Holocron Engineering]], a gravity modification research and development company, presenting historical anti-gravity experiments and discussing modern black projects associated with the [[TR-3B]] craft and anti-gravity propulsion development.

## Death

Eskridge's death in 2022 was ruled a self-inflicted instance. A significant amount of controversy has surrounded this determination, given her active involvement in lecturing publicly on anti-gravity research and alleged classified programs. UAP Gerb has noted her case as potentially warranting a dedicated investigation.

## Pattern of Suspicious Deaths

Eskridge's death is cited in the context of a broader alleged pattern of researchers and advocates in free energy, anti-gravity, and ARV research dying under suspicious or implausible circumstances, alongside filmmaker [[James Allen]] (radioisotope poisoning, 2014), aerospace illustrator [[Mark McCandlish]] (apparent self-inflicted gunshot wound, 2021), cold fusion researcher Eugene Mallove (beaten to death, 2004), physicist Stefan Marinov (fell from a window, 1997), zero point field investor Arie Deus (found dead in his car, 2007), and stardrive inventor Mark Toman (died unexpectedly after a successful prototype demonstration, 2009).
