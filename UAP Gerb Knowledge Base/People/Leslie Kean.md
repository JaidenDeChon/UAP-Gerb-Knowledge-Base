**Leslie Kean** is an investigative journalist and author, and one of the most influential figures in modern UAP research and disclosure efforts. She is best known as a co-author of the landmark 2017 *New York Times* article that revealed the existence of the Pentagon's [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]] (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program) and brought UAP videos ("Gimbal," "GoFast," and "FLIR1") into mainstream public discourse. Earlier in her career, Kean led a legal battle lasting several years to force [[NASA]] to release documents about the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]].

## Early Career and Kecksburg Investigation

In the late 1990s and early 2000s, Leslie Kean began investigating the [[Kecksburg, Pennsylvania]] crash case. In May 2000, she organized a conference titled *"Combating Media Ridicule and Searching for Evidence on the 1965 Kecksburg Crash Case,"* where she presented findings from her correspondence with [[Nicholas Johnson]], NASA's Chief Scientist for Orbital Debris at the [[Johnson Space Center|NASA Johnson Space Center]].

Johnson analyzed orbital data for the Soviet spacecraft [[Cosmos 96]] and concluded definitively that it could not have caused the Kecksburg event. Johnson stated: "No man-made object came down over Pennsylvania" on December 9, 1965, at the time of the crash. He also ruled out [[Project Corona]], a U.S. spy satellite program, as an explanation.

### Legal Action Against NASA

In the early 2000s, Kean joined the [[Coalition of Freedom of Information]] (CFI) in taking legal action against NASA for withholding documents about Kecksburg. After years of stonewalling, Kean and CFI won a settlement in October 2007 that required NASA to:

- Search for and release all relevant Kecksburg files
- Pay all legal fees incurred by Kean and CFI

NASA finished its document search in August 2009. The agency posted 20 pages of documents about [[Project Moon Dust]], a Cold War program for recovering foreign space vehicles that had come down, but none of them bore directly on Kecksburg. According to a statement from NASA's public affairs office, the agency had transferred its Kecksburg files to the [[National Archives]] about two years after the 1965 incident. In 1996 the National Archives told NASA that the files had been marked as lost since 1987.

Although the search was disappointing, Kean's legal victory set an important precedent for using the courts to compel government transparency on UAP matters.

## The 2017 New York Times Article

On December 16, 2017, Leslie Kean, Helene Cooper and Ralph Blumenthal published the *New York Times* article "Glowing Auras and 'Black Money': The Pentagon's Mysterious U.F.O. Program." The article revealed:

- The existence of the Pentagon's [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]], funded with $22 million in "black money"
- Analysis of UAP encounters by military personnel, including the release of three declassified videos: "Gimbal," "GoFast," and "FLIR1"
- Testimony from former Pentagon official [[Luis Elizondo]], who ran AATIP and resigned in protest over the lack of attention given to UAP threats

The article was a watershed for UAP disclosure. It made the subject legitimate in mainstream media and led to Congressional hearings, the creation of the [[All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office]] (AARO), and renewed public and scientific interest in the phenomenon.

### Subsequent Coverage

After the 2017 article, Kean kept reporting on UAP news, including:

- Coverage of whistleblower [[David Grusch]]'s claims regarding crash retrieval programs
- Reporting on [[Carl Nell]] and other high-level officials supporting disclosure
- Articles in *The Debrief* discussing the 40 whistleblowers Grusch interviewed

## Books and Advocacy

Leslie Kean is the author of *UFOs: Generals, Pilots, and Government Officials Go on the Record* (2010), a comprehensive study of credible UFO cases backed by testimony from military and government officials around the world. The book is widely regarded as one of the most serious and evidence-based works on the subject.

Kean has consistently called for scientific study of UAP, for government transparency, and for an end to the stigma around the subject. Her journalism is very carefully sourced and relies on official documents and credible witnesses. She presents UAP as a legitimate subject of investigation rather than fringe speculation.

## Impact and Legacy

Leslie Kean's work has fundamentally changed how UAP are discussed in the 21st century. Her 2017 *New York Times* article is credited with breaking the decades-old taboo on serious UAP reporting in mainstream media. Her earlier work on Kecksburg showed how effective legal action and investigative journalism can be against government secrecy.

Kean's approach, based on evidence, official testimony and work with credible sources, has set a standard for UAP journalism and still influences disclosure efforts worldwide.
