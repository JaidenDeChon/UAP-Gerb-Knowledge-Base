**Leslie Kean** is an American investigative journalist and author, and one of the most influential people in modern UAP reporting and the push for disclosure. She co-wrote the 2017 *New York Times* article that revealed the Pentagon's [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]] (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program) and, in Gerb's words, brought the [[Pentagon UFO Videos|Pentagon UAP videos]] ("FLIR", "Gimbal" and "GoFast") and AATIP into the public zeitgeist. In 2023 she co-wrote the article in *The Debrief* that introduced the whistleblower [[David Grusch]]. Earlier, she led a lawsuit lasting several years to force [[NASA]] to release its documents on the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]].

## Career

Kean began covering UFOs in 2000 with a feature story in the *Boston Globe*. Before that she had reported on other subjects, among them the struggle for democracy in Burma. In 2002 she co-founded the Coalition for Freedom of Information (CFi), an alliance pressing for government openness about UFOs and for responsible media coverage, and as its director she sued NASA over the Kecksburg records. Her book *UFOs: Generals, Pilots, and Government Officials Go on the Record* (2010) collects accounts of UFO cases from military and government officials around the world.

## Kecksburg Investigation

Kean took up the [[Kecksburg, Pennsylvania]] case around 2000. In May 2000 she organized a conference titled *"Combating Media Ridicule and Searching for Evidence on the 1965 Kecksburg Crash Case,"* where she presented her correspondence with [[Nicholas Johnson]], NASA's chief scientist for orbital debris at the [[Johnson Space Center|NASA Johnson Space Center]].

Johnson had analyzed the orbital data of the Soviet Venus probe [[Cosmos 96]] and concluded that it could not have come down at Kecksburg at about 5 p.m. on 9 December 1965. He also said that no man-made object landed in Pennsylvania that day, and ruled out film capsules from [[Project Corona]], the U.S. spy satellite program.

### Legal Action Against NASA

Working with the Coalition for Freedom of Information (CFi), which she had co-founded, Kean took legal action against NASA for withholding its Kecksburg documents. In October 2007 she and CFi won a settlement that required NASA to:

- search for the relevant Kecksburg files and provide them to Kean and CFi
- pay the legal fees incurred by Kean and CFi

NASA finished its search in August 2009. It posted 20 pages of documents about [[Project Moon Dust]], a Cold War program for recovering foreign space vehicles that had come down, but none of them bore on Kecksburg. According to a statement from NASA's public affairs office, the agency had sent its Kecksburg files to the [[National Archives]] for safekeeping two years after the 1965 incident, and in 1996 the National Archives told NASA that the files had been marked as lost since 1987.

News of the lawsuit also drew out a new witness: a man called Joel contacted the Kecksburg investigator [[Stan Gordon]] after hearing local coverage of Kean's effort.

## The 2017 New York Times Article

On 16 December 2017, Helene Cooper, [[Ralph Blumenthal]] and Kean published the *New York Times* article "Glowing Auras and 'Black Money': The Pentagon's Mysterious U.F.O. Program." It revealed:

- the existence of the Pentagon's [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]], funded with $22 million in "black money"
- military encounters with UAP, documented in Navy videos released through the efforts of [[Luis Elizondo]] and [[Chris Mellon]]
- the account of former Pentagon official [[Luis Elizondo]], who ran AATIP and resigned in protest at the lack of attention given to UAP

In his SOL Foundation presentation, [[Karl Nell]] cites the article as a major paradigm shift that brought the FLIR, Gimbal and GoFast videos into the public domain, and Gerb's iceberg video credits the *Times*, with Kean and Blumenthal, with publishing them. The article changed how mainstream media treated the subject, and came before the congressional hearings and the creation of [[AARO]] in the years that followed.

## The Debrief and David Grusch

On 5 June 2023, Kean and Blumenthal published "Intelligence Officials Say U.S. Has Retrieved Craft of Non-Human Origin" in *The Debrief*. It introduced [[David Grusch]] and his claims, and quoted [[Karl Nell]], a retired Army colonel who had worked with Grusch on the [[UAP Task Force]]. In Nell's one quote, which Gerb calls astounding, he says Grusch is fundamentally correct that a terrestrial arms race has run sub rosa for 80 years, focused on reverse-engineering technologies of unknown origin.

The day after Grusch's July 2023 hearing, Kean was asked in an interview why none of the 40 people Grusch said he had interviewed had spoken out. She answered that one of them was in her Debrief article, "a former army colonel named Karl Nell", and that the silence of the others was not surprising given the personal and professional repercussions Grusch had described. Gerb plays the clip in his video on Nell.
