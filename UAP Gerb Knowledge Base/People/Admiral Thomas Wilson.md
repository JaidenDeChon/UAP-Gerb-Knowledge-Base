Vice Admiral Thomas R. Wilson served as Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) and is central to one of the most significant documents in modern UAP discourse: the Wilson-Davis memo. According to testimony and leaked notes from a 2002 meeting, Wilson attempted to gain access to a deeply compartmentalized Special Access Program (SAP) involving recovered non-human technology but was denied entry despite his position as DIA Director. The encounter, documented by physicist Dr. Eric Davis, describes Wilson's frustration at being "stonewalled" by a contractor-run program operating outside normal oversight channels. Wilson allegedly confirmed the program's existence but stated he lacked the proper "bigot list" credentials to be read into it.

## Wilson-Davis Memo

The Wilson-Davis notes, leaked in 2019, detail a meeting between Wilson and Dr. Eric Davis in which Wilson described his attempts to penetrate what he believed to be a UFO reverse-engineering program managed by a private aerospace contractor. Wilson reportedly learned of the program's existence from conversations with former program managers but was told by the contractor's attorneys and Special Security Officers that he did not have a "need to know." The memo has become a foundational document for understanding alleged compartmentalization of legacy UFO programs within the defense-industrial complex.

## Alleged SAP Reorganization

In the transcript of the MOON DUST video, the narrator references Wilson's claim that in the early 1990s, Special Access Programs related to crash retrieval and reverse engineering were reorganized to hide them within deeper compartments, making them effectively inaccessible even to senior intelligence officials. This reorganization is cited as a possible explanation for why the Air Force denied the existence of Project Moon Dust and claimed documents had been destroyed when Senators requested them in the mid-1990s.
