The **Infographic Show Whistleblower** is an anonymous US Army veteran whose account was told in [[The Infographic Show]]'s March 2024 video "US Special Forces Confession - I Recovered Crashed UFOs". He says he saw a craft crash in Vietnam in 1968, was forced into a classified crash retrieval program a year later, and served in it for about 35 years. UAP Gerb analysed the account in June 2024 and theorized that he may be Lieutenant General [[John M. Riggs]].

## Account

- **Vietnam, 1968.** While on a long-range reconnaissance patrol near the Cambodian border, his platoon saw a red-orange object crash and found an egg-shaped, seamless metallic craft. Personnel already in the program took two days to remove it (see [[1968 Vietnam UAP Crash Retrieval]]).
- **Induction, 1969.** After five of the original twelve platoon members were killed in combat, the survivors were read into "the program" under threat of general discharge, drug charges or an Article 15.
- **The program.** He describes rapid reaction units in theater, three craft recovered from the Vietnam theater (one recovery nearly started a war with China), collections of craft kept in different places, and "co-op sites" with contractors including [[Raytheon]], [[Boeing]] and [[Texas Instruments]]. He calls [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] a red herring.
- **Craft.** Eggs and bells crash most often and are almost always unmanned. Saucers are rare, crewed "crown jewels"; he helped recover two, one in South America.
- **Biologics.** The crews are biological androids that die within days. He took part in a live recovery at a "near-UK" crash that UAP Gerb identifies with [[1996 Bole, Ireland Crash Retrieval|Bole, Ireland, in 1996]], under the [[Live Biologics Protocols]].
- **Theories.** The program believed the craft were manufactured elsewhere in the solar system ([[Fabrication Hypothesis]]) and often left for humans to find ([[Craft Donation Theory]]).

He says he was out of the program for about twenty years before speaking, and UAP Gerb puts his age at 74 or older and his service at about 1969 to 2004.

## Assessment by UAP Gerb

UAP Gerb said he was "pretty confident" of the crash date and unit ([[Company E, 52nd Infantry (LRP)]]), of five likely names among the unit's dead, and of the 1969–2004 service dates, and "medium confident" of the South American and Irish cases. He offered the identification with Riggs as a theory that "doesn't hold much water" beyond matching dates.
