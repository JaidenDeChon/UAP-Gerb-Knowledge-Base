Dr. Robert M. "Bob" Wood (1928–2024) was an American physicist and aerospace engineer who spent his career at Douglas Aircraft Company and McDonnell Douglas, and a leading researcher of the alleged [[Majestic 12]] documents. With his son [[Ryan S. Wood]] he spent decades analysing the documents leaked between 1984 and 1999, and he is best known for his work authenticating the [[Special Operations Manual (SOM 1-01)]]. In Gerb's videos the Woods are the premier researchers trying to authenticate or disprove the MJ-12 papers, and Gerb cites their confidence ratings when he draws on individual documents. Wood died in August 2024; Gerb's later videos call him "the late Dr. Robert Wood".

## Career

Wood earned a PhD in physics from Cornell University in 1953 and joined Douglas Aircraft in 1956. Douglas merged into McDonnell Douglas in 1967, and Wood stayed with the company until he retired in 1993. After retiring he served on the board of [[MUFON]] for 24 years.

## Authentication of SOM 1-01

When [[Don Berliner]] received the [[Special Operations Manual (SOM 1-01)|Special Operations Manual]] in 1994 as undeveloped 35mm Tri-X film, he and Wood developed it together in a darkroom and recovered the original images, which showed pages photographed from an Echo binder. The Woods then took the manual to a retired Government Printing Office supervisor in Washington, D.C. He identified raised letter Zs as an artifact of the hot-lead press printing that government facilities used in the 1950s. He also confirmed period-correct fonts, indentation, the M-dash in "MJ-12 Long Dash clearance" and the capitalization of "First Aid", all conventions that changed in later decades. He concluded that the CIA probably printed it "in a cage down on 10th Street."

Other details the Woods point to as authentic include the abbreviation "N.Mex" for New Mexico (correct for 1954; "NM" came later), "craft tape" instead of "duct tape" for packaging materials, "screw driver" written as two words, the mention of "down satellites" before Sputnik, and a single typographical error ("desicant" for "desiccant"), consistent with professional government printing. The manual lists a UFOB Identification Guide that was not supplied with it. A typed version later came out through FOIA, and when Wood retyped it to the manual's format, its page numbers matched the table of contents exactly.

In his book *UFOs: Myths, Conspiracies, and Realities*, [[John B. Alexander]] wrote that Wood told him he believed SOM 1-01 was created in 1954 to be leaked to Soviet spies, so that the Soviets would waste resources on UFO crash recoveries.

## Bowen Manuscript Authentication

The Woods carried out forensic ink and paper analysis on the [[Bowen Manuscript]] (*Encyclopedia of Flying Saucers*). Vernon Bowen sent his typed history of UFO cases to the Air Force for review in 1960 and never got it back. In 1999 [[Tim Cooper]] obtained the original onion-skin copy through the FOIA office at Fort Meade, Maryland, stamped "Top Secret MAGIC" and annotated by hand in ink. The Woods attribute the marginalia to [[Vannevar Bush]] and date the ink to about 1962.

## Early Douglas Aircraft UFO Program

In about 1967, Wood convinced his management at Douglas Aircraft Company that UFO propulsion was worth investigating. He asked them, "are we going to figure out how the UFOs work before or after Lockheed does?" Over a couple of years, through the merger that created McDonnell Douglas, the company spent internal R&D money equivalent to about $4.5 million on gravity experiments and theory, interviews with abductees and field investigations. Ryan Wood recalls going to the Mojave Desert at 13 or 14, where one of his father's engineers used magnetometers and strip chart recorders to document anomalous events.

During this period Wood hired [[Stanton Friedman]] to examine the nuclear physics of UFO propulsion. The team had no access to classified information.

## Other Research Cited by Gerb

- The Army has acknowledged that an [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] existed as an in-house project for an unnamed Assistant Chief of Staff for Intelligence (G-2). Ryan and Robert Wood argue that the G-2 was a compartmented office and that the unit's real leader was [[George C. Marshall]].
- Gerb agrees with Wood that underground installations at Groom Lake, built in an old mine, probably predate the official opening of Area 51 in 1955.
- For Project Palladium, Gerb cites the Woods' moderate-to-high rating of a Majestic document dated 28 June 1961, in which [[John F. Kennedy]] allegedly asks CIA director Allen Dulles for a review of MJ-12 intelligence operations.
- Wood is among the named witnesses on the list of more than 700 names that [[Steven Greer]]'s Disclosure Project published in 2024.

## Collaboration with a Forensic Illustrator

Forensic illustrator [[Bill McDonald]] and Wood drew the extraterrestrial entities EBE-1 and EBE-2 as the Special Operations Manual describes them, and Gerb has featured those designs. McDonald's reconstructions of the Roswell craft and the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident|1976 Tehran incident]] are his own work.
