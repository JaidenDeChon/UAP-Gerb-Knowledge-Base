Travis Walton (born 1957) is an American forestry worker from Snowflake, Arizona, best known for his claim of being abducted by a UFO on November 5, 1975, while working with a logging crew in the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest. It is one of the most extensively investigated and highest-profile abduction cases in UFO history. It stands out because multiple independent witnesses saw the initial encounter, and because Walton then disappeared for five days.

## The Encounter

On the evening of November 5, 1975, Walton and six coworkers were driving home from a logging site when they saw a glowing disc-shaped craft hovering above a clearing. Walton got out of the truck and walked toward it. His coworkers reported that a beam of bluish-white light struck Walton and hurled him several feet backward, and that they fled in panic. When they returned to the site, Walton was gone.

A police search of the area found no trace of Walton over the following five days. All six coworkers passed polygraph examinations about what they had seen. Walton reappeared on November 10, 1975, disoriented and confused. He described what happened to him aboard the craft, including meetings with two distinct types of beings.

## Beings Encountered

In his testimony, Walton describes two kinds of beings aboard the craft:
- Small, large-eyed humanoids that closely match the [[Grays|Gray alien archetype]]: hairless and large-headed, with wrap-around dark eyes and small features
- Taller humanoids resembling the [[Nordic Aliens]] archetype: human in appearance, with normal-sized eyes, and wearing helmet-like headgear

Few abduction accounts in the literature include both archetypes. Researchers have cited this as potentially significant for understanding how the different alleged extraterrestrial types relate to one another.

## Investigation and Legacy

Multiple parties investigated Walton's account, including the National Enquirer's UFO investigative panel and independent researchers. His coworkers' consistent testimonies and passed polygraphs were treated as some of the strongest corroborating evidence in any abduction case on record. Walton himself took and passed multiple polygraph examinations.

His story was adapted into the 1993 feature film *Fire in the Sky*, directed by Robert Lieberman. Walton has said that the film significantly dramatized and altered his account, particularly in how it showed the beings and the interior of the craft.
