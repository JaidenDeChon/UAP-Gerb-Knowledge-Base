Captain Richard P. Osedacz, USAF, wrote *Orbit Determination of Sunlight Illuminated Objects Detected by Overhead Platforms* (1989, AFIT/GA/ENY/89J-3), a master's thesis at the [[Air Force Institute of Technology]] that developed a technique for working out the orbit of a sunlit object seen crossing a space-based sensor's field of view: the objects designated [[Fast Walkers|fast walkers]]. It is one of the two papers US Northern Command released to [[John Greenewald]] in answer to his 2013 FOIA request on fast walkers. The transcript of UAP Gerb's video renders his name "Richard P. Oszx".

## The 1989 thesis

As Gerb summarises it in [[FASTWALKERS – UFOs Outside Earth]], the thesis describes joint work by [[NORAD]] and the [[Air Force Foreign Technology Division]] on uncatalogued spacebound objects detected by reflected sunlight. It states that data on such objects had been recorded since 1972 by "certain ballistic missile early warning satellite systems located at various geostationary locations", notes that an earlier effort by a researcher named Wong had found the orbit problem unsolvable, and says fast walker analysis had become increasingly important because geosynchronous satellites detect these objects while ground-based sensors cannot. Gerb takes the satellites in question to be the [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]].

The thesis was followed in 2008 by [[Bradley R. Townsend]]'s AFIT study of the same problem.
