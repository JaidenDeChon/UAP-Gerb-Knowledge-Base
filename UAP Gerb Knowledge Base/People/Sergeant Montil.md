Staff Sergeant Montil (also rendered as "Montgre" in some transcriptions) is a former United States Marine Corps staff sergeant who served with [[Marine Air Control Group 28|Air Control Group 28]] during [[Operation Laser Strike]] in Peru in 1997. Witness [[Jonathan Weygandt]] named Montil directly as someone who knew about the crashed aircraft that set off the incident.

## Role in the Peru Incident

On the night of the encounter, sometime between March and April 1997, Montil was one of the NCOs, along with [[Sergeant Allen]] and [[Sergeant Atkins]], who approached Weygandt during his guard shift (around 11 p.m. to midnight). They told him that a downed aircraft, possibly friendly, had been detected and that Marines were needed to secure the crash site. That Montil knew of the situation and helped direct personnel to the site indicates he had operational knowledge of the crash before the Marines deployed.

## Post-Incident Career

Montil stayed in the Marine Corps for many years after Operation Laser Strike and retired with a prestigious enlisted rank. Gerb identified Montil as a real USMC veteran and wrote to him, along with Allen and Atkins, during his 2025 investigation. Only one of the three replied, and Gerb has kept that Marine anonymous.
