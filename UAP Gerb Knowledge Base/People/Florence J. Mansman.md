Florence J. Mansman was a US Air Force Major and, in 1964, the commanding officer of the optical instrumentation unit at [[Vandenberg Air Force Base]]. He is known mainly as the superior officer who summoned Lieutenant [[Robert Jacobs]] to a classified screening of the [[Vandenberg Air Force Base UFO Film Incident|Big Sur UFO film]] and ordered him to keep silent about it. Mansman later confirmed Jacobs's account in writing, including that government agents confiscated the footage.

## Role in the Vandenberg UFO Incident

During a missile test on September 14, 1964, Jacobs's unit filmed an unidentified disc-shaped craft interacting with a dummy nuclear warhead. Within the next day or two, Mansman summoned Jacobs to his office. Three men in gray suits were with Mansman when Jacobs arrived; Mansman himself later identified them as the Director of the Office of the Chief Scientist and two assistants who were government agents. A 16mm projector had been set up, and Jacobs was ordered to watch the footage.

After the screening, Mansman demanded to know if Jacobs or his team had been "screwing around" at the launch site. When Jacobs said the footage looked like a UFO, Mansman ordered him to classify the incident and told him that, if anyone ever asked, he was to attribute the beam of light to "[[Laser Tracking Strikes]]". Jacobs later noted that this cover story was technically impossible, because practical laser tracking did not exist in 1964. Mansman warned that the "dark consequences of a security breach" should not require emphasis.

After Jacobs left, the men in gray suits cut the UFO footage from the film reel with scissors, put it in their briefcase and handed Mansman only what remained. At the same time, Mansman threatened both men, telling them to consider the incident closed.

## Corroboration and Legacy

In the end, Mansman's most consequential act was to confirm Jacobs's story. Jacobs disclosed the incident publicly in 1982, arguing that he had never signed a non-disclosure agreement. Mansman later wrote a signed letter to personnel at Paramount Pictures, who were producing a documentary on the case, confirming Jacobs's account as "absolute fact." His letter stated that he could confirm the incident "as he described it in the January 1989 MUFON journal."

With Mansman's written confirmation, the Vandenberg case no longer rested on one whistleblower's word. Backed by a second participant, it carries considerably more weight as evidence in UAP research.
