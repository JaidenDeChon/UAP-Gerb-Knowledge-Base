Frank Borman (1928–2023) was a U.S. Air Force officer and NASA astronaut who commanded the Apollo 8 mission in December 1968, the first crewed spacecraft to orbit the Moon. He had earlier flown on the Gemini 7 mission in December 1965. Borman comes up in UAP research in two ways: a "bogey" report he made during Gemini 7, and allegations that he filmed six UFOs descending onto the lunar surface during Apollo 8.

## Gemini 7 Bogey Report (1965)

During the Gemini 7 mission in December 1965, Borman reported seeing a "bogey", the standard military aviation term for an unidentified contact. The media covered the incident as a possible UFO sighting. Borman himself said the object was the rocket's own spent booster stage, and he held to that ordinary explanation consistently. In UAP research the incident is cited as an example of how astronaut sightings in low Earth orbit are often quickly put down to known hardware.

## Apollo 8 UFO Film Allegation (1968)

An anonymous witness listed as number 101179 in [[Steven Greer]]'s Disclosure Project archives claimed to have seen an uncut [[NASA]] film depicting six UFOs descending onto the lunar surface, allegedly taken by Borman during the Apollo 8 mission. Gerb identified this witness as [[Alain Boudier]], at that time president of the French national aeronautical and astronautical Association UAP division ([[3af-p (French national aeronautical and astronautical Association UAP division)|3af-p]]).

Separately, [[Harland Bentley]] claimed to have overheard Houston Mission Control and the Apollo 8 crew discussing a saucer-like craft paralleling their trajectory during the mission, with visible portals and movement suggesting occupants. Gerb treats the Bentley and Boudier claims as parallel, unverified threads that appear to be unconnected. No authenticated film of the alleged encounter has surfaced, and when he made the video Gerb could find no independent documentation of the incident.
