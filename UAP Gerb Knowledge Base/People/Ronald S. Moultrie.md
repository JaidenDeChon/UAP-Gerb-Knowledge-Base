Ronald S. Moultrie served from June 2021 to February 2024 as the [[Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USDINS)|Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security]] (USD I&S), the Defense Secretary's principal adviser on intelligence, counterintelligence and security, overseeing the NSA, DIA, NGA, NRO and the other defense intelligence and security agencies. As USD I&S, Moultrie announced the creation of [[AARO]] on July 20, 2022, with [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] as its first director, and AARO reported to him. Gerb treats him as a gatekeeper of UFO legacy programs: he accuses Moultrie of shaping AARO into a "whistleblower honeypot", of spreading disinformation by denying crash recoveries, and of scrubbing his ties to [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] and [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] from his public record.

## Career

Moultrie began as a US Air Force Russian linguist. Over a career of more than 36 years in the Department of Defense and the intelligence community he was a member of the CIA's Senior Intelligence Service, leading a major office in its Directorate of Science and Technology, and a senior executive officer serving the Director of National Intelligence. He retired from the National Security Agency in 2015 as its Director of Operations, and later served as a senior adviser to the Secretary of the Navy. He was sworn in as USD I&S on June 1, 2021, and held the post until February 29, 2024.

## Role in Establishing AARO

Gerb's videos on [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] and on special access programs describe Moultrie, Kirkpatrick's "good buddy", as the man who "played a major role in establishing Arrow [AARO] and provided oversight and direction to Arrow in its early years", across its lines of operation from surveillance, collection and reporting to science and technology. Gerb argues that what was supposed to be a joint UAP program office, the UAP JPO, was "distorted and twisted", likely by Moultrie, into AARO, which he calls the latest step in the Robertson-Condon playbook and a honeypot for whistleblowers. He also names Moultrie's deputy, [[David M. Taylor]], as the man who got Kirkpatrick employed at AARO.

In "The Hidden Wing", Gerb puts the USD I&S office at the center of oversight for sensitive programs: the office is responsible for the classified DoD directive on sensitive activities that the Air Force's [[SAFAHA]] office carries out, and Gerb calls it one of the most critical linchpins of National Security Council UFO programs. In his Manhattan Project 2.0 video he counts intelligence and security, the office of Moultrie and [[James Clapper]], among the three under secretaries he ties to legacy activity.

## 2022 Denials and the Sand Corp Contract

Gerb says Moultrie spread disinformation by denying UAP at the 2022 congressional hearings. On December 16, 2022, Moultrie and Kirkpatrick held a media roundtable at which they said they had seen no evidence of crashed UFOs from another planet or dimension; Gerb singles out this "round table" as an example of the two dismissing any extraterrestrial presence on Earth. In 2022 the Department of Defense also awarded [[Sand Corp]] $1.9 million for AARO support services, a contract Gerb says Moultrie's office oversaw. Sand Corp, he says, specializes in preventing leaks and stopping whistleblowers, which he takes as evidence that AARO was designed to neutralize disclosures rather than invite them. Gerb also considers Moultrie almost certainly one of the "hostile witnesses" on [[David Grusch]]'s list.

## Battelle and MITRE Ties

According to Gerb, Moultrie has sat on the advisory board of [[Battelle Memorial Institute]]: since 2016, as the Battelle video puts it, or "for nearly two years", as Gerb says in "The Hidden Wing". He says Moultrie tried to scrub the seat from his record, including removing it from his LinkedIn page, but the Project On Government Oversight (POGO) had listed him as a Battelle advisory board member and kept screenshots. Gerb alleges that Battelle analysed [[Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium Alloy)|nitinol]] for Wright-Patterson in 1949, which he links to Roswell debris, and that it is the private contractor [[Jacques Vallee]] and [[James Fox]] hinted at as holding UFO material. He cites Moultrie's seat on its board while he oversaw AARO as a conflict of interest.

Gerb draws a parallel with Kirkpatrick: after AARO, Kirkpatrick went to the [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]] FFRDC, which Battelle manages, and that job posting was later deleted and does not appear on Kirkpatrick's public CV. Moultrie, Gerb says, is "not innocent of deleting job history" either: he served as an intelligence advisory board member at MITRE and later removed MITRE from his public CV, as well as erasing Battelle.

## Connections to the Carlyle Group

Gerb also points to Moultrie's ties to the [[Carlyle Group]], a private equity firm with extensive defense industry investments and a former ownership stake in [[EG&G]]:

- He served as a board member of iCapital, a Carlyle-invested group.
- He served on the board of directors of the Better Angels Society, which Gerb describes as funded by Carlyle founder David Rubenstein.

These connections link Moultrie to the same private equity network that, under [[Frank Carlucci]]'s chairmanship, oversaw EG&G at the time EG&G's parking lot hosted the Wilson-Davis meeting. The Wilson-Davis document is central to UAP legacy program claims.
