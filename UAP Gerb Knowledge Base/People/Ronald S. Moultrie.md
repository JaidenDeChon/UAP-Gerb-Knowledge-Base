Ronald S. Moultrie served from June 2021 to February 2024 as the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USD I&S), the senior civilian intelligence official in the Department of Defense overseeing the Defense Intelligence Agency, National Security Agency elements, and other intelligence components. As USD I&S, Moultrie announced the creation of [[AARO]] on July 20, 2022, and selected [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] as its first director. Before that post he retired from the National Security Agency as its Director of Operations, and he had served in the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, in the CIA's Senior Intelligence Service, and as a US Air Force Russian linguist and analyst.

## Role in UAP Programs

In "The Hidden Wing", Gerb accuses Moultrie of deliberately establishing [[AARO]] as a whistleblower honeypot rather than a genuine UAP investigative body: a mechanism designed to identify and neutralize individuals with knowledge of UAP legacy programs. Moultrie is also alleged to have deleted references to [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] and [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory FFRDC]] from his public professional biography. Both organizations are rumored to be involved in analyzing recovered non-human vehicle systems. According to Gerb, the USD I&S role placed Moultrie at the top of the classified oversight chain for any such legacy programs operating through [[SAFAHA]] sensitive activities channels.

## Role in Establishing Arrow/AARO and MITRE Connection

Gerb's investigation into [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] identifies Moultrie as having "played a major role in establishing Arrow [AARO] and provided oversight and direction to Arrow in its early years," alongside his good friend and AARO's first director, [[Sean Kirkpatrick]]. Gerb also notes that Moultrie has removed MITRE from his own public curriculum vitae. The channel cites this, together with Moultrie's separately documented removal of his [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] advisory board membership from public view, as evidence of a broader pattern of senior officials obscuring institutional ties relevant to UAP legacy program oversight.

## Connections to the Carlyle Group

Moultrie has ties to the [[Carlyle Group]], a private equity firm with extensive defense industry investments and a former ownership stake in [[EG&G]]:
- He served as a board member of iCapital, a Carlyle-invested group.
- He served on the board of directors for the Better Angels Society, an organization funded by Carlyle Group founder David Rubenstein.

These connections link Moultrie to the same private equity network that, under [[Frank Carlucci]]'s chairmanship, oversaw EG&G at the time EG&G's parking lot hosted the Wilson-Davis meeting. The Wilson-Davis document is central to UAP legacy program claims.

## 2022 AARO Denial and UAP Oversight

At the 2022 Congressional UAP hearings, Moultrie and [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] jointly denied the existence of UAP crash recoveries and reverse engineering programs. UAP researchers have characterized these denials as coordinated institutional disinformation. Moultrie's USD I&S office was also involved in awarding the 2022 [[Sand Corp]] contract to AARO. Sand Corp is a firm specializing in whistleblower suppression, and critics cite the award as evidence that AARO was designed to neutralize disclosures rather than investigate UAP.

## Battelle Memorial Institute Advisory Board

Moultrie has been a member of [[Battelle Memorial Institute]]'s advisory board since 2016. UAP researchers allege that Battelle has conducted classified analysis on recovered extraterrestrial materials, including [[Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium Alloy)]], since at least 1949. They also allege that it was the private contractor [[Jacques Vallee]] and [[James Fox]] implicitly identified as holding UFO materials during a December 2020 Joe Rogan Experience episode. Moultrie's service on the Battelle advisory board at the same time as he was the senior official overseeing AARO is cited as a foundational conflict of interest within the UAP oversight apparatus.

Moultrie attempted to remove his Battelle advisory board membership from his public LinkedIn profile. POGO.org preserved the association in screenshots before the deletion. Gerb cited it as evidence that Moultrie knew the Battelle connection was problematic given his UAP-related oversight role.
