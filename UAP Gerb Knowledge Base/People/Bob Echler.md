Bob Echler (more commonly spelled **Bob Oechsler**, and rendered "Eschler" in some captions) was a former NASA mission specialist and UFO researcher who, in 1989, conducted a personal inquiry into the possibility of making recovered UFO vehicles available for civilian scientific and technological research. British Admiral Lord Hill-Norton helped arrange the inquiry, which brought Echler into direct contact with several of the most senior US intelligence officials of the era and produced alleged admissions of US possession of recovered non-human craft. His exchanges with Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]], former Director of Naval Intelligence [[Sumar Shapiro|Sumner Shapiro]] and CIA DS&T Deputy Director [[Everett Heinman]] are some of the most directly sourced accounts in UAP research history. Gerb uses Inman's and Echler's exchanges as his "estimate of the situation" for the late Cold War, a fixed point against which he checks other claims of the period.

## Background and Lord Hill-Norton Connection

Echler was born in 1949 and lived for some forty years in Edgewater, Maryland. According to his obituary he worked for NASA as a mission specialist, including on the docking collar for the Apollo-Soyuz mission, and became a well-known UFO investigator in the 1980s and 1990s. He died on 6 June 2020.

His inquiry was facilitated by Admiral of the Fleet Lord Hill-Norton, a former Chief of the British Defence Staff and Chairman of NATO's Military Committee, who had relationships with senior figures in the US and UK intelligence communities. Hill-Norton's introduction gave Echler access to officials who would not otherwise have engaged with civilian UAP researchers. The British researcher Timothy Good, who was present at Echler's home for his first call to Inman's office, later published his account.

## 1989 Phone Call with Bobby Ray Inman

In 1989 Echler telephoned Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]], by then retired from his roles as Director of Naval Intelligence, Director of the NSA, Deputy Director of the CIA, and Director of the classified [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] (NURO). His first call to Inman's office was on 14 July 1989. Echler asked whether recovered UFO vehicles might ever become available for technological research outside military circles (see [[Inman-Echler Telephone Call]]).

Inman's response, as Echler and Gerb read it, confirmed the existence of recovered vehicles. He said that ten years earlier the answer on their availability would have been "no", but that things might be opening up. He named CIA DS&T Deputy Director [[Everett Heinman]] as the best person to ask, calling him a person "of very substantial integrity" who would "clearly be high on the list". Gerb notes that Inman, a NURO director, sent Echler to the DS&T, one of NURO's two founding agencies, and that Heinman went on to run the NRO's Program B from 1982 to 1989.

A portion of the Inman–Echler call is available as recorded audio, and Gerb plays it in several videos. In a later follow-up call, Inman's office warned Echler that he would be "breaching confidence and/or violation of the secrecy laws" by discussing Inman's involvement.

## Meeting with Everett Heinman at CIA Langley

Following Inman's referral, Echler met [[Everett Heinman]] at CIA headquarters in Langley, Virginia, on 10 August 1989. Heinman denied any knowledge of UFOs or UFO legacy programs at this meeting. In 2022, the researcher [[RGH UFOs]] contacted Heinman and cited Inman's claim that Heinman was the right person to ask. This time Heinman did not issue a flat denial. He said instead that he was "a long way and quite a few years from working in the area you are researching", offered to answer questions, and then stopped replying. Gerb's own attempts to reach him have gone unanswered.

## Meetings with Sumner Shapiro

Inman also put Echler in touch with former Director of Naval Intelligence [[Sumar Shapiro|Sumner Shapiro]], then an executive at the defense contractor [[BDM International]]. Echler met Shapiro in 1989, presenting himself as interested in working with the "group" that managed UFO information. According to Echler, Shapiro said that factions within the US government possessed extraterrestrial vehicles, that he had studied one at close quarters, and that teams would disassemble the craft, pack them up and ship them across the country to different laboratories. The craft had unique interlocking components that had to be taken apart in an exact sequence. Shapiro declined to specify where the craft were taken. Gerb compares the detail to the 1948 [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|Aztec crash]] and asks whether a component could have reached [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane|Crane]].

Gerb's *UFO Legacy Programs - Northrop Grumman* uses this meeting to tie the chain from Inman to Shapiro to Echler back to Northrop Grumman. At the time, Inman sat on the boards of [[SAIC]] and [[Wackenhut Services|Wackenhut]]. Shapiro was a BDM board member, and BDM was bought by [[TRW]] in 1997 and passed to [[Northrop Grumman]] with TRW in 2002. The video counts this among the few documented cases of a senior Navy intelligence officer privately describing the physical handling of recovered non-human vehicles.

A second meeting at Shapiro's Virginia home in 1990 ended abruptly. When Echler displayed a small hologram of an alien head to draw a link between the technology and its non-human source, Shapiro became visibly upset. He paced about his living room, expressed concern that he had not been able to reach Inman to confirm the purpose of the meeting, and terminated the visit, suspecting Echler was there under false pretenses.

## EMPRESS / EMP Experiments Claim

Echler also claimed that the Navy ran EMP (electromagnetic pulse) experiments out of Pensacola Naval Air Station, using onboard generators designated EMPRESS and EMPRESS 2. According to Echler, the experiments used directed electromagnetic pulses to disable UFOs and bring them down for retrieval operations.
