**Dr. Carl August Heiland** (1899–1956) was a German-born American geophysicist who founded the geophysics program at the Colorado School of Mines and co-founded the Heiland Research Corporation, a Denver maker of geophysical instruments. In UFO research he is best known as the scientist whom [[William Steinman]] identified as one of the anonymous "Dr. G" sources behind the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|1948 Aztec, New Mexico crash]] story, and as an alleged [[Majestic 12]] member who was present at the recovery. Earlier pages in this knowledge base followed the video captions in spelling his name "Highland"; the correct spelling is Heiland.

## Career

Heiland was born in Hamburg on 16 July 1899 and studied at the universities of Heidelberg and Hamburg, taking his doctorate at Hamburg in 1923. After working in the geodetic and geophysical instrument department of Askania Werke in Berlin, he came to Houston in 1925 as Askania's American representative. He joined the Colorado School of Mines late in 1926, taught its first geophysics course in January 1927, and headed its geophysics department until he retired from teaching in 1948. In 1934 he co-founded the Heiland Research Corporation in Denver, which later became the Heiland Division of Minneapolis-Honeywell, and remained its president until his death in Denver on 23 February 1956. He wrote the textbook *Geophysical Exploration* (1940).

UAP Gerb's video notes his work with magnetometers, both for oil and mineral exploration and, during the Second World War, for detecting submarines. That dual use is at the heart of the theory connecting him to the Aztec case.

## Alleged Role in the Aztec Case

According to Steinman's 1986 book *UFO Crash at Aztec: A Well Kept Secret*, Heiland was one of the scientists on site at the March 1948 recovery and later an MJ-12 member. Steinman argued that Heiland was also the original, unintentional source of the leak to oilman [[Silas Newton]] and magnetics scientist [[Leo GeBauer]]. On this account Heiland's company knew devices for detecting oil and water, GeBauer consulted with it, and the two had adapted wartime magnetic airborne detection equipment to prospecting. Steinman claimed they had even worked together on a government project in Antarctica in 1935, though the video notes he gives no evidence for this.

The theory continues that when MJ-12 learned Heiland was the source, he was withdrawn from the 8 March 1950 University of Denver lecture, where Newton stood in as "Scientist X." During the 1953 fraud trial Heiland was asked to testify against GeBauer's oil-finding devices and refused, telling his visitor he could not do so with a clear conscience because they had developed the devices together. Dr. James Meredith, from Heiland's own Colorado School of Mines department, testified instead, using a war-surplus-store device rather than GeBauer's.

Researcher Richard Ogden independently named Heiland as a likely Dr. G in an unpublished 1960s manuscript, reasoning from [[Frank Scully]]'s statement that one of the scientists lived in Denver and could fly to the crash site in two or three hours. The video adds that the Ramseys could place Heiland in the Southwest at the time of the crash. Gerb considers the thesis attractive but says it needs documentary proof, such as evidence that GeBauer worked for Heiland's company.
