Habib "Henry" Azadehdel, born in Soviet Armenia and later living in Nottingham, England, wrote on UFOs, mind control and crop circles under the pseudonym Dr. Armen Victorian. He made his name in the early 1990s on UFO bulletin boards and in magazines such as *Lobster*, and his book *The Mind Controllers* came out in 1999. Before that he had been convicted of orchid smuggling at the Old Bailey in 1989. John Lundberg's 2004 documentary *The Mythologist* is about him. Gerb's videos mention him only for his recorded conversations with Penn State president [[Eric A. Walker]].

## Conversations with Eric Walker

[[Robert Sarbacher]] had named Walker, in an interview with [[Stanton Friedman]], as a central figure in UFO crash retrieval alongside [[Vannevar Bush]]. Once Friedman made Walker's name public, researchers including [[William Steinman]] and Azadehdel began questioning him. Steinman's interview came in 1987. About three years later Azadehdel recorded three telephone calls with Walker, which Gerb compares with Steinman's interview in his video on Sarbacher.

As Gerb relays them, Walker told Azadehdel that [[Majestic 12]] existed but that its documents were probably forgeries, and confirmed Vannevar Bush's involvement. They discussed the [[1978 Bolivia UFO Crash]] and the possibility that insect-like bodies had been recovered. Asked about access to the programs, Walker put a question back to him: "How good is your sixth sense? How much do you know about ESP?" Gerb calls the third call the most cryptic of the three. He sets the ESP question beside the talks former Lockheed vice president [[James T. Ryder]] gave for the Lucis Trust, one of which contrasts the US, where ESP research "had to hide very carefully", with Russia.

Walker also told Azadehdel that he had gone to the site of the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg crash]] with a colleague and two off-duty military men. He would not say whether a UFO was recovered or how long he stayed, and said none of them wrote a report. [[Stan Gordon]] had heard of someone matching Walker's description at the scene. Gerb reasons that off-duty men could not have been the three-man Blue Book team, and asks whether Walker's party were the men in civilian clothes the witnesses saw.
