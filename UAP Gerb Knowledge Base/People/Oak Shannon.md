Oak Shannon is a former manager of special projects at [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]], a former Department of Energy nuclear physicist and a US Navy veteran. His name is usually written Oke (James Oke Shannon); Gerb's videos and their captions give it as "Oak" or "Oki." He helped bring about the October 16, 2002 meeting between Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] and physicist [[Eric Davis]] that is documented in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], and later confirmed that the memo is authentic. His notes from a 1985 classified working group on advanced physics, published in 2022, are a recurring source in Gerb's videos.

## Role in the Wilson-Davis Meeting

According to the Wilson-Davis memo, the meeting opened with Wilson bringing up Shannon, who had asked Wilson to speak to Davis about what Wilson had told Commander [[Will Miller]] about crashed or retrieved UFO craft. His involvement suggests he knew of Wilson's 1997 investigation into crash retrieval programs and had stayed in contact with Wilson after the admiral retired from the DIA.

## Confirmation of Authenticity

Shannon later confirmed the authenticity of the Wilson-Davis memo in a 2022 interview with Jay Anderson for the Project Unity podcast. Gerb gives his confirmation particular weight for two reasons: he managed special projects at Los Alamos, one of the most sensitive research facilities in the United States, and he was directly involved in setting up the meeting. When the video weighs the memo, it counts Shannon with [[Chris Mellon]], [[Luis Elizondo]], [[Ross Coulthart]] and [[Richard Dolan]] among those who vouch for it.

## Notes from the 1985 Advanced Theoretical Physics Working Group

From 20 to 25 May 1985, [[BDM International]] hosted a classified Advanced Theoretical Physics working group at its secure facility in McLean, Virginia, under DOE classification controls. Gerb says it was supervised by Army Colonel [[John B. Alexander]], a year after General [[Albert Stubblebine]] joined BDM. Shannon attended, and his notes from the meetings were published in 2022. According to Gerb's reading of them, they record:

- discussion of UFOs and legacy programs, and a major engineering project under Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]];
- UAP hotspots including Mount Hayes, Alaska, and Navy intelligence on an underwater UFO hotspot near Golfo San Matías, off the coast of Argentina;
- an organization that "collects, manages, and destroys" UFO information;
- a plan to approach [[Donald M. Kerr]], a future SAIC executive, for "influence and money", and elsewhere the entry "Don K/ET" beside other names, which Gerb takes to mean Kerr.

Gerb cites the Argentina hotspot as part of the growing evidence for a classified Navy program that tracks UAP at sea, and says Shannon's position in highly sensitive government research gives it weight. In his DUMBs video he wonders whether the working group, Stubblebine, a 1985 BDM study of deep-basing ICBMs and the working group [[Philip J. Corso]] described are connected. In his SAIC video he says he would like to speak to Shannon himself.
