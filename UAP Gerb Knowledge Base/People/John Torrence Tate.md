**John Torrence Tate Sr.** (1889–1950) was an American physicist at the University of Minnesota, long-time editor of *Physical Review*, and a senior figure in [[Vannevar Bush]]'s wartime research organisation. [[Scott and Suzanne Ramsey]] identify him as the most likely of the anonymous "Dr. G" scientists behind [[Frank Scully]]'s account of the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|1948 Aztec crash]].

## Career

Tate took his doctorate at the University of Berlin. After the First World War he was known for experimental work on electron impact, verifying discrete atomic energy levels and distinguishing excitation from ionization energies, which supported early quantum theory. During the Second World War Bush recruited him to the National Defense Research Committee, the forerunner of the Office of Scientific Research and Development, to lead anti-submarine research. Among his posts were scientific adviser to the commander-in-chief of the US Fleet and assistant chief of the OSRD's Office of Field Service. With biophysicist Otto Schmitt he worked on fitting PBY Catalina patrol aircraft with magnetic anomaly detection (MAD) equipment for finding submarines.

## The Dr. G Candidate

Scully described one of his scientists as having "more degrees than a thermometer" and said some of them had worked out how Japanese submarines were detected magnetically. The Ramseys concluded that the description points to Tate. In July 2011 they examined his papers at the University of Minnesota and found that he had travelled to the Southwest around the time of the Aztec crash. On his return he reportedly told a large audience that something he had seen on a recent trip had changed his life, and could change how energy is generated, how food is produced and how humanity is served. The archive described this as a complete reversal of his normally retiring manner. UAP Gerb's video places Tate among several wartime magnetic-detection scientists linked to [[Geophysical Service Inc. (GSI)]] and the oil industry who could have made up Dr. G.
