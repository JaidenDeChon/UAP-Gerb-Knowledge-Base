Strom Thurmond (1902–2003) was a U.S. Senator from South Carolina who served for 48 years. When he retired he was the longest-serving member of the Senate. Thurmond knew [[Philip J. Corso]] professionally through Corso's military and intelligence career. He was drawn by accident into the controversy over *[[The Day After Roswell]]* after he was asked to write a foreword for what he believed was a different book.

## The Day After Roswell Foreword Controversy

Thurmond originally wrote a foreword for *The Day After Roswell* that praised Corso's military service and character. According to researcher [[Stanton Friedman]] and others, though, Thurmond meant the foreword for an entirely different book about Corso's military career and activities in general, rather than for a book centered on UAP crash retrieval and the reverse engineering of alien technology.

When Thurmond learned that the book was really about Roswell, extraterrestrial biological entities and the seeding of alien technology into American industry, he angrily retracted his statements and withdrew the foreword. As a result, the book was reprinted early without Thurmond's endorsement.

Corso's original manuscript, *Dawn of a New Age*, mentions his relationship with Thurmond but never refers to any advocacy or foreword by the senator. This suggests that co-author [[Bill Burns]] or the publisher arranged the foreword without Corso's full oversight or Thurmond's complete understanding of the book's content.

## Relationship with Corso

Despite the foreword controversy, Thurmond and Corso had a documented professional relationship during Corso's military career. How much Thurmond knew about Corso's UAP-related claims, and whether or how far he was involved with them, remains unclear.
