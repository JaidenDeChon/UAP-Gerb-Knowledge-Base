Harold E. "Hal" Puthoff is an American physicist and parapsychology researcher best known for his work on remote viewing programs at [[Stanford Research Institute (SRI)]] for the CIA and his later work on [[Zero Point Energy]] and UAP-related propulsion research. He co-founded [[EarthTech]] International, an Austin-based research organization focused on advanced and speculative physics, and has been a central figure in multiple UAP-related research efforts including [[AAWSAP]].

## Lockheed Martin Advanced Technology Center Research

Puthoff and astrophysicist [[Bernard Haisch]] co-authored a paper on the [[Zero Point Energy]] of the quantum vacuum for the [[Lockheed Martin Advanced Technology Center (ATC)]]. It examined resonant frequencies that may be associated with interacting with the quantum vacuum for propulsion. The paper is published, institutional evidence that Lockheed Martin researched exotic propulsion physics at the level of its Advanced Technology Center.

## Comments on Kona Blue and Kingman

Gerb credits Puthoff with indirect confirmation that materials involved in the attempted [[Kona Blue]] technology transfer may trace back to the alleged 1953 [[Kingman, Arizona]] UFO crash retrieval. When asked directly about a possible Kingman connection, Puthoff reportedly gave "a bit of a cheeky look and refused to comment further". Gerb treats this non-denial as increasing confidence in the theory that the Kona Blue materials came from Kingman. Puthoff has also remarked more broadly that the Department of Homeland Security, in attempting to facilitate the Kona Blue transfer, ran into resistance from what he characterized as "legacy program gatekeepers."

## Aerospace Corporation Identification

Puthoff, working alongside [[Jacques Vallee]], [[Kit Green]], [[Eric Davis]], and Kristen B. Zimmerman, identified [[The Aerospace Corporation]] as one of the suspected legacy private corporations engaged in covert UAP research. This analysis, documented in Vallee's *Hidden Science 5*, fits with evidence that Aerospace Corporation has been involved in analyzing [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellite data related to [[Fast Walkers]] and developing "hazard support systems" mentioned in official DSP documentation.
