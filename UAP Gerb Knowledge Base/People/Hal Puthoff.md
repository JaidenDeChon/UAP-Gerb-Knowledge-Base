Harold E. "Hal" Puthoff is an American physicist and parapsychology researcher best known for his work on government-funded remote viewing research at the Stanford Research Institute (SRI), the program later known as [[Project Stargate|Stargate]], and for his later work on [[Zero Point Energy]] and UAP-related propulsion research. He founded the Institute for Advanced Studies at Austin, run through [[EarthTech]] International, an Austin-based organization focused on advanced and speculative physics, and has been a central figure in several UAP-related research efforts, including [[AAWSAP]] and [[To The Stars Academy]]. In Gerb's videos he appears as a source, a briefer and, on the question of who holds the legacy programs, a sparring partner.

## Career

Puthoff was born in Chicago on 20 June 1936. He took bachelor's and master's degrees in engineering at the University of Florida in 1958 and 1960, and was then commissioned in the [[US Navy]], serving with the [[NSA]]. He earned a PhD in electrical engineering at Stanford University in 1967. In 1972 he joined SRI, where with Russell Targ and others he led the CIA- and DIA-funded remote viewing experiments. In 1985 he left to found the Institute for Advanced Studies at Austin, incorporated with EarthTech in 1991. Under AAWSAP, EarthTech was contracted by [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]] to produce the program's Defense Intelligence Reference Documents, Puthoff among their authors; Gerb's SAIC video lists his paper on space-time metric engineering among them. In 2017 he co-founded To The Stars Academy with [[Tom DeLonge]] and Jim Semivan, as its vice president for science and technology.

## Lockheed Martin Advanced Technology Center Research

Puthoff and astrophysicist [[Bernard Haisch]] published a paper on the [[Zero Point Energy|zero-point energy]] of the quantum vacuum for the Lockheed Martin [[Advanced Technology Center (ATC)|Advanced Technology Center]], which Gerb dates to 1998. It examined resonant frequencies that might be used to interact with the quantum vacuum for propulsion. Gerb calls it "pretty breakthrough stuff" and compares it to the 2017 patent awarded to [[Salvatore Pais]] and the Navy for a triangular craft using specific frequencies to interact with the vacuum. Puthoff was not a Lockheed employee. Ryder, the Advanced Technology Center's later head, worked with Puthoff and [[Eric Davis]] on the Starfire project's science review team after leaving Lockheed, and quoted "my friend Dr. Puthoff" in a 2015 talk (see [[James T. Ryder]]).

## Comments on Kona Blue and Kingman

Gerb credits Puthoff with indirect confirmation that materials involved in the attempted [[Kona Blue]] technology transfer from Lockheed may trace back to the alleged [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|1953 Kingman, Arizona]] UFO crash retrieval. When Gerb brought up a possible Kingman connection with him, Puthoff "gave me a bit of a cheeky look and refused to comment further", which Gerb says gave him even more confidence that the Kona Blue materials came from Kingman. Puthoff has also said that during the Kona Blue saga the [[DHS (Department of Homeland Security)|Department of Homeland Security]] element ran into some of the legacy program gatekeepers.

## Who Holds the Legacy Programs

Puthoff holds that the UFO legacy programs were moved largely under prime contractors such as [[Lockheed Martin]] and [[Northrop Grumman]], because work done through contractors is out of reach of the Freedom of Information Act. Gerb disagrees, in his [[Videos/The Hidden Wing - US Air Force UFO Reverse Engineering Programs/summary|Hidden Wing]] and [[Videos/The Manhattan Project 2.0 - the Secrecy of UFO Crash Retrieval Programs Vol.1/summary|Manhattan Project 2.0]] videos: in his model most programs are overseen, through stripped, parallel oversight chains, and contractor custody would be far too exposed to leaks and espionage.

In *[[The Age of Disclosure]]*, Puthoff sits beside Eric Davis and speaks about reverse-engineered vehicles. Gerb objects to the film presenting [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|AATIP]] as a funded program with its own scientists, naming Puthoff and Davis; in his account AATIP was an unfunded informal working group, separate from the DIA's funded AAWSAP.

## AATIP, AARO and To The Stars

Former AARO director [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] has alleged that the reverse-engineering story is a conspiracy born in 2008 around AATIP and a small group that includes Puthoff, [[Luis Elizondo]], [[Harry Reid]], Eric Davis and [[Chris Mellon]]. Mellon answers that he introduced Kirkpatrick to Elizondo, Davis and Puthoff, each of whom spent hours briefing him in a classified setting without receiving any feedback. Puthoff was among the advisers Tom DeLonge named when he launched To The Stars Academy in 2017, alongside Mellon, Elizondo and others, and Gerb ties the academy to what he describes as a Clapper-era partial-disclosure effort. In February 2024 the [[Sol Foundation]] released interviews and panels with Puthoff, [[David Grusch]], Mellon and [[Kevin Knuth]] ([[Sol Foundation Interview Release]]).

## Wilson-Davis Memo and Will Miller

The 2002 meeting recorded in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo|Wilson-Davis notes]] was set off by a letter from Naval Reserve Commander [[Will Miller]] to Davis and Puthoff. Miller asked for anonymity and offered them a senior official who knew about alien reproduction vehicles, a list of civilian contractors in crash retrieval programs, and a team possibly involved in retrievals.

## Aerospace Corporation Identification

In *Forbidden Science 5*, [[Jacques Vallee]] records that he, [[Kit Green]], Puthoff, Eric Davis and others suspected the [[Aerospace Corporation]], Lockheed Martin and Northrop Grumman of being among the government contractors doing deep UFO work. Gerb connects the suspicion about the Aerospace Corporation with its role in analyzing [[Defense Support Program (DSP)]] satellite data related to [[Fast Walkers]] and in developing the "hazard support systems" mentioned in official DSP documentation.

## Stargate

Gerb's videos on Northrop Grumman and [[SAIC]] recall the Stargate psychic research that Puthoff directed at SRI, and which SAIC took over in the early 1990s.
