Carl Gustav Jung (1875–1961) was a Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst who founded analytical psychology, one of the major branches of depth psychology. His ideas, including the collective unconscious, archetypes, synchronicity, individuation and the psychological shadow, have had a lasting influence on psychology, philosophy, literature and culture. UAP research mostly cites Jung in two ways: as the originator of the mass hysteria explanation for UFO sightings shared by many people, and as the author of *Flying Saucers: A Modern Myth of Things Seen in the Skies* (1959), in which he analyzed the UFO phenomenon as a psychological and mythological expression of the modern unconscious.

## Connection to UAP Discourse

Skeptical accounts of mass UFO sightings, especially historical cases like the [[1561 Celestial Phenomenon over Nuremberg]], cite Jung as someone who explained shared anomalous perceptions as mass hysteria or collective projection. The argument is that when a group shares social anxiety or cultural expectations, its members can together produce and reinforce perceptions of things that have no physical basis outside their minds.

Jung's own 1959 book on UFOs was more careful than that. He did not dismiss UFOs as simple delusions. He argued that, whether or not the objects were physically real, their widespread appearance in the mid-20th century reflected deep collective psychological forces: anxieties about the Cold War, about nuclear annihilation, and about humanity's relationship to technology and the cosmos. He specifically did not rule out that they were physically real.
