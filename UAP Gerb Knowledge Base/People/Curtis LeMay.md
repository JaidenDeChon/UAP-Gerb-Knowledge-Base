**General Curtis Emerson LeMay** (1906–1990) was a highly decorated U.S. Air Force general who was Chief of Staff of the U.S. Air Force from 1961 to 1965. He is a central figure in the history of UFO secrecy. He created the [[Strategic Air Command Elite Guard]], a security unit that wore blue berets, and he refused Senator Barry Goldwater access to classified UFO materials at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]].

## Strategic Air Command Elite Guard (1956)

In 1956, General LeMay created the Strategic Air Command (SAC) Elite Guard, an Air Force police unit that wore distinctive blue fatigues and blue berets. From 1956 to 1966 the SAC Elite Guard provided base security for Strategic Air Command headquarters and installations, a span of years that includes the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]].

Witnesses at Kecksburg specifically described Air Force personnel in blue fatigues and blue berets arriving at the crash site within 15 minutes of civilian first responders, although the nearest known military base was 45 miles away. The speed of their arrival and the blue berets have led researchers to ask whether the SAC Elite Guard or a related covert unit was involved in UAP crash retrievals.

LeMay created this security force in the same period in which researcher [[Leonard Stringfield]] was documenting alleged UAP crash retrieval operations. Stringfield described a rapid-reaction unit known as the [[Blue Berets]] whose task was to secure crashed UFOs.

## Denial of Access to the "Blue Room"

LeMay is infamous in UFO research circles for denying Senator Barry Goldwater access to a classified facility at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] known as the "Blue Room," where retrieved UFO materials were allegedly stored. Goldwater, a close personal friend of LeMay and a brigadier general in the Air Force Reserve, later recounted that LeMay became angry when Goldwater asked for access and told him, "Not only can't you get into it, but don't you ever mention it to me again."

Goldwater held high-level security clearances and had served in military and intelligence roles, so LeMay's refusal suggests that the materials were protected by compartmentalized programs above the standard Top Secret classifications.

## Air Force Chief of Staff During Key UAP Period

LeMay was Air Force Chief of Staff from 1961 to 1965. That period included:

- The height of [[Project Blue Book]]'s operations
- Multiple alleged UAP crash retrievals documented by researchers
- The [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]]

General [[John P. McConnell]] succeeded LeMay in February 1965. On December 10, 1965, the day after the Kecksburg crash, McConnell attended President [[Lyndon B. Johnson]]'s meetings at his Texas residence, along with the full Joint Chiefs of Staff, the Defense Secretary, the NASA Director and other senior officials.

## Connection to UAP Secrecy

General LeMay created the SAC Elite Guard with its blue berets, refused access to the Blue Room, and was Air Force Chief of Staff during major UAP incidents. Together these make him a central figure in the institutional management of UFO secrecy. His actions suggest that he had considerable authority over who could see retrieved materials, and that he was directly involved in keeping UAP-related information compartmentalized, even from senior Congressional and military figures.

## James Conley Air Force Base Incident

The July 1999 MUFON UFO Journal cites an incident in which LeMay was present at James Conley Air Force Base during a close encounter between an F-89J Scorpion aircraft and a 30-foot UFO with four blue-white lights. After a near collision, the object departed vertically and disappeared at 990,000 feet altitude. His presence at a documented UFO encounter further ties him to the institutional management of such incidents.
