General Charles C. Krulak is a retired United States Marine Corps officer who served as the 31st Commandant of the Marine Corps from 1995 to 1999. His tenure is associated with the Marine Corps' push into experimentation for future warfare, including the "Sea Dragon" series of advanced warfighting experiments run by the newly renamed [[Marine Corps Warfighting Laboratory]]. The first large one was the 1997 [[Hunter Warrior Advanced Warfighting Experiment]] at [[Marine Corps Air Ground Combat Center 29 Palms]].

## Hunter Warrior and the Navy adjunct

According to UAP Gerb's video on the 1997 encounter of Sergeant [[Rodrik Castle]], Krulak made a late-1996 request, at the behest of Chief of Naval Operations Admiral [[Jay L. Johnson]]. He asked the commander of the Naval Strike and Air Warfare Center at Fallon, Nevada (today's [[Naval Aviation Warfare Development Center (NAWDC)]]) to assemble aircrews from Fallon and [[Naval Air Station Lemoore (NAS Lemoore)|Naval Air Station Lemoore]] to explore a "Hunter Air" concept during Hunter Warrior. The request followed Vice Admiral [[Robert J. Spane]]'s December 1995 study of sea-based aerial fire support for troops ashore.

Gerb argues that this naval element, bolted onto the experiment late in its planning, is the key to understanding what Castle saw near [[Emerson Dry Lake]]. His theory is that Navy and Air Force programs used the adjunct to put a triangular [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicle]] and an armed special mission unit into the exercise's area of operations. Krulak is cited only as the officer who made the documented request. He is not alleged to have known of any such activity.
