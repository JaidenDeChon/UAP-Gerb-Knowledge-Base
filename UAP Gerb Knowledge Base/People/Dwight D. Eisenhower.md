Dwight David "Ike" Eisenhower was the 34th President of the United States from January 1953 to January 1961. Before that he was Supreme Commander of Allied Expeditionary Forces in Europe during World War II, and then Supreme Allied Commander Europe for NATO. UAP research identifies Eisenhower as the president who did the most to set up the legal, administrative and organizational framework for what Gerb calls the "Manhattan Project 2.0": the structure of UFO legacy programs that grew out of the atomic bomb program's security apparatus.

## Classification Framework

Eisenhower's Executive Order 10501 standardized the modern three-tier US classification system of confidential, secret and top secret by eliminating a fourth "restricted" tier that had existed before. As an unintended result, managers of sensitive programs developed their own ad hoc security protocols, in particular top-secret codeword read-on access, to make up for the lost "restricted" tier. These informal controls became the de facto security mechanism for UFO legacy programs through the 1950s, 1960s and 1970s, the period Gerb calls the "wild wild west" era of legacy program security.

## NSC 5412 Committee

In Gerb's analysis, Eisenhower's most significant contribution to the structure of UFO legacy programs was setting up the NSC 5412 Committee on March 15, 1954. Officially, the 5412 Committee was an interdepartmental executive body that reviewed and approved covert US operations and defined what counted as a covert operation for US policy. It made the CIA the agency that carried out covert operations. Its original members were the Deputy Under Secretary of State, the Deputy Secretary of Defense, the President's Special Assistant for National Security Affairs and the CIA Director.

Gerb theorizes that the [[Majestic 12]] control group, under whatever name it had by 1954, was hidden within the 5412 Committee, also known as the "Special Group". In his view, this let the UFO control group work with a very small, close-knit membership, few paper trails, and deniability from the normal channels of government oversight. Informal NSC meetings, held off the books and not recorded, were the means of this covert governance.

Gordon Gray, an accused Majestic 12 member, was Truman's Assistant Secretary of the Army when MJ-12 was allegedly formed in 1947. He was Eisenhower's National Security Advisor from 1958 to 1961, which put him on the 5412 Committee and makes him a personnel link between the alleged MJ-12 and the 5412 structure.

## Military-Industrial Complex Warning

Eisenhower's farewell address to the nation on January 17, 1961 contained a now-famous warning about the "military-industrial complex": the danger of "unwarranted influence, whether sought or unsought" from a large military establishment combined with a large defense industry. Gerb suggests the warning may allude to Eisenhower's awareness that the UFO control group he had set up within the 5412 Committee was already slipping beyond presidential control toward the end of his administration. In Gerb's account, this loss of executive oversight would only accelerate in the decades that followed.

## 1954 Atomic Energy Agreement

The 1954 Atomic Energy Agreement, passed during Eisenhower's administration, is identified as the law that gave the Manhattan Project 2.0's classification system its strongest teeth. Section 142 established transclassified foreign nuclear information (TFNI), and Section 51 defined "special nuclear material" broadly enough to cover recovered non-human materials with radiological properties. It has been alleged that both provisions were exploited to classify UFO materials and programs under DOE statutory authority, beyond the reach of executive orders or normal congressional oversight.
