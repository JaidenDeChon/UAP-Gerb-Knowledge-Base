Ben Rich (1925–1995) was an American aerospace engineer and the second director of [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works|Lockheed's Advanced Development Programs (Skunk Works)]], from 1975 to 1991, succeeding the legendary [[Kelly Johnson]]. He is known as the "father of stealth" for leading the F-117A Nighthawk program. Rich is among the most controversial figures in UAP research because of alleged statements suggesting that Lockheed had achieved interstellar travel and possessed knowledge of extraterrestrial technology.

## Career

Benjamin Robert Rich was born on 18 June 1925 in Manila, in the Philippines, and his family reached the United States in 1942. He joined Lockheed in 1950 as a specialist in thermodynamics, aerodynamics and propulsion, and moved to the Skunk Works in December 1954, where he worked on the U-2, A-12 and SR-71 among others. In 1975 he succeeded Kelly Johnson as the Skunk Works' director, and he led it until 1991, through the development of the F-117 stealth fighter. His memoir, *Skunk Works: A Personal Memoir of My Years at Lockheed*, written with Leo Janos, came out in 1994. He died of cancer in Ventura, California, on 5 January 1995.

## Alleged 1993 UCLA Lecture

At a lecture at [[UCLA (University of California, Los Angeles)|UCLA]] in 1993, Ben Rich allegedly made extraordinary statements about advanced aerospace capabilities. There is no audio or video of the talk: the quotation was recorded by author [[Linda Moulton Howe]], who, Gerb notes, is known to take some reportorial liberties. Aerospace engineer Tom Keller, who confirmed his attendance in a 2010 MUFON article, says Rich stated: "We already have the means to travel among the stars, but these technologies are locked up in black projects, and it would take an act of God to ever get them out to benefit humanity. Anything you can imagine, we already know how to do."

Rich ended the lecture with a slide of a black disc zipping into outer space and said: "We now have the technology to take ET home." The audience reportedly broke into laughter, not grasping that he meant it literally.

[[Jan Harzan]], a nuclear engineer and former [[MUFON]] director who also attended as a UCLA alumnus, confirmed that the lecture took place and that Rich made these statements. Harzan recalls a question-and-answer session after the lecture, when approximately 20-25 attendees gathered around Rich. Asked "How does this getting ET home work?", Rich at first deflected, then asked an engineer in the group: "Do you think it's possible to travel to the stars?" The engineer said it would take a long time, and Rich replied: "No, it wouldn't. We found an error in the equations and we now know how to travel to the stars and it won't take us a lifetime to do it."

According to Harzan, Rich made three major revelations in that informal discussion:
1. Interstellar travel has already been figured out: "it's known".
2. There was an error in fundamental physics equations (Harzan suspects Maxwell's equations for electromagnetic theory).
3. Rich asked "How does ESP work?", and Harzan blurted out "I don't know, all points in time and space are connected". Rich looked him in the eye and said: "That's how it works."

In "The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 2)", Gerb quotes the same statement as one of Wright-Patterson Air Force Base's connections to the phenomenon, and places Rich's 1993 presentation at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]].

## The 1986 Letter to John Alexander

Rich's interest in UFOs is documented in writing. On 10 July 1986, [[John B. Alexander]] wrote to him proposing two categories of UFO, man-made and extraterrestrial vehicles, and asked for his thoughts. Rich replied by hand: "Dear John, yes, I'm a believer in both categories. I feel everything is possible. Many of our man-made UFOs are unfunded opportunities. In both categories there are a lot of kooks and charlatans. Be cautious." Gerb says Rich's 1993 disclosure can be neither confirmed nor denied, but that the letter shows he distinguished man-made from non-human UFOs.

## Corroboration by James Goodall

Aviation journalist [[James Goodall]], a close friend of Rich, provided further corroboration. In a conversation with [[Jesse Michaels]], Goodall said Rich told him: "We have things out there that are literally out of this world. Better than Star Trek or anything you can see in the movies."

During a hospital visit before Rich's death, Rich told Goodall: "Jim, we have things out in the desert that is 50 years beyond what you can comprehend. If you've seen movies like Star Trek or Star Wars, we've been there, done that, or decided it wasn't worth the effort." Rich specified, however, that the advanced technology was "not at Area 51. It's out in the desert there", possibly a reference to [[Helendale]], [[Tehachapi, California|Tehachapi]] or other Lockheed test ranges. Gerb calls the Goodall account the most interesting mention of Rich.

## ESP and Consciousness Connection

Rich's alleged comments link ESP (extrasensory perception) to advanced physics and interstellar travel. Offering "all points in time and space are connected" as the mechanism for ESP suggests that Rich saw consciousness or non-local phenomena as integral to advanced propulsion or communication systems.

The same theme appears later in the career of [[James T. Ryder]], the Lockheed Martin VP who attempted the [[Kona Blue]] material transfer. Ryder's 2018 Lucis Trust lectures explored ESP, consciousness and UAP phenomena. Ryder stated: "If you wanted to study certain kinds of things related to what's called ESP in the United States until very recently, you had to hide it very carefully because you were crazy and the government didn't want people to think they're supporting crazy stuff. But if you wanted to do the work in Russia, go right ahead. Fine. Here's your money."

Rich's comments also parallel alleged statements by [[Eric A. Walker]], a self-admitted UFO crash retrieval scientist, who told researcher Henry Azadehdel: "How good is your sixth sense? How much do you know about ESP? Unless you know about it and how to use it, you would not be taken in. Only a few know about it."

## TR-3B and XF-131 Connections

Asked whether Kelly Johnson had been the senior project designer for the [[TR-3B]], the triangular alien reproduction vehicle, [[Edgar Fouche|Edgar Fouché]] answered: "No, it would have been Ben Rich." Of the four engineers who described the [[XF-131 Super Sentinel]] to [[Bill McDonald]], two claimed to have worked for Rich at Skunk Works (in "Glendale, California", as the video has it), operating the radar cross-section range at [[Helendale]].

## Controversy and Skepticism

Rich's alleged statements are controversial. [[Jan Harzan]], the former MUFON director who corroborated the UCLA lecture, was later charged with crimes involving a minor, which may undermine his credibility as a witness; Gerb says he has no interest in pursuing the Howe and Harzan accounts for that reason. Rich also made these statements, if they are authentic, at a public university lecture and not in a secure briefing. That raises questions about whether they were hyperbole, misdirection or genuine disclosure.

However, the accounts from multiple independent witnesses (Keller, Harzan, Goodall) are consistent, and as Skunk Works director Rich oversaw the most classified aerospace programs in US history. Together these lend weight to the possibility that he possessed direct knowledge of reverse-engineered non-human technology.
