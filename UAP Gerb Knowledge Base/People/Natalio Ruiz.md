Natalio Ruiz was a Bolivian Army Corporal and a direct eyewitness to the [[1978 Bolivia UFO Crash]], one of the best-documented UFO crash incidents to involve a US government response. He saw the cylindrical object before it struck the mountainside near El Taire, Bolivia, and gave detailed testimony about its appearance and flight characteristics.

## Eyewitness Testimony

At about 4:15 PM on May 6, 1978, Corporal Ruiz saw a cylindrical object fly directly over his head and crash into a nearby mountainside. Ruiz described the object as resembling "a gigantic wine container emitting a trace of white smoke."

His description of a cylinder is consistent with the account of the Bolivian military personnel who later reached the crash site. They described the object as "a dull metallic cylinder 12 feet long with a few dents."

## Significance of Testimony

Ruiz's testimony is particularly significant because he saw the object in flight, not only after the crash. His description of its size, shape and smoke trail gives data on its flight characteristics. As a military witness, he carries institutional credibility, and his account corroborates the physical evidence the Bolivian military examined at the crash site.

The white smoke trail suggests either that the propulsion system was active or that the object suffered structural damage during atmospheric entry or flight.

## Bolivian Military Response

After Ruiz's sighting and the crash, the Bolivian military sent three jets to investigate the wreckage. Its rapid response, and its later request for technical help from US personnel, suggest that Bolivian authorities saw the object as anomalous and beyond their ability to analyse.

## US Government Interest

Ruiz's sighting contributed to the documented chain of events that led to:

- US Secretary of State [[Cyrus Vance]] contacting "appropriate government agencies"
- [[Project Moon Dust]] receiving film of the object from Bolivian military
- Deployment of US Air Force officers [[Colonel Robert Simmons]] and Major [[John Heise]] to Bolivia

Because Ruiz was the primary witness to see the object before impact, his account likely influenced the decision to treat the crash as a significant event that needed international coordination and a US technical assessment.
