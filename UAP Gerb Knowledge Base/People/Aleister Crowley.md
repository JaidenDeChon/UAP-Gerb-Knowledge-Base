Aleister Crowley (1875–1947) was a British occultist, ceremonial magician and poet who founded the mystical system Thelema. He is widely regarded as the most influential figure in 20th-century Western occultism. UFO researchers mention him mainly because of a contact experience he claimed in 1917, in which he allegedly drew the face of a being he called "Lam". Many researchers note that the drawing looks strikingly like the modern Gray alien archetype, several decades before that image became widely popular.

## Background

Crowley studied at Cambridge and became involved in esoteric orders, among them the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn. He then founded his own system, Thelema, based on a text he claimed a spirit named Aiwass dictated to him in Cairo in 1904. He founded the religious order A∣A∣ and later led the Ordo Templi Orientis (OTO). His provocative persona and practices, including ritual magic, drug use and deliberate public transgression, earned him the tabloid title "the wickedest man in the world."

## Lam and the Gray Connection

In 1917, during a series of rituals described as Amalantrah Workings, Crowley claimed to have contacted an entity he called Lam. He produced a portrait of the being, with an egg-shaped head, large eyes and a tiny nose and mouth, which appeared as the frontispiece of a publication in the early 1920s. The image is strikingly similar to the Gray alien archetype, which became common in UFO culture after the [[Roswell Crash]] of 1947 and especially after Whitley Strieber's *Communion* was published in 1987.

Some researchers suggest that Crowley's Lam contact may be an early documented encounter with the same non-human intelligence later associated with Gray aliens. Others treat the similarity as coincidence, or as evidence that the Gray archetype came from culture and not from genuine encounters. UAP research cites the Lam image as one possible precursor to the modern Gray mythology.
