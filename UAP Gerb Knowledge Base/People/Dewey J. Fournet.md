Major Dewey J. Fournet Jr. was a United States Air Force officer who, by January 1953, had spent fifteen months in Washington as the Pentagon's UFO desk coordinator (project officer for UFOs, in the [[Robertson Panel]]'s record), handling UFO affairs for the Pentagon. Gerb gives the stint as a year. He personally supported the extraterrestrial hypothesis as the explanation for some unidentified cases. He is known for his testimony before the [[Robertson Panel]] and for his work with civilian UFO researchers.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said Fournet spent one year, around 1952–1953, on the UFO desk. The Robertson Panel's record of his 16 January 1953 briefing describes "his fifteen months experience in Washington as Project Office [sic] for U.F.O.'s"; the one year is Gerb's figure.

## Robertson Panel Testimony

Fournet was invited to speak before the [[Robertson Panel]] in January 1953. According to the panel's record, he briefed it at 1000 on Friday 16 January, in its fifth session, on his fifteen months in Washington as project officer for UFOs and his personal conclusions. He told the panel that the extraterrestrial hypothesis best explained some unexplained UFO cases. That view ran directly against the panel's predetermined directive to debunk every case put before it. Gerb says the panel spent the rest of that day discussing Fournet's briefing, which he takes as a sign that his arguments caused considerable debate; the record says the case histories he referred to were discussed, then further case histories were examined with Fournet, Ruppelt and Hynek, and the session adjourned at 1200 for lunch. Even so, the panel's final report ignored his position entirely.

> [!edit] Corrected 17 October 2026
> Earlier versions said Fournet spoke at the panel's "fourth and final formal session" and that the panel spent the rest of that day on his briefing. The report puts his briefing in the fifth of eight sessions, on Friday morning, followed by other case histories, with the session adjourning at 1200; the rest of the day is Gerb's account.
