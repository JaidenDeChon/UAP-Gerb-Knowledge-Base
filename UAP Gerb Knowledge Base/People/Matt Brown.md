Matthew "Matt" Brown is a former US national security official and UAP whistleblower. He wrote the report describing the alleged [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)]] program, an unacknowledged special access program said to collect imagery and other intelligence on UAPs and reproduction vehicles, which journalist Jeremy Corbell delivered to [[US Congress|Congress]] in 2024. Brown later went public on the *Weaponized* podcast of Corbell and [[George Knapp]]. Gerb cites the report on IMCON and its sources, such as [[NRO Sentient]], and counts Brown among the whistleblowers he says he stands with, alongside [[David Grusch]] and [[Dylan Borland]].

## Background

Brown worked in national security inside the Office of the Secretary of Defense, the office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security, and the State Department. He says he found the program while working on classified networks, was rebuffed when he tried to report it through his chain of command, and wrote the Immaculate Constellation report for Congress after a four-year investigation. He identified himself publicly in 2025 in a three-part *Weaponized* interview.

## Immaculate Constellation

According to the report, IMCON was established in 2017 with the primary mission of "collecting imagery intelligence on unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) and ARV/RV reproduction vehicles". Gerb's NRO video reads the report's cases as products of NRO overhead infrared collection, and he believes that [[NRO Sentient]], begun in 2010 and built out by 2016, is a primary resource of the program. He also notes the report's description of IMCON's holdings as scattered across imagery databases in various organizations and services, which leaves the program's ownership fragmented. In his 80-year timeline Gerb places IMCON's creation within the National Security Council in 2017 and credits Brown and Grusch with confirming that it existed. In the 29 Palms video he cites the report's black triangles over INDOPACOM as an example of reproduction vehicles in the record.

## Legacy Security and Whistleblowers

In a clip played in Gerb's *Special Access Required Vol.2*, Brown speaks about an individual whose name and photograph are redacted, most recently at [[Northrop Grumman]], and whose title he gives as "deputy SAPCO for DARPA". He calls this person "a nexus point for legacy security" and "part of the dirty tricks department": people bring names "that need to be handled" to him, and he matches them to the capabilities to act on them. The result, Brown says, is "an ecosystem flooded with disinformation and psychological operations and targeted attacks against whistleblowers and their allies." He says he cannot reveal everything because he is communicating it on behalf of others. Gerb sets the clip beside [[David Grusch]]'s account of reprisals as an example of what the video calls [[Administrative Terrorism|administrative terrorism]].

Gerb returns to the subject in the outro of his second Northrop video, recorded the day after Brown's 18 March 2026 *Weaponized* appearance. He says the individual Brown described works under [[Terry Phillips]], Northrop's vice president for security, and is not DARPA's SAPCO Michael Smith, who worked at Northrop from 2004 to 2006. That Brown, Knapp and Corbell redacted the name, Gerb says, shows they "are not playing around".

Brown's June 2025 post about UFO materials at [[Naval Air Station Patuxent River]] led Gerb, in his SAIC video, to Amentum's joint venture with SAIC, which had won more than $1.3 billion in IDIQ contracts by 2020.
