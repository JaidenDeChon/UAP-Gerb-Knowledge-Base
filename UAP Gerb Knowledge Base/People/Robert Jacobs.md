Robert "Bob" Jacobs is a former US Air Force lieutenant who commanded a 100-man optical instrumentation unit at [[Vandenberg Air Force Base]], California, in the 1960s. He is best known for his account of a disc-shaped UFO striking a dummy nuclear warhead with beams of light during a September 1964 missile test, the [[Vandenberg Air Force Base UFO Film Incident]], filmed by his unit, and for then being ordered by the Air Force to stay silent about it. Gerb counts him among the most credible military UFO witnesses on record: his commanding officer, Major [[Florence J. Mansman]], later confirmed his account in writing, and Jacobs testified under oath to [[AARO]] in 2023. Across Gerb's videos the case recurs as the clearest example of UFOs taking an interest in nuclear weapons and ballistic missiles.

## The 1964 Big Sur Incident

Jacobs was the officer in charge of optical instrumentation at Vandenberg, responsible for the engineering photography of every nuclear payload and ballistic missile launched from the Western Test Range near [[Big Sur, California]]. His unit's 180-inch lenses and telescopes were sensitive enough to photograph a missile's "nuts and bolts" from 160 miles away. On 14 September 1964 his team filmed the launch of a dummy nuclear warhead, identical in size and weight to a real one. The test used a three-stage rocket and was designed so that [[Missile Chaff Decoy|chaff]] countermeasures would separate from the warhead, deceiving Soviet air defenses into targeting the decoy instead of the actual payload.

The launch proceeded normally through all three powered flight stages. Then, Jacobs says, an object flew into the frame, keeping pace with the warhead at about 6,000 to 8,000 miles per hour on the fringe of space. The craft was shaped like a classic flying saucer: two saucers pressed together with rounded edges and a hemisphere on top that Jacobs compared to "half of a ping-pong ball". The beams of light came from this dome. The craft fired a beam at the warhead, flew up and fired again, went around and fired a third time, went down and fired a fourth, then flew out the way it had come. The warhead fell out of the frame.

## Suppression and Disclosure

A day or two after the test, Jacobs was summoned to the office of his commanding officer, Major Mansman, who was meeting with three men in grey suits, later identified by Mansman as the director of the Office of the Chief Scientist and two government agents. After the film was screened on a 16 mm projector, Mansman told Jacobs the incident had never happened and that, if he was ever asked, he should explain the beams as "[[Laser Tracking Strikes|laser tracking strikes]]". Gerb notes that this cover story was impossible, because lasers were still in their infancy in 1964 and were not used for tracking. Mansman warned him of "the dark consequences of a security breach". Nobody else at Vandenberg saw the film. Years later Mansman told Jacobs that the men in grey suits had cut the UFO footage from the reel with scissors, put it in a briefcase and handed the major the edited film, ordering him to consider the incident closed.

Jacobs kept silent for 18 years. He reasoned that because he had never signed a confidentiality or non-disclosure agreement and had never been told the incident was classified, he was within his rights to speak. He went public in 1982 and faced immediate reprisals: telephone calls through the night telling him "you're going down". [[MUFON]] reported the case in 1985, and Jacobs's account appeared in the MUFON Journal in January 1989.

Mansman later confirmed Jacobs's account in a signed reply to staff at Paramount Pictures who were making a documentary on the case, describing the incident as Jacobs had told it in the January 1989 MUFON Journal as "absolute fact".

## Significance

Several things set Jacobs's case apart within the [[UFO Interference with Nuclear Weapons]] pattern. It involves physical film evidence (later confiscated), corroboration in writing from his commanding officer, a detailed craft description consistent with other classic disc sightings, and a documented effort by government agents to suppress it. Gerb pairs it with [[Robert Salas]]'s account of the 1967 [[Malmstrom Air Force Base UFO Incident]], and cites it again when discussing the 1953 Kingman crash during nuclear testing, the Vietnam-era retrievals of the "Infographic Show" whistleblower, and the V-2 sightings around [[Robert Sarbacher]]. [[Edgar Mitchell]] took a particular interest in UFOs disabling nuclear weapons, as in Jacobs's testimony. Gerb also cites Jacobs as an example of who really silences witnesses: not suit-wearing Men in Black but officers and officials in uniform.

The craft's physical description, two discs with a hemisphere dome, closely matches the craft in the [[Flyby Footage]] and in the [[McMinnville UFO Photographs]]. Gerb cites this match as support for a consistent saucer shape across independent sightings.
