**"Myron"** (pseudonym) was a trucker who, along with his cousin "JS," delivered specialty bricks to [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] two to three days after the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|1965 Kecksburg UFO crash]]. Myron claimed to have glimpsed the bell-shaped craft inside a warehouse at Wright-Patterson and, in later testimony, stated he also saw a non-human body on a workbench near the object. His account is one of the most detailed alleged firsthand observations of a retrieved UAP craft and its occupant.

## The Brick Delivery

Myron and his cousin JS worked for a family-owned shale and tile brick business in Ohio. According to Myron, 2–3 days after the December 9, 1965 Kecksburg crash, the company was hired to deliver a large shipment of double-glazed engineered bricks to Wright-Patterson AFB. The customer was the U.S. Navy, which sent a representative to inspect and choose the bricks. Myron later claimed the bricks were intended to construct a double-thick, lead-lined protective structure around the Kecksburg object to shield against radiation. Receipts of the transaction were reportedly lost when the business was sold.

At Wright-Patterson, a two- or three-star general in a blue uniform escorted Myron and JS to a brick warehouse reached by a narrow blacktop driveway. Outside the building, Myron saw:

- Four to five personnel wearing white coveralls, visor headgear, rubberized gloves and boots, and holstered Colt .45 pistols
- Navy insignia, not Air Force, worn by the personnel
- A 35-foot Army triaxle lowboy flatbed truck nearby, with a tarp still draped over its midsection

## Observation of the Craft

During a break from unloading the bricks, Myron looked through the warehouse doorway without being noticed. Inside, he saw:

- Metal scaffolding around a large bell-shaped object
- The object, about 10 feet tall and less than 10 feet wide
- A metallic surface of dull copper or bronze color, covered in charred residue or soot
- Parachute silk hanging from the ceiling and partly covering the object
- A single floodlight pointed downward, lighting the scene

Myron approached a worker using an acetylene torch and asked what he was doing. The man, apparently assuming Myron had proper clearance, explained that he was trying to get inside the craft. Every attempt had failed: acetylene torches, diamond-tipped drill bits and acid had made no progress through the hull. The worker added: "If there are bodies inside, they might be too hot for the mortician to handle."

A guard then found Myron and warned him: "**Forget what you've seen or we'll lock you up and throw away the keys.**"

## The Body Claim

Myron first contacted researcher [[Stan Gordon]] after seeing the 1990 *Unsolved Mysteries* broadcast on Kecksburg. He provided detailed testimony about observing the craft but did not initially mention bodies. In 1998, during an interview with Gordon for a documentary titled *Kecksburg: The Untold Story*, Myron revealed a new detail for the first time: while looking into the warehouse, he had also seen a body lying on a workbench beside the craft.

Myron's description:

- The body was covered by a white sterile sheet.
- It was about 4 to 4.5 feet tall, and he estimated it weighed about 80 pounds.
- A left hand was visible, sticking out from under the cover.
- The hand had three fingers and was covered in dark green or brownish skin, which he described as "lizard-like".

Myron said he had kept this to himself for decades out of fear, and decided to come forward because his health was declining. He said: "Right now is the time to tell it before something drastic happens... I may not live till tomorrow."

## Corroboration by JS

Myron's cousin "JS" was initially reluctant to speak with investigators but eventually confirmed delivering bricks to Wright-Patterson with Myron. JS said he saw the object the day before Myron did, sitting upright under a tarp on a lowboy trailer. He described it as shaped like the Liberty Bell and 8 to 10 feet tall and wide. He also saw armed guards and men in fatigue suits, but he declined to give much detail, citing family reasons.

Between March 30 and April 1, 1991, [[Leonard Stringfield]] interviewed JS, who confirmed his observations but did not mention the body.

## Credibility Assessment

Leonard Stringfield, who extensively interviewed Myron on videotape with the assistance of medical researcher Dr. [[Brian Thompson]], stated: "**I feel convinced of Myron's sincerity.**" Stringfield was known for a balanced approach to witness testimony and would not have said this lightly.

Myron's decision to hold back the body claim for nearly a decade before revealing it in 1998 raises questions. He said he delayed out of fear of government reprisal. Other UAP cases show the same pattern of late disclosure: [[Robert Willingham]], a witness in the [[1955 Del Rio UFO case]], at first denied seeing bodies and changed his account years later.

[[Stan Gordon]], speaking at the 2003 Annual Crash Retrieval Conference, noted: "In more recent years I have heard rumors that are not widely known that two small bodies were supposedly found at the impact location in 1965. I have generally accepted this as hearsay since there has never been any evidence to back up these claims." Gordon acknowledged the problems with credibility but noted that independent witnesses gave consistent physical descriptions.

## Significance

Myron's description of the craft matches those of witnesses [[Jim Romansky]] and [[Bill Bully Bush]] at the Kecksburg crash site. His claim that technicians could not get through the hull with conventional tools mirrors testimony from other alleged retrieval cases, including witness "[[RB]]," who stated he guarded a saucer in 1963 for two weeks while a science team failed to gain entry.

Whether Myron's body claim is accurate is still debated. His account of the craft itself is consistent with other witnesses and adds important detail about the alleged transport and storage of the Kecksburg object at Wright-Patterson AFB.
