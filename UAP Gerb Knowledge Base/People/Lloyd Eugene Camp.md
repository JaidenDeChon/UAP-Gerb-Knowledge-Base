Master Sergeant Lloyd Eugene Camp was a US Army radar operator stationed at [[White Sands Missile Range]] in the 1940s, where he did high-level research on captured German V-2 rockets. He is known in UAP history for reporting several occasions when unidentified craft intercepted V-2 rocket tests, which he confirmed both by sight and on radar. The craft circled the missiles and then accelerated at what Camp described as astonishing rates.

## Background and Assignment

Camp was assigned to White Sands Missile Range while the US Army was testing V-2 rockets captured from Nazi Germany under [[Operation Paperclip]]. As a radar operator there, he worked on advanced rocketry at a base that became a frequent site of UAP sightings during weapons tests in the late 1940s, when the UFO phenomenon was first emerging.

## The V-2 Intercept Observations

According to Camp's documented account, during at least one V-2 rocket launch he saw two silver disc-shaped objects appear beside the rising missile. He tracked the objects on radar while watching them by eye at the same time, so the sighting was confirmed in two ways. The discs circled the V-2 for several minutes, then turned on their sides and accelerated away at a rate Camp described as far beyond any known human technology.

Camp's daughter later confirmed that her father witnessed similar events "several times," which indicates that UAP interest in ballistic missile testing at White Sands was a recurring pattern rather than a single event.

## Corroboration with Project Twinkle

Camp's observations match documented UAP activity at White Sands in the same period. [[Project Twinkle]], a 1949 program set up to observe UFOs and green fireballs, recorded an incident on August 31, 1950, in which the UFO phenomenon was seen following a V-2 rocket launch from White Sands. Camp's firsthand account is independent corroboration, from a witness, of the pattern recorded in official reports.

## Connection to Sarbacher's Account

[[Robert Sarbacher]]'s son, Robert Sarbacher Jr., told researcher D.M. Duncan that his father had been asked to develop missiles carrying cameras, similar to those on V-2 rockets, that could track UAP at high speed, because conventional aircraft were too slow. Camp saw UAP tracking and circling V-2 rockets himself, and his account gives context for why the DoD Research and Development Board, on which Sarbacher served, would have made camera-equipped missiles for pursuing UAP a priority.

## Significance

Camp is one of a class of military witnesses, the radar operators and technical personnel, whose observations were made both by eye and by radar or other instruments. Such accounts are some of the most credible UAP evidence. His account documents early UAP interest in US ballistic missile programs. The pattern recurred throughout the Cold War, including in the famous [[1964 Big Sur Incident]] in which a UAP allegedly disabled a test warhead tracked by Lieutenant [[Robert Jacobs]].
