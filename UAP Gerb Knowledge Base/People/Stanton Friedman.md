Stanton Terry Friedman was an American-Canadian nuclear physicist and professional UFO researcher, widely regarded as one of the most rigorous and productive investigators in the history of the field. A graduate of the University of Chicago, Friedman worked as a nuclear physicist for major defense contractors, including General Electric, General Motors, TRW Systems, Aerojet General Nucleonics, McDonnell Douglas and Westinghouse, before turning to UFO research full-time. He is credited with starting the modern public investigation of the Roswell Incident and with tracking down its living witnesses, beginning with [[Jesse Marcel]] in 1978. With [[Bill Moore]] and [[Jaime Shandera]] he brought the [[MJ-12 Documents]] to public attention in 1987, and he spent years defending them. In Gerb's videos he is "the legendary Stanton Friedman": the researcher who obtained the [[Wilbert B. Smith]] memo, recorded [[Robert Sarbacher]] in 1983, worked to authenticate the [[Eisenhower Briefing Document]], and became [[Philip J. Corso]]'s sharpest critic.

## Career

Friedman was born on 29 July 1934 in Elizabeth, New Jersey. He studied at Rutgers before transferring to the University of Chicago, where he earned a bachelor's degree in 1955 and a master's in nuclear physics in 1956. He then spent fourteen years as an industrial nuclear physicist on nuclear and space programs for General Electric, General Motors, Westinghouse, TRW Systems, Aerojet General Nucleonics and McDonnell Douglas. According to [[Ryan S. Wood]], his father [[Robert Wood]] hired Friedman at Douglas Aircraft around 1967 to study nuclear propulsion for a company-funded effort to understand how UFOs work, which had no access to classified information. Friedman began lecturing on UFOs in 1967 and became a full-time UFO researcher in 1970. He died in Toronto on 13 May 2019, at 84.

## The Wilbert Smith Memo and the Sarbacher Conversation (1983)

In 1978 Friedman and [[Arthur Bray]] obtained copies of the 1950 memo in which Canadian radio engineer [[Wilbert B. Smith]] told Canada's Department of Transport that flying saucers exist, that the subject was classified higher than the H-bomb, and that a small group headed by [[Vannevar Bush]] was studying it. The memo had been downgraded from top secret in 1969. Smith's Washington source was [[Robert Sarbacher]], a physicist and consultant to the DoD's Research and Development Board.

In 1983, the same year [[William Steinman]] wrote to Sarbacher, Friedman tracked him down and recorded a conversation in which Sarbacher repeated the main points of his 1950 disclosures, including:

- Describing a meeting at [[Wright Field]] about a crash where recovered beings were described as "made like insects — they didn't have any skin on their bodies."
- Placing the crash "very likely" in 1947, consistent with the Roswell timeline. Friedman asked because [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] would then presumably have been involved.
- Alluding to several crash recoveries when pressed on why the subject outranked the H-bomb.
- Noting that the DoD's Research and Development Board was deeply interested in the UAP flight characteristics observed in the early 1950s.
- Speculating that a manipulation of gravity would be necessary to explain UAP flight behavior.

For Gerb the most important moment came when Friedman asked who attended the Wright Field meeting. Sarbacher remembered a man from Philadelphia who went to all the meetings "acting very smug about it". He was later identified as Dr. [[Eric A. Walker]]. Friedman and Steinman kept Walker's name private through much of the 1980s, hoping he could be persuaded to speak, and Friedman found an early-1950s Army document on "indoctrination for special intelligence for Mr. Eric Arthur Walker." Once Friedman made the name public, Steinman and others questioned Walker. Friedman's recording of the Sarbacher conversation is one of the most significant primary-source interview records in the history of UAP research.

## The Majestic 12 Documents

The [[MJ-12 Documents]] allege that a secret group called [[Majestic 12]] (MJ-12) was created by President Truman to oversee the investigation and management of recovered UAP craft and non-human materials. The first, the [[Eisenhower Briefing Document]], reached [[Jaime Shandera]] on undeveloped film in December 1984, and Friedman, Shandera and [[Bill Moore]] made it public in 1987. Weeks later, Gerb notes, Eric Walker told Steinman: "yes I know of MJ-12, I have known of them for 40 years."

The release caused significant controversy in the UFO research community and in the mainstream media. The FBI declared the briefing document bogus, many researchers and government officials have called the MJ-12 documents forgeries, and some specific documents in the set are widely believed to be fabricated. Friedman did much of the archival work on them. In 1989 he went to the National Archives and found several 18 November 1952 briefings for President-elect Eisenhower in Office of the Secretary of Defense files, none mentioning MJ-12 but confirming the date. He also placed [[Roscoe H. Hillenkoetter]], named as the briefing officer, in New York in late 1952 as head of the Third Naval District, not in Korea as critics claimed. Gerb quotes his conclusion: "The Majestic 12 documents problem is complex and extensive. On balance it appears that the EBD, TFM and CTM are almost certainly genuine. The SOM 1-01 memo is very likely genuine."

Gerb leans on that work. He calls the briefing document "somewhat convincing" because of Friedman's authentication, and recommends Friedman's *Top Secret/Majic* to anyone who wants to look into it. In his video on the [[Del Rio, Texas|Del Rio]] crashes he says the MJ-12 papers "combine a lot of disinformation and factual information similar to what Stanton Friedman claims." [[Tim Cooper]], who received the largest cache of the documents, later shared a five-page affidavit on his source with Friedman and Robert Wood.

## Roswell and Aztec

Friedman's 1978 interview with [[Jesse Marcel]] is the moment Gerb's videos use to date the reopening of the [[Roswell Crash|Roswell case]]: Marcel admitted he had been ordered to take part in the cover-up, posing with substitute wreckage for the press while the real debris was taken away. In his Kingman video Gerb uses that date to argue that [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]]'s story, which surfaced in 1973, could not have been riding on Roswell's coat-tails. On the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|Aztec case]], [[Scott and Suzanne Ramsey|Scott Ramsey]] says the identity of his witness "George", an airman who said the retrieval was coordinated out of Walker Air Force Base, was known to several researchers including Friedman.

## Critic of Philip Corso

Friedman was the sharpest critic of [[Philip J. Corso]] and his 1997 book *The Day After Roswell*. Gerb answers each of his points, but notes that Friedman's criticism was aimed at the book, the only source he had at the time; Corso's own manuscript was published later.

## Legacy

Friedman was skeptical of individual claims but persuaded by the cumulative weight of documentary evidence and witness testimony, and he consistently argued that a government UFO cover-up was demonstrably real. That approach set a standard for rigorous civilian UAP investigation. His decades of primary research, particularly his interviews with government-connected figures such as Sarbacher, remain foundational references in the field.
