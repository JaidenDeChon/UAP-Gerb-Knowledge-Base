Wernher von Braun (1912–1977) was a German-American aerospace engineer who led the development of the V-2 rocket for Nazi Germany and, after the war, the rockets of the US Army and NASA, ending with the Saturn V that took Apollo to the Moon. He is often called the father of the U.S. space program. In Gerb's videos he is never a witness himself. He turns up in three ways: as the protégé of [[Hermann Oberth]], a rocket pioneer who believed flying saucers were real; as the man whose Army rocket team was once considered for transfer into a "Defense Advanced Research Center", a name [[Edgar Fouche|Edgar Fouché]] gave to a laboratory at [[Area 51]]; and as one of the scientists named in the IPU Report, a Roswell document Gerb discusses with Ryan S. Wood.

## Career

Von Braun was born on 23 March 1912 in Wirsitz, in the Prussian province of Posen (now Wyrzysk, Poland). He joined the German Army Ordnance rocket program in October 1932 and earned his doctorate in 1934 on the secret rocket work at Kummersdorf. In 1937, at 25, he became technical director of the Army's new rocket center at Peenemünde, where the V-2 was built. He surrendered to U.S. forces in May 1945 and was brought to the United States under [[Operation Paperclip]], arriving at [[Fort Bliss]], Texas, in September 1945. In 1950 the team moved to Redstone Arsenal in Huntsville, Alabama, and in 1956 it became the core of the new Army Ballistic Missile Agency (ABMA), commanded by Major General John Medaris, with von Braun as technical director of its Development Operations Division. Gerb's videos describe ABMA as "commanded" by von Braun; formally Medaris commanded it and von Braun ran its rocket development.

In 1960 the team moved to [[NASA]], and von Braun became the first director of the Marshall Space Flight Center, where the Saturn V was developed. In 1970 he moved to NASA headquarters as Deputy Associate Administrator for Planning. He left NASA in 1972 to become vice president for engineering and development at Fairchild Industries, retired because of cancer at the end of 1976, and died in Alexandria, Virginia, on 16 June 1977.

## Oberth, His Mentor

Gerb's videos come back to von Braun mostly through [[Hermann Oberth]], the German father of modern rocketry, whom they call von Braun's mentor. [[Philip J. Corso]] quotes Oberth as saying: "We cannot take the credit for our record advancements in certain scientific fields alone. We have been helped by people of other worlds." In a 1954 lecture, which [[Kevin Knuth]] cited and Gerb had not heard of before, Oberth said flying saucers had been clocked at 19 km/s on more than 50 USAF and US Navy radar measurements, and the same year he wrote in *American Weekly* that flying saucers are real and are spaceships from another solar system.

In Corso's account, Paperclip scientists were part of what the Army brought to bear on [[Technologies of Unknown Origin]]. Lieutenant General [[Arthur Trudeau]], as Chief of Army Research and Development, gathered funds to attract the best scientists, including foreign ones brought over under Operation Paperclip, and Corso, as chief of the [[U.S. Army Foreign Technology Division]] that Trudeau created, said he led a team of Paperclip scientists and American technicians. Corso does not name von Braun as one of them.

## The "Defense Advanced Research Center"

Fouché said Area 51 held an underground laboratory called the Defense Advanced Research Center (DARC), near [[Papoose Lake]]. To the objection that no such center ever belonged to [[DARPA]], Gerb answers with a declassified 1973 historical study of ARPA. Under advanced aerospace projects it records a draft DoD directive considering the transfer of von Braun's Army Ballistic Missile Agency into a "Defense Advanced Research Center" linked to the Jet Propulsion Laboratory. Gerb is careful about what this shows: whether the center was ever created, and whether any of von Braun's concepts reached it, is unconfirmed. He returns to the same document in his videos on the TR-3B, SAIC and Sandia National Laboratories.

## The IPU Report

The IPU Report, one of the documents attributed to the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]], first became public in July 1995. It describes the Roswell recovery, including a radiobiological team from Sandia, and names Atomic Energy Commission scientists, among them [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] and von Braun, as chosen for the work. Discussing it with [[Ryan S. Wood]], Gerb anticipates that this will sound fantastical, and reminds viewers that von Braun's mentor Oberth seriously analysed the flight characteristics of UFOs.
