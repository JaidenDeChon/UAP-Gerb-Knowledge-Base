Richard Milhous Nixon (1913–1994) was Vice President under Dwight D. Eisenhower (1953–1961) and later the 37th President of the United States, from 1969 until his resignation in 1974. In UAP legacy program research he matters for two reasons: a 1972 executive order that reshaped US classification policy, and recurring allegations that, like his predecessor Lyndon B. Johnson, he was personally briefed on and involved in UFO crash-retrieval programs.

## Executive Order 11652 and the Rise of Special Access Programs

In 1972, Nixon signed Executive Order 11652, "Classification and Declassification of National Security Information and Material," which formalized the policy framework that would become special access programs (then referred to as black programs). The order legitimized enhanced, structured access controls for programs involving severe national security concerns. It replaced the informal, ad hoc practices of the Truman and Eisenhower years with a defined policy structure, while still keeping tight limits on how much Congress and other agencies knew.

EO 11652 drew significant criticism. The criticism prompted a 1973 House Committee on Government Operations report, which documented widespread, uncontrolled use of special-access, distribution and control labels, both by agencies with legitimate classification authority and by agencies without it. Gerb identifies this 1973 report as the first visible crack in the unified "golden era" UFO legacy program structure. He theorizes that unacknowledged special-access precursors expanded aggressively during the 1970s and 1980s, ostensibly to protect DoD acquisition programs and stealth aircraft, but were substantially driven by the need to protect UFO retrieval and exploitation programs. In his view, this same expansion ultimately helped expose the broader legacy program ecosystem to unwanted scrutiny.

## Alleged Involvement in UFO Legacy Programs

Gerb has argued there is a strong case that Nixon, like Lyndon B. Johnson before him, was personally briefed on and involved in UFO legacy program activities. Presidents such as John F. Kennedy and Jimmy Carter, by contrast, are assessed as having been kept outside the program's "need to know" circle during its golden era (1947–1978).
