Richard Milhous Nixon (1913–1994) served as the 37th President of the United States from 1969 until his resignation in 1974, following an earlier term as Vice President under Dwight D. Eisenhower (1953–1961). In UAP legacy program research, Nixon is significant both for a specific 1972 executive order that reshaped US classification policy and for recurring allegations that, like his predecessor Lyndon B. Johnson, he was personally briefed on and involved in UFO crash-retrieval programs.

## Executive Order 11652 and the Rise of Special Access Programs

In 1972, Nixon signed Executive Order 11652, "Classification and Declassification of National Security Information and Material," which formalized the policy framework that would become special access programs (then referred to as black programs). The order legitimized enhanced, structured access controls for programs involving severe national security concerns, replacing the informal, ad hoc practices of the Truman and Eisenhower years with a defined policy structure — while preserving tight bottlenecks on congressional and interagency awareness.

EO 11652 drew significant criticism, prompting a 1973 House Committee on Government Operations report that documented widespread, uncontrolled use of special-access, distribution, and control labels — by both agencies with legitimate classification authority and agencies without it. UAP Gerb identifies this 1973 report as the first visible crack in the unified "golden era" UFO legacy program structure, theorizing that the aggressive expansion of unacknowledged special-access precursors during the 1970s and 1980s, ostensibly to protect DoD acquisition programs and stealth aircraft, was substantially driven by the need to protect UFO retrieval and exploitation programs — and that this same expansion ultimately helped expose the broader legacy program ecosystem to unwanted scrutiny.

## Alleged Involvement in UFO Legacy Programs

UAP Gerb has argued there is a strong case that Nixon, like Lyndon B. Johnson before him, was personally briefed on and involved in UFO legacy program activities, in contrast to presidents such as John F. Kennedy and Jimmy Carter, who are assessed as having been kept outside the program's "need to know" circle during the program's golden era (1947–1978).
