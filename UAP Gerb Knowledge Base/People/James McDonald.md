Dr. James E. McDonald was an atmospheric physicist at the University of Arizona. In the 1960s he became one of the most rigorous and credible scientists to argue for serious investigation of UFOs. Along with [[J. Allen Hynek]], he is regarded as one of the leading academics to argue publicly that many UFO reports were genuine unknowns that deserved scientific study. The government and the [[Condon Committee]], which he criticized, consistently blocked his efforts.

## Advocacy for Scientific Investigation

McDonald was one of several credible researchers who asked for access to the [[Condon Committee]] study and offered to share research files and give briefings. [[Edward Condon]] turned down many of these requests. McDonald and Hynek both asked for seats on the independent civilian panel that Congress had called for in 1966, and both were refused. McDonald later obtained [[Robert J. Low]]'s internal memo and made it public in 1967. The memo described the Condon Committee's strategy of appearing objective while reaching predetermined dismissive conclusions.

McDonald called the final [[Condon Report]] "inadequate" and said publicly that it "represents an examination of only a tiny fraction of the most puzzling UFO reports of the past two decades" and that its "quality of scientific argument is wholly unsatisfactory." He and Hynek testified together that the committee had ignored important evidence and could not explain 25–30% of the cases it examined.
