**Robert Spencer Carr** (1909–1994) was an American science-fiction writer and former University of South Florida instructor who, in October 1974, publicly claimed that the US government held a crashed flying saucer and the bodies of its crew. He tied the claim to the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|1948 Aztec, New Mexico crash]] and helped revive the case two decades after it had been dismissed as a hoax. His claims are also an early source of the "Hangar 18" story about alien bodies held at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. UAP Gerb's video refers to him once as "John Spencer Carr."

## The 1974 Claims

In October 1974, speaking on a Florida radio show and in the press, including the *Clearwater Sun*, Carr said credible information on Aztec had reached him from five sources, among them a senior Air Force officer involved in the retrieval. He said the craft and its dead crew were flown to [[Edwards Air Force Base]] and then taken to Wright-Patterson. UAP Gerb notes that Carr garbled or conflated several details. He gave the crash date as 12 February 1948, described military men approaching a 30-foot saucer with guns drawn, and said 12 occupants had died of decompression. Mike McClellan's 1975 article "The UFO Crash of 1948 Is a Hoax," in *Official UFO*, knocked the case down again.

## Carr and Stringfield

Carr would not name his sources publicly, but he and [[Leonard Stringfield]] corresponded at length. On 6 February 1982, fearing for his health, he named his Air Force source to Stringfield, asking him to keep it secret "as long as I live." Stringfield wrote that he sat back dumbfounded because he knew the name well. Carr's disclosures, together with [[William Steinman]]'s research, revived Stringfield's interest in Aztec.
