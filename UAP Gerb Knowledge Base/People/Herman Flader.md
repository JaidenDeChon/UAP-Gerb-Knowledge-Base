**Herman Flader** was a Denver businessman whose complaint led to the 1953 fraud conviction of oilman [[Silas Newton]] and magnetics scientist [[Leo GeBauer]], the men behind the published [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|Aztec crash]] story. The verdict was widely taken as proof that the Aztec account was a con. Video captions sometimes render his name "Flater."

## The Complaint

Flader had invested substantial sums in Newton's oil wells, which, according to UAP Gerb's 2025 video on Aztec, were paying him handsome royalties, and had also bought an interest in GeBauer's oil-detecting devices, the so-called "doodlebugs." Journalist [[J. P. Cahn]] found him and told him he would "never see a nickel" of his investment. Encouraged by Cahn and with FBI help, Flader alleged in 1951 that he had been defrauded of $250,000. He said the device worked every time GeBauer used it but never when he did. When no federal judge in Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico or Utah would take a federal case, his attorney went to a Denver magistrate. Charges were filed on 10 October 1952, and Newton and GeBauer were arrested on 18 October.

## At Trial

Flader was the prosecution's first witness and testified for about two weeks. The defence produced a contract, signed by Flader, giving him an interest in three of the devices; Flader denied signing it, but witnesses said they saw him sign. The defence also subpoenaed a "health machine" in which Flader had been trying to sell Newton and GeBauer an interest, but the judge ruled it irrelevant. The FBI had found 32 of 33 investors satisfied with their dealings with Newton and GeBauer, leaving Flader as the sole complainant. The two men were found guilty on 29 December 1953 but never sentenced. The video and the Ramseys argue the prosecution was retaliation for leaking the Aztec story.
