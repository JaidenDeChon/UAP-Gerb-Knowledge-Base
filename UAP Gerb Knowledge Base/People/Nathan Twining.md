Lieutenant General Nathan F. Twining was a senior United States Air Force officer whose assertion that flying discs were "real and not visionary or fictitious" led directly to the creation of [[Project Sign]], the Air Force's first official UFO investigation program. Twining made the statement in a letter to Air Force Commanding General George Shugen. In the late 1940s it was the highest-level acknowledgment from the Air Force that the UFO phenomenon was physically real.

## Creation of Project Sign

Twining's definitive assertion that flying discs were real gave the Air Force its mandate to set up Project Sign in 1948. The program's job was to investigate UFO reports systematically. Every Project Sign report had to be sent to the [[Army and Navy Research and Development Board]], the [[USAF Scientific Advisory Board]] and the [[Atomic Energy Commission]], which shows that the phenomenon concerned officials at a high level and in several agencies at the time.

The arrangement set up under Twining's directive treated UFOs as a legitimate subject for military intelligence and scientific analysis instead of dismissing them as misidentifications or hoaxes. It was a significant moment in the government's official handling of UFOs.
