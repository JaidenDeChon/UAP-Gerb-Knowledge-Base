Lloyd A. Duscha was Deputy Director of Engineering and Construction for the [[US Army Corps of Engineers]]. He is known in UFO and underground-base research for a conference talk, "Underground Facilities for Defense: Experience and Lessons", in which he said that several of the Corps' most interesting underground facilities were still classified. Researchers including [[Richard Sauder]] and UAP Gerb treat the remark as one of the most direct official acknowledgments of a classified underground construction program. His name is sometimes transcribed as "Dua" or "Ducha".

## "Underground Facilities for Defense"

UAP Gerb dates the talk to 1989; its text appears in the proceedings of a 1987 conference on tunneling and underground transport. Duscha said that after World War II, political and economic factors changed the underground construction picture and caused a renewed interest to "think underground", and that as a result the Corps "became involved in the design and construction of some very complex and interesting military projects". He then departed from his stated topic because "several of the most interesting facilities that have been designed and constructed by the Corps are still classified".

Describing the Corps' 1960s work on the NORAD facility at the [[Cheyenne Mountain Complex]], Duscha added that "there are other projects of similar scope which I cannot identify but which include multiple chambers up to 50 ft wide and 100 ft high", excavated the same way.

## Significance in DUMB Research

In *Deep Underground Military Bases (D.U.M.Bs.) - UFO Legacy Programs*, UAP Gerb opens with Duscha's remarks to establish that [[Deep Underground Military Bases (DUMBs)|deep underground military bases]] are real and that some remain classified. He pairs them with Air Materiel Command's 1947 request for German underground specialists to bound a roughly 42-year window, from 1947 to 1989, in which the facilities of interest to him were built.
