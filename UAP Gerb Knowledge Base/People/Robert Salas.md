Robert Salas is a former US Air Force officer. On the night of March 24, 1967, as a first lieutenant, he was on duty as a missile launch officer in an underground nuclear launch control facility of Oscar Flight, near [[Malmstrom Air Force Base]], Montana. He is the principal witness to the [[Malmstrom Air Force Base UFO Incident]], in which, by his account, a pulsating red oval craft hovered over the launch facility's front gate while all 10 of its Minuteman nuclear missiles failed. Salas later put the event on record in a sworn affidavit, and three other Malmstrom men corroborated it in affidavits of their own.

## Background

Salas graduated from the US Air Force Academy in 1964 and served on active duty until 1971, as an ICBM launch officer and a missile propulsion engineer, and later took a master's degree in aerospace engineering from the Air Force Institute of Technology. In 1967 he was assigned to the 490th Strategic Missile Squadron at Malmstrom Air Force Base. Gerb calls him the on-duty commander of the launch control facility; Salas's own accounts give his post as deputy missile combat crew commander. The crew's main duty was to monitor the readiness and security status of 10 Minuteman ICBMs in underground silos. After leaving the Air Force he worked for the Federal Aviation Administration. Since the 1990s he has spoken publicly about the incident, and in 2005 he published *Faded Giant*, written with James Klotz, about it.

## The March 1967 Incident

During the night of March 24, 1967, Salas received the first in a series of increasingly urgent calls from his flight security controller (FSC). The first call reported lights in the sky moving in ways inconsistent with any known aircraft: high velocity, abrupt changes of direction and no engine noise. Minutes later the same FSC called again, alarmed, to report that a pulsating red oval-shaped object about 30 to 40 feet in diameter was hovering directly over the front gate. Its glow was so bright that no structure could be made out. Security personnel outside had drawn their weapons on the craft.

At the same time, alarms and indicators on Salas's launch commander console began to fail in rapid succession. All 10 Minuteman nuclear missiles showed red fault status, a guidance and control system failure state that left every missile unable to launch. The craft then departed. The missiles stayed inoperable for the rest of the night, though a later inspection found no permanent damage.

The following morning, squadron commander Colonel George Eldridge briefed Salas and his crew. Eldridge confirmed the incident was not part of any Air Force exercise and offered no explanation for why it had occurred. An officer from the Air Force Office of Security and Intelligence informed the Malmstrom crew that the event was classified Secret and directed them not to discuss it.

## Corroboration and Documentation

According to Gerb, Salas swore his account into the record in a 2010 affidavit. Three other Malmstrom personnel submitted independent sworn affidavits corroborating the events of that night:

- Robert Jamison, then a first lieutenant and Minuteman missile targeting officer
- Patrick McDonough, then an airman first class
- Dwynne Arneson, then the officer in charge of the base communications centre

(Gerb renders their names as "Robert C. Jameson", "Patrick McDonah" and "Dwin C. Arneson".)

With four sworn statements about the same incident, Salas's among them, the Malmstrom case is one of the most formally documented instances of [[UFO Interference with Nuclear Weapons]] in the unclassified record.

## Significance

Salas's account is one of the main cases cited to establish a pattern of UFO interference with US nuclear weapons systems during the Cold War (see [[UFO-Nuclear Connection]]). Gerb pairs it with [[Robert Jacobs]]'s account of a UFO striking a dummy warhead in 1964, and treats the simultaneous failure of 10 ICBMs as a demonstration of an apparent capability to disable nuclear deterrent assets without physical access. [[David Grusch]] confirmed the Malmstrom incident as fact in his interview with [[Ross Coulthart]] on NewsNation. Gerb also cites Salas when weighing other accounts: the red-orange colour of the Malmstrom object in the [[Infographic Show Whistleblower]]'s Vietnam story, and the UFO activity reported around the 1953 nuclear tests in the Kingman case. In his whistleblower series he groups Salas with [[Michael Herrera]] as witnesses whose testimony is sworn into the legal record.
