George Trimble was Vice President for Aviation and Advanced Propulsion Systems at the Glenn L. Martin Company in the 1950s. He is known in UAP-adjacent research for recruiting physicist [[Lou Whitten]] into the Research Institute for Advanced Studies (RIAS) in 1955 to work on anti-gravity propulsion. This is a documented case of corporate-funded anti-gravity R&D inside a company that would eventually become part of [[Lockheed Martin]].

## Role in Anti-Gravity Research

In 1955, Trimble recruited Whitten to the RIAS with an explicit brief to develop anti-gravity propulsion. The work was carried out at the corporate level within the Glenn L. Martin Company, one of the major American aerospace manufacturers of the postwar era, and journal records confirm that it took place. The Glenn L. Martin Company later became Martin Marietta, which merged with Lockheed Corporation in 1995 to form Lockheed Martin. That gives a direct institutional line from Trimble's 1955 anti-gravity program to the advanced research divisions of today's Lockheed Martin.
