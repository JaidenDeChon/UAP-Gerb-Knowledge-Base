**Italo Balbo** (1896–1940) was an Italian Air Marshal and a leading figure in Italian Fascism. He was Commander-in-Chief of the Italian Air Force (Regia Aeronautica) and later Governor-General of Italian Libya. One of the most prominent aviators of the interwar period, he was known for leading mass formation flights across the Atlantic and for pioneering long-distance aviation. He was also one of the quadrumviri (four leaders) who organized Mussolini's 1922 March on Rome.

## Role in RS-33

As Air Marshal and head of Italy's air forces, Balbo was a natural member of the top-secret [[RS-33 (Gabinetto RS 33)]] group, which [[Benito Mussolini]] set up in June 1933 to examine the unknown craft recovered near [[Magenta, Italy]]. The RS-33 group was made up of Italy's highest political and military leaders: Mussolini himself as prime minister, [[Galeazzo Ciano]] as foreign minister, and Balbo for the air force. Astronomer [[Gino Cecchini]] gave scientific support, possibly along with radio pioneer [[Guglielmo Marconi]].

Since the object was a flying craft and Balbo was an expert in aviation technology, his involvement would have been critical in judging whether it was an advanced aircraft built by a foreign power (as Mussolini initially believed) or something beyond known capabilities.

## Death

Balbo died in June 1940 when Italian anti-aircraft fire shot down his aircraft over Tobruk, Libya. The incident was attributed to friendly fire, though some historians have raised questions about whether it may have been deliberate, given Balbo's growing friction with Mussolini over Italy's alliance with Nazi Germany.
