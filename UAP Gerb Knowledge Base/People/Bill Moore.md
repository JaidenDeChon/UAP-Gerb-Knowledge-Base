**William L. "Bill" Moore** is an American UFO researcher and author, co-author with Charles Berlitz of *The Roswell Incident* (1980), one of the books that brought the [[Roswell Crash]] back to public attention. At the 1989 MUFON symposium he admitted that during the 1980s he had supplied information to US government counterintelligence, including on researchers such as Paul Bennewitz, with [[Richard Doty]] of the [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations|AFOSI]] at [[Kirtland Air Force Base, New Mexico|Kirtland Air Force Base]] acting as the go-between. The admission has made him a central figure in debates over government disinformation, including the provenance of the [[MJ-12 Documents|Majestic documents]].

## The Aztec Debunk

In Gerb's video on the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|1948 Aztec crash]], Moore appears as the author of one of the debunks of the case, presented at a 1985 MUFON symposium. [[Leonard Stringfield]] later wrote, in his foreword to [[William Steinman]]'s 1986 book on Aztec, that he had been taken in by the work of [[J. P. Cahn]] and Bill Moore. Gerb notes theories that Moore and Doty had a hand in producing some Majestic documents, and wonders whether a 2017 Majestic document retelling Steinman's Aztec account was part of the same kind of effort.

## The Blue Room requests

In [[Videos/NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering/summary|NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering]], Gerb cites Moore ("yeah, I know, problematic, but still worthy") for two Freedom of Information Act requests about the [[Blue Room]]. In 1980 Moore asked for a records search among the directorates of the [[Air Force Foreign Technology Division|Foreign Technology Division]]; the Air Force forwarded it to the Aeronautical Systems Division instead. After an Air Force Form 1350 record of a 1955 film titled "Blue Room Radar Scope" surfaced, a follow-up request drew a reply treating "Blue Room" as an Air Force project, probably a radar beacon film, and saying there were no records. Gerb also notes that [[Leonard Stringfield]] insisted the alleged [[Twining White Sands Air Accident Report (1947)|Twining White Sands report]] he published had nothing to do with the Majestic document packages sent to Moore and Jaime Shandera.
