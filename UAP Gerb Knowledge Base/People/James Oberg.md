**James Oberg** is an American space journalist and author who spent more than two decades as an engineer in NASA's mission control at the Johnson Space Center, working on the Space Shuttle program, before writing about spaceflight for television and print. He specialises in the Soviet and Russian space programs and is a long-standing sceptic of UFO claims.

## The Cosmos 96 theory

Oberg is the best-known proponent of the theory that the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|Kecksburg object]] was Soviet. In a television interview shown in UAP Gerb's *The 1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania UFO Crash*, he says that if anything came down it was probably a spacecraft "from Russia and not from Mars". He points to a failed Soviet Venus probe, [[Cosmos 96]], that fell out of Earth orbit that day. He argues the US would have wanted its heat shield, since the shield's thickness, material and weight would reveal the size of Soviet ICBM warheads. The host notes that a 1965 NASA release, records obtained by [[Stan Gordon]], and NASA orbital-debris scientist [[Nicholas Johnson]]'s later analysis all put Cosmos 96's re-entry over Canada about 13 hours before the Kecksburg fireball.
