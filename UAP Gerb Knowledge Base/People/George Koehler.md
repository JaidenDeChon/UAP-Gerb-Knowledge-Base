**George Koehler** was a radio salesman and engineer at Denver radio station KMYR and a friend of oilman [[Silas Newton]]. He helped set up Newton's 8 March 1950 University of Denver lecture on the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|Aztec crash]] and recorded it. The day after the lecture, the [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations]] began looking for him, and he became the first known person questioned by a US agency about the Aztec story. The video's captions render his name "Kohler."

## The AFOSI Interrogation

According to Gerb's 2025 video on Aztec, AFOSI agents came to KMYR the day after the lecture, months before [[Frank Scully]]'s *Behind the Flying Saucers* was published. After two days of trying, they questioned Koehler about Newton, flying saucers and cube-like radios said to have been recovered from the crash. [[Scott and Suzanne Ramsey]] found and reproduced the interrogation, and the recording of Newton's lecture and his chalkboard drawings turned up in Scully's archive. Koehler is also the first link in a documented chain of retellings that ran from Newton to Koehler, then through several Denver and Kansas City businessmen to a Kansas newspaper. It was one of the routes by which the Aztec story spread in early 1950.

At the 1953 fraud trial, Denver Post reporter Thor Severson produced a photograph of Scully, Newton and Koehler together and claimed, without evidence, that it showed a meeting about oil-detecting devices.
