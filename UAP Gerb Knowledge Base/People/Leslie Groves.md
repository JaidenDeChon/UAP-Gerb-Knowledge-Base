**General Leslie Richard Groves Jr.** (1896–1970) was a United States Army Corps of Engineers officer and the military director of the [[Manhattan Project]], the secret wartime program that developed the first atomic bombs. Groves oversaw the construction of the Pentagon (1941–1942) and then took command of the Manhattan Project in September 1942. In that role he managed a sprawling network of facilities, contractors and scientists across the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom. He supervised the building and running of the main sites, including [[Oak Ridge, Tennessee]] (uranium enrichment), Hanford, Washington (plutonium production), and [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]] (weapons design). The first atomic device was detonated at the Trinity test site in New Mexico on July 16, 1945, and atomic bombs were dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki the following month.

## Post-War Career

After the war, Groves remained in charge of the Manhattan Project until 1947, when responsibility for U.S. nuclear weapons was transferred to the newly established [[Atomic Energy Commission]] (AEC). On February 28, 1947, Groves was appointed the first chief of the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] (AFSWP). It took over the Manhattan Project's military functions and was responsible for the custody, assembly and delivery of nuclear weapons for the armed forces. He retired from the Army in early 1948 and then joined Remington-Rand (later Sperry Rand), where he oversaw the company's advanced research division.
