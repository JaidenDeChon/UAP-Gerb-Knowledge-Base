General Leslie Richard Groves Jr. (1896–1970) was a [[US Army Corps of Engineers]] officer and the military director of the [[Manhattan Project]], the secret wartime program that developed the first atomic bombs. He oversaw the construction of the [[Pentagon]] (1941–1942) and then took command of the Manhattan Project in September 1942. In that role he managed a large network of facilities, contractors and scientists across the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom, including the main sites at [[Oak Ridge, Tennessee]] (uranium enrichment), Hanford, Washington (plutonium production) and [[Los Alamos National Laboratory|Los Alamos]] (weapons design). The first atomic device was detonated at the Trinity test site in New Mexico on 16 July 1945, and atomic bombs were dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki the following month. Gerb does not think Groves took part in the UFO programs, but his videos treat him as their model. Gerb credits him with "outlining the conceptual blueprint" of the compartmented secrecy that, following [[David Grusch]], he argues was copied onto UFO crash retrieval from 1947, in what Gerb calls the "Manhattan Project 2.0".

## Career

Groves was born in Albany, New York. After a year at the University of Washington and two at MIT, he graduated from West Point in 1918 and was commissioned into the Army Corps of Engineers. He oversaw the building of the Pentagon before taking over the Manhattan Project. After the war he stayed in charge of the project until responsibility for U.S. nuclear weapons passed to the newly established [[Atomic Energy Commission]] (AEC) on 1 January 1947. On 28 February 1947 he became the first chief of the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] (AFSWP), which took over the Manhattan Project's military functions: the custody, assembly and delivery of nuclear weapons for the armed forces. Promoted to lieutenant general in January 1948, he retired on 29 February 1948 and became a vice president of Remington Rand, later [[Sperry Rand]], where he ran its research work until 1961.

## Manhattan Project Security Architecture

To anyone researching UAP legacy programs, the security system Groves built for the Manhattan Project is as important as the way he ran its operations. He introduced a tiered system of compartmentation, which gave people access only to the specific information their task required, whatever their clearance level; Gerb sees in it the forerunner of today's sensitive compartmented information and special access programs. He ran the whole program through the deliberately mundane Manhattan Engineer District (MED), an Army Corps of Engineers "district" whose name had nothing to do with nuclear weapons and which handled acquisition, contracting, construction and personnel security. Inside that shell he had near-total autonomy and reported through an extremely short chain, directly to the Secretary of War and Army Chief of Staff [[George C. Marshall]], which let him skirt standard acquisition channels. His informal practice of treating nuclear information as "born secret" was written into the Atomic Energy Act of 1946 as [[Restricted Data|restricted data]], the only U.S. classification that applies automatically by subject matter.

[[David Grusch]] says the early UFO programs "took the Manhattan Project secrecy and overlaid it on this issue", and Gerb builds on that in *The Manhattan Project 2.0*. He sees Groves's design repeated in the compartmentation of the legacy programs, in their "short, stripped, yet extremely concise chain of command", and in the Air Force's modern [[Hidden Wing]], whose [[Rapid Capabilities Office (RCO)|Rapid Capabilities Office]] works in the gray area of acquisition much as Groves used the MED. Gerb names [[Vannevar Bush]] and Marshall as the two who "constructed the very skeleton" of the Manhattan Project 2.0, with "a huge shout-out to General Leslie Groves for outlining the conceptual blueprint".

The compartments caused friction. [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] complained, often through Bush, that Groves's need-to-know rules hindered scientific progress. Gerb speculates, calling it "total speculation", that if Oppenheimer later joined the UFO effort he may have objected to its secrecy in the same way.

Groves also commanded the [[Alsos Mission|Alsos Missions]], proposed by Marshall in 1943 because Manhattan Project secrecy kept military intelligence from being briefed on atomic energy. Overseen by Groves, Marshall, Marshall's G-2 Colonel Boris Pash and Bush, Alsos ran in Italy, France and Germany, and its security arm, the T-Forces, seized German scientists, uranium and research documents, some of them in Soviet-controlled territory. Gerb identifies Alsos and the T-Forces as the direct historical blueprint for today's rapid-reaction teams that allegedly retrieve crashed UFOs. He adds that Groves disliked the name, since "Alsos" is Greek for "groves".

## Alleged Connection to the 1947 Roswell Crash

Groves's name reaches the [[Roswell Crash]] through two disputed documents. An alleged [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] report dated 22 July 1947 says that Colonel [[Sherman V. Hasbrouck]] of AFSWP, the body Groves then headed, ordered a radiobiological team, a Special Engineer Detachment and a Sandia security detail to secure the crash site. Gerb confirms that Hasbrouck was a real colonel in the postwar nuclear weapons programs who worked closely with Groves and helped build AFSWP. Researcher [[Ryan S. Wood]] says the alleged White Hot Report, the product of the team that worked on Roswell through the summer of 1947, lists Groves on its mission roster alongside Oppenheimer, Theodore von Kármán and others. Wood also identifies the IPU report's second autopsist, Dr. Charles Ethan Ray, as a medical corps expert who worked with Groves on the Manhattan Project.

Gerb himself does not believe Groves carried on into the Manhattan Project 2.0: "Leslie Groves, I don't believe so. I think he was forced to retire shortly after the Manhattan Project."
