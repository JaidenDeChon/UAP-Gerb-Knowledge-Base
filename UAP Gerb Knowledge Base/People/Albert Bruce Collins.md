Albert Bruce Collins (died December 30, 1990), known in the literature by the pseudonym "Barnabas," was an American who claimed to be a retired metallurgical engineer and government employee. He said that from 1942 through the late 1950s he developed metal alloys for electromagnetic propagation and magnetic field propulsion in an official capacity. UFO researcher [[Tim Cooper]] interviewed him on November 20, 1990, weeks before he died, and [[Leonard Stringfield]] published the account, much of it gathered by Cooper, in his 1991 crash retrieval Status Report VI, *The Inner Sanctum*. Gerb draws on Collins in three videos: for his 1947 sighting of an [[Egg-shaped UAP|egg-shaped craft]], for his claims about agencies studying recovered technology, and for what he said about the origins of the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]].

## Claimed Observations

Collins told Cooper that in 1947 he watched a UFO on a long flatbed trailer under a green tarp being backed into a large warehouse at the [[University of California, Berkeley]]. Gerb is reminded of the Army lowboy trucks described in the 1953 Kingman and 1965 Kecksburg retrieval cases. From about 100 feet away, Collins saw an oval craft "like an egg with the shell cracked and the yolk still inside." It was metallic, with a silvery, polished-aluminum finish and a seam running all the way around its edge. Collins guessed that it had suffered an internal explosion, since the metal exterior seemed pushed out. The craft was badly damaged: a large section had been ripped off, and a huge gash on top ran down the side below the seam. Collins saw a bulkhead in front with a multi-layered honeycomb skin and, at the center of the dark interior, a shiny sphere surrounded by another bulkhead. A forward compartment was closed to his view. The craft measured 30–40 feet in diameter and about 15 feet high.

Gerb says the multi-layered honeycomb skin is the detail that lends Collins some credibility. In his NRO video he sets it beside later accounts of egg-shaped craft with layers inside layers, and in his Peru video beside [[Jonathan Weygandt]]'s 1997 egg, which also had a seam around its edge and was somewhat larger.

## Alleged Knowledge of UAP Programs

Collins claimed that around 1949 he studied metal of unknown composition and origin in an official capacity, and in doing so learned of multiple agencies involved in [[Technologies of Unknown Origin (TUO)]] research. These included the intelligence offices of the US Army, Air Force, and Navy, the [[CIA]], the [[RAND Corporation]] (founded the year before), and the [[Vatican]]. He named several alleged joint projects:

- Project Archangel, between the CIA, RAND, and the Vatican
- Project Black Book, under the Air Force
- Project Blue Book, under the Air Force
- Project White Book, between the CIA and the Vatican
- Project Yellow Book, a scientific panel of an unknown government scientific review board

Collins also said he had heard that [[Los Alamos National Laboratory|Los Alamos National Labs]] had been studying green fireballs and strange debris found in the desert. Gerb notes that the claim matches [[Project Twinkle]], a real Atomic Energy Commission investigation of green fireballs at Los Alamos, Sandia, White Sands, and Holloman Air Force Base in the late 1940s and early 1950s. Gerb mentions that Collins also claimed to know that Project Archangel had determined some UFO occupants were biological extraterrestrials, and says he may return to these claims in future.

## The NRO and the "Black Sky" Threat

Collins asserted that the NRO collected imagery and other intelligence on "orbiting spacecraft of unknown origin" and had been founded for that purpose. In his words:

> "The Air Force wanted to counter the black sky UFO threat by the formation of the high frontier programs for space-based weapon systems in the early 1960s. Talon Gold and Teal Ruby projects were a direct result of satellite recon photos of orbiting spacecraft of unknown origins. This led to the establishment of the NRO in 1960."

Gerb finds the statement intriguing because the NRO's existence was not declassified until 1992, but cautions that newspapers had leaked the office's existence in the early 1970s, so Collins may have learned of it that way. He notes that Talon Gold and Teal Ruby were real experimental programs, led mainly by DARPA and the Air Force from the late 1970s and folded into Reagan's [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)|Strategic Defense Initiative]], and he ties Collins's claim to his own view that SDI back-channelled money to legacy programs and hid anti-UFO capabilities. In his Northrop Grumman and TRW video Gerb returns to Talon Gold, the main SDI responsibility of [[Donald C. Winter]] at DARPA, recalling that Collins described it and Teal Ruby as programs to track and possibly down UFOs.

## Verification

Gerb says that all he can confirm from Collins's obituary is that he died on 30 December 1990 and was a veteran and a member of the NRA and a nature conservancy. His claimed career as a metallurgical engineer on classified programs has not been independently verified. Gerb finds the joint UAP programs with the Vatican that Collins described noteworthy given the [[1933 Magenta UFO CrashRetrieval|1933 Magenta, Italy UFO crash]], in which, according to [[David Grusch]] and the late Harald Malmgren, Vatican intelligence alerted the United States to a UAP in Axis possession.
