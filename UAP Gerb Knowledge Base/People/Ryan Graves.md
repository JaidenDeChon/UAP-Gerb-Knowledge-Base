Ryan Graves is a former U.S. Navy F/A-18 Super Hornet pilot and the founder of Americans for Safe Aerospace, an organization that collects UAP reports from military and commercial aviators and advocates on their behalf. Graves became one of the most prominent pilot witnesses in the modern UAP disclosure era after publicizing his and fellow Navy pilots' encounters with unidentified aerial objects off the U.S. East Coast beginning around 2014–2015. He testified before the House Oversight Committee in July 2023, alongside [[David Grusch]] and [[David Fravor]], in the most significant public congressional UAP hearing in decades.

## UAP Encounters

Graves has described repeated encounters with objects characterized as a "dark gray or black cube within a clear sphere", a UAP shape that multiple Navy pilots operating in the same airspace reported consistently. The objects reportedly appeared stationary in high winds, moved seamlessly from hovering to hypersonic speeds, and showed no visible propulsion. The encounters recurred persistently over several years.

[[Sean Kirkpatrick]], as AARO director, publicly dismissed reports of this type as likely Chinese "ball drones". UAP researchers, Gerb among them, criticized that characterization as a modern equivalent of the [[Robertson Panel]]'s weather balloon dismissals. Graves himself has noted that if Chinese drones were violating U.S. military airspace that often and with such impunity, the failure to respond would itself be an extraordinary national security lapse.

## Americans for Safe Aerospace

Graves founded Americans for Safe Aerospace as a formal channel through which military and civilian pilots can report UAP encounters without repercussions for their careers. The organization collects testimony from aviators who had not disclosed their encounters before because of stigma or institutional pressure. It also advocates for aviation safety policies that treat UAP as a genuine flight safety concern, whatever their origin.

## Congressional Testimony

In his July 2023 House Oversight Committee testimony, Graves stated that UAP encounters in military airspace are neither rare nor isolated, and that pilots routinely do not report them because of professional and cultural stigma. He framed the UAP phenomenon as primarily a flight safety issue that requires systematic, unclassified investigation.
