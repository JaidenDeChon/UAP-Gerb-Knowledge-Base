Steven Harvey Schiff (March 18, 1947 – March 25, 1998) was an American lawyer and Republican politician who served as US Representative for New Mexico's 1st congressional district from 1989 until his death in 1998. Before entering Congress, he served as Bernalillo County District Attorney and as Attorney General of New Mexico. He is central to UFO crash-retrieval research as the congressman who prompted the first formal US government records review of the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]].

## The Roswell GAO Investigation

Schiff's inquiry about the "Roswell Incident" was referred by the Department of Defense to the files of the defunct Project Blue Book, which the National Archives confirmed did not cover Roswell. Feeling that he had been stonewalled, Schiff requested an audit by the General Accounting Office (GAO). Gerb's research credits early-1990s disclosures by fellow New Mexico researcher [[Andrew Kissner]] with prompting Schiff's request.

GAO did the work from March 1994 to June 1995, and its report, the [[GAO Roswell Report (1995)]], is dated 28 July 1995. It found no government record of a crashed flying saucer near Roswell in mid-1947; the only 1947 records it turned up were the 509th Bomb Group's July history and an FBI teletype, both describing a radar-tracking balloon. It also found that Roswell Army Air Field's outgoing messages from October 1946 through December 1949, a span that covers July 1947, had been destroyed, and that the disposition form did not say who destroyed them, when, or under what authority. Schiff's own statement on the report went further: he said the messages "were destroyed without proper authority" and "were permanent records, which never should have been destroyed." The Defense Department told GAO that the Air Force's July 1994 *Report of Air Force Research Regarding the Roswell Incident*, which pointed to a Project Mogul balloon, was the extent of its records. The Air Force followed it in 1997 with *The Roswell Report: Case Closed*, which Gerb says was managed by SAF/AAZ, the Air Force's component-level special access program central office.

> [!edit] Corrected 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions said the search took 18 months, that the GAO found the messages destroyed "without proper authorization" and noted they were permanent records that should never have been destroyed, and that *Case Closed* was the Air Force's response to the GAO inquiry. The report (GAO/NSIAD-95-187) gives the review as March 1994 to June 1995 and says only that the disposition form recorded no one, no date and no authority; the other two points are Schiff's, from his statement on the report. The Air Force report GAO was pointed to is the July 1994 one; *Case Closed* followed in 1997.
