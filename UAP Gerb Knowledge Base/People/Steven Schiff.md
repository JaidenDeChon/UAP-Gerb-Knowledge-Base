Steven Harvey Schiff (March 18, 1947 – March 25, 1998) was an American lawyer and Republican politician who served as US Representative for New Mexico's 1st congressional district from 1989 until his death in 1998. Before entering Congress, he served as Bernalillo County District Attorney and as Attorney General of New Mexico. He is central to UFO crash-retrieval research as the congressman who triggered the first formal US government records review of the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]].

## The Roswell GAO Investigation

After the Department of Defense referred his inquiry about the "Roswell Incident" to the defunct Project Blue Book files — records that the National Archives confirmed did not actually cover Roswell — Schiff, perceiving he had been stonewalled, requested a General Accounting Office (GAO) audit. UAP Gerb's research credits fellow New Mexico researcher [[Andrew Kissner]]'s early-1990s disclosures with prompting Schiff's request.

The resulting 18-month GAO records search, completed and released on 28 July 1995, could not locate documentary evidence that the US government recovered a crashed flying saucer near Roswell in mid-1947. Critically, the report found that outgoing message traffic from Roswell Army Airfield covering the relevant period in July 1947 had been destroyed without proper authorization; the GAO could not determine who destroyed the messages or why, noting only that they were permanent records which should never have been destroyed. The Air Force's own response to the GAO inquiry, released as "The Roswell Report: Case Closed," was managed by SAF/AAZ, the Air Force's component-level special access program central office.
