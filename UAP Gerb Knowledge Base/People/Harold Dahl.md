Harold Dahl is the central witness in the [[Maury Island Incident]] of June 27, 1947, which is widely cited as the origin of the [[Men in Black]] archetype in modern UFO lore. While doing conservation work on Puget Sound near the eastern shore of [[Maury Island, Washington]], Dahl reported seeing six donut-shaped craft hovering approximately half a mile above his boat. Debris fell from one of the craft, killing his dog and injuring his son. Dahl photographed and filmed the objects.

## The Encounter

The six craft were described as donut-shaped, with dark outer surfaces like a doughnut mold. They hovered in formation until one appeared to emit or jettison metallic-looking debris onto Dahl's boat. The debris reportedly included two types of material: a lightweight whitish foam-like substance and heavier dark metallic fragments. Dahl photographed the incident and reported it to his superior, Fred Crisman.

## The Man in Black Visit

The following morning, June 28, 1947, a man in a black suit whom Dahl did not know approached him and suggested they have breakfast at a nearby diner. At the diner, the man described Dahl's encounter of the previous day with extraordinary accuracy, although Dahl had not made it public. The man then told Dahl:

> "What I have said to you is proof to you that I know a great deal more about this experience of yours than you will want to believe."

The man warned Dahl that if he spoke of the incident, bad things would happen to himself and his family. This exchange is regarded as the prototype of the Men in Black phenomenon: suited strangers with inexplicable foreknowledge who intimidate UFO witnesses into silence.

## Credibility and Disputes

The Maury Island Incident is among the most hotly contested cases in UFO history. Shortly afterwards, two Army Air Forces investigators flew to Seattle to retrieve the debris samples, and both were killed when their B-25 crashed on the return flight. Some researchers have treated this as suspicious; others point out that the B-25 was already having mechanical problems. Critics have questioned elements of Dahl's account, and some have characterized the incident as a hoax. Even so, the incident entered UFO history as the founding narrative of the Men in Black phenomenon.
