William J. "Bill" Perry was U.S. Secretary of Defense from 1994 to 1997 under President Bill Clinton. According to the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], Perry personally organized the special project records group that contained the crash retrieval program in 1994, and he advised Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] to examine those records during Wilson's 1997 investigation.

## The 1993 "Last Supper"

In July 1993, while he was Deputy Secretary of Defense under Secretary of Defense Les Aspin, Perry told the leaders of the United States' prime defense contractors that post-Cold War cuts in defense spending would leave only a handful of contractors in each defense sector after the coming consolidation. The defense industry came to call this meeting the "Last Supper." Between 1993 and 1997, a wave of mergers and acquisitions cut the number of major US defense contractors from 51 to five, the "Big Five": [[Lockheed Martin]], Raytheon, [[Northrop Grumman]], General Dynamics and Boeing. The deals included Lockheed's 1995 merger with Martin Marietta, Boeing's 1996–1997 acquisitions of Rockwell and McDonnell Douglas, and Northrop Grumman's acquisition of Ryan Aeronautical. Gerb identifies several contractors allegedly involved in UFO legacy programs that were absorbed into the Big Five during this consolidation.

## Role in the 1997 Wilson Investigation

In April 1997, Vice Admiral Thomas Wilson, then Deputy Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency, launched his investigation into UFO crash retrieval programs. General [[Marshal Ward]] and Secretary of Defense Bill Perry both advised him to go through records group files in [[OUSD(AT) (Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology)|OUSD(AT)]], the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology.

According to the Wilson-Davis memo, Ward and Perry both told Wilson of a "special project records group not belonging to usual special access programs". This group was a special subset of the unacknowledged carve-outs and waived programs that sat outside the usual SAP divisions, and Perry himself had organized it in 1994.

## Significance

Perry's role matters for several reasons:

1. As Secretary of Defense, Perry personally organized the special records group structure in 1994 that housed the crash retrieval program, which suggests high-level awareness and deliberate compartmentalization.

2. Perry's advice to Wilson on where to look suggests that Perry knew Wilson would find the program. It is unclear whether Perry expected Wilson to be denied access.

3. The 1994 reorganization created the framework that let the crash retrieval program exist outside "usual special access programs," possibly as a [[Waived SAP]] with minimal oversight.

4. The 1994 reorganization took place shortly after the end of the Cold War, when defense contractors were consolidating and many black programs were being restructured.

Perry was Secretary of Defense from 1994-1997, the period when the crash retrieval program's protection mechanisms were formalized. The program itself predated his tenure, judging by the [[Bigot List]] dates (1990-1993) shown to Wilson.

## SAPOC Reorganization and the Cementing of the Great Schism

Gerb's investigation into the Manhattan Project 2.0 treats Perry's 1994 actions as the decisive event that made the break-up of the centralized UFO legacy program structure permanent. As Deputy Secretary of Defense, before he became Secretary, Perry reorganized the Special Access Program Oversight Committee (SAPOC). He greatly expanded the SAP Central Office (SAPCO) and set up the Senior Review Group (SRG). In effect, the reorganization handed legacy program oversight to a quasi-government and industry control panel of slightly more than two dozen individuals.

In Gerb's analysis, 1994 was "the last year that legacy programs had any semblance of centralized leadership." After Perry's SAPOC reorganization, the UFO legacy program portfolio was "fully scattered into the wind." The Wilson-Davis notes say the gatekeeping structure was "formed out of necessity to protect themselves after a near disaster in the past", and they mention a specific audit investigation that "nearly outed" the program. These passages are interpreted as referring to the cascade of events in the early 1980s (Yellow Fruit, mid-decade audits) that made the 1994 Perry reorganization both necessary and final.

Perry appears to have played two roles. He organized the 1994 oversight structure, and in 1997 he advised Wilson on where to look for crash retrieval records within that same structure. This raises the question of how far Perry understood that he was directing Wilson toward a program whose gatekeepers would deny Wilson access.
