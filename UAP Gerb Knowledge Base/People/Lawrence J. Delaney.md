Lawrence J. Delaney (born 1935) is an American chemical engineer and defense executive who served as Assistant Secretary of the Air Force (Acquisition), the head of [[SAFAQ|SAF/AQ]], from 1999 to 2001, and briefly as Acting Secretary of the Air Force in 2001. He holds a Ph.D. in chemical engineering from the University of Pennsylvania (1961). After government service he became president and CEO of Arete Associates.

## Career before SAF/AQ

Delaney worked at the Institute for Defense Analyses, an FFRDC, in 1961-1962. From 1963 he was at the [[Aerospace Corporation]], where he directed its propulsion technology and systems study group, and from 1968 to 1975 he worked at [[Martin Marietta]], where the video says he was director of advanced programs and of its Navy systems division. UAP Gerb's account adds a period at [[SAIC]] as deputy manager of its military sciences and information systems sector, and later manager of its information systems group, followed by work for [[BDM International|Braddock Dunn & McDonald]] (BDM), later bought by [[Northrop Grumman]].

## Role in the Hidden Wing thesis

In *The Hidden Wing*, UAP Gerb offers Delaney as the first example of a pattern he finds among Air Force acquisition chiefs. In eight of the 14 former heads of SAF/AQ he examined, he finds prior corporate careers at contractors he has accused of legacy-program work, including [[Lockheed Martin]], SAIC, Northrop Grumman, TRW, LTV, E-Systems, the Aerospace Corporation and General Dynamics. He compares Delaney's route, from FFRDCs to contractors to government, with gatekeepers such as [[Mary K. Sturdivant]] and [[Terry Phillips]], and argues Delaney had exposure to UFO programs before taking office. The video dates his SAF/AQ tenure to 1991-1992. Published biographies place it in 1999-2001.
