Almiro Baraúna was a Brazilian professional photographer, a specialist in underwater photography, who took the [[1958 Trindade Island UFO Photographs]] from the deck of the Brazilian Navy ship *Almirante Saldanha* on 16 January 1958. His sequence of a Saturn-like disc passing over Trindade Island's peaks is among the most reproduced UFO photographs in history, and is one of the seven images physicist [[Kevin Knuth]] chose to open his 2023 [[Sol Foundation 2023 Presentation by Kevin Knuth|Sol Foundation talk]] on UAP physics. The transcript of UAP Gerb's video on that talk renders his name as "Aliro Barana".

## The Trindade Island photographs

Baraúna was aboard the *Almirante Saldanha*, then on an oceanographic mission to the island about 600 miles off the Brazilian mainland, when crew and passengers on deck called out an object approaching the island. According to the account Gerb summarizes, Baraúna and many of the other 48 people on deck, including Lieutenant Homero, watched a gleaming object fly behind Desejado Peak, reverse direction seconds later and come back closer, lower and noticeably faster. Baraúna took a sequence of photographs (Gerb speaks of three) showing a gray, metallic, solid-looking disc wrapped in a greenish haze, with a ring running through its midsection, which witnesses likened to a flattened planet Saturn.

Brazilian president Juscelino Kubitschek released the photographs publicly in February 1958.

## In Kevin Knuth's talk

Knuth included the Trindade sequence in a slide of seven UFO images used to present UAP as a class of well-observed phenomena. Gerb compares the disc's sharp edges to the Calvine photograph.
