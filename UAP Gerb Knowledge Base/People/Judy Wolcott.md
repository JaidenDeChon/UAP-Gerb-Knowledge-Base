Judy Wolcott was a woman who approached UFO researcher [[Don Schmidt]] claiming that her husband, a Vietnam-era military officer, had written to her to say he had been present at the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval]], where non-human biologics were allegedly recovered. She also claimed he had been killed in action during the Vietnam War.

Author [[Nick Redfern]] later investigated Wolcott's account and exposed it as fabricated. Her husband had not died in Vietnam, multiple details of her story did not hold up, and her own daughter confirmed that Wolcott often invented stories. It is unknown whether Wolcott made the story up for her own reasons, such as wanting attention or recognition, or whether it was deliberate disinformation fed into the Kingman research community.

Wolcott's case is cited in discussions of the Kingman evidence as an example of the false witness problem in UAP crash retrieval research, where fabricated testimony that enters a case can cloud the evidence around otherwise credible witnesses such as [[Arthur Stansel Jr.]] and [[Bill Uhouse]].
