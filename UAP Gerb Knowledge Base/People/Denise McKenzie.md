Denise McKenzie is a former administrative employee of [[SAIC]] (Science Applications International Corporation) who testified at [[Steven Greer]]'s 2001 Disclosure Project briefing in Washington, D.C. She alleged that black budget money for classified programs, possibly including work on reverse engineering UAP, is systematically hidden inside legitimate SAIC government contracts, and that she was harassed at the company after pointing out the discrepancies. McKenzie has reportedly not been heard from in public since her 2001 testimony.

## Background

McKenzie was working at a fabric store in a San Diego mall in 1992 when a part-time co-worker named Sophia, who mainly worked at SAIC's La Jolla campus, asked her to join the company. According to Gerb's reading of her full testimony, McKenzie had never heard of SAIC. She was hired on the spot as an administrative assistant, with no paperwork or interview and without knowing which department she would work in. Sophia then did not appear at work for another two to three weeks. With no real tasks at first, McKenzie wandered the building and worked out that she was in SAIC's military contract division; she also noticed a highly classified biological division with strict access control.

## Contracts with no activity

McKenzie's duties picked up when letters arrived from the U.S. military and defense contractors asking for project report updates. Her superiors told her to answer with a generic "we're working on it". When she pulled the relevant files and disks, she found that every contract held only two or three form letters saying "it's being worked on" and no other activity at all. Some of the contracts were recent, and some were several years old and worth millions of dollars. Some of the letters were signed by senior SAIC personnel and some were unsigned. She reported this to Sophia, who reportedly worked under SAIC's head scientist, and Sophia grew furious. McKenzie was ordered to hand over all the files and disks and forget the matter.

In the short time she remained, McKenzie was harassed by her head supervisor, a man named Stuart Stanley. She left after choking on food at work while her immediate supervisor watched without helping or calling an ambulance. Searching the early internet afterwards, she found Sophia's photograph, with a different first name and the same surname, on a CIA web page listing deceased agents. This "Sophia" had supposedly died in 1987 or 1988, years before McKenzie met her.

In her own words, SAIC seemed to be "getting a lot of money for doing nothing", and hiding funds that way was "like Joe's Market on a huge scale".

## 2001 Disclosure Project Testimony

The 2001 Disclosure Project briefing, hosted by Steven Greer, brought together dozens of military, intelligence and government witnesses who testified that UAP programs are real. At the briefing McKenzie stated the following:

- Of the government contracts behind hundreds of millions of dollars awarded to SAIC, most showed almost no activity for what they were worth.
- She concluded that black budget money is hidden inside legitimate SAIC programs and contracts to fund classified projects without congressional oversight or public accountability.
- After she raised these discrepancies inside SAIC, she was harassed within the company.

This testimony established SAIC as a main channel for hiding UAP-related or black program funding inside ordinary defense budgets. The claim was later reinforced in two ways. Commander Will Miller named SAIC as one of the "keepers of the secrets" in connection with the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], and several SAIC contract announcements for [[Naval Surface Warfare Center Crane]] were removed from the company's website around 2024.

## Rediscovered tape

In his 2025 investigation of SAIC, Gerb reports that he found McKenzie's complete March 2001 videotaped interview in the Disclosure Project archive, and that he has tried to contact both McKenzie and Stuart Stanley but has had no reply. In a clip the video plays, [[Steven Greer]] retells her account as proof that money was put into "fake" programs so that audits showed spending on "more helicopters". Gerb sets her story beside a 1992 sworn deposition, reported by *Vanity Fair* in 2007, in which a former SAIC executive said "mischarging" had become institutionalized at the company, and beside Aldric Saucier's SDI whistleblower case the same year.

## Significance

McKenzie's testimony is one of the most direct insider accounts of how the money for classified programs is hidden in dealings with defense contractors. UAP researchers cite it to support the claim that programs such as the alleged [[Off-World Technologies Division]] at Naval Surface Warfare Center (NSWC) Crane could be funded through SAIC contracts without leaving a public paper trail.
