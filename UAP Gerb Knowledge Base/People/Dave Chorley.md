Dave Chorley (1935–1996) was a British artist and the co-creator, with [[Doug Bower]], of the crop circle phenomenon that drew worldwide attention from the 1970s to the early 1990s. The two men spent approximately fifteen years making crop formations across southern England before publicly confessing in 1991. Chorley died of cancer in 1996.

## Involvement

Chorley and Bower began creating crop formations around 1976, working at night with simple tools: a plank of wood, lengths of rope, and a wire sight fitted to a baseball cap for navigating straight lines. They made hundreds of formations, starting with simple circles and moving on to more and more complex geometric designs as public interest and media scrutiny grew.

In September 1991, the two men demonstrated their technique to journalists and showed the ordinary tools behind formations that had prompted serious academic discussion, paranormal tourism and international media coverage. The confession took away much of the credibility of crop circles, but it did not end the wider subculture, because by 1991 independent hoaxers around the world had taken up the practice.

## Legacy

Chorley did not live to see the crop circle phenomenon fully resolve into the art-form subculture it became. His work with Bower established that two people with basic tools could make elaborate, geometrically precise formations overnight. That undermined the extraterrestrial hypothesis behind much of the public interest.
