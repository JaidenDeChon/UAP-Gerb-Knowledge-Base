Ner Liddell was a physicist who served with the Office of Naval Research (ONR) of the United States Navy. In 1951, Liddell published a statement in a public magazine article in which he categorically dismissed all unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) reports as misidentifications of natural phenomena or conventional objects — mirages or balloons — and asserted that trained observers were being fooled. This public dismissal, delivered in an official capacity by an ONR scientist, is cited in UAP research as an early institutional example of the US Navy's pattern of public UAP denial, a pattern that persisted until Admiral Timothy Galedet's more forthcoming congressional testimony in the 2020s.

The significance of Liddell's 1951 statement lies in its institutional context: the ONR is the same organization later alleged by journalist Christopher Sharp's sources to be the primary recipient of non-human craft recovered from undersea retrieval operations before those craft are transferred to defense contractors. UAP Gerb presents Liddell's public statement as part of a documented institutional posture of denial that contradicts private admissions by senior ONR-adjacent officials — including Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]]'s 1989 disclosure to [[Bob Echler]] that the US government possessed recovered non-human craft.
