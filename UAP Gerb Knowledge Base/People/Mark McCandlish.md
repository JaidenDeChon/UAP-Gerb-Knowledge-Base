Mark McCandlish was an American aerospace illustrator and concept designer who produced the most widely recognized technical depictions of the [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)]] Flux Liner, based on the firsthand testimony of [[Brad Sorenson]]. McCandlish's detailed cutaway drawings of the disc-shaped ARV — including its concentric capacitor plate structure, central column, crew compartment, and red trackball control sphere — have become iconic reference materials in UAP research.

## Flux Liner Illustrations

McCandlish translated Sorenson's verbal descriptions of the craft he witnessed at the 1988 [[Norton Air Force Base]] exhibit into precise technical illustrations. His cutaway drawing depicts a smooth, seamless disc with a crew compartment above a ring of concentric capacitor plates, powered by an unknown energy source. The pilot controls the craft via a red trackball sphere mounted on the armrest — a control mechanism that was independently described in [[Tom DeLonge]] and AJ Hartley's *Secret Machines* for the Astra [[TR-3B]] "Locust," allegedly based on real information from military advisors.

McCandlish's illustrations also depict off-the-shelf human components integrated into the craft, such as F-4 Phantom ejection seats, suggesting the ARV was a human attempt to reproduce an advanced propulsion concept within existing engineering capabilities.
