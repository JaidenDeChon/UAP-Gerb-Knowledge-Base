Robert M. Powell is a UAP researcher and co-author of the 2019 peer-reviewed paper "Estimating Flight Characteristics of Anomalous Unidentified Aerial Vehicles," published in *Entropy* alongside [[Kevin Knuth]] and [[Peter Reali]]. The paper is one of the most rigorous academic analyses of UAP performance characteristics using multisensor data from well-documented historical cases.

## Academic Contribution

Powell, Knuth and Reali used physics-based methods to analyze a handful of well-documented UAP cases, among them:

- the 1951 Bethune encounter, a US Navy flight crew's sighting over the North Atlantic in February 1951
- the [[1986 Japan Airlines UFO Incident]], a 31-minute tracked encounter with radar confirmation
- the [[2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter (Tic Tac)]], a military encounter recorded on multiple sensors, with extreme performance characteristics

The paper calculated g-forces, velocities, power requirements and other physical parameters from conservative estimates and established physics. For the Nimitz Tic Tac, the team estimated that the craft underwent approximately 5,400 Gs of acceleration, requiring roughly 1,100 gigawatts of power, ten times the total nuclear output of the United States.

## Methodology

Powell and his colleagues worked in ways that set their study apart from speculative UAP analysis:

1. Conservative assumptions. When a craft's mass was unknown, they used deliberately low estimates, so their results are lower bounds on performance.
2. Multisensor corroboration. They chose cases that had several independent sources of confirmation (visual, radar, infrared).
3. Peer review. The paper went through formal academic peer review before it was published in a scientific journal.
4. Physics-based calculations. Every estimate comes from established physics equations, not speculation.

Because of this approach, their performance calculations represent minimum capabilities rather than exaggerated claims.

## Significance

Powell's collaboration with Knuth is a rare case of UAP data receiving serious academic treatment. Published in *Entropy*, a peer-reviewed journal, the research brings scientific credibility to UAP analysis. It also shows that credentialed researchers can apply rigorous methods to the phenomenon without compromising professional standards.
