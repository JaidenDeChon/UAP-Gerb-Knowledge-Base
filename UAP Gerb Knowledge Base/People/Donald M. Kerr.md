Donald M. Kerr (1939–2025) was a US physicist who held senior posts at a national laboratory, in intelligence and in the defense industry. His career ran through an unusual number of the institutions Gerb associates with the UFO legacy programs: he directed Los Alamos and the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|National Reconnaissance Office]], ran the CIA's science and technology directorate, served as Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence, and later chaired the [[MITRE Corporation]]. Gerb calls him the equivalent of "MJ-1" in Majestic 12 terms and names him among the gatekeepers Congress should have questioned.

## Career

Kerr earned all three of his degrees at Cornell University, finishing with a PhD in plasma physics and microwave electronics in 1966. He was director of [[Los Alamos National Laboratory]] from 1979 to 1985, president of [[EG&G]] from 1989 to 1992, and corporate executive vice president and a director of [[SAIC]] from 1993 to 1996. From 1997 to 2001 he ran the FBI's Laboratory Division. In August 2001 he became the CIA's Deputy Director for Science and Technology, heading the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]] until 2005. He then directed the NRO (July 2005 to October 2007) and served as Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence under Mike McConnell (October 2007 to January 2009). He joined the MITRE board of trustees in 2009, became vice chairman in 2014 and chairman in 2018, and handed the chair to Mike Rogers in November 2021. He had a stroke late in 2023 and died in Denver on 13 July 2025, aged 86.

## Alleged Legacy Program Role

Gerb describes Kerr as "one of the legacy program leads for a great many years", the equivalent of "MJ1 if you use Majestic 12 terminology". He also says Kerr "did help in transparency for this topic before the end of his life". The channel's SAIC investigation cites his career as an example of one person combining contractor leadership with intelligence science portfolios. Later videos use him as a point of comparison. The [[Videos/National Reconnaissance Office - UFO Crash Retrievals, Surveillance, and Legacy Program Gatekeepers/summary|NRO investigation]] counts him among thirteen officials with NRO ties whom Gerb considers likely legacy-program members or gatekeepers, and calls his résumé "exactly the type of CV a highlevel legacy program member would possess". The Lockheed Martin video sets [[Mary K. Sturtevant]] beside him as another product of the CIA's science and technology directorate, the Northrop Grumman and TRW video says only [[Edward C. Aldridge]] and [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]] have CVs to rival his, and the Sandia video lists him among MITRE's figures.

## SAIC and the gatekeeper case

The SAIC investigation says Kerr joined [[SAIC]] in 1993 as a corporate executive vice president and director, between his EG&G presidency and his CIA appointment. Gerb notes that the [[CIA Office of Global Access]], which he accuses of carrying out foreign UFO crash retrievals with JSOC, was established within the CIA Directorate of Science and Technology while Kerr was its deputy director. He also cites the notes of [[Oak Shannon|Oke Shannon]] from the 1985 Advanced Theoretical Physics working group at [[BDM International|BDM]]'s facility in McLean, Virginia, which include a plan to approach Donald Kerr for "influence and money", and a separate entry reading "Don K/ET". Gerb names Kerr among the gatekeepers he believes Congress should have questioned, saying his CV (Los Alamos, EG&G, SAIC, CIA science and technology, the NRO and MITRE) "would lend" more than anyone's to legacy program work.

## MITRE Corporation

In the channel's MITRE investigation, Gerb places Kerr first among the former senior officials who took leadership roles at MITRE after government service, giving his tenure as trustee and chairman as 2009–2021 (he chaired the board from 2018). The channel also notes that Kerr served a second stint on the Defense Science Board (from 2010 into the early 2020s), where it says he would have crossed paths with [[William LaPlante]]. It argues that the pattern of Principal Deputy DNIs with strong intelligence-community and FFRDC ties (Kerr, then [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]] and [[Susan Gordon]]) is consistent with its thesis that MITRE has long been the R&D lead on classified, Navy-administered UFO reverse-engineering and USO-monitoring programs.
