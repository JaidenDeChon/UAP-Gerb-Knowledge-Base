Terry Phillips spent roughly three decades in Air Force and AFOSI service and reached the Senior Executive Service (SES) in 2020. He was Executive Director of the [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations]] (AFOSI) Office of Special Projects (designated AFOSI/PJ) and director of the Air Force's departmentwide Special Access Program (SAP) security enterprise, a unit responsible for the security of highly classified Air Force special access programs. His work combined counterintelligence, SAP security and the special operations elements of Air Force intelligence. He left federal service around the start of 2022 to become senior vice president and chief security officer of [[Leidos]], and a year later, in 2023, became vice president for security at [[Northrop Grumman]]. [[Lee M. Russ]] succeeded him in his AFOSI/PJ and Air Force SAP security roles.

## Role in UAP Programs

According to the video, Phillips is alleged to be the head of a dedicated security infrastructure within [[Northrop Grumman]] that protects the company's alleged UAP retrieval and material exploitation program. *Northrop Grumman & TRW* names three directors of security Gerb believes work under him on this portfolio: senior director John Freestone (at Northrop since 2000), Kathy Andrews, and Vince Devine, director of security and SAP central office for the Space Systems sector, who earlier supported [[Randall G. Walden]] at the Air Force [[Rapid Capabilities Office (RCO)|Rapid Capabilities Office]]. Gerb also places [[Michael Dempsey]], Northrop's vice president for government affairs, among the executives alongside Phillips, and says there are others he is "not quite bold enough to name yet". Gerb calls his theory about Freestone "just that, a theory".

Gerb distinguishes two roles. At the Air Force, he says, Phillips and AFOSI PJ were deputized assets of the government's "blue" legacy architecture, originating with the National Security Council. At Northrop, he alleges, Phillips "is literally in charge of the entire security apparatus for Northrop's breakaway programs", which Gerb believes are self-funded through [[Independent Research and Development (IRAD)]]. The video plays [[Ross Coulthart]] endorsing the naming of Phillips. Gerb says the Northrop figure whom [[Matt Brown]] described on the *Weaponized* podcast on 18 March 2026 as coordinating attacks on whistleblowers is someone under Phillips, not Phillips himself.

Gerb repeats this allegation in *Special Access Required Vol.2*. He says he is aware of "specific instances" in which Phillips, before his 2021 retirement from federal service, acted as "an enforcer for the UFO activity administrative structure, leveraging administrative terrorism and much worse against prospective whistleblowers," and he alleges that Phillips has continued similar work at Northrop Grumman.

## Career as told in *The Hidden Wing*

Gerb's *The Hidden Wing* gives a fuller account of Phillips's career. Phillips began as an Air Force officer and became an OSI special agent around 1997. By 2001 he was a program security officer and special agent for AFOSI/PJ at [[Air Combat Command]] headquarters, protecting special access programs for the combat air forces. By 2006 he was a deputy director in AFOSI, directing counterintelligence and investigations in support of Air Mobility Command. He was AFOSI's director of counterintelligence from 2012 to 2015, and deputy director of PJ by 2016. From December 2016 to December 2021 he was both the Air Force and Space Force Special Access Program Security Director and executive director of PJ. That made him responsible for every Air Force and Space Force SAP and for non-Air Force SAPs assigned to the Air Force. On 6 August 2020 he was selected for Senior Executive Service tier 1, equivalent to a brigadier general and a first for a PJ executive director.

The video plays parts of an episode of the AFOSI podcast *OSI Today* ("My OSI Journey 16") recorded on the eve of his retirement. In it Phillips says his pride in the special projects mission had "grown 25-fold". He describes PJ's philosophy as "the art of the possible": rather than refusing, the security organization works out the mitigations, such as sound attenuation and covered windows for a classified aircraft built in a hangar, that let a program "get to yes".

## Allegations in *The Hidden Wing*

Gerb calls Phillips a "boogeyman" of UFO legacy-program gatekeepers, says he takes a personal risk in naming him, and lists him first among five "hostile witnesses" he urges the administration to subpoena or serve with interrogatories. The others are [[Lee M. Russ]], [[William E. MacLure]], [[Russell E. Wiler]] and [[Randall G. Walden]]. Gerb suspects that the reprisals [[David Grusch]] faced before filing his complaint with the Intelligence Community Inspector General, and those faced by [[Dylan Borland]], came partly or wholly from offices such as the DARPA Security and Intelligence Directorate and AFOSI/PJ. He asks whether PJ worked with FBI personnel under the [[Guardian Angel Program]] or a successor. He proposes asking Phillips whether he took part in reprisals against UFO whistleblowers, including "administrative terrorism", loss of pensions and benefits, and threats to life.

As "the cherry on top", the video cites FOIA records obtained by John Greenwald of [[The Black Vault]] on the DoD Inspector General's UAP evaluation (project D2021-DEVSEN-0116.000), from a response dated 6 February 2025 and an article published 15 July 2025. In them Phillips repeatedly takes part in classified UAP discussions alongside former AFOSI commander Brigadier General Terry Bullard. At a 4 March 2021 meeting that Bullard could not attend, the deputy inspector general shared redacted information Phillips had supplied as a subject-matter expert. A further release places Phillips, as PJ executive director, among UAP points of contact including USD(A&S), USD(I&S), SAPCO, [[SAFAAZ|SAF/AAZ]], the Army SAP office and the Navy special programs division.

Gerb argues that Phillips's move to [[Leidos]], which spun out of [[SAIC]] in 2013 and merged with Lockheed Martin's IT business in 2016, and then to Northrop Grumman follows the pattern he sees in officials who "trade in their stars and stripes for a suit" at SAIC to continue legacy work on the contractor side. He names [[Bobby Ray Inman]] and [[Lawrence J. Delaney]] as earlier examples. He believes most contractor staff are compartmented and unaware of what they work on, and that corporate SAP security directors such as Phillips are among the few read in.

## MITRE Counterintelligence Support

Gerb's investigation into the [[MITRE Corporation]] says that AFOSI's Office of Special Projects, historically headed by Phillips, provided counterintelligence support for MITRE's alleged UFO technology reverse-engineering and USO-monitoring legacy activities. It adds that the office has permanent locations in [[Crystal City, Virginia]] and at [[Hanscom Air Force Base, Massachusetts|Hanscom Air Force Base]], the base where MITRE opened its first program office.
