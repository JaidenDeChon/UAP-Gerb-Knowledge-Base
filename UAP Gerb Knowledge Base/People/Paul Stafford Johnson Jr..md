Paul Stafford Johnson Jr. (13 September 1933 – 3 August 2014) was a US Navy rear admiral and decorated naval aviator from Dallas, Texas. His obituary records hundreds of carrier landings, service as a Naval Exchange Officer with the US Air Force's 354th Tactical Fighter Wing, and a briefing he gave President John F. Kennedy during the Cuban Missile Crisis. In [[Videos/NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering/summary|NASIC - Air Force UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering]], Gerb identifies him, with what he calls "99 plus percent certainty", as "PJ", the pilot who told [[Leonard Stringfield]] he had walked into a [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] hangar and seen a saucer-shaped craft under guard.

## Life and career

Johnson was born in Chicago to Paul Stafford Johnson Sr. and Inita Mae Busch, graduated from Maine Township High School in Park Ridge, Illinois, and from Iowa State University, and married Leona Meta Riedemann in 1957. He served as a Navy combat pilot and later as a Navy recruiter, and was a published author. He was inurned at Dallas–Fort Worth National Cemetery.

## The "PJ" testimony

Stringfield's [[Stringfield Status Reports|Status Report III]] records a witness he calls PJ, who spoke to him on New Year's Eve 1981. According to Gerb's reading of it, PJ was then a captain in the US Navy Reserve and a naval exchange pilot with 6,100 civilian and 7,500 test-pilot hours, and was flying commercially for Braniff Airlines. PJ said that in April 1962 or 1963, while attached to the 352nd Tactical Fighter Squadron of the 354th Tactical Fighter Wing, his flight was diverted from Myrtle Beach to Wright-Patterson by severe weather. On the first of five days there, he and his crew crossed two baseball fields looking for athletic gear and ran into the first hangar they found. An armed sentry stopped them about 8 feet inside. Beyond him was a saucer-shaped object 12 to 15 feet long and about 8 feet deep, "like a large discus", resting on two engine stands, with no markings, insignia, rivets, welds or joints, roped off and ringed by guards. The crew were ordered out. Back at Myrtle Beach, the wing's brigadier general debriefed PJ and told him he had broken security; asked what he had seen, PJ answered "Nothing."

Gerb's identification rests on matches between Stringfield's notes and Johnson's obituary: both men lived in Dallas; PJ went out of his way to mention Wright-Patterson's handball courts and Johnson was an avid handball player; Johnson was a naval exchange officer with the 354th and, Gerb says, D-flight commander of its 352nd squadron; and, Gerb says, the obituary lists him as a Braniff pilot. On that basis Gerb gives the account considerable weight.

### Hangar 4E

From PJ's description, Gerb places the sighting in the Hangar 4 complex in Area B of Wright-Patterson, and specifically building 4E. Citing *Against the Wind: 90 Years of Flight Test in the Miami Valley*, he notes that Hangar 4 was built in 1944 as Wright Field's aircraft modification facility; that hangars 4A to 4E housed Allied and captured foreign aircraft; that 4C to 4E carried on experimental modification into the 1970s; that 4E was home to the Air Force Test Pilot School before it moved to [[Edwards Air Force Base]]; and that the 4950th Test Wing of the [[Wright Air Development Center (WADC)|Wright Air Development Center]] used the area. If the account is accurate, Gerb concludes, PJ saw a recovered craft in the custody of the [[Air Force Foreign Technology Division|Foreign Technology Division]], and Hangar 4E is his best candidate for one location of the [[Blue Room]].
