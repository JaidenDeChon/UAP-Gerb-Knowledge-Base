Pete Vichi Domenici was a United States Senator from New Mexico from 1973 to 2009. He held senior positions on the Senate Appropriations Committee, Senate Budget Committee, and Senate Energy Committee. Together with fellow New Mexico Senator [[Jeff Bingaman]], Domenici helped force the United States Air Force to acknowledge the existence of [[Project Moon Dust]], the classified UFO crash retrieval program.

## 1992 Moon Dust Inquiry and Air Force Denial

In 1992, Senators Domenici and Bingaman made formal inquiries to the Air Force about Project Moon Dust. The Air Force categorically denied that the program existed, stating "there is no Project Moon Dust. These missions have never existed."

The Senators answered with documents proving that Moon Dust had operated. This forced the Air Force to revise its denial and acknowledge that the program existed and that it had a role in UFO investigation and retrieval.

## 1994 Document Request and Alleged Destruction

In 1994, Senator Domenici asked the Air Force for 11 specific documents about Moon Dust. The Air Force replied that:

- The project no longer existed
- Moon Dust files, including any classified reports that may have existed, had been destroyed

This claim is demonstrably false. Moon Dust documents have since been obtained through Freedom of Information Act requests, which proves the files were not destroyed and suggests the Air Force deliberately deceived a sitting US Senator to withhold material from him.

## Timing and SAP Reorganization

The Air Force's denial and alleged document destruction in the mid-1990s coincided with what Admiral [[Admiral Thomas Wilson|Thomas Wilson]] described in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] as a reorganization of Special Access Programs in the early 1990s. According to Wilson, crash retrieval and reverse engineering programs were reorganized into deeper compartments (SAP-X structures) to make them inaccessible even to senior intelligence officials.

The timing suggests the Air Force may have destroyed or reclassified Moon Dust files as part of this broader compartmentalization effort, using the reorganization as cover to deny Congressional access to UAP legacy program documentation.

## New Mexico Connection

Domenici's persistent interest in Moon Dust fits New Mexico's unique place in UFO history:

- [[Roswell]], New Mexico, site of the famous 1947 crash
- Multiple alleged retrieval sites and underground facilities
- High concentration of sensitive military installations including [[White Sands Missile Range]] and [[Sandia National Laboratories]]

Having represented New Mexico in the Senate for 36 years, Domenici would have been intimately familiar with constituent reports and with the state's history of UFO incidents and alleged government programs.

## Congressional Oversight Failure

Domenici's Moon Dust document requests show the limits of Congressional oversight over compartmentalized UFO programs. Despite his seniority and committee positions, he could not compel the Air Force to produce documents or give a truthful account of the program's status. This shows that UAP legacy programs operate beyond normal legislative branch authority.
