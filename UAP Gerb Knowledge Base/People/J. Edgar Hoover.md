John Edgar Hoover was the first Director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), from the Bureau's reorganization in 1924 until his death in 1972. That tenure of nearly five decades made him the most powerful law enforcement official in American history. In UAP research, Hoover matters as the recipient of the 1950 [[Hottel Memo|Hottel Memo]], in which FBI Special Agent [[Guy Hottel]] relayed claims about three recovered flying saucers and humanoid occupants in New Mexico. His files also intersected with cases related to alleged UAP cover-up principals, including Silas Newton.

## UAP-Relevant Context

On March 22, 1950, FBI Special Agent in Charge [[Guy Hottel]] sent Hoover an internal memorandum describing the alleged recovery in New Mexico of three circular craft about 50 feet in diameter, occupied by three-foot-tall humanoid figures dressed in metallic suits. Hoover was the memo's final recipient, so it was prepared for him to review directly. The memo shows that the most senior US domestic law enforcement official was receiving intelligence about potential UAP recoveries, however secondhand, at the same time that the classified study of flying saucers was allegedly at its most intense within DoD circles.

Hoover's files also contain records on Silas Newton and Leo Gebauer, who were convicted of fraud in connection with claims about the Aztec, New Mexico crash. These files do not establish that Hoover personally believed in or knew of crash recoveries. They do document that the FBI kept active files on people whose claims overlapped with accounts of UAP recoveries.
