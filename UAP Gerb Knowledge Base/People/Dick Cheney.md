Richard Bruce "Dick" Cheney (1941–2025) was the 46th Vice President of the United States, serving under President George W. Bush from 2001 to 2009. Earlier he was Secretary of Defense under President George H. W. Bush from 1989 to 1993 and White House Chief of Staff under President Gerald Ford from 1975 to 1977. In Gerb's videos Cheney is the last person to hold effective centralized authority over the UFO legacy program portfolio. [[David Grusch]] has said that when Cheney left office in 2009, "that was the last time that these activities really had central leadership." Grusch and Gerb both call him "Darth Vader himself". Cheney died in November 2025, and Gerb's later videos call him "the late Dick Cheney".

## Career

Cheney was born in Lincoln, Nebraska. He was White House Chief of Staff under Ford from 1975 to 1977 and represented Wyoming in the US House of Representatives from 1979 to 1989. He became Secretary of Defense on 21 March 1989, succeeding [[Frank Carlucci]], and directed Operation Desert Storm before leaving the post in January 1993. From 1995 to 2000 he was chairman and chief executive of Halliburton, and from 2001 to 2009 he was Vice President. He died on 3 November 2025, aged 84.

## Role in UFO Legacy Programs

Gerb visualizes the UFO legacy programs as a pyramid. At the top sits a control group, once held by presidents and senior officials and last held, he says, by Cheney: "the last man to harness centralized leadership of these activities." Below it sit program administrators in the armed forces and intelligence agencies, then federally funded research and development centers, then prime contractors. In Gerb's account the programs splintered from the early 1980s, and by 1994 oversight had passed to a quasi-government and industry group of just over two dozen people. It took "a Machiavellian statesman like Dick Cheney" to hold that collection of factions even slightly together. Gerb describes him as "the head of the snake" for the programs while Vice President, and in other videos as one of a rotating few who could see across the silos, perhaps because of his earlier service as Secretary of Defense.

Grusch put it in similar terms: "there's not really a mob boss. The closest person we got that I was aware of was unfortunately now deceased Vice President Dick Cheney, Darth Vader himself. Not shocking that he was involved in this. And essentially, when he left in 2009, that was the last time that these activities really had central leadership."

Gerb also points to people Cheney chose. As Secretary of Defense he created a post for [[Richard Haver]], a former deputy director of Naval Intelligence who later ran intelligence programs at TRW and [[Northrop Grumman]], and he picked Haver again in 2001 for the intelligence transition team. His senior military assistant, Admiral [[William A. Owens]], later ran [[SAIC]]. Colonel [[Christopher Haave]], whom Gerb examines as a possible reverse-engineering program official, advised him on national security affairs. In 1992 Cheney also reviewed the case of Strategic Defense Initiative whistleblower Aldric Saucier, which Gerb raises while arguing that UFO programs were hidden inside SDI.

## Post-Cheney Leadership Vacuum

Gerb describes Cheney's departure in January 2009 as "the departure of the last great leader to rule the legacy programs with an iron fist." After Cheney, Gerb identifies [[James Clapper]], Director of National Intelligence from 2010 to 2017, as "the closest thing to a Cheney-esque centralized oversight figure", but says Clapper served as "mob boss in a severely diminished capacity, primarily over the remnants of the National Security Council's UFO Legacy Program portfolio", with "his goons": Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]], Deputy Director of National Intelligence for Intelligence Integration [[Michael Dempsey]], and DNRO [[Betty Sapp]]. If progress on the programs has stagnated, Gerb says, it would be after 2009.

Since Cheney, in Gerb's view, the head of the pyramid has sat effectively empty, with no one holding full control of all the siloed programs. He gives different estimates of how few people now see the whole: "slightly over two dozen alive at any given time" in *The Manhattan Project 2.0*, and "only 40 to 50 people alive at any given time" who know "the full breadth and history of the programs" in *Special Access Required*.
