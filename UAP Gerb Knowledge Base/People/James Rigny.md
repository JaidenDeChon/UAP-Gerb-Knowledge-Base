James Rigny made the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] public in 2018. He obtained the document while helping to disperse the estate of Apollo 14 astronaut [[Edgar Mitchell]] after Mitchell's death in February 2016.

## Relationship with Mitchell Estate

Around 2013, while attending a space conference in the United States, Rigny formed a close relationship with a confidential source. The source had close relationships with many Apollo astronauts, including Edgar Mitchell, the sixth man to walk on the moon.

When Mitchell died unexpectedly in February 2016, his immediate family invited Rigny's source to help disperse the estate. Much of the estate was belongings and documents marked for destruction. While sorting it, the source contacted Rigny about documents among Mitchell's possessions that mentioned UFOs.

## Release of the Wilson-Davis Memo

Once he was given access to the materials, Rigny copied many documents of interest. One was the 15-page Wilson-Davis memo, which records the alleged October 16, 2002 meeting between Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] and physicist [[Eric Davis]]. The meeting was about Wilson's 1997 discovery of a crash retrieval program and his being denied access to it.

Rigny's name became public when he appeared on [[Richard Dolan]]'s podcast to discuss where the memo came from and what it contains. The document has since become one of the most significant pieces of evidence in the modern debate over UAP disclosure, and in 2022 it was entered into the official U.S. Congressional Record.
