Sean Kirkpatrick was the first director of [[AARO]] (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office), which was established on July 20, 2022, under the authority of [[Ronald S. Moultrie]] as Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security. He is a career defense intelligence officer whose time at AARO became highly controversial among UAP researchers and advocates. After he left AARO, Kirkpatrick is alleged to have joined the [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory FFRDC]], which is managed by [[Battelle Memorial Institute]], as Chief Technology Officer for defense and intelligence programs. The job posting was later deleted from public view.

## Role in UAP Programs

In "The Hidden Wing", Gerb accuses Kirkpatrick of lying, deception, and active interference against UAP whistleblowers during his time as AARO director. Kirkpatrick and [[Ronald S. Moultrie]] are both alleged to have deleted references to [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] and [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory FFRDC]] from their public professional biographies. Both organizations are rumored to be involved in the analysis of recovered UFO systems. The [[Hidden Wing]] thesis alleges that AARO under Kirkpatrick was deliberately structured as a whistleblower honeypot rather than a genuine investigative body. Kirkpatrick has repeatedly responded emotionally to David Grusch, and he has answered his critics through podcast media. His name also comes up in discussions of the counter-intelligence and oversight structures around UAP legacy programs and program protection offices.

## Michael Herrera Interview

In April 2023, Kirkpatrick interviewed UAP whistleblower Michael Herrera about his 2009 encounter in Indonesia. Herrera describes the interview as being like an interrogation. He says Kirkpatrick showed little interest and asked only three repetitive questions, all about the identity of the operators Herrera encountered. Herrera also states that Kirkpatrick claimed AARO had access to satellite imagery of the incident location but never followed up on the promised access. Herrera further claims that AARO's Historical Report Volume 1 deliberately misrepresented his testimony. According to Herrera, the report falsely attributes to him statements about "extraterrestrial vehicles" and "US Special Forces" that he never made, and he calls this a purposeful omission and distortion.

## Scientific American Op-Ed and Disinformation Accusations

Kirkpatrick wrote an op-ed in *Scientific American*, "Here's What I Learned as the U.S. Government's UFO Hunter" (19 January 2024). In it he dismissed UAP claims and attributed the entire reverse engineering narrative to a "conspiracy" that began in 2008 through [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)]] and centered on a small group: [[Luis Elizondo]], former Senator [[Harry Reid]], [[Hal Puthoff]], [[Eric Davis]], and [[Chris Mellon]]. Gerb has called this framing historically illiterate and points out that documented crash retrieval evidence predates AATIP by decades, including [[Jesse Marcel]]'s forced retraction at Roswell, [[Project Moon Dust]], and the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]].

In the same op-ed, Kirkpatrick claimed that no UAP witnesses or whistleblowers had contacted AARO. Mellon and Elizondo both publicly rebutted this. Mellon stated he personally introduced Kirkpatrick to Elizondo, Davis, and Puthoff, each of whom spent hours briefing Kirkpatrick in a classified setting with zero follow-up. [[David Grusch]] has stated he attempted to approach AARO but received no response. Gerb also sets the op-ed's description of AARO as "unwaveringly committed to harnessing science and technology" to resolve the issue against a finding of the DoD Office of the Inspector General. In the unclassified summary of its UAP evaluation, released on 25 January 2024, the Inspector General found that the DoD "has no overarching UAP policy".

Kirkpatrick consistently uses the terms "alien" or "extraterrestrial" in place of the legally precise "non-human intelligence (NHI)", the term used in the 2024 NDAA and the Congressional Record under [[Schumer Amendment (NDAA)|Chuck Schumer's amendment]]. Critics argue this is a deliberate strategy to avoid legally binding terminology. Kirkpatrick has also compared alleged UAP encounters, including [[Ryan Graves]]'s documented "cube within a sphere" sightings, to Chinese ball drones. UAP researchers compare this dismissal to the [[Robertson Panel]]'s use of weather balloons and swamp gas as explanations.

## Nonlinear Solutions and MITRE Subcontracting

Gerb's investigation into [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] notes that Nonlinear Solutions, the LLC Kirkpatrick registered shortly after leaving AARO, is now subcontracting under MITRE in connection with work for U.S. Space Command. It also notes that [[Ronald S. Moultrie]], who established Kirkpatrick's AARO directorship and was his superior as Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USD I&S), has separately removed MITRE from his public curriculum vitae.

## Post-AARO Connections and Departure

Kirkpatrick stated he departed AARO "willingly" in late 2023, but investigative journalist [[Ross Coulthart]] reported that, as of October 16, 2023, Kirkpatrick had registered an LLC called Nonlinear Solutions at a North Carolina address he and his wife had owned since 2017. At the same time, he was registered with the DOE's [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]], which is managed by [[Battelle Memorial Institute]], in a defense and intelligence capacity. UAP researchers have separately alleged that both organizations are involved in the analysis of recovered non-human craft. These circumstances raise conflict-of-interest questions about how independent his leadership of AARO was.

## George Mason University Oversight

Anthony Miller attended a public reception at George Mason University in Arlington, Virginia, described as Kirkpatrick's last public event as AARO director. He reported on LinkedIn that Pentagon spokesperson [[Susan Gough]] never left Kirkpatrick's side for the full hour of the event. Miller described Kirkpatrick visibly pausing and looking to Gough for confirmation before answering audience questions, and he characterized Kirkpatrick's responses as "rife with double speak, patronizing dismissal, word play and semantics."

## Yankee Blue and the Wall Street Journal Article

Gerb's *Special Access Required Vol.2* accuses Kirkpatrick of promoting the claim, reported in a *Wall Street Journal* article titled "Pentagon Disinformation Fueled America's UFO Mythology," that the entire impetus for UFO reverse-engineering claims traces to an illegal Air Force hazing ritual called "Yankee Blue." Gerb alleges Kirkpatrick privately told podcasters, off-record, that each branch of the armed forces had its own Yankee Blue equivalent. Gerb states that this detail was never mentioned in the *Journal* article. Journalist [[Steven Greenstreet]] later obtained and published a copy of the Yankee Blue document, which Gerb states has inaccurate security control markings. Responding to the Yankee Blue narrative in his Judicial Watch interview, [[David Grusch]] stated he was well aware of hazing activities from his own career. He stated, however, that the individuals he brought forward to the Intelligence Community Inspector General were not confused by hazing programs, and that the actual code words he provided investigators were not the Yankee Blue names circulated in media reports.
