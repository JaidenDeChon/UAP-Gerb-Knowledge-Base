Sean M. Kirkpatrick is an American physicist and defense intelligence official who was the first director of [[AARO]] (All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office), the Pentagon's UAP office, from its founding on 20 July 2022 until 1 December 2023. AARO reported to [[Ronald S. Moultrie]], the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security. Kirkpatrick's time at AARO, and what he has said since, made him one of the most controversial officials in UAP circles: he has dismissed the claims of crash retrieval and reverse engineering, while whistleblowers and their supporters accuse him of ignoring or misrepresenting their testimony. After leaving AARO he became chief technology officer for defense and intelligence programs at the [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]], which [[Battelle Memorial Institute]] manages for the Department of Energy. Gerb calls him "a modern-day disinformation agent" and has made him a recurring target since January 2024.

## Career

Kirkpatrick earned a doctorate in physics at the University of Georgia in 1995. In 1997 the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]] recruited him to build an ultrafast laser physics lab, and at some point he also worked as a senior research scientist at [[SAIC]], where, Gerb notes, he filed a 2003 patent on holographic two-photon-induced photopolymerization. In 2003 he became a CIA program manager assigned to the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]], and in 2005 he moved to the CIA's [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology|Directorate of Science and Technology]] as a staff scientist. His later posts included deputy director of intelligence at U.S. Strategic Command and at U.S. Space Command, and chief scientist at the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]]'s Missile and Space Intelligence Center, the post he held when AARO was set up.

Moultrie announced AARO on 20 July 2022 with Kirkpatrick as its director; Gerb credits [[David M. Taylor]], Moultrie's deputy, with hiring him. He announced his departure in November 2023 and left on 1 December. Kirkpatrick says he left willingly; Gerb points to [[Ross Coulthart]]'s reporting that by 16 October 2023 he had registered an LLC, Nonlinear Solutions, and was registered with Oak Ridge. Gerb also reports that the Oak Ridge web page celebrating his appointment was later deleted, and that by 2025 Nonlinear Solutions was subcontracting under [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] for U.S. Space Command.

## Michael Herrera's Testimony

In April 2023, UAP whistleblower [[Michael Herrera]] testified to AARO in a secure facility about his 2009 encounter in Indonesia. Kirkpatrick is the only official Herrera can confirm was in the room. Herrera describes the session as "like an interrogation to an extent". He says Kirkpatrick seemed uninterested and asked only three questions, all of them the same: who were the operators Herrera encountered? Herrera says AARO told him it could get satellite imagery of the location that day, but more than a year later it had not come back to him. He also says that AARO's [[AARO Historical Report Volume 1|Historical Report Volume 1]] misrepresented his testimony, attributing to him statements about an "extraterrestrial" spacecraft and "US Special Forces" that he never made.

## Scientific American Op-Ed and Disinformation Accusations

Kirkpatrick wrote an op-ed in *Scientific American*, "Here's What I Learned as the U.S. Government's UFO Hunter" (19 January 2024). In it he dismissed UAP claims and attributed the entire reverse engineering narrative to a "conspiracy" that began in 2008 through [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)]] and centered on a small group: [[Luis Elizondo]], former Senator [[Harry Reid]], [[Hal Puthoff]], [[Eric Davis]], and [[Chris Mellon]]. Gerb has called this framing historically illiterate and points out that the paper trail on crash retrieval predates AATIP by decades, including [[Jesse Marcel]]'s forced retraction at Roswell, [[Project Moon Dust]], and the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]].

In the same op-ed, Kirkpatrick wrote that none of the whistleblowers in the public eye had come to AARO. Mellon and Elizondo both publicly rebutted this. Mellon stated he personally introduced Kirkpatrick to Elizondo, Davis, and Puthoff, each of whom spent hours briefing Kirkpatrick in a classified setting with zero follow-up. [[David Grusch]] has stated he attempted to approach AARO but received no response. Gerb also sets the op-ed's description of AARO as "unwaveringly committed to harnessing science and technology" to resolve the issue against a finding of the DoD Office of the Inspector General. In the unclassified summary of its UAP evaluation, released on 25 January 2024, the Inspector General found that the DoD "has no overarching UAP policy".

Kirkpatrick consistently uses the terms "alien" or "extraterrestrial" in place of "non-human intelligence (NHI)", the term used in the 2024 NDAA and the Congressional Record under [[Schumer Amendment (NDAA)|Chuck Schumer's amendment]]. Gerb reads this as a deliberate way to sidestep the wording Congress actually uses. Kirkpatrick has also explained the "cube within a sphere" objects that pilots have described to [[Ryan Graves]] as Chinese ball drones, which Gerb compares to the [[Robertson Panel]]'s use of weather balloons and swamp gas as explanations.

## Role in UAP Programs

Across his videos Gerb accuses Kirkpatrick of deception and of active interference against UAP whistleblowers. In *The Hidden Wing* he calls AARO a whistleblower honeypot rather than a genuine investigative body, and notes that Kirkpatrick went on to the Battelle-managed Oak Ridge laboratory while Moultrie dropped a Battelle advisory-board post from his CV. In *Special Access Required Vol.2* Gerb alleges that Kirkpatrick was partially briefed into the legacy portfolio to limit its exposure, criticizes him and AARO's former deputy director, Tim Phillips, for personal attacks on Grusch, and describes him "emotionally lashing out" through the journalist [[Steven Greenstreet]]. Gerb's NRO and SAIC videos count him, through his NRO, CIA and SAIC years, among the officials he suspects of guarding legacy programs, and a witness known as [[Ed (Witness 11063)|Ed]] of the 412th Test Wing refused to testify to AARO while Kirkpatrick led it. In the SAIC video Gerb also plays a former official who read the transcripts of Kirkpatrick's AARO briefings to Senators Rubio, Warren and Gillibrand and says Kirkpatrick "distorted, downplayed" the big picture.

On 10 February 2023, former Air Force officer [[Robert Jacobs]] gave sworn testimony to AARO and to Kirkpatrick himself, which Gerb sets against AARO's position that there were no credible witnesses.

## Nonlinear Solutions and MITRE Subcontracting

Gerb's investigation into [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] notes that Nonlinear Solutions, the LLC Kirkpatrick registered shortly before leaving AARO, is now subcontracting under MITRE on work for U.S. Space Command. It also notes that [[Ronald S. Moultrie]], who made Kirkpatrick director of AARO and was his superior as Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USD I&S), has separately removed MITRE from his public curriculum vitae.

## Post-AARO Connections and Departure

Kirkpatrick stated he departed AARO "willingly" in late 2023, but investigative journalist [[Ross Coulthart]] reported that, as of October 16, 2023, Kirkpatrick had registered an LLC called Nonlinear Solutions at a North Carolina address he and his wife had owned since 2017. At the same time, he was registered with the DOE's [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]], which is managed by [[Battelle Memorial Institute]]. Gerb notes that Battelle has "a very serious link to UAP", asks whether Kirkpatrick left willingly or was sent to a job set up by the DOE, and raises the question of how independent his leadership of AARO was.

## George Mason University Reception

Anthony Miller attended a public reception at George Mason University in Arlington, Virginia, which he described as Kirkpatrick's last public event as AARO director. He reported on LinkedIn that Pentagon spokesperson [[Susan Gough]] never left Kirkpatrick's side for the full hour of the event. Miller described Kirkpatrick visibly pausing and looking to Gough for confirmation before answering audience questions, and he characterized Kirkpatrick's responses as "rife with double speak, patronizing dismissal, word play and semantics."

## Yankee Blue and the Wall Street Journal Article

Gerb's *Special Access Required Vol.2* accuses Kirkpatrick of promoting the claim, reported in a June 2025 *Wall Street Journal* article titled "The Pentagon Disinformation That Fueled America's UFO Mythology," that the entire impetus for UFO reverse-engineering claims traces to an illegal Air Force hazing ritual called "[[Yankee Blue]]." Gerb alleges Kirkpatrick privately told podcasters, off the record, that each branch of the armed forces had its own Yankee Blue equivalent, a detail never mentioned in the *Journal* article. Journalist [[Steven Greenstreet]] later obtained and published a copy of the Yankee Blue document, which Gerb states has inaccurate security control markings. Responding to the Yankee Blue narrative in his Judicial Watch interview, [[David Grusch]] stated he was well aware of hazing activities from his own career. He stated, however, that the individuals he brought forward to the Intelligence Community Inspector General were not confused by hazing programs, and that the actual code words he provided investigators were not the Yankee Blue names circulated in media reports. In his SAIC video Gerb also asks how two of Kirkpatrick's explanations can both hold: that [[Kona Blue]] was a project "tricking all Air Force personnel", and that witness accounts were circular reporting.
