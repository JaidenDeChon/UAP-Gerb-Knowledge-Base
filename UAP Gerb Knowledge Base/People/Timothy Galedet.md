Timothy "Tim" Galedet is a retired US Navy Rear Admiral who has emerged as one of the most forthcoming senior naval officials regarding UAP phenomena. Galedet served in senior positions within the Navy and is notable in UAP research for testifying on congressional record about the Navy's awareness of UAP legacy program testimony, for publicly acknowledging the credibility of UAP reports from naval aviators, and for his willingness to engage substantively with UAP disclosure efforts — a stark contrast to the institutional pattern of dismissal that characterized Navy UAP statements from 1951 onward.

## Congressional Testimony and Public Statements

Galedet has stated on congressional record that while he is aware of testimony from naval aviators and others regarding UAP legacy programs, he was not directly briefed on or involved in such programs during his own naval service. UAP Gerb cites Galedet's candor as an example of the "recent openness" of some naval officials — contrasting it with the historical pattern of public denial exemplified by [[Ner Liddell]]'s 1951 dismissal of all UAP reports as mirages or balloons.

Galedet's statements are significant both for what they affirm — that UAP reports from credible naval sources warrant serious attention — and for what they illustrate about the compartmentalized structure of naval UAP programs. His lack of direct briefing despite serving in senior naval roles is consistent with UAP Gerb's broader thesis that Navy UAP programs operated in deep compartmentalization, with even high-ranking officers kept outside the program unless there was a specific need to know.
