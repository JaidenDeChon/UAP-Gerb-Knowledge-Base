Steven Lovekin (1940–2009; his own records spell the name Stephen) was a U.S. Army technical specialist who served at the [[White House Army Signaling Agency]] under Presidents [[Dwight D. Eisenhower]] and [[John F. Kennedy]]. He became known as a UFO whistleblower through [[Steven Greer]]'s 2001 [[Disclosure Project]]. He said that during his White House service he saw the classified side of [[Project Blue Book]] and a piece of wreckage from the 1947 [[Roswell Crash]]. He also said he watched Eisenhower lose control of the UFO problem to corporations. Gerb pairs him with Roswell press officer [[Walter Haut]] in the first volume of his whistleblower series, and comes back to Lovekin's Roswell debris claim in three later videos.

## Service

Lovekin was born in Cynwyd, Pennsylvania. He entered the Army in 1958 and joined the Pentagon's radio frequency engineering office. In 1959 he moved to the White House Army Signal Agency as a technical specialist, holding an above-top-secret clearance, and served into the Kennedy administration until his honorable discharge in June 1961. His work there involved codebreaking and secure communications. In an interview with author [[Grant Cameron]], he said he went to Camp David with Eisenhower and other staff several times. Lovekin was only a sergeant, but he found that the President made a point of knowing every staff member's name.

After the Army he took a degree at Catawba College in 1964 and a law degree from Wake Forest in 1967, and practised as a lawyer in Hickory, North Carolina, where he died in November 2009.

Greer's Disclosure Project billed him as "Brigadier General Steven Lovekin." The rank came from the North Carolina State Guard, a volunteer militia with no official military standing. Gerb calls the title an embellishment by Greer, or a credential Greer did not check, and the biggest stain on Lovekin's case.

Several sources, including researcher Joe Murgia, place Lovekin at the briefing with Greer, [[Edgar Mitchell]], Rear Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] and Commander [[Will Miller]] that is recorded in the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] (see [[1997 Meeting (Wilson, Miller, Mitchell, Greer, Hughes)]]; Gerb dates it to 1998).

## Claims

### The classified Blue Book

Lovekin said Blue Book was "discussed quite openly in the office" and that its internal findings were highly scientific, exact, and meant only for particular military personnel. They included UFO photographs taken by Air Force, civilian, Marine Corps and foreign pilots, and, by his estimate, 200 to 300 authentic radar lock-on cases. Gerb sets the estimate against Major [[Hector Quintanilla]]'s testimony to Congress that Blue Book found no radar lock-on cases, which [[J. Allen Hynek]] later corrected. Lovekin recalled that talking about UFOs was explained to staff as the fastest way to end a career. Gerb ties this to [[USAF Regulation 200-2]].

### Roswell wreckage and bodies

At a meeting about Blue Book material, Lovekin said, a colonel he recalled as "Hollard" (possibly a misspelling in Greer's material) produced a yardstick-like metal piece. It was covered in what the colonel called symbols of instruction. The colonel said it had come from a craft that crashed in New Mexico in 1947, out of a box of material the military was studying in what would now be called reverse engineering. Gerb links it to the marked "I-beam" that [[Jesse Marcel]] showed his son. The next day, Lovekin said, colleagues discussed three or five extraterrestrial bodies from the crash, one of them partially alive.

### Eisenhower's loss of control

Lovekin said he overheard Eisenhower on the phone discussing the [[1952 UFOs Over Washington DC]], and that UFOs were a very important concern of the President's. As the problem outgrew any single agency, he said, it was parcelled out across the government to keep it secret and cheap. Eisenhower was "sold out" without knowing it, as control slid toward corporations. His frustration lasted for months, and Lovekin reads the 1961 farewell address on the military-industrial complex as his warning. Gerb treats this as the point where the [[Legacy Programs]] broke free of congressional and presidential oversight.

### Threats

Lovekin said an older officer with [[CIA]] and [[Defense Intelligence Agency]] ties warned him that exposing the secret could get someone "erased." The officer said such threats had been made and carried out since 1947, and extended to families. Lovekin said he strongly suspects people have been murdered to protect the secret.

## In Gerb's Later Videos

Gerb returns three times to Lovekin's claim that a classified part of Blue Book held Roswell debris. In his Majestic-12 video he sets it beside the MJ-12 briefing document's statement that the public projects Sign, Grudge and Blue Book had "very limited interaction" with MJ-12. In his Kecksburg video it is one of the parallels he draws for the debris and hieroglyph-like markings reported there. In his video on the [[Off-World Technologies Division]], he is reminded of Lovekin's testimony when he turns to attorney [[Daniel Sheehan]]'s account of searching the classified portions of Blue Book in 1977.
