David Barnett is a New Zealand naval radar operator and seaman who was involved in tracking the [[1987 HMNZS Southland USO Incident]], one of the most dramatic encounters on record between a military vessel and an Unidentified Submerged Object (USO).

## HMNZS Southland Incident (1987)

In February 1987, Barnett was serving aboard the HMNZS Southland when the New Zealand naval vessel encountered a large USO north of New Zealand. The craft, about 150 feet wide and 800 feet long, closed a 20-kilometer distance in less than 30 seconds. That gives an estimated minimum speed of 1,492 mph and an average acceleration of 4.5 Gs.

As the USO passed directly underneath the ship, it cut all ship power and completely drained the batteries, an example of the electromagnetic effects often reported in UAP encounters.

## Significance

As the radar operator, Barnett gives the incident technical credibility. His tracking data established:

- Precise distance: the craft closed a 20 km gap on the ship's radar
- Speed: at least 1,492 mph, worked out from the time and distance
- Electromagnetic effects: the power drain coincided with the craft's approach

[[Kevin Knuth]] cites the incident in his analysis of transmedium UAP capabilities and USO phenomena. It shows that craft capable of extreme performance in the air also move through water with ease.
