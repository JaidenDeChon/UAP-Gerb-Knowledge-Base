Thomas Townsend Brown (1905–1985) was an American inventor and physicist who pioneered research into [[Electrogravitics|electrogravitic]] propulsion in the 1930s. Brown discovered that a high-voltage discharge applied to a capacitor assembly produces a net thrust in the direction of the positive electrode. The phenomenon is now known as the Biefield-Brown effect, after Brown and his mentor, physicist Paul Alfred Biefield. His early experiments suggested that electrostatic energy could be converted into kinetic propulsion, and UAP researchers argue that this principle was later classified and developed into black budget aerospace programs.

Brown's work is considered foundational to claims by [[Richard Boylan]] and retired USAF Colonel [[Donald Ware]] that the [[Northrop Grumman]] B-2 Spirit stealth bomber incorporates onboard electrogravitic systems for internal mass reduction on its wings and fuselage, a capability that allegedly adds to the aircraft's extraordinary per-unit cost.
