Thomas Townsend Brown (1905–1985) was an American inventor and physicist who pioneered research into [[Electrogravitics|electrogravitic]] propulsion in the 1930s. Brown discovered that high-voltage discharge applied to a capacitor assembly produce a net thrust in the direction of the positive electrode — a phenomenon now known as the Biefield-Brown effect (named after Brown and his mentor, physicist Paul Alfred Biefield). His early experiments suggested that electrostatic energy could be converted into kinetic propulsion, a principle that UAP researchers argue was subsequently classified and developed into black budget aerospace programs.

Brown's work is considered foundational to claims by [[Richard Boylan]] and retired USAF Colonel [[Donald Ware]] that the [[Northrop Grumman]] B-2 Spirit stealth bomber incorporates onboard electrogravitic systems for internal mass reduction on its wings and fuselage — a capability allegedly contributing to the aircraft's extraordinary per-unit cost.
