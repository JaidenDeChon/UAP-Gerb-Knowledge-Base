Ryan W. Harris is the director of Dugway Proving Ground's West Desert Test Center (WDTC), responsible for "overall planning, directing, and evaluating all aspects of work for the chemical, biological, and other acquisition test and training programs executed by the test center." Harris rose from project manager at Battelle Memorial Institute to director of the primary Army Major Range and Test Facility Base (MRTFB) where UAP legacy programs are alleged to operate. His career is a significant personnel link between Battelle, which has long been suspected of deep involvement in UAP programs, and Dugway's most sensitive test operations.

## Education and Early Army Service

Harris holds a bachelor's degree in chemical engineering and served in the United States Army as a chemical officer in the Chemical Corps. His combined background in military chemical operations and chemical engineering made him ideally suited to work at Dugway Proving Ground, the nation's leading chemical and biological weapons testing facility.

## Battelle Memorial Institute Career (2000-2004)

In 2000, Harris took a job as a project manager for Battelle Memorial Institute at Dugway Proving Ground. In that role he oversaw several testing and training programs at the installation and came to know Dugway's structure, operations, classified facilities, and contractor relationships closely.

Battelle has an extensive presence at Dugway. Its work there includes:
- Operating the Chemical and Biological Defense Information Analysis Center (CBIAC)
- Facility design and construction, including specialized clean rooms and sealed containment structures
- Chemical and biological defense program management
- Testing and evaluation support for the Joint Services Chemical and Biological Defense Program (CBDP)

As Battelle project manager at Dugway, Harris gained direct oversight experience in chemical/biological defense testing, specialized facility operations, and classified testing protocols. These are exactly the types of programs that would be necessary for managing UAP materiel exploitation operations requiring chemical analysis, clean room environments, and compartmented testing.

## Transition to Direct Dugway Employment (2004-2012)

In 2004, Harris left Battelle to work for Dugway Proving Ground directly as a project manager. Over the following eight years he rose through the leadership of the West Desert Test Center, taking on progressively broader responsibility for WDTC operations.

His path from Battelle project manager to Dugway Proving Ground (DPG) project manager, then to WDTC leadership and finally WDTC Test Director, mirrors a pattern seen in other senior WDTC personnel. They also came from Battelle before taking charge of programs where UAP operations are alleged.

## Director, West Desert Test Center (2012-Present)

By 2012, Harris had become WDTC Test Director. In that post he is now responsible for evaluating "all aspects of the work for the chemical, biological, and other acquisition test and training programs executed by the test center." The phrase "and other acquisition test and training programs" is particularly notable, because it leaves unclear what "other" programs exist beyond the acknowledged chemical/biological defense work.

As WDTC director, Harris oversees operations at:
- Ditto Technical Center (the operational center of WDTC)
- Avery Technical Center (radiobiological research, clean rooms, internal rail system, alleged UAP operations site)
- Michael Army Airfield (joint Army-Air Force aerospace testing)
- All chemical and biological defense testing ranges and facilities

Approximately 77% of acknowledged WDTC work is done for the Joint Services Chemical and Biological Defense Program (CBDP). That leaves 23% of operations outside standard biochem defense testing, potentially including classified aerospace, materials science, or the "other acquisition" programs that the wording of his title suggests.
