Everett Heinman is a former CIA official. He was Deputy Director for Science and Technology (DS&T) at the Central Intelligence Agency from 1979 to 1982, then directed NRO Program B, the CIA's SIGINT satellite initiative, from 1982 to 1989. He was the first chief of the ground element for Program B at [[Pine Gap, Australia]]. His name entered UAP research through the 1989 [[Inman-Echler Telephone Call]], in which Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]] named Heinman as "the best person to ask" about recovered UFO vehicles being made available for research. Inman offered the name unprompted, and UAP researchers treat it as the clearest documented pointer from a senior intelligence official to the CIA DS&T as the office that managed alleged UAP material holdings.

## Career Background

Heinman worked in the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology at a time when the DS&T was one of the two founding agencies of the [[National Underwater Reconnaissance Office]] (NURO), along with the US Navy. His later role as NRO Program B director put him inside the CIA's most sensitive technical collection infrastructure. Program B's ground control element at [[Pine Gap, Australia]], a joint US–Australian facility, processed satellite SIGINT data for the NSA and related agencies.

After leaving government service, Heinman wrote about Project Palladium in a 1998 CIA journal article, the first public discussion of that program. Palladium was a classified CIA program of the 1960s that used naval assets to create false UAP radar signatures and to map Soviet air defenses.

## 1989 Meeting with Bob Echler

Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]] directed NASA mission specialist [[Bob Echler]] to Heinman as "the best person to ask" about recovered UAP vehicles. Echler met Heinman at CIA headquarters in Langley, Virginia, on 10 August 1989, and Heinman denied any knowledge of UFOs or UFO legacy programs.

## 2022 Contact by RGH_UFOs

In 2022, the Twitter researcher RGH_UFOs contacted Heinman about Inman's claim that Heinman knew about US UFO programs. Heinman did not deny it outright. He said he was "a long way and quite a few years from working in the area you are researching" and offered to answer questions, but then did not respond. Gerb has also tried to contact Heinman separately and received no reply. UAP researchers note that someone with no knowledge of the subject would be expected to give a straightforward denial, so they treat his equivocal response as an ambiguous partial acknowledgment.

## Significance

UAP researchers treat three facts together as circumstantial evidence that the CIA DS&T had a central part in managing alleged UAP material holdings. Heinman held the DS&T post (the DS&T was one of NURO's founding agencies). He directed NRO Program B, whose satellite capabilities would be relevant to UAP tracking. And Inman specifically named him as the right contact for research on recovered vehicles. Heinman also directed NRO Program B from [[Pine Gap, Australia]], a ground control station for US reconnaissance satellites, which adds to speculation that the facility may have overlapped with UAP tracking infrastructure.
