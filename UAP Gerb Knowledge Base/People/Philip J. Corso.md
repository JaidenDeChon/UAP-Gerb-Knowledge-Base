Philip J. Corso (1915–1998) was a retired US Army Colonel who served in various intelligence and research roles during his career, including a position on President Eisenhower's National Security Council staff. He became a controversial figure in UAP research following the 1997 publication of his book *The Day After Roswell*, co-written with William Birnes, in which he claimed to have personally managed a program at the Army Research and Development division to seed recovered Roswell crash technology into the US industrial and defense sectors — including transistors, fiber optics, and night vision — without those contractors knowing the technology's origin.

## The Day After Roswell

Corso's central claim is that following the 1947 Roswell incident, the US Army and later the Foreign Technology Division maintained physical artifacts from the recovered craft, and that Corso, while serving under General [[Arthur Trudeau]] at the Army's Office of the Chief of Research and Development in the early 1960s, personally oversaw the dissemination of this material to private contractors as "foreign technology." The book was controversial upon publication, with some researchers finding corroborating details and others, including [[John B. Alexander]], noting the published version contained sensational material absent from the original manuscript.

## UFO Working Group and INSCOM Funding

Corso claimed that a UFO working group was established in the mid-1980s and received substantial funding from US Army INSCOM under General [[Albert Stubblebine]]'s direction. This claim, if accurate, would establish INSCOM as an organizational hub for Army UAP programs during Stubblebine's command tenure.

## Historical Context in UAP Programs

Corso's testimony is cited as establishing a pre-1960 framework for US UAP technology exploitation programs, before the later DARPA, contractor, and FFRDC infrastructure that UAP Gerb documents in later videos. His references to "UAP control mechanisms" dating to the 1950s support a continuity argument for long-running UAP legacy program operations.
