Philip J. Corso (1915–1998) was a retired U.S. Army Lieutenant Colonel who spent 21 years in military intelligence and research roles and reached nine clearance levels Above Top Secret. He became one of the most controversial and credible UFO whistleblowers of the 20th century. He claimed firsthand involvement with recovered non-human craft and biological entities, and with a systematic program in the early 1960s to seed UAP-derived technologies into American industry and defense contractors.

## Career

Corso was born on 22 May 1915 and served in the U.S. Army from February 23, 1942, to March 1, 1963. During World War II he served in Military Intelligence in Europe, and at the war's end he became chief of the U.S. Counter Intelligence Corps (CIC) in Rome. He arranged safe passage out of Rome for many Jewish refugees and was personal emissary to Giovanni Battista Montini, who later became Pope Paul VI.

During the Korean War, Corso did intelligence work directly under General Douglas MacArthur as chief of the Special Projects Branch of the Intelligence Division, Far East Command. His work centered on prisoners of war (POWs) and soldiers missing in action (MIA), and he investigated North Korean POW camps. In 1992 he testified before the Senate Select Committee on POW/MIA Affairs about hundreds of American soldiers abandoned at these camps.

From 1953 to 1957, Corso served on President Eisenhower's National Security Council staff as a member of the Operations Coordinating Board. Eisenhower set up the board under Executive Order 10483 as a covert operations committee that reported directly to the NSC. Corso's superior was C.D. Jackson, Special Assistant to Eisenhower and government psychological warfare adviser. In this period Corso obtained clearances for Eyes Only material and specialized in air defense and ballistic missiles. He commanded missile units whose radar allegedly picked up unidentified objects over New Mexico and Germany traveling at over 3,000 mph, and after these incidents Corso was ordered to destroy all records and tapes.

After commanding the 71st Missile Battalion in Germany and Inspector General of the 7th Army, Corso was recalled by Lieutenant General [[Arthur Trudeau]] in 1960 to become Special Assistant to the Chief of Army Research and Development and chief of the newly created [[U.S. Army Foreign Technology Division]]. He retired from the Army on 1 March 1963, shortly after Trudeau.

## Fort Riley 1947: Observation of Non-Human Biological Entity

Corso claimed that on or about July 6, 1947, while he was stationed at [[Fort Riley, Kansas]] as post duty officer, he personally saw a 4-foot non-human creature in a crate filled with fluid. The being had an oversized head shaped like an incandescent light bulb, almond-shaped eye sockets, four-fingered hands, thin legs and feet, and no facial hair or eyebrows. Its skull was so overgrown that all the facial features sat at the front, in a small circle on the lower part of the head, with a tiny flat slit for a mouth.

Corso later learned that the crate was part of a convoy of five trucks carrying biological entities from an airfield in New Mexico along Route 40, through Fort Riley, to [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. A sergeant of the guard told him the trucks had come from New Mexico and were carrying "something sensitive." Corso's official military records confirm he was stationed at Fort Riley on this date, which supports the timing of his account.

## The Roswell File and Foreign Technology Division

In 1961, General Trudeau gave Corso what Corso called the "[[Roswell File]]", a box of materials and documents containing field reports, medical autopsy reports and technical debris from the [[Roswell Crash|1947 Roswell crash]]. The autopsies were allegedly performed at [[Walter Reed Army Medical Center]]. Their reports called the recovered beings "[[Extraterrestrial Biological Entity|Extraterrestrial Biological Entities]]" (EBEs) and described creatures about 4 feet tall whose anatomy differed significantly from human anatomy. They had very large hearts and lungs, and skin and bone structure built to protect against cosmic radiation or gravitational forces. A transparent film over the EBEs' eyeballs led Trudeau to order the development of [[Passive Night Vision Technology]].

Corso was designated chief of the [[U.S. Army Foreign Technology Division]] in 1960 and given four specific subject areas to track: (1) foreign development of this world, (2) foreign intelligence and developments not of this world, (3) interim project officer on select R&D projects, and (4) all other duties as special assistant to the Chief of R&D for the U.S. Army. The Foreign Technology Division had a budget of about $2 billion (over $10.5 billion in 2024 dollars). Its job was to manage [[Technologies of Unknown Origin]] (TUOs) from crashed UAP and [[Technology Seeding|seed them into U.S. industry]], disguised as normal research and development proposals.

## Technology Seeding and the Golden Age of Army R&D

Under Trudeau's direction, Corso oversaw a program that fed UAP-derived artifacts to U.S. industry scientists and contractors working in related research areas. Industry could hold the patents, on condition that it fed the results back to the Army and the public. Technologies allegedly drawn from UAP crash materials included:

- [[Passive Night Vision Technology|Image intensifiers]], developed at Fort Belvoir's night vision laboratory with help from 48 U.S. industries
- [[Fiber Optics]], from a broken wiring harness in which each strand gave off a different color, seeded to [[Bell Labs]]
- [[Super Tenacity Fibers]], from single strands that could not be damaged, used for flak jackets, parachutes and stealth composites
- [[Molecular Alignment]] techniques, which showed atoms aligned in patterns that gave incredible hardness and radiation resistance
- [[Transistor]] and [[Integrated Circuit]] technology, seeded to [[Bell Labs]] and Texas Instruments
- [[Laser]] technology, believed to be how the EBEs communicated over vast distances

Trudeau created the [[Office of the Chief of Army R&D]] in 1958, bringing all Army research and development under one department. This began what Corso called the "[[Golden Age of Army R&D]]", which lasted from 1958 to 1963. The program brought large industry, laboratories and many leading universities into Army R&D, including through partnerships with [[Federally Funded Research and Development Center (FFRDC)|FFRDCs]] and [[University Affiliated Research Centers (UARCs)|UARCs]]. It contacted the top 25 industries on the Fortune 500 list and arranged meetings with their boards of directors and chiefs of Army R&D laboratories.

## Cloned Beings and Consciousness-Craft Integration

After studying the Roswell File materials, Corso and his team concluded that the recovered beings were [[Cloned Beings|clones or expendable creatures]], made by some outside intelligence specifically for space travel. They based this on the recovered craft having no food, water, waste disposal, refrigeration or medical facilities, which suggested the beings were engineered not to need any of these. The team theorized that the [[Roswell Crash|Roswell craft]] was a reconnaissance vehicle designed to return to a mothership, and that the species had solved gravitational or dimensional travel, allowing an instantaneous return to base.

The team's most significant conclusion was that the biological entities were integrated into the flying saucer's guidance system and in effect acted as the craft's navigation and control mechanism. This led to research into harnessing [[Brain Wave Aircraft Control|brain waves to control aircraft and missiles]], a concept that later inspired investigations into [[Consciousness-Craft Integration]].

## White Sands 1957-1958: Telepathic Encounter with Non-Human Intelligence

Corso claimed that in 1957-1958, while he commanded the ballistic missile firing range at [[Red Canyon Range]], part of [[White Sands Missile Range]], he encountered a non-human being in an abandoned mine where he had gone to shelter from the heat. The being's physiology was different from that of the entity he saw at Fort Riley in 1947, and it communicated with him telepathically. It asked Corso to lower the radar coverage from nearby installations, because the radar was keeping its craft from departing.

According to Corso, he asked what he would receive in return, and the being answered: "A new world if you can take it." Corso claimed he went back to his installation and ordered the radar shut down, which let the craft escape. The experience affected him deeply, and he thought about the meaning of the statement for the rest of his life. He later told the story to [[John B. Alexander]], [[Jacques Vallee]], [[George Knapp]] and [[Daniel Sheehan|Danny Sheehan]]. He also made an 8mm home movie for his grandchildren describing the encounter, which was never meant to be made public.

## Dawn of a New Age vs. The Day After Roswell

Corso's testimony exists in two primary forms: *The Day After Roswell* (1997), co-written with [[Bill Burns|William Birnes]], and *Dawn of a New Age*, his original unfiltered manuscript released in Italy in 2003 and in the U.S. in 2011, after his death. According to Corso's son [[Philip Corso Jr.]], Corso went through *The Day After Roswell* and highlighted everything he did not say or did not like, and gave up halfway through out of frustration. Corso Sr. had no plans to release the original manuscript. His son published it to correct inaccuracies that had made his father furious during his lifetime.

*The Day After Roswell* is 130 pages longer than *Dawn of a New Age* and contains sensational details that are not in the original manuscript. Several people who saw the manuscript before Corso's death, including Colonel [[John B. Alexander]], noticed details in the book that the manuscript lacked, such as Corso intimidating a CIA director, or Corso describing the Roswell crash in detail even though he was not there. Senator [[Strom Thurmond]] wrote a foreword for the book but angrily withdrew it when he learned the book centered on UAP, which led to an early reprint.

## UFO Working Group and Intelligence Support Activity

Corso claimed to have learned that in February 1987, Colonel Harold E. Phillips of the Defense Intelligence Agency chaired a group called the [[UFO Working Group]]. Its 17 members included one Army general and three Air Force generals, DIA scientists, three NSA officials, a supervisor from the CIA's Domestic Collection Division, and a technical team from the CIA's [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology|Science and Technology Directorate]]. The working group allegedly received funding from U.S. [[Army INSCOM]] (Intelligence and Security Command) under Major General [[Albert Stubblebine]]'s direction. The Intelligence Support Activity (ISA), founded by INSCOM, was handed over to [[JSOC (Joint Special Operations Command)|JSOC]] in 2003, the same year the [[CIA Office of Global Access]] was created. That office was later accused of acting as a logistics coordinator for UAP crash retrieval operations.

## Sworn Affidavit and Legal Action

Shortly before his death in July 1998 at age 83, Corso signed a sworn affidavit for [[Citizens Against UFO Secrecy (CAUS)|CAUS]] attorney and executive director Peter A. Gersten to support a lawsuit against the Department of the Army. In the affidavit, which CAUS published, Corso stated under penalty of perjury that he was a member of Eisenhower's National Security Council and former head of the Foreign Technology Desk, that he personally observed a 4-foot non-human creature at Fort Riley on July 6, 1947, that he came into possession of the Roswell file in 1961, and that he personally read medical autopsy reports referring to the creature as an "Extraterrestrial Biological Entity."

## Criticism and Controversy

Corso's claims have faced substantial criticism. In July 2024, [[John Greenewald]] and [[The Black Vault]] released FBI documents from a 1964-65 background check. In them the FBI described Corso as someone who spread unfounded claims, including that Lee Harvey Oswald was an FBI informant, and allegations about Fabian socialists. One FBI document stated "another government agency has characterized Corso as a parasite who has never produced any intelligence through his own efforts." None of the FBI documents discussed UFOs or his Army Foreign Technology Division duties, however, and all the files were cross-references rather than main investigatory files.

Researcher [[Stanton Friedman]] said he had checked with the Eisenhower Library, and that Corso was never a member of the National Security Council and never attended one of its meetings but was a liaison between the council and other groups. Gerb's video counters that the 1992 report of the Senate Select Committee on POW/MIA Affairs lists Corso as NSC staff under Eisenhower, and that his military records document his service on the Operations Coordinating Board, which reported directly to the NSC. Friedman also said a retired colonel with a PhD had sent him a list of 92 technically false claims in *The Day After Roswell*. Senator Thurmond's withdrawal of his foreword, after he discovered the book's UAP focus, further damaged the book's credibility. Physicist Dr. [[Lou Cameron]], director of the night vision laboratory at Fort Belvoir, flatly denied that the eye lens of a non-human intelligence (NHI) had aided the breakthroughs in passive night vision.

Colonel [[John B. Alexander]] found discrepancies in the technology seeding claims, particularly in the genealogy of integrated circuitry. Even so, Alexander and others, including [[Jacques Vallee]], maintained that Corso's story never wavered. Across many tellings he never altered, added or dropped a detail, which points either to remarkable consistency or to a fixed narrative.

Gerb dates the start of active reverse engineering, as distinct from recovery and storage, to Corso's 1958–1963 "golden age", and sets it beside the founding of the [[MITRE Corporation]] in July 1958, months after Trudeau took over Army R&D. He follows Corso's named contractors forward: [[Bell Labs]], which Corso called the Army's premier contractor and on which [[Sandia National Laboratories|Sandia]]'s operations were modelled, and [[Sperry Rand]], whose board Corso says Trudeau met often and whose divisions later passed to Honeywell, Lockheed Martin and Northrop Grumman. He also cites Corso's 1961 project on "control guidance by brain waves" as an early link between UFOs and cognition, and Corso's statement in *The Day After Roswell* that Roswell debris and bodies were shipped to [[Fort Bliss]].

## Legacy and Historical Significance

Corso's testimony, about events of the late 1950s and early 1960s, gave what may be the first detailed public account of a structured U.S. program to exploit UAP technology through defense contractors, FFRDCs and university research centers. His claims parallel recent disclosures about [[Waived Unacknowledged Special Access Programs (USAPs)|Unacknowledged Waived Special Access Programs]], which operate in stovepiped compartments protected from congressional oversight. Whether or not he was fully credible, his picture of how Legacy Programs might be structured is still influential in UAP research: technology seeded through normal R&D channels, patents kept by contractors, a partnership between the military and industry, and hypersecrecy justified by Cold War competition.

According to [[Jacques Vallee]]'s *Forbidden Science: Pacific Heights*, Dr. [[Eric Davis]] asked former President George H.W. Bush whether Corso's material could have been Nazi hardware. In Vallee's account, Bush told him it was "impossible" that Corso was mistaken about the material he handled, saying "the two topics were clearly separated by that time", by which he meant that German/Nazi hardware and UAP materials were distinct by the 1960s. Bush also claimed that Trudeau briefed him on Legacy Programs around the time of the 1968 Mendel Rivers Congressional hearings. He said Trudeau told him "his man Corso" had been asked to testify before Congress about alien hardware distributed to industrial labs, but that the testimony was squashed.

Corso kept a strict oath of secrecy for about 35 years. He had promised Trudeau, his mentor, not to discuss their work until after Trudeau died, which he did in June 1991. Corso said he had never intended to write a book, and that even his family complained he had kept quiet for 35 years.
