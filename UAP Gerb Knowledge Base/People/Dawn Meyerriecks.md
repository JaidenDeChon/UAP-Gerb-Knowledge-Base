Dawn Meyerriecks is a former senior US intelligence technology and acquisition official. She was Assistant Director of National Intelligence for Acquisition, Technology and Facilities (ADNI/AT&F) from 2009 to 2013, then moved to the CIA's [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology|Directorate of Science and Technology]] (DS&T), which she led as Deputy Director for Science and Technology from 2015 until she retired in 2021. Earlier in her career she worked at [[TRW]] and NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, was chief technology officer of the Defense Information Systems Agency, and was a senior vice president at AOL. In 2022 [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] named her a senior visiting fellow. Gerb counts her among the senior intelligence officials he believes act as gatekeepers of the UFO legacy programs, and she appears in four of his videos.

## Career

Meyerriecks began her career in aerospace, at TRW (now part of [[Northrop Grumman]]) and at the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, where she was a senior engineer and product manager. Gerb dates her time at TRW to 1981 to 1993. She then spent nearly ten years at the Defense Information Systems Agency (DISA), as its chief technology officer and as technical director of its Joint Interoperability and Engineering Organization, before serving as AOL's senior vice president for product technology from 2004 to 2006. From 2009 to 2013 she was ADNI/AT&F. She joined the CIA on 17 June 2013 as Associate Deputy Director for Science and Technology and became Deputy Director on 21 October 2015, a post she held until her retirement in 2021. MITRE named her a senior visiting fellow in February 2022.

## Alleged Legacy Program Relevance

In his video on the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]], Gerb names Meyerriecks as one of thirteen officials with NRO ties who he believes are likely legacy-program members or gatekeepers. He notes that she began her career at TRW, a company he has "directly implicated" in work on derivative technologies, and that she served as CIA DS&T deputy director "for 8 years following her three-year position" as ADNI/AT&F. Her CV has no direct reference to the NRO or the [[CIA Office of Global Access]], but Gerb says "we can strongly assume she performed intimate work with both offices" in those two posts, and he shows a photograph of her with [[Glenn Gaffney]]. In *Special Access Required* Vol. 2 he names her and [[Doug Wolfe]] as "spook legacy gatekeepers" who both served in the ODNI acquisition office and as deputy directors of the DS&T.

In his MITRE investigation, Gerb says that "immediately following agency DS&T" Meyerriecks joined "the corporate board of MITRE". He says the ADNI/AT&F office she once led, together with its Department of Defense counterpart, the [[Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment (USD A&S)]], is "imperative to legacy program operations, specifically through NRO acquisitions." He names her alongside [[Donald M. Kerr]], [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]] and [[Susan Gordon]] as part of a pattern: senior intelligence-community figures allegedly close to legacy programs who took roles at MITRE.

## TRW and Alleged Data Masking

In *Northrop Grumman & TRW* Vol. 2, Gerb says Meyerriecks worked at TRW from 1981 to 1993, overlapping for about two years with [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]], and that from 2009 to 2013 she served "under Clapper and O'Sullivan" as ADNI/AT&F. He suggests her CV may be "data masked", pointing to her work as a senior vice president at AOL, then her own consulting firm, then an immediate jump into ADNI/AT&F. He speculates either that the CV leaves out earlier exposure to "clandestine exotic USAPs" or that her corporate post was interwoven with the CIA, and he considers both possibilities feasible.
