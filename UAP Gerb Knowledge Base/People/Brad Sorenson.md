Brad Sorenson is an American industrial designer who claims to have witnessed classified alien reproduction vehicles (ARVs) at a restricted exhibit at [[Norton Air Force Base]] on November 12, 1988. Sorenson's firsthand testimony forms the primary evidentiary basis for the [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)]] known as the Flux Liner, and his detailed descriptions were translated into technical illustrations by aerospace artist [[Mark McCandlish]].

## 1988 Norton Air Force Base Encounter

Sorenson attended a private aerospace exhibit at Norton Air Force Base in San Bernardino, California, where he observed three disc-shaped craft of varying sizes displayed in a hangar. The craft — referred to as Flux Liners — were described as smooth, seamless vehicles featuring a distinctive central column surrounded by concentric capacitor plates, with a crew compartment above. Sorenson reported that the craft employed no conventional propulsion systems and were allegedly powered by zero-point energy or electrogravitic principles.

At the same exhibit, Sorenson also observed an unmanned pulse detonation craft described as a "pumpkin seed" shape — later associated with the [[TR-3A]] designation. Officials at the exhibit informed Sorenson and other attendees that they were seeking additional funding from Congress to build more of these reverse-engineered craft, during a period when the Reagan administration's [[Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI)]] was channeling significant resources into classified aerospace programs.

## Control System Description

Sorenson described the Flux Liner's control mechanism as a red sphere or trackball mounted on the pilot's armrest, which the operator rolled to maneuver the craft. This description was independently corroborated in Tom DeLonge and AJ Hartley's novel *Secret Machines*, which described an identical red trackball control system for the Astra TR-3B "Locust" — a detail allegedly sourced from real military and intelligence advisors including [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works]] officials.

## Relationship to Bill Scott Interview

On December 3, 1990, Sorenson gave a detailed interview to aerospace journalist Bill Scott about what he had witnessed at Norton AFB. This interview, along with subsequent collaborations with [[Mark McCandlish]], produced the most widely recognized technical depictions of the Flux Liner ARV.
