James Clapper is a retired U.S. Air Force Lieutenant General who held several of the most senior positions in American intelligence. He served as Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) from 1991 to 1995, as Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (USDI) from 2007 to 2010, and as Director of National Intelligence (DNI) from 2010 to 2017 under President Obama. As DNI, Clapper was the nominal head of the entire 17-agency U.S. Intelligence Community. In the 2025 documentary *[[The Age of Disclosure]]*, Clapper acknowledged that while he was DNI there was a program tracking vehicles over [[Area 51]], the classified test site. David Grusch has said he was disappointed that this was all Clapper said.

## Career

Clapper was born on 14 March 1941 in Fort Wayne, Indiana. Commissioned in the [[US Air Force]] in 1963 after graduating from the University of Maryland, he spent 32 years in uniform as an intelligence officer, ending as director of the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]] from November 1991 to August 1995, and retired as a lieutenant general. After six years in industry he returned to government in September 2001 as director of the National Imagery and Mapping Agency, which became the [[NGA]] while he ran it, until 2006. He was Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence from 2007 to 2010 and the fourth Director of National Intelligence from 9 August 2010 to 20 January 2017.

## Role in UAP Programs

[[David Grusch]] and other sources cited in UAP research allege that Clapper is the closest thing to a single controlling figure over UAP legacy programs since [[Dick Cheney]] left government in 2009. Clapper is alleged to have managed crash retrieval and reverse engineering programs across his multiple tours at DIA, USDI, and ODNI, holding informal, dual-hatted authority over them beyond his official portfolio. He allegedly recruited [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]] specifically to serve as Principal Deputy DNI, in part to keep management of the legacy programs continuous within the ODNI. Gerb also names Deputy Director of National Intelligence for Intelligence Integration [[Michael Dempsey]], along with O'Sullivan and NRO Director [[Betty Sapp]], as part of the reduced leadership that managed what remained of the legacy program portfolio under Clapper after [[Dick Cheney]] left government in January 2009. An Obama-era initiative allegedly involving Clapper, O'Sullivan, and [[Luis Elizondo]] reportedly sought to set up [[Hillary Clinton]] as a potential disclosure president if she won the 2016 election.

## Grusch's Direct Testimony

In public statements, [[David Grusch]] has tied Clapper to the programs more directly than almost anyone else in the UAP disclosure field. He said: "General Clapper was well aware of the crash retrieval issue, managed the crash retrieval issue, and when he was the DNI, he placed people in critical roles to manage this issue both publicly and I'll just say not publicly as well." Grusch asked Clapper, O'Sullivan, and others "in rooms discussing this issue" to provide greater public leadership.

Within the program portfolio held by the National Security Council (NSC), Clapper is alleged to have provided "top cover" for the AATIP program as a partial transparency initiative, a controlled attempt to speak about the UFO issue "outside the onion". He allegedly did this while also managing the larger, undisclosed legacy portfolio.

In Gerb's account Clapper held a role with "remarkably less power" than Cheney, and Gerb counts the loss of that oversight after he left in 2017 as one reason the programs are ungoverned today. He says that at any given time, slightly over two dozen living people have any idea of the full breadth of the siloed UFO programs.

## Alleged Absorption of MITRE's Legacy Programs (2011–2017)

Gerb's investigation into [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] ties Clapper to an alleged major turning point in how MITRE secured its UFO legacy programs. Beginning in 2011, MITRE allegedly clamped down on briefings for new program personnel, holding them to a strict need-to-know. That was the same year intelligence community partners, specifically the CIA, were reportedly brought into MITRE's Navy-administered technology reverse-engineering program. In 2017 MITRE allegedly lost full control of that program, or had leadership of it shifted away from MITRE. Gerb attributes this shift to Clapper's tenure as Director of National Intelligence (2010–2017). During those years Clapper is alleged to have worked to fold previously siloed DoD legacy activities, including MITRE's Navy-administered programs, into a broader National Security Council and intelligence-community crash-retrieval portfolio. That portfolio may have included the alleged 2017 Unacknowledged Special Access Program [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)]].

## ATIP and the National Program Special Management Staff

In *Special Access Required Vol.2*, Gerb names Clapper as the specific official he believes gave "top cover" for [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|ATIP]]. The video argues that ATIP was an unfunded informal working group rather than a genuine DIA program, and that it was distinct from the separately funded [[AAWSAP]]. Gerb connects this to [[Luis Elizondo]]'s self-described 2013–2017 role as director of the [[National Program Special Management Staff (NPMS)]], a staff function under the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USD I&S), which was Clapper's former office. He argues that Elizondo's closeness to Clapper through NPMS gave him access to Special Access Programs controlled by the National Security Council, beyond anything Elizondo's public persona from the ATIP era has acknowledged.

## Lockheed Martin's Divestment Attempt

In his Lockheed Martin video Gerb recalls, "off the top of my head", that Clapper was involved in shutting down Lockheed Martin's first attempt, in 2008 or 2009, to move crash-retrieval material to a DIA program, perhaps an entity like OAP-L. The second attempt, through [[Kona Blue]] in 2011, was stopped by [[Glenn Gaffney]] of the CIA and possibly Robert Cardillo.
