Luis "Lou" Elizondo is a former U.S. intelligence official who served as director of the [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)]], the Pentagon's UAP investigation program. After leaving government service he became a prominent figure in the push for UAP disclosure. He has publicly called for transparency about the UAP evidence the government holds, and he has made significant claims about the quality and extent of the classified UAP footage in U.S. government possession.

## AATIP's Five Characteristics of UAP

Elizondo is widely credited with setting out AATIP's "five observables", the characteristics that set anomalous UAP apart from conventional aircraft. They have become a standard framework for analyzing UAP encounters:

1. Instantaneous acceleration: sudden changes in velocity far beyond what known aircraft can do
2. Hypersonic velocity: movement at extreme speeds without sonic booms or visible propulsion
3. Low observability: the ability to avoid radar detection or being seen
4. Trans-medium travel: seamless movement between air, water, and space
5. Anti-gravity/No visible propulsion: flight that suggests control of gravity, or propulsion beyond current human technology

Several videos use these five characteristics to analyze UAP, including the evaluation of the Metapod footage.

## Public Statements on UAP Footage

In a 2021 interview with GQ magazine, Elizondo spoke about how much UAP evidence the U.S. government holds. He claimed that the three Pentagon videos released to the public (Gimbal, Go Fast, and the Tic Tac footage) are "some of the least compelling" examples available. According to him, government archives contain far more dramatic footage, including a 23-minute video, footage of a UAP approximately 50 feet from an aircraft cockpit, and cases where observers "just couldn't believe it." Elizondo said he was 99% certain that these objects are not foreign adversarial technology, implying that a non-human origin is the only viable explanation left.

## Alleged UAP Program Involvement

Elizondo allegedly took part in an Obama-era initiative with [[James Clapper]] and [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]] that aimed to position [[Hillary Clinton]] as a potential disclosure president if she won the 2016 election. The allegation suggests that Elizondo was involved in UAP matters before his publicly known tenure at AATIP.

## ATIP, AAWSAP, and the National Program Special Management Staff

Gerb's video *Special Access Required Vol.2* draws a sharp line between two programs. It argues that Elizondo's public accounts and the 2025 documentary *Age of Disclosure* often blur the two together, and in the video's view they do so deliberately. The first is the DIA-run, congressionally funded [[AAWSAP]] (Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program, 2008–2012), which had a budget of approximately $22 million and was directed by [[Jay Stratton]]. The second is [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|ATIP]] itself, which the video describes as an unfunded, informal working group with no appropriations or formal administrative structure of its own. The video alleges that ATIP worked in effect as a cover vehicle for the National Security Council, a way for parts of the legacy program apparatus to "talk about the onion outside of the onion". Top cover allegedly came from [[James Clapper]], first as Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (2007–2010) and later as Director of National Intelligence (2010–2017).

The argument rests on Elizondo's time, from approximately 2013 to 2017, as director of the National Program Special Management Staff (NPMS). This staff function sat under the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USD I&S), which was Clapper's former office. Gerb describes it as coordinating access between National Security Council special access programs and the DoD/intelligence community. The video calls the NPMS director "usually the most cleared person in the entire DoD besides the secretary of defense themselves," more cleared even than the director of the DoD Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO). It argues that this earlier role gave Elizondo far deeper access to SAPs controlled by the National Security Council than he has publicly disclosed. Elizondo left the NPMS director role to join [[To The Stars Academy]] in 2017.

## Criticism of Age of Disclosure and TTSA's Legacy Program Narrative

Gerb is sharply critical of Elizondo's role in the 2025 documentary *Age of Disclosure*. In it, Elizondo, [[Jay Stratton]], and others present an organizational chart of the alleged legacy program structure. The chart puts the [[CIA]] Directorate of Science and Technology at the top, with the Department of Energy, the Air Force, and defense contractors on an equal tier below it. Gerb argues that the chart is both historically inaccurate and self-serving. He points out that it conspicuously leaves out the National Security Council, the U.S. Army, the U.S. Navy, the Office of Naval Intelligence, and most of the intelligence community's other major agencies. The video speculates that they were left out to protect associates of Elizondo and Stratton and to control how any future disclosure event would be framed.

The video ties Elizondo, [[Tom DeLonge]], [[John Podesta]], [[Hal Puthoff]], Lockheed Martin Skunk Works leadership, and others to [[To The Stars Academy]] (TTSA, founded 2017). In its account, TTSA was the vehicle for a theorized partial-disclosure campaign meant to position Hillary Clinton as a "disclosure president" ahead of the 2016 election. The video says this campaign grew out of an earlier concept it calls "Project Forum". Gerb further alleges that Elizondo may have disclosed parts of the [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)|Immaculate Constellation]] program to DeLonge around the time he left the NPMS directorship. Gerb says he "stands with the whistleblower" and with [[David Grusch]] in particular, but he states directly that he does not trust Elizondo. He calls on Elizondo to publicly clarify the full extent of his past involvement in legacy program structures.

## Connection to Reported Incidents

Elizondo has referred to knowledge of underwater UAP bases and of a 1950 crash near La Indio and Guerra. The available sources give few details about these claims.

## 1933 Magenta, Italy UFO Crash

In an interview, Elizondo corroborated the [[1933 Magenta UFO Crash/Retrieval|1933 Magenta, Italy UFO crash/retrieval]] case. He said:

> "Roswell may not have been the first event. There's been some reporting internationally as well that there have been some crashed vehicles and they were obtained by local authorities and there was some scientific study done on them. I want to be very careful not to go too far down the rabbit hole, but I've seen personally some documentation that's very compelling from a particular foreign country where they had a recovery of a vehicle and there they had some countries conducting scientific analysis on it, and after World War II, allegedly, parts of that vehicle were brought to the United States."

When the interviewer suggested Italy, Elizondo confirmed: "Yeah, it was Italy. That's as it was explained to me. I saw some documentation that has been validated, that was from Mussolini himself. It's authentic, it's been proven to be authentic, the documentation."

This statement directly validates the authenticity of the documents given to researcher [[Roberto Pinotti]]. It also supports [[David Grusch]]'s public testimony that the Magenta retrieval was the first NHI craft recovered by the United States, which got it by transfer from Italian to U.S. possession.

## Caribbean USO Account

In a 2022 interview with NASA astronaut [[Terry Virts]], Elizondo described a military USO encounter. A Navy helicopter crew was carrying out routine cruise missile recovery operations near a Caribbean island above the [[Puerto Rico Trench]]. On consecutive monthly recovery missions, the crew saw a large, dark, circular object, approximately the size of a small island, rise from approximately 22,000 feet below the surface. The second time, the object appeared while a Navy frogman was on a rope getting ready to attach to the recovered missile. The crew made an emergency ascent and left, and the object pulled the missile back into the depths. According to Elizondo, a later question to a senior naval figure about "the fast mover program" brought an implicit confirmation that the program exists.
