Luis "Lue" Elizondo is a former U.S. Army counterintelligence agent and Pentagon intelligence official who says he directed the [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)]], the Pentagon's UAP investigation effort. He resigned from the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence in October 2017, joined [[Tom DeLonge]]'s [[To The Stars Academy]], and helped bring the three [[Pentagon UFO Videos]] to the public in the *New York Times* story of December 2017. Since then he has been one of the best-known voices for UAP disclosure: he wrote the 2024 book *Imminent*, testified to Congress in November 2024 and appeared in the 2025 documentary *[[The Age of Disclosure]]*.

Gerb cites Elizondo often, for AATIP's [[Five UAP Characteristics (AATIP)|five characteristics of UAP]], for his account of the [[1933 Magenta UFO CrashRetrieval|1933 Magenta]] documents and for his descriptions of unreleased footage. But Gerb says plainly that he does not trust him. He argues that Elizondo's earlier post as director of the [[National Program Special Management Staff (NPMS)|National Program Special Management Staff]] gave him far deeper access to the legacy programs than he admits, and that the organizational chart he presented in *The Age of Disclosure* misrepresents how those programs are run.

## Career

Elizondo enlisted in the [[US Army]] in 1995 and was assigned to military intelligence. He went on to work as a counterintelligence officer for the Department of Defense. From 2008 until his resignation in October 2017 he worked in the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (now the [[Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USDINS)|USD(I&S)]]). He says he led AATIP during those years; the Pentagon has said he had no assigned AATIP responsibilities, and Gerb calls the ATIP he describes an unfunded informal working group rather than a program. Gerb adds that Elizondo was a contracting officer's representative at the Counterintelligence Field Activity (CIFA) in the early-to-mid 2000s, that he is a former member of the Army's Intelligence Support Activity, and that he directed the NPMS from 2013 to 2017.

After resigning he became To The Stars Academy's director of global security and special programs. He announced in December 2020 that he, [[Chris Mellon]] and Steve Justice had left the company.

## AATIP's Five Characteristics of UAP

Elizondo is widely credited with setting out AATIP's "five observables", the characteristics that set anomalous UAP apart from conventional aircraft. They are now a standard framework for analyzing UAP encounters:

1. Instantaneous acceleration: sudden changes in velocity far beyond what known aircraft can do
2. Hypersonic velocity: movement at extreme speeds without sonic booms or visible propulsion
3. Low observability: the ability to avoid radar detection or being seen
4. Trans-medium travel: seamless movement between air, water, and space
5. Anti-gravity/No visible propulsion: flight that suggests control of gravity, or propulsion beyond current human technology

Gerb uses them to measure the [[Videos/Incredible UFO Footage - METAPOD/summary|Metapod footage]], and [[Kevin Knuth]] built on them in his talk on the physics of UAP. [[Karl Nell]]'s list adds a sixth, physical or invasive biological effects, which current legislation now references.

## Public Statements on UAP Footage

In a 2021 interview with GQ magazine, Elizondo spoke about how much UAP evidence the U.S. government holds. He said the three Pentagon videos released to the public (Gimbal, Go Fast and the Tic Tac footage) are "some of the least compelling" examples available. According to him, government archives contain far more dramatic footage, including a 23-minute video and footage of an object about 50 feet from an aircraft's cockpit: "sometimes you just couldn't believe it." He said he was "99% sure it's not foreign adversarial technology, so that only leaves really one other option. It's someone or something else." He has also remarked that footage has leaked into the public realm that he was shocked never broke global news, and Gerb suggests the [[Videos/Incredible UFO Footage - FLYBY/summary|Flyby footage]] may be such a clip.

## ATIP, AAWSAP, and the National Program Special Management Staff

Gerb's video *Special Access Required Vol.2* separates two programs that, it argues, Elizondo's public accounts and the 2025 documentary *[[The Age of Disclosure]]* often blur together, and deliberately so in the video's view. The first is the DIA-run, congressionally funded [[AAWSAP]] (Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program, 2008–2012), which had a budget of approximately $22 million and was directed by [[Jay Stratton]]. The second is [[AATIP (Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program)|ATIP]] itself, which the video describes as an unfunded, informal working group with no appropriations or formal administrative structure of its own. Elizondo has called ATIP a sub-SAP of AAWSAP, which Gerb calls "brazen nonsense". The video alleges that ATIP worked in effect as a cover vehicle for the National Security Council, a way for parts of the legacy program apparatus to "talk about the onion outside of the onion". Top cover allegedly came from [[James Clapper]], first as Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence (2007–2010) and later as Director of National Intelligence (2010–2017).

The argument rests on Elizondo's time, from 2013 to 2017, as director of the [[National Program Special Management Staff (NPMS)]]. This staff function sat under the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (USD I&S), which was Clapper's former office. Gerb describes it as coordinating access between National Security Council special access programs and the DoD/intelligence community. The video calls the NPMS director "usually the most cleared person in the entire DoD besides the secretary of defense themselves," more cleared even than the director of the DoD Special Access Program Central Office (SAPCO). It argues that this role gave Elizondo far deeper access to SAPs controlled by the National Security Council than he has publicly disclosed. In a clip the video plays, Matt Ford asks Elizondo whether ATIP came out of the NSC under Clapper; Elizondo says he worked for Clapper, that his entry point was the NSC for "the programs that I was running... in the special access world," and that he could say little more. Elizondo left government to join [[To The Stars Academy]] in 2017.

At the November 2024 UAP hearing, asked whether he was read into crash retrieval programs, Elizondo said he had signed documents three years earlier restricting what he could say about crash retrievals. Gerb reads this as a forced change of posture after Grusch went public.

## Criticism of Age of Disclosure and TTSA's Legacy Program Narrative

Gerb is sharply critical of Elizondo's role in *The Age of Disclosure*, which premiered on 21 November 2025. In it, Elizondo and [[Eric Davis]] describe a single legacy program. The chart puts the [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]] at the top, with the Department of Energy, the Air Force and defense contractors on an equal tier below it. Gerb argues that the chart is both historically inaccurate and self-serving. He points out that it leaves out the National Security Council, the U.S. Army, the U.S. Navy, the Office of Naval Intelligence, the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|NRO]] and most of the intelligence community's other major agencies. He believes it was drawn to protect Elizondo's and [[Jay Stratton]]'s "legacy friends" and to decide "who gets to catch the live hand grenade" when disclosure comes. He stresses that the criticism is not aimed at the film's director, Dan Farah.

The video ties Elizondo, [[Tom DeLonge]], [[Chris Mellon]], [[John Podesta]], [[Hal Puthoff]], Lockheed Martin Skunk Works leadership and others to [[To The Stars Academy]] (TTSA, founded 2017). In its account, TTSA was the public vehicle for a partial-disclosure campaign meant to position [[Hillary Clinton]] as a "disclosure president": a national-security framing and an acknowledgment that "we are not alone", with nothing on crash retrievals, reverse engineering or the legacy programs. Gerb links this to "Project Forum", an idea of disclosure through popular figures described in the BAASS ten-month report of 2009. When Clinton lost and TTSA failed, he says, the effort changed shape and resurfaced as *The Age of Disclosure*. In his video on Northrop Grumman and TRW, Gerb says he believes Elizondo, [[James Clapper]] and [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]] were involved in an Obama-administration effort to set up that candidate as the "disclosure president", an effort Grusch alluded to on Megyn Kelly's show in January 2026.

Gerb also alleges that Elizondo may have exposed DeLonge to parts of the [[Immaculate Constellation (IMCON)|Immaculate Constellation]] program. While he was TTSA's director of global security and special programs, Gerb says, Elizondo acted as its liaison to the DIA, CIA and NRO, and Gerb believes he was the "high up" NRO-connected source behind DeLonge's October 2017 description of "episodic visits" on Joe Rogan's show. Gerb says he "stands with the whistleblower", naming Grusch, [[Dylan Borland]], Matthew Brown and "Witness Ed", but he states directly that he does not trust Elizondo, whose account of his AATIP role has changed over the years. He calls on Elizondo to clarify the full extent of his past involvement: "If Lou Elizondo was part of the legacy programs, he should just come out and say it."

## Connection to Reported Incidents

In his 2024 book *Imminent*, Elizondo describes the December 1950 crash retrieval near the Mexico–Texas border between El Indio and Guerrero ([[1950 El Indio UFO Crash]]), the same crash the [[Eisenhower Briefing Document]] records. The book also names [[Lyndon B. Johnson]] among the presidents briefed on UFOs, which Gerb uses in his video on the [[1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania Crash Retrieval|Kecksburg crash]], and touches on a crash retrieval program at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] long tied to [[TRW]]. Elizondo has also said that unacknowledged UAP programs ran in the 1980s, and Gerb mentions his statements about underwater bases that non-human intelligence might inhabit.

He is among those Gerb names as confirming the authenticity of the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]]. In an interview with Ross Coulthart and Bryce Zabel, Elizondo said of the memo, "That thing isn't going to die... DOD should have saw this coming," and he has spoken of AATIP finding a black DoD program focused on UFOs to which it was denied access.

## 1933 Magenta, Italy UFO Crash

In an interview, Elizondo corroborated the [[1933 Magenta UFO CrashRetrieval|1933 Magenta, Italy UFO crash/retrieval]] case. He said:

> "Roswell may not have been the first event. There's been some reporting internationally as well that there have been some crashed vehicles and they were obtained by local authorities and there was some scientific study done on them. I want to be very careful not to go too far down the rabbit hole, but I've seen personally some documentation that's very compelling from a particular foreign country where they had a recovery of a vehicle and there they had some countries conducting scientific analysis on it, and after World War I[I], allegedly, parts of that vehicle were brought to the United States."

Asked which country, the answer first went to South America, maybe Brazil or Argentina, before Elizondo settled on Italy: "I think it's Italy, yeah it was Italy. That's as it was explained to me. I saw some documentation... There is some documentation that has been validated, that was from Mussolini himself. It's authentic, it's been proven to be authentic, the documentation."

Gerb closes his Magenta video on this statement, calling it a "powerful statement" to match Grusch's. It supports the authenticity of the documents given to researcher [[Roberto Pinotti]], and [[David Grusch]]'s public testimony that the Magenta retrieval was the first NHI craft recovered by the United States, which got it by transfer from Italian to U.S. possession.

## Response to Sean Kirkpatrick

In a *Scientific American* op-ed, former [[AARO]] director [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] named Elizondo, Harry Reid, Hal Puthoff, Eric Davis and Chris Mellon as the small group around AATIP behind what he called a 2008 conspiracy, and wrote that no whistleblower had come to AARO. Mellon answered that Elizondo, Davis and Puthoff each spent hours briefing Kirkpatrick in a classified setting without feedback. Elizondo answered on X that many people he knows personally have given AARO detailed information for the record: "If AARO isn't willing to tell the truth to Congress, we are."

## Caribbean USO Account

In a 2022 interview with NASA astronaut Terry Virts, Elizondo described a military USO encounter, from a source he would not name. A Navy helicopter regularly flew from a Caribbean island to recover test-fired cruise missiles, which sink when they run out of fuel and later rise to be collected for their telemetry. On one recovery, the crew saw something round, dark and about the size of a small island rising toward the surface, though it did not break the water. The next month, with a Navy frogman hanging from the rope to latch onto another missile, the object rose again out of the [[Puerto Rico Trench]], where the water is 22,000 feet deep. The frogman scrambled up the rope in an emergency ascent, and the object sucked the missile underwater and disappeared. See [[Caribbean Island Cruise Missile Recovery USO Encounter]].

Gerb later likens this account to a light seen during the recovery of data from [[Missile Test 1802 Data Recovery|missile test 1802]] off Ascension Island.
