Andrew Kissner (referred to in some UAP Gerb captions as "Kner" or "Knisser," likely transcription variants of the same name) is a former New Mexico state representative. Beginning in the early 1990s, he spent years privately interviewing people in the US armed forces, the intelligence community, the Department of Energy and private industry about alleged UFO crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering programs. His work directly prompted fellow New Mexico representative [[Steven Schiff]]'s 1993–1995 General Accounting Office inquiry into the Roswell incident, after the US Air Force reported it could not locate relevant Roswell records.

## The 1994 Estimate of the Situation

In 1994, Kissner compiled a statement summarizing his research. It drew on interviews with personnel connected to White Sands Proving Ground, the Naval Research Laboratory and former Atomic Energy Commission laboratories, as well as former military officers and private defense contractors. The findings covered the period from the alleged 1947 Roswell and White Sands crash retrievals through 1994. Researcher Linda Moulton Howe presented Kissner's disclosures publicly at the second annual UFO Crash Retrieval Conference in 2004.

According to Kissner, "T-forces" (units modeled on the Allied T-Forces that secured German atomic-research sites at the end of World War II) were assigned from 1947 onward to secure downed discs and collect debris. He stated that teams under [[Vannevar Bush]]'s research and development board processed and analyzed the collected material. In his account, the Atomic Energy Commission's Los Alamos Scientific Laboratory and its Sandia Base division were intimately involved in housing and studying the recovered material, supported by the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] and by personnel drawn from MIT, Brookhaven National Laboratory, the RAND Corporation, the National Advisory Committee on Aeronautics, and the Rockefeller Institute.

Kissner further stated that custody of recovered discs was "permanently vested in civilian authorities by classified presidential executive order" in July 1948. He named the Atomic Energy Commission and its national laboratories as that civilian authority, and said the Department of Defense's Air Force Systems Command, the Naval Research Laboratory and the Secretary of Defense's Research and Development Board also took part, with the newly formed National Security Council coordinating the whole effort.

Gerb treats Kissner's account as one of the most credible "estimates of the situation" in UFO legacy program research. He notes that it is consistent with the earlier 1950 statements of [[Wilbert B. Smith]] and [[Robert Sarbacher]], and with his own independent research into the period.

## Sandia and Custody of the Discs

In his investigation of [[Sandia National Laboratories]], Gerb examines the statement in detail. He gives its title as "Peculiar Phenomenon: Early United States Efforts to Collect and Analyze Flying Discs", and says Kissner's sources described Army elements acting offensively to bring down two to four discs in a series of events beginning 15 May 1947, Roswell among them. Discs may have contributed to the deaths of several US servicemen between 9 May and 31 July 1947, and their "special weapons" were at once classified as enemy weapon systems. Recovered discs were analyzed for Vannevar Bush with the help of Los Alamos, its Z Division at Sandia Base, Air Materiel Command and AFSWP personnel at Sandia Base, together with the Department of Terrestrial Magnetism, Detlev Bronk, MIT, the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics and the [[RAND Corporation]]. The statement also describes a "lash-up" radar network at White Sands, Los Alamos, Sandia Base and a nearby radar site that could have tracked UFOs.

Because the statement was presented by Linda Moulton Howe, whom he considers unreliable, Gerb contacted Kissner himself and says he can confirm it; Kissner told him "the truth is stranger than fiction." Gerb reads the 1948 transfer of custody to AEC laboratories as the origin of his model in which FFRDCs, not contractors, hold recovered material, and he calls Kissner "a mini Dave Grusch precursor". He notes that the Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit documents, which also place AFSWP at the 1947 recoveries, appeared only in 1995, a year after Kissner finished, so Kissner could not have drawn on them.
