J. Andrew Kissner is a former New Mexico state representative. (The captions of Gerb's videos spell his name in many ways, among them "Kner", "Kisner", "Kistner", "Kitchener" and "Knisser".) In the early 1990s he spent years privately interviewing people in the US armed forces, the intelligence community, the Department of Energy and private industry about alleged UFO crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering programs. Gerb calls him "a mini David Grusch prototype". His findings led New Mexico congressman [[Steven Schiff]] to open a General Accounting Office inquiry into the Roswell incident. Its report of 28 July 1995 found that Roswell Army Airfield's outgoing messages for July 1947 had been destroyed without proper authorization. Kissner's 1994 statement is the "estimate of the situation" Gerb uses to close his Cold War era of the legacy programs, and he returns to it in at least eight videos.

## The 1994 Estimate of the Situation

In 1994, Kissner compiled a statement summarizing his research. It drew on interviews with personnel connected to [[White Sands Missile Range|White Sands Proving Ground]], the Naval Research Laboratory and former [[Atomic Energy Commission]] laboratories, as well as former military officers and private defense contractors. The findings covered the period from the alleged 1947 Roswell and White Sands crash retrievals through 1994. Researcher [[Linda Moulton Howe]] presented Kissner's disclosures publicly at the second annual UFO Crash Retrieval Conference in 2004.

According to Kissner, "T-forces" (units modeled on the Allied T-Forces that secured German atomic-research sites at the end of World War II) were assigned from 1947 onward to secure downed discs and collect debris. He stated that teams under [[Vannevar Bush]]'s research and development board processed and analyzed the collected material. In his account, the Atomic Energy Commission's Los Alamos Scientific Laboratory and its Z Division at Sandia Base were intimately involved in housing and studying the recovered material, supported by the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] and by personnel drawn from MIT, Brookhaven National Laboratory, the RAND Corporation, the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics, and the Rockefeller Institute. He also cited "Project Supremacy", a program he said was created in 1947 to track and gather data from triangulated New Mexico radar sites.

Kissner further stated: "It also appears likely that custody of flying discs was permanently vested in civilian authorities by classified presidential executive order in July of 1948." He named the Atomic Energy Commission and its national laboratories as that civilian authority, and said [[Air Force Systems Command]], the Naval Research Laboratory and the Secretary of Defense's [[Research and Development Board]] also took part, with the newly formed [[National Security Council]] coordinating the whole effort. In his video on Navy programs, Gerb lists [[MITRE Corporation|MITRE]] alongside Los Alamos, Sandia and RAND among the institutions Kissner's sources said studied the discs.

Gerb treats Kissner's account as one of the most credible "estimates of the situation" in UFO legacy program research. He notes that it is consistent with the earlier 1950 statements of [[Wilbert B. Smith]] and [[Robert Sarbacher]], and with his own independent research into the period. He says he is "in firm agreement" with most of it, including Kissner's claim that coordination of the early program went to the National Security Council, and he ties the July 1948 orders to [[Hoyt Vandenberg]]'s General Order No. 29 of 15 July 1948, which empowered the new [[Air Force Office of Special Investigations|Office of Special Investigations]] the same month. In his Sarbacher video Gerb also points to claims of Kissner's about UFOs and V-2 rockets at White Sands.

## Sandia and Custody of the Discs

In his investigation of [[Sandia National Laboratories]], Gerb examines the statement in detail. He gives its title as "Peculiar Phenomenon: Early United States Efforts to Collect and Analyze Flying Discs", and says Kissner's sources described Army elements acting offensively to bring down two to four discs in a series of events beginning 15 May 1947, Roswell among them. Discs may have contributed to the deaths of several US servicemen between 9 May and 31 July 1947, and their "special weapons" were at once classified as enemy weapon systems. Recovered discs were analyzed for Vannevar Bush with the help of Los Alamos, its Z Division at Sandia Base, Air Materiel Command and AFSWP personnel at Sandia Base, together with the Department of Terrestrial Magnetism, [[Detlev Bronk]], MIT, the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics and the [[RAND Corporation]]. The statement also describes a "lash-up" radar network at White Sands, Los Alamos, Sandia Base and a nearby radar site that could have tracked UFOs. In his Aztec video Gerb ties Kissner's two to four discs of 1947–1948, analyzed at Los Alamos and Sandia, to the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|1948 Aztec case]].

Because the statement was presented by Linda Moulton Howe, whom he considers unreliable, Gerb contacted Kissner himself and says he can confirm it; Kissner told him "the truth is stranger than fiction." Gerb reads the 1948 transfer of custody to AEC laboratories as the origin of his model in which FFRDCs and government-owned, contractor-operated labs, not contractors, hold recovered material. He notes that the [[Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] documents, which also place AFSWP at the 1947 recoveries, appeared only in 1995, a year after Kissner finished, so Kissner could not have drawn on them. By 2026 Gerb says he knows Kissner well and calls him "a wonderful man", and he follows Kissner in counting White Sands among several 1947 crash sites in the American Southwest alongside Roswell.
