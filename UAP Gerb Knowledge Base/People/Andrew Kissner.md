Andrew Kissner (referred to in some UAP Gerb captions as "Kner" or "Knisser," likely transcription variants of the same name) is a former New Mexico state representative who, beginning in the early 1990s, conducted years of private interviews with individuals across the US armed forces, intelligence community, Department of Energy, and private industry regarding alleged UFO crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering programs. His work directly prompted fellow New Mexico representative [[Steven Schiff]]'s 1993–1995 General Accounting Office inquiry into the Roswell incident, after the US Air Force reported it could not locate relevant Roswell records.

## The 1994 Estimate of the Situation

In 1994, Kissner compiled a statement summarizing his research, drawing on interviews with personnel connected to White Sands Proving Ground, the Naval Research Laboratory, former Atomic Energy Commission laboratories, former military officers, and private defense contractors. His findings covered the period from the alleged 1947 Roswell and White Sands crash retrievals through 1994. Kissner's disclosures were presented publicly by researcher Linda Moulton Howe at the second annual UFO Crash Retrieval Conference in 2004.

According to Kissner, "T-forces" (units modeled on the Allied T-Forces that secured German atomic-research sites at the end of World War II) were assigned to secure downed discs and collect debris beginning in 1947. He stated that collected material was processed and analyzed by teams under [[Vannevar Bush]]'s research and development board, with the Atomic Energy Commission's Los Alamos Scientific Laboratory and its Sandia Base division intimately involved in housing and studying recovered material, supported by the [[Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] and personnel drawn from MIT, Brookhaven National Laboratory, the RAND Corporation, the National Advisory Committee on Aeronautics, and the Rockefeller Institute.

Kissner further stated that custody of recovered discs was "permanently vested in civilian authorities by classified presidential executive order" in July 1948, naming the Atomic Energy Commission and its national laboratories as the relevant civilian authority, with participation from the Department of Defense's Air Force Systems Command, the Naval Research Laboratory, and the Secretary of Defense's Research and Development Board, under overall coordination by the newly formed National Security Council.

UAP Gerb treats Kissner's account as one of the most credible "estimates of the situation" in UFO legacy program research, noting its consistency with [[Wilbert B. Smith]] and [[Robert Sarbacher]]'s earlier 1950 statements and with the host's independent research into the period.
