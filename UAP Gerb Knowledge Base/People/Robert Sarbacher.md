Dr. Robert Irving Sarbacher (1907–1986) was an American physicist, a graduate of Harvard University and a student of Albert Einstein. In the early 1950s, during the Truman administration, he was a scientific consultant to the US Department of Defense's [[Research and Development Board]]. He is one of the most significant firsthand sources in early UFO research. In 1950 he privately told Canadian government engineer [[Wilbert B. Smith]] that the study of flying saucers within the United States government was classified at a higher level than the hydrogen bomb, and in 1983 he repeated and expanded on the claim in a letter to researcher [[William Steinman]] and a recorded call with [[Stanton Friedman]]. Gerb devotes a video to him and returns to his account in ten others, usually as the earliest insider evidence for a US crash-retrieval group.

## Career

Sarbacher founded the Washington Institute of Technology and was its dean, and he was a scientific consultant to the US Marine Corps. He also taught at Harvard, the Illinois Institute of Technology and Radcliffe, and was dean of the graduate school at the Georgia Institute of Technology. His principal area of research was the remote control of rockets. He was a member of the DoD's joint Research and Development Board, chaired by [[Vannevar Bush]], which Sarbacher described as the institutional home of a small group conducting classified UFO analysis. He died in Palm Beach, Florida, on 26 July 1986, aged 78. (Gerb's video places his death in 1987.)

His son, [[Robert Sarbacher Jr.]], later told researcher D.M. Duncan, who located him close to the time of Sarbacher's death, that his father spoke sparingly about UFOs. The son said his father knew they were real "for the obvious reason that they would be going 600 mph and then make a 90-degree turn in midair without slowing down." According to the son, Sarbacher was called upon to develop a missile fitted with cameras, like those on the V-2 rockets, that could track UFOs rather than destroy them, because conventional aircraft were too slow to pursue them.

## The 1950 Smith Confirmation

In 1950, Wilbert B. Smith, a senior Canadian government radio engineer, met Sarbacher in Washington, DC, and recorded four main claims that Sarbacher relayed about the US government's UFO program:

1. The matter was the most highly classified subject in the US government, rated higher than the hydrogen bomb.
2. Flying saucers exist.
3. Their mode of operation is unknown, but a small group headed by [[Vannevar Bush]] is actively studying them.
4. The entire matter is considered by US authorities to be of tremendous significance.

Smith had read [[Frank Scully]]'s 1950 book *Behind the Flying Saucers*, which publicized the alleged [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|Aztec, New Mexico crash]], and asked how much of it was fact. Sarbacher replied, "The facts reported in the book are substantially correct." Smith based a 1950 memo to the Canadian Department of Transport's Controller of Telecommunications on these disclosures. The memo led directly to Canada setting up two official UAP investigations: [[Project Magnet]] and [[Project Second Story]]. Historian [[Arthur Bray]] later found both the Department of Transport memo and Smith's handwritten meeting notes in Smith's estate, which gave documentary corroboration of the meeting.

## The 1983 Steinman Letter

In November 1983, UFO researcher [[William Steinman]] wrote to Sarbacher asking for details of his UFO involvement. Sarbacher replied with a letter answering twelve questions:

- He had no personal association with the individuals involved in crash retrievals, nor knowledge of specific crash dates.
- He was invited to take part in several discussions about crash retrievals at the Research and Development Board, but was not given access to attend them in person.
- [[Vannevar Bush]], [[John Von Neumann]], and Dr. [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] were definitely involved in the program.
- He did not receive photographs of UAP but did view official reports at his Pentagon office.
- Materials reportedly recovered from flying saucer crashes were "extremely light and very tough."
- The instruments or people operating the craft were reported to be very light as well, so that they could withstand the acceleration and deceleration of their machines.
- He gathered from people at the office that the occupants "were constructed like certain insects we've observed on Earth," whose low mass meant the inertial forces on them would be quite low.

Sarbacher added that he still did not know why the subject had been given such a high classification, or why the existence of the devices was denied. Gerb notes that Sarbacher put the word "aliens" in quotation marks, as if unwilling to settle on an origin for the beings, and reads the light, tough materials as Roswell wreckage, pointing to [[Battelle Memorial Institute|Battelle]]'s 1949 work on lightweight shape memory alloys.

## The 1983 Stanton Friedman Conversation

In the same year, UFO researcher [[Stanton Friedman]] tracked down Sarbacher after learning of the Wilbert Smith memo. In their recorded conversation, Sarbacher repeated the insectoid occupant description: one of the fellows told him that "if they were people", they were made like insects and had no skin, which was how they could accelerate and decelerate without being torn apart. He said the meeting he was referring to was held at [[Wright Field]] and indicated that a crash had occurred out west, which is consistent with the timeline of the 1947 Roswell crash. When Friedman pressed him on whether the crash happened in 1947, Sarbacher replied "very likely."

Sarbacher also identified another attendee of the [[Wright Field Crash Retrieval Meeting|Wright Field meeting]] whom he disliked, describing him as someone from Philadelphia who went to all the meetings "acting very smug about it." This person was later identified as Dr. [[Eric A. Walker]], who in 1987 confirmed to Steinman that he had attended the meeting.

## Connection to the V-2 Rocket Program and Project Twinkle

Gerb sets the son's account of camera-equipped missiles beside the record of V-2 launches. [[Project Twinkle]], set up in 1949 to observe green fireballs alongside [[Project Grudge]], documented an incident on 31 August 1950 in which the phenomenon was observed after a V-2 rocket launch from [[White Sands Missile Range]]. Radar operator Master Sergeant [[Lloyd Eugene Camp]], who did high-level research on captured German V-2 rockets at White Sands, reported seeing two silver discs circling a V-2, confirmed both visually and on radar, before the objects turned on their sides and accelerated away. Camp's daughter said her father witnessed similar events several times. Gerb does not claim this proves Sarbacher photographed UFOs from V-2s, only that V-2 rockets and UFOs demonstrably intersected.

## Significance

Gerb and other researchers treat Sarbacher's testimony as among the most credible early accounts from a government insider. He was a credentialed figure with documented access to the Research and Development Board, and he made his disclosures privately, to Smith in 1950, long before UFO crash retrievals became a public subject. His descriptions of recovered materials and insect-like occupants parallel accounts from separate sources. His statement that [[Vannevar Bush]] led the small study group is echoed by the [[MJ-12 Documents]], which name Bush as a member of the team President Truman tasked with investigating crashed UAP, although Gerb regards those documents as a mix of forgeries and possibly real material. In *80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering* Gerb rests his "Golden Era" of 1947 to 1977, a single centralised effort run by a National Security Council control group, on Smith's and Sarbacher's 1950 account.
