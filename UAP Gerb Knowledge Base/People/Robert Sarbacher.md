Dr. Robert Irving Sarbacher was an American physicist, a graduate of Harvard University and a student of Albert Einstein. In the early 1950s, during the Truman administration, he was a scientific consultant to the US Department of Defense's Research and Development Board. He is one of the most significant firsthand sources in early UFO research. In 1950 he privately confirmed to Canadian government researcher [[Wilbert B. Smith]] that the study of flying saucers within the United States government was classified at a higher level than the hydrogen bomb, and in 1983 he repeated the claim publicly in letters and recorded conversations with prominent UFO researchers.

## Background and Career

Sarbacher founded the Washington Institute of Technology and was its Dean, and he was a scientific consultant to the US Marine Corps. He also taught at Harvard, the Illinois Institute of Technology, and Radcliffe, and was dean of the graduate school and chairman of the graduate council at the Georgia Institute of Technology. His principal area of research was the remote control of rockets. He was a member of the DoD's joint Research and Development Board, chaired by [[Vannevar Bush]]. Sarbacher described the board as the institutional home of a small group conducting classified UFO analysis.

His son, Robert Sarbacher Jr., later told researcher D.M. Duncan (who located him near the time of Sarbacher's death in 1987) that his father spoke sparingly about UFOs. The son confirmed that his father knew they were real "for the obvious reason that they would be going 600 mph and then make a 90-degree turn in midair without slowing down, separated from all inertia and gravity." According to the son, Sarbacher was called upon to develop a missile equipped with cameras, similar to those on the V-2 rockets, that could track UAP at speed, because conventional aircraft were too slow to pursue them.

## The 1950 Smith Confirmation

In 1950, Wilbert B. Smith, a senior Canadian government radio engineer, met Sarbacher in Washington, DC, and recorded four main claims that Sarbacher relayed about the US government's UFO program:

1. The matter was the most highly classified subject in the US government, rated higher than the hydrogen bomb.
2. Flying saucers exist.
3. Their mode of operation is unknown, but a small group headed by [[Vannevar Bush]] is actively studying them.
4. The entire matter is considered by US authorities to be of tremendous significance.

Smith based a 1950 memo to the Canadian Department of Transport's Controller of Telecommunications on these disclosures. The memo led directly to Canada setting up two official UAP investigations: [[Project Magnet]] and [[Project Second Story]]. Historian [[Arthur Bray]] later uncovered both the Department of Transport memo and Smith's handwritten meeting notes from Sarbacher's estate, which gave documentary corroboration of the meeting.

## The 1983 Steinman Letter

In November 1983, UFO researcher [[William Steinman]] wrote to Sarbacher asking for details of his UFO involvement. Sarbacher replied with a letter confirming the following:

- He had no personal association with the individuals involved in crash retrievals, nor knowledge of specific crash dates.
- He was invited to take part in several discussions about crash retrievals at the Research and Development Board, but did not attend in person.
- [[Vannevar Bush]], [[John Von Neumann]], and Dr. [[J. Robert Oppenheimer]] were definitively involved in the program.
- He did not receive photographs of UAP but did view official reports at his Pentagon office.
- Materials reportedly recovered from flying saucer crashes were described as extremely light and very tough.
- Instruments or crew operating the craft were described as very lightweight, "sufficient to withstand the tremendous deceleration and acceleration associated with their machinery."
- He recalled an impression from colleagues that the occupants "were constructed like certain insects we've observed on Earth," which suggested that their low mass let them to withstand the extreme inertial forces of UAP flight.

Researchers note that Sarbacher put the word "aliens" in quotation marks, which they take to suggest he was not prepared to reach a final conclusion about the non-human or extraterrestrial nature of the phenomenon.

## The 1983 Stanton Friedman Conversation

In the same year, UFO researcher [[Stanton Friedman]] tracked down Sarbacher after learning of the Wilbert Smith memo. In their recorded conversation, Sarbacher repeated the insectoid occupant description: "One of the fellows said to me that those guys — if they were people — were made like insects. They didn't have any skin on their bodies. So they were saying that's how they were able to accelerate and decelerate without being torn apart." He said the meeting he was referring to was held at [[Wright Field]] and indicated that a crash had occurred out west, which is consistent with the timeline of the 1947 Roswell crash. When Friedman pressed him on whether the crash happened in 1947, Sarbacher replied "very likely."

Sarbacher also identified another attendee of the Wright Field meeting whom he disliked, describing him as someone from Philadelphia who attended all the meetings and "acted very smug about it." This person was later identified as Dr. [[Eric A. Walker|Eric A. Walker]].

## Connection to the V-2 Rocket Program and Project Twinkle

The historical record partially corroborates the son's account of camera-equipped missiles for tracking UAP. Project Twinkle, a 1949 program tasked with observing UFOs alongside green fireballs, documented an incident on August 31, 1950, in which UFOs were observed following a V-2 rocket launch from [[White Sands Missile Range]]. Radar operator Master Sergeant Lloyd Eugene Camp, who did high-level research on captured German V-2 rockets at White Sands, reported seeing two silver discs circling a V-2, confirmed both visually and on radar, before the objects turned on their sides and accelerated away. Camp's daughter confirmed that her father witnessed similar events multiple times.

## Significance

Sarbacher's testimony is considered among the most credible early accounts from a government insider, for several reasons. He was a verifiable, credentialed figure with documented access to the Research and Development Board. He made his disclosures privately, to Smith in 1950, before UFO research had become a public phenomenon, which reduced the incentive for fabrication. His descriptions of recovered materials and insectoid occupants also independently parallel other accounts from separate sources. His confirmation that [[Vannevar Bush]] led the small UFO study group is corroborated by the [[MJ-12 Documents]], which name Bush as a member of the team President Truman tasked with investigating crashed UAP.
