Edgar Mitchell (1930-2016) was a US Navy officer, NASA astronaut and the lunar module pilot of Apollo 14, which landed at Fra Mauro in February 1971 and made him the sixth person to walk on the Moon. After leaving NASA he became one of the most prominent public figures to say that Earth has been visited by non-human intelligence and that the US government has concealed it. In UFO research he is best known for his part in the 1997 Pentagon briefing of Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]], and for the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]], which surfaced from his estate after his death.

## Public statements on UFOs

Mitchell began speaking publicly about UFOs in 1973, two years after his Moon landing. He grew up in the New Mexico region and argued that the [[Roswell Crash]] was real. He also took a close interest in reports of UFOs interfering with nuclear weapons, such as the account of Lieutenant [[Robert Jacobs]]. One of his most quoted statements is that he had been "privileged enough to be in on the fact that we've been visited on this planet." [[NASA]] distanced itself from him, saying that it does not track UFOs, is not involved in any cover-up about alien life, and does not share his opinion on the issue.

He is one of several astronauts and cosmonauts who commented publicly on UFOs after retiring. Others include [[Gordon Cooper]], Eugene Cernan, James Irwin and Victor Afanasyev.

## The 1997 Pentagon briefing

In April 1997 Mitchell, retired Navy Commander [[Will Miller]] and [[Steven Greer]] briefed Vice Admiral Thomas Wilson, then the Joint Staff's director of intelligence (J-2), in a Pentagon conference room about UFOs, Roswell and crashed craft. The [[Wilson-Davis Memo]] alleges that the briefing prompted Wilson's 45-day search for a hidden crash-retrieval program, which ended with a contractor's "watch committee" refusing him access.

Mitchell described the episode on CNN's *Larry King Live* in 2008. He said he and "another naval officer" met the intelligence staff of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. A vice admiral told them he would find out, called weeks later to say he had found the source of the black-budget funding, and reported much later that he had found the people responsible and had been told he did not have a need to know. In [[1997 Meeting (Wilson, Miller, Mitchell, Greer, Hughes)|accounts of the meeting]], Mitchell's version is the closest public match to the memo's.

## The memo and his estate

Mitchell died in February 2016. His family invited a confidential source who had known several Apollo astronauts to help disperse the estate, much of which was marked for destruction. The source told [[James Rigny]] about UFO-related papers among Mitchell's belongings, and Rigny copied several documents, including the Wilson-Davis notes, which became public in 2018. How Mitchell came to hold the notes is not known. Physicist [[Eric Davis]], who is said to have written them, has confirmed only that they "were leaked out of Ed Mitchell's estate."
