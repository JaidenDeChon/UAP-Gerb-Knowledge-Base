Gerald R. Ford was a Republican congressman from Michigan before he became the 38th President of the United States (1974–1977). In UAP history, Ford is known for arranging the first closed congressional hearing on UFOs, held before the House Armed Services Committee on April 5, 1966. He said the move brought him significant public ridicule, although he meant it seriously.

## Congressional UFO Hearing (1966)

Ford called for the congressional investigation partly out of dissatisfaction with the Air Force's handling of UFO reports, particularly [[J. Allen Hynek]]'s dismissal of the 1966 Dexter, Michigan mass UFO sighting as "swamp gas." Ford found the explanation unconvincing and believed the public deserved a proper inquiry. He then arranged the first closed session on UFOs before the House Armed Services Committee, the first time Congress had officially addressed the subject.

On the day of the hearing, Ford openly acknowledged the ridicule he had received for arranging it: "As I expected, some persons have ridiculed my call for a congressional investigation into unidentified flying objects." Despite Ford's efforts, the hearing produced limited results. [[Project Blue Book]] chief [[Hector Quintanilla]] testified at the session and made statements about Blue Book's radar case record that Hynek later identified as factually false.
