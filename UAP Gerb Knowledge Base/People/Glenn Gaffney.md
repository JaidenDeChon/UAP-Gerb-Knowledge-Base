Glenn Gaffney is a former Central Intelligence Agency official who headed the CIA's [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology|Directorate of Science and Technology]] (DS&T) from 2009 to 2015, a post Gerb's videos and the documents they cite call Deputy Director for Science and Technology. The DS&T is the CIA directorate responsible for technical intelligence collection systems, overhead reconnaissance, and technology development programs. In Gaffney's first years there, [[Lockheed Martin]] tried to hand recovered non-human material to the Defense Intelligence Agency's [[AAWSAP]] program, the second time through the proposed [[Kona Blue]] program. A document entered into the congressional record in 2024 says Gaffney killed that transfer. He has denied involvement. To Gerb he is "the infamous Glenn Gaffney", one of the best-known alleged gatekeepers of the UFO legacy programs.

## Career

Gaffney spent 31 years in the US intelligence community. He began at the CIA as a technical analyst studying the Soviet space program, spent the next decade on interagency and clandestine technical collection operations, and served as the CIA's chief of computer operations and then chief of its Information Operations Center. He was Deputy Director of National Intelligence for Collection from 2008, became head of the DS&T in 2009, and held that post for six years. He also served as the CIA's Associate Director for Talent. From 2017 to 2022 he was an executive vice president at In-Q-Tel, the CIA's venture capital arm, where [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]], a former deputy director of the DS&T herself, was a trustee. Gerb's NRO video says that Gaffney's career allegedly began in NURO, the NRO's still-classified undersea counterpart.

## Role in Blocking the Kona Blue Technology Transfer

Between 2008 and 2011 Lockheed Martin allegedly tried twice to hand over recovered non-human craft material to the Defense Intelligence Agency's AAWSAP program and its contractor [[Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies]] (BAASS). According to [[David Grusch]] and investigative reporting by [[Christopher Sharp]], Lockheed Martin Vice President [[James T. Ryder]] proposed a UAP material divestment plan for crash retrieval materials from the 1950s, which Gerb believes were hull fragments or the entire vehicle from the [[1953 Kingman, Arizona Crash Retrieval|1953 Kingman, Arizona crash]]. Grusch says the CIA, the material's other government customer, told DIA and Lockheed no.

The second attempt, in 2011, was to go through a prospective special access program called [[Kona Blue]], championed by Senator [[Harry Reid]] as a secure "catcher's mitt" for the materials under Science and Technology oversight at the [[DHS (Department of Homeland Security)|Department of Homeland Security]]. This waived USAP structure would have made the materials visible to Congress while keeping SAP-level security. In a clip Gerb plays, an AAWSAP insider says the Lockheed executives, among them a senior VP and an executive VP of the corporation, had to talk with Gaffney, who was "very antagonistic" about the company releasing the materials to DIA and BAASS. The document packet Representative [[Tim Burchett]] entered into the record of the November 2024 House Oversight hearing, whose author is unknown, says the technology transfer agreement "was killed by Glenn Gaffney", then deputy director of the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology. Gerb recalls that the first attempt, in 2008 or 2009, was shut down with the involvement, he believes, of [[James Clapper]]; some of his other videos date Gaffney's block to 2008.

Gerb interprets Gaffney's actions as deliberate program protection. He theorizes that Gaffney and "other agency spooks freaked out" at Lockheed bringing an uninitiated contractor outside the CIA's control into the UFO portfolio, and that the DS&T keeps strict control of the legacy programs.

## Identification as Gatekeeper

Several sources have publicly named Gaffney as a central gatekeeper within the UAP SAP structure:
- In a panel clip Gerb plays, a speaker asked for the names of gatekeepers answers: "Glenn Gaffney, CIA."
- An interviewer in another clip recalls that "one WINPAC member" (from the CIA's Weapons Intelligence, Nonproliferation, and Arms Control Center) had recently named Gaffney as a gatekeeper of legacy programs, adding that [[Christopher Sharp]] had touched on it as well.
- [[Eric Davis]] said in a 2025 interview that he would want to "string Glenn Gaffney up like Jesus" and parade him around. Gerb thinks that went "a little too far". He also doubts that a subpoena would work, since Gaffney could plead national security or the Fifth, and suggests "trading up" from his subordinates of the time instead.

Gaffney has reportedly denied to journalists that he was involved in blocking any UAP material transfer. Gerb's answer is that "of course, he is going to say that". Gerb counts him in a "rogues gallery" with [[Doug Wolfe]], [[Sean Kirkpatrick]] and [[Bobby Ray Inman]], but stresses that DS&T officials like Gaffney and Wolfe are not the people in charge of the whole legacy program portfolio.

## CIA DS&T and UFO Legacy Programs

The [[CIA Directorate of Science and Technology]] has been repeatedly implicated in UFO legacy program operations, including:
- Oversight of technical intelligence collection related to UAP crash retrievals
- Operation of the [[CIA Office of Global Access]] (created in 2003 with the help of DS&T deputy director [[Doug Wolfe]]), alleged to coordinate logistics for foreign UAP crash retrieval operations
- Partnership with the CIA Directorate of Operations in joint clandestine UFO recovery missions

As head of the DS&T during the Kona Blue period, Gaffney sat where these alleged legacy program structures met, with direct authority to approve or deny technology transfers involving recovered materials. Gerb counts the directorate's leaders, among them Gaffney, Wolfe and [[Stephanie O'Sullivan]], as significant legacy figures, and in his Northrop Grumman video contrasts Lockheed "obediently standing down" at the DS&T's behest with Northrop, which he says does not honor such chains of command.

## Potential Connections to Lockheed Personnel

[[Mary K. Sturtevant]] was Lockheed Martin VP for intelligence, joint, and science and technology programs during the same period as the Kona Blue attempts. She had an extensive CIA background, including work in a joint venture between the CIA DS&T and the Directorate of Operations. Because she was at Lockheed Martin while Gaffney blocked the transfer, and because of the "once agency, always agency" principle, Gerb has argued that she may have been a CIA liaison within Lockheed, and later said she back-channeled to Gaffney about the proposed transfer. A third possible blocker, [[Robert Cardillo]], was raised by researcher Rob Jones.
