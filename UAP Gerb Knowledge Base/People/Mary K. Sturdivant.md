Mary K. Sturdivant is a former US intelligence and national security official whose career spanned the CIA, the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)]], the National Security Council, and ultimately the defense industry. She began her CIA career in 1985 at the Directorate of Intelligence's Technology Transfer Assessment Center, analyzing Soviet acquisition of Western dual-use technology, before moving to the CIA Counterintelligence Center. She subsequently served approximately eight years on the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence as budget director, evaluating military programs. She served as NRO Deputy Director for National Support from 2004 to 2006, and was then appointed Special Assistant to the President and Senior Director for Intelligence Programs under George W. Bush. From 2006 to 2011 she served as Lockheed Martin Vice President of Government Affairs, followed by VP of Intelligence, Joint, and Science and Technology Programs.

## Role in UAP Programs

Sturdivant is identified as a possible co-conspirator in the alleged blocking of the [[Kona Blue]] technology transfer in 2011. Her career trajectory is considered significant by UAP researchers: she moved from CIA DS&T-adjacent roles through the NRO, the NSC, and finally to [[Lockheed Martin]] — the defense contractor alleged to hold recovered non-human materials — during the exact period when the Kona Blue transfer was thwarted. The principle of "once agency, always agency" is invoked to suggest her loyalty to CIA interests may have persisted into her Lockheed Martin tenure. Chris Mellon, who worked closely with Sturdivant at the SSCI, declined when asked to question her about the transfer.
