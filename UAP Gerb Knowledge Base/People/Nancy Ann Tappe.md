Nancy Ann Tappe (1931–2012) was an American author and parapsychologist best known for originating the concept of "[[Indigo Children]]", the New Age belief that a new type of child with unusual psychological, spiritual or supernatural traits began appearing in the late 1960s. She introduced the idea in her 1982 book *Understanding Your Life Through Color*. She linked it to her claimed synesthetic ability to see human auras as colors, and said these children gave off a distinctive indigo aura.

## The Indigo Children Concept

Tappe claimed that in the late 1960s she began to see a new category of children with indigo auras. She read the color as a sign of heightened psychic sensitivity, non-conformity and resistance to authority, and in some cases of telekinetic or other paranormal abilities. She proposed that these children were a new phase of human evolution or spiritual development.

The concept spread more widely in the 1990s through the work of Lee Carroll and Jan Tober, whose 1998 book *The Indigo Children* brought it to a mainstream New Age audience. Some parents and alternative practitioners have used it to recast children's ADHD diagnoses as signs of spiritual advancement rather than a neurodevelopmental condition. Mainstream psychology and medicine reject that view.
