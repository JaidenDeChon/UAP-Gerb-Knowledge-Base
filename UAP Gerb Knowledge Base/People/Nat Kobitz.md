Nat Kobitz was the United States Navy's longtime Director of Science and Technology Development — effectively the service's chief research and development scientist — holding the position for more than 30 years before his retirement. Shortly before his death from cancer, Kobitz contacted journalist [[Ross Coulthart]] and, over several months, disclosed that he had been officially briefed in his government capacity on a classified program involving the retrieval and reverse engineering of multiple non-human craft. His testimony represents one of the most senior official admissions on record regarding an active US Navy UAP crash retrieval and exploitation program.

## Disclosure to Ross Coulthart

Kobitz contacted Coulthart unsolicited on an open telephone line. According to Coulthart, Kobitz was dying and chose to speak because he believed the American public and Congress had the right to know what was being done with taxpayer dollars, and he could find no good justification for continued secrecy. Over subsequent months, Kobitz confirmed:

- He had been officially read into a classified program in his capacity as Navy Director of Science and Technology Development.
- The program involved the retrieval of multiple non-human craft.
- He was aware of a decades-long secret operation to reverse engineer recovered non-human technologies.
- He was flown to [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] and taken deep underground to a secure room, where he was shown what appeared to be a bulkhead from a craft. The skin and inner bulkhead were described as composites bonded at an atomic level — constructed in a way that, in his expert scientific judgment, defied conventional explanation.
- Most of what he knew was, in his own characterization, "effectively hearsay from some excellent official sources," though his briefing into the program was formal and conducted in his official capacity.

Kobitz declined to state definitively that the material was alien technology — characterizing it instead as something he had never seen any human be capable of making. Coulthart described him as a patriot and a "decent, honorable public servant" who respected the classification of the program but felt transparency was warranted.

## Significance

Kobitz's seniority — as the Navy's chief R&D scientist for over three decades — means his briefing into the program would have been consistent with his role overseeing materials research and technology development. His description of the craft bulkhead aligns with other witness accounts of non-human vehicle construction, particularly the account of [[Sumar Shapiro]], who described recovered craft with unique interlocking components requiring disassembly in an exact sequence. Kobitz's Wright-Patterson reference also connects to a broader pattern of testimony placing non-human craft storage and related research at that facility.
