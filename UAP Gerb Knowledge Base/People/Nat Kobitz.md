Nat Kobitz (died 2020) was the US Navy's long-time Director of Science and Technology Development, described by journalist [[Ross Coulthart]] as the Navy's chief R&D scientist for more than 30 years. Near the end of his life he told Coulthart that, in his official capacity, he had been briefed into a classified program holding multiple retrieved non-human craft. His account is one of the most senior naval testimonies cited in UAP research on the Navy's alleged crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering programs.

## Disclosures to Ross Coulthart

According to Coulthart, Kobitz called him "on an open line out of the blue" while dying of cancer, and over several months gradually opened up about what he had been read into. Coulthart made the account public in 2021. Kobitz said:

- He had been officially briefed that the United States had retrieved multiple non-human craft, and that a secret operation had been underway for decades to reverse-engineer them.
- He did not work directly with the technologies, and most of what he knew was, in his words, hearsay, though from excellent official sources.
- On one occasion he was flown to [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]], taken deep underground to a secure room, and shown what looked like a bulkhead from a craft. Its skin and inner bulkhead were composites bonded at an atomic level, in a way he believed defied conventional explanation.

As a scientist, Kobitz would not call the material alien, only something he had never seen that any human could make. Coulthart describes him as a patriot who respected the reasons for secrecy but believed Congress and the public had a right to know how taxpayer money was being spent, and who saw no good justification for keeping the program secret.

## Significance

UAP Gerb places Kobitz alongside Admiral [[Bobby Ray Inman]] and former Director of Naval Intelligence [[Sumar Shapiro|Sumner Shapiro]] as senior naval officials who have spoken of recovered craft. Because his briefing came through his role as the Navy's senior R&D official, Gerb treats it as evidence that the Navy was read into a UFO crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering program, even though the material Kobitz saw was held at an Air Force installation.
