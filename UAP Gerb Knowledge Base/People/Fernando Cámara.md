Fernando Cámara was the Spanish Air Force captain who flew a Mirage F1 from [[Los Llanos Airbase, Spain|Los Llanos Air Base]], near Albacete, in the early hours of 12 November 1979 to identify the lights that had forced [[TAE (Trabajos Aéreos y Enlaces)|TAE]] flight JK 297 to divert to [[Manises Airport, Valencia, Spain|Manises]]. His account of a supersonic chase, jammed avionics and a missile-radar lock warning makes the [[Manises UFO Incident]] one of the best-known military UFO intercepts in Europe. In Gerb's video the transcript renders his name "Fernando Kamaro".

## The pursuit

Cámara took off at about 00:40. According to the account Gerb relays, he had to reach Mach 1.4 (1,074 mph) just to gain visual contact with an object he described as a truncated cone showing a bright, changing colour, which then disappeared from sight. Told of a new radar echo that suggested a second object near [[Sagunto, Spain|Sagunto]], he flew several pursuit vectors over the Mediterranean and inland Spain, and each time the object darted away as he approached.

When he finally drew near, the object seemed to respond to his jet. His Mirage F1's electronic flight systems were scrambled, and the onboard alert system warned him that he was locked on by a continuous-wave missile radar. Cámara also said the object jammed his attempt to lock onto it with an infrared missile. The object then accelerated away toward mainland Africa, and after nearly an hour and a half of pursuit, dangerously low on fuel, he returned to base.

## The Sixth Fleet explanation

One explanation for the jamming credits the electronic emissions of two helicopter carriers of the [[US 6th Fleet]], in the Mediterranean during the Iran hostage crisis. Cámara rejected it: he said he was too far from the fleet for it to interfere with his navigation, radio and emergency controls. Gerb adds that it accounts neither for the cone Cámara saw with his own eyes, nor for an object that sped away from a jet doing Mach 1.4, nor for the jammed missile lock.

## Parallels

Gerb singles out Cámara's jammed sensors as the most striking part of the case and sets them beside two other military encounters: Commander [[David Fravor]]'s 2004 [[2004 Nimitz UAP Encounter (Tic Tac)|Tic Tac encounter]], in which Fravor says the objects jammed the jets' radar and sensors, and Major [[Parviz Jafari]]'s F-4 Phantom II intercept in the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident]], in which his targeting and radio failed as a smaller sphere approached him.
