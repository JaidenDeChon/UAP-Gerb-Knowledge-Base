Chris Mellon is a former senior U.S. government official. He was Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for Intelligence in both the Clinton and George W. Bush administrations, and staff director for the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence. He later became a prominent public advocate for UAP transparency. He was an early member of [[To The Stars Academy (TTSA)]] and its national security affairs adviser, and had left by about 2021. Mellon comments often in public on UAP policy and disclosure. He has published op-eds, taken part in congressional briefings, and corresponded directly with journalists and researchers about alleged government UAP programs.

## Public Statements on Close-Proximity UAP Encounters

On the Joe Rogan Experience podcast, Mellon said he had personally seen footage of a UAP immediately beside a military pilot's aircraft, described as "right next to" the cockpit. The claim matches statements by [[Luis Elizondo]] about government-held footage showing UAP as close as 50 feet from aircraft. Together they corroborate that such incidents have been documented and that the footage is held in classified military archives. Mellon's statement lends credibility to claims that the most compelling UAP evidence is still classified and has not been released to the public.

## Role in UAP Programs

In April 2024, Mellon posted partially redacted screenshots on social media of a Signal messaging exchange with an unidentified person. That person claimed their group had made substantial progress in accessing the UAP crash retrieval portfolio and had deciphered its management structure, security control system, and program ownership. They also mentioned the 1953 Kingman, Arizona landing and a still-classified Secretary of the Air Force memo from the 1950s that is allegedly still in effect. Researcher [[Alex Catz]] later counted the characters in the redacted name from the exchange and identified [[Russell E. Wiler]] as the most probable candidate for the senior Air Force official described as gatekeeper for the crash retrieval portfolio. In "The Hidden Wing", Gerb alleges that Mellon and figures associated with him have followed a strategy of limited, selective transparency, releasing partial information about UAP legacy programs while protecting certain individuals and program equities.

## Response to Kirkpatrick's AARO Claims

Kirkpatrick wrote an op-ed in *Scientific American* claiming that no whistleblowers had approached AARO with evidence. Mellon replied with a public rebuttal saying he was "astonished" by the claim. Mellon wrote that he personally introduced Kirkpatrick to [[Luis Elizondo]], [[Eric Davis]], and [[Hal Puthoff]], each of whom spent hours briefing Kirkpatrick in a classified setting, and that "none have received any feedback." He also called on AARO's forthcoming congressional report to describe what specific claims these witnesses made and what AARO did to evaluate them.
