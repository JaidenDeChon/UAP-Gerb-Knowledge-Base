William Neil McCasland is a retired U.S. Air Force major general and astronautical engineer who commanded the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]] (AFRL) at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] from 2011 to 2013, where he oversaw the Air Force's science and technology program. A 1979 Air Force Academy graduate with advanced degrees from MIT, he earlier served as chief engineer of the Global Positioning System program, system program director of the Space Based Laser project, and director of special programs at the Pentagon. News reports in March 2026 said he had been reported missing in New Mexico in late February 2026.

## Role in UAP research

Gerb's video *The Hidden Wing* notes that before commanding AFRL, McCasland was materiel wing director for AFRL's Space Vehicles Directorate for three years and vice commander of the Ogden Air Logistics Center at Hill Air Force Base in the [[Utah Test and Training Range]]. He also worked closely with the [[NRO (National Reconnaissance Office)|National Reconnaissance Office]]. After retiring he advised [[Tom DeLonge]]'s [[To The Stars Academy]]. The video quotes a DeLonge message to [[John Podesta]] saying that McCasland publicly posed as a skeptic but was "very, very aware as he was in charge of all the stuff". The message said that when Roswell crashed the material was shipped to the Wright-Patterson laboratory McCasland had led until a few years earlier. Gerb uses this claim to open his analysis of [[Air Force Materiel Command (AFMC)]]'s lineage. Because AFRL dates only from 1997, he assigns the original custody to its predecessor, [[Air Materiel Command]].
