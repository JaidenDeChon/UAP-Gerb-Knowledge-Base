William A. Owens is a retired U.S. Navy admiral who served as Vice Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff in the mid-1990s and then moved into the defense and technology industry, including a senior executive role at [[SAIC]]. In Gerb's reading of the [[Wilson-Davis Memo|Wilson-Davis notes]], Owens was a permanent member of the restructured [[SAPOC (Special Access Program Oversight Committee)|Special Access Program Oversight Committee]] (SAPOC), which allegedly kept Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] out of a UFO legacy program. Gerb also counts him as one of two SAIC alumni among six SAPOC members.

> [!edit] Corrected 16 October 2026
> Earlier versions said Owens was one of the six SAPOC figures Wilson dealt with. Gerb never says Wilson dealt with Owens; he names Kostelnik and Kaminski as the SAPOC members Wilson spoke to.

## Career

Before becoming Vice Chairman, Owens served as senior military assistant to Secretary of Defense [[Dick Cheney]]. After leaving the Navy he joined SAIC; the UAP Gerb video describes him as the company's chief executive.[^gerb-owens-saic-dates]

## Alleged role as a gatekeeper

Gerb's SAIC investigation places Owens on SAPOC after the January 1994 restructuring of special access program oversight, alongside chairman [[John Deutch]] (also a former SAIC director), [[Paul Kaminski]], Brigadier General Michael Kostelnik and [[Linton Wells II]]. Because two of those six figures served SAIC at some point, before or after, Gerb wagers that SAIC was the large defense contractor that Wilson said worked alongside SAPOC to protect the program. He names Owens among the officials he believes should be subpoenaed by Congress, and cites him as an example of senior officers who "trade in their stripes and stars for a pinstriped suit".
