General Hoyt S. Vandenberg was the second Chief of Staff of the United States Air Force, from 1948 to 1953. His place in UAP history comes from ending [[Project Sign]] in 1949. He cited a lack of proof for the extraterrestrial or interplanetary interpretation of UFO reports, even though the project's own written findings concluded that some reports described actual objects of undeterminable origin.

## Termination of Project Sign

Lieutenant General [[Nathan Twining]] set up Project Sign in 1948, and it produced a classified report that accepted some UFO sightings as real, unidentified objects. UFO researcher and future [[Project Blue Book]] director [[Edward J. Ruppelt]] later stated that Sign had privately endorsed the interplanetary explanation for some of its unexplained cases. In 1949 Vandenberg overruled Sign, dismissing its conclusions for lack of sufficient evidence, and shut the program down.

[[Project Grudge]] was commissioned the same year, immediately after Sign closed. It worked under an explicit directive to explain UFO sightings away instead of investigating them objectively. UAP researchers cite this order of events as early evidence of deliberate institutional suppression: Sign was dissolved for reaching inconvenient preliminary conclusions, and Grudge was formed to produce predetermined, dismissive ones.
