General Hoyt S. Vandenberg was the second Chief of Staff of the United States Air Force, from 1948 to 1953. His place in UAP history comes from [[Project Sign]]'s Top Secret [[Project Sign Estimate of the Situation (1948)|Estimate of the Situation]] of 1948, which concluded that UFOs were interplanetary. According to [[Edward J. Ruppelt]], Vandenberg rejected it because it "lacked proof". Gerb goes further: he says Vandenberg shut Sign down in 1949 for "lack of proof", and that he ordered the Estimate destroyed.

## Termination of Project Sign

Lieutenant General [[Nathan Twining]] set up Project Sign in 1948, and it produced a classified report that accepted some UFO sightings as real, unidentified objects. Future [[Project Blue Book]] director Edward J. Ruppelt wrote in 1956 that Sign had gone further in private. In the late summer or early autumn of 1948 it sent up a Top Secret Estimate of the Situation concluding that UFOs were interplanetary. The Estimate reached Vandenberg, who, Ruppelt writes, "wouldn't buy interplanetary vehicles" and sent it back because "the report lacked proof". A group from ATIC went to the Pentagon to argue for it and failed. Some months later, Ruppelt says, it was declassified and burned, with a few copies kept; he doesn't say who ordered that. No copy has been made public, and in 2024 [[AARO]] reported that it could not verify the account or find the document. Sign was renamed [[Project Grudge]] by an order of 11 February 1949.

In *Special Access Required* Vol. 2, Gerb says that Vandenberg, "one of the original accused members" of [[Majestic 12]], ordered the Estimate destroyed. In *The Origin of the UFO Stigma* he says Vandenberg shut Sign down in 1949 for "lack of proof".

[[Project Grudge]] was commissioned the same year, immediately after Sign closed. It worked under an explicit directive to explain UFO sightings away instead of investigating them objectively. UAP researchers cite this order of events as early evidence of deliberate institutional suppression: Sign was dissolved for reaching inconvenient preliminary conclusions, and Grudge was formed to produce predetermined, dismissive ones.
