Major Dewey Fett was a United States Air Force officer and member of [[Project Blue Book]] who in 1951 launched [[Project Interloper]], a joint USAF-Navy investigation into aerial and underwater UAP that ran alongside Blue Book but independently of it. Fett's Interloper project is the earliest known US government investigation aimed specifically at Unidentified Submerged Objects (USOs).

## Project Interloper

Although Fett was officially a Blue Book member, he ran Project Interloper as an extra-governmental investigation. It was deliberately set up outside the normal chain of command and was exempt from Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests. Setting it up this way while Fett was still in Blue Book indicates one of two things. Either he or his superiors believed investigating USOs needed more secrecy than Blue Book could give, since Blue Book was classified but still open to investigation, or Interloper was designed specifically to avoid any future public disclosure.

The US Navy assisted with Interloper, which set an early precedent for the USAF and the Navy working together on maritime UAP investigation. In 1953 the project briefed the CIA on its findings. Project Blue Book's files were eventually declassified, but Interloper's findings never reached Congress or the American public, and only three of its case files are known to survive.

## Known Case Files

Of the surviving Interloper cases (numbered 26, 27, and 28), only Case 26 is directly relevant to USO research. It records that [[Lieutenant George P. Williams]] and his nine-man Navy Fleet Logistics Air Wing crew saw a trans-medium UFO rise out of the ocean and fly across the sky between Keflavik, Iceland, and Newfoundland. The object was described as elliptical or cigar-shaped and at least 200 feet long, with a red-orange glow around its edge.

## Significance

Fett's Project Interloper is the earliest documented institutional effort to investigate USOs, decades before the public became aware of the phenomenon. It was deliberately exempted from FOIA, it briefed the CIA without reporting to Congress, and it almost vanished from the record. All of this fits Gerb's wider argument that the most substantive US government UAP investigations were systematically kept outside any process accountable to the public.
