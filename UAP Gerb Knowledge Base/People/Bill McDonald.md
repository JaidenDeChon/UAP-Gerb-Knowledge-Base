Bill McDonald (William McDonald) is a forensic illustrator and UFO researcher best known for his 1992 drawing of the [[XF-131 Super Sentinel]], a triangular craft that, according to the engineers who described it to him, was reverse-engineered from non-human technology. Gerb describes him as an old-school freelance commercial illustrator, concept designer and forensic reconstruction illustrator who is now a sworn law-enforcement professional. Gerb says he has spoken with McDonald on numerous occasions and holds him in very high regard.

Gerb's channel has featured McDonald's other work too: his drawings, made with [[Robert Wood]], of the EBE-1 and EBE-2 entities described in the Majestic-12 special operations manual, and his work on the [[Roswell Crash|Roswell]] craft and the [[1976 Tehran UFO Incident|1976 Tehran incident]]. McDonald also appeared in a four-part series titled *Roswell Revisited* on [[Michael Schratt]]'s YouTube channel.

## The Denny's Meeting

In the spring of 1992, McDonald met four veteran engineers at a Denny's in the [[Antelope Valley]], California. Two said they were from [[Northrop Grumman]]'s radar cross-section facility at [[Tejon Ranch]], which UFO researchers call Tehachapi, and two said they worked for [[Ben Rich]] at [[Lockheed Martin Skunk Works]] and at Lockheed's [[Helendale]] radar cross-section range. All four were fed up with security impositions that were harming their lives, and wanted their story told without threats to their lives, jail time or the loss of their pensions. They had found one another over decades of work at facilities around the California high desert. Their work was so tightly compartmentalized that each held only a piece of the name of the triangular craft Antelope Valley residents had been reporting for years: the XF-131 Super Sentinel.

McDonald says he was able to confirm the Northrop engineers' testimony through friends of his father and through his father's mentor, [[Robert Scandrett]], of Rockwell International's Strategic Systems Division. He still maintains that the four engineers told the truth. When Gerb asked for their identities or leads so that he could interview them, McDonald declined: he has sworn to protect their identities.

He turned their account into the drawing titled *Tehachapi Triangle 1992*. Gerb presents the XF-131 as a line of research independent of [[Edgar Fouche]]'s [[TR-3B]] that leads to a very similar reverse-engineered triangle. He notes that Fouché also named Ben Rich, not Kelly Johnson, as the TR-3B's designer.

## Helendale and Tejon

McDonald said the Helendale site was designed to hide in plain sight behind the cover of a radar cross-section facility, and that its storm-drain flood-control channel is really used as a short runway or platform for testing craft. Gerb notes that McDonald said this five years before [[Steven Greer]] first named the Helendale plant as a program location. Helendale lies close to [[Edwards Air Force Base]] and [[Air Force Plant 42]] in Palmdale, both of which Gerb has accused of housing reverse-engineering programs.

In Gerb's Northrop Grumman and underground-bases videos, McDonald's engineers are one of several sources that point to Tejon as a site of UFO reverse engineering and material exploitation, alongside [[Bill Hamilton|William Hamilton]], [[Richard Sauder]] and [[Richard Boylan]]. Gerb puts "much credence" in McDonald's testimony.

## The X-Files Connection

On the drawing, McDonald asks whether viewers recognise the craft. Fox contracted his work and made a computer render of the vehicle for "Deep Throat", the first regular episode of *The X-Files*, which aired on 17 September 1993. In the episode, Mulder investigates the disappearance of an Air Force test pilot, learns that a craft is reverse-engineered from the 1947 Roswell crash, and watches a triangular craft dart and hover over the airfield. Gerb can find no credible source for rumours that military officers asked the show's creator, [[Chris Carter]], how he knew so much.

## The 29 Palms Encounter

In his video on [[Rodrik Castle]]'s 1997 encounter at 29 Palms, Gerb weighs McDonald's XF-131 as a candidate for the triangle Castle saw, though he considers Fouché's TR-3B the more likely one.
