Bill McDonald is a forensic illustrator and UFO researcher best known for producing detailed technical drawings of the [[XF-131 Super Sentinel]], a purported Air Force designation for a triangular craft in the [[TR-3B]] family. He based the illustrations on firsthand testimony from aerospace engineers who claimed to have been directly involved in developing the craft.

## The Denny's Meeting

McDonald's most significant contribution to UAP research came from a meeting at a Denny's restaurant, where he interviewed four engineers who claimed to have worked on classified triangular craft programs. According to [[Edgar Fouche]], who corroborated elements of the account, the engineers gave McDonald detailed technical descriptions of the XF-131 Super Sentinel's design, propulsion systems, and operational characteristics. McDonald turned their verbal descriptions into precise forensic illustrations, using the same method law enforcement uses to build composite images from witness accounts.

The resulting drawings depict a large triangular vehicle consistent with other accounts of the TR-3B: a rounded triangular planform with a central [[Magnetic Field Disruptor (MFD)]] ring, multi-mode rocket thrusters at each vertex, and a cockpit section. McDonald's illustrations are now among the most widely circulated technical depictions of alleged black triangle craft in UAP research.

## Connection to Tehachapi and Helendale

McDonald's research into the XF-131 Super Sentinel overlaps with facilities that Gerb identifies as connected to UFO legacy programs. They include the [[Lockheed Martin]] Helendale radar cross-section measurement range and the Northrop Grumman Tehachapi Mountain facility, both in the Antelope Valley region near Edwards Air Force Base.

## The X-Files Connection

McDonald's Tehachapi Triangle illustration shows the XF-131 Super Sentinel as described in testimony from real [[Northrop Grumman]] and [[Lockheed Martin]] engineers. Fox Network contracted it for use in the first regular season episode of *The X-Files*, titled "Deep Throat." [[Chris Carter]], the show's creator, used McDonald's design as the basis for a computer render of the triangular craft featured in the episode. The storyline of "Deep Throat", which involves a USAF base with missing test pilots, a triangular craft, and a government cover-up, closely paralleled the real claims McDonald's sources had described to him.
