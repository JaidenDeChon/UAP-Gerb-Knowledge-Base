Guy Hottel was a Special Agent in Charge of the FBI's Washington Field Office who on March 22, 1950 wrote a memorandum to FBI Director [[J. Edgar Hoover]] relaying claims that three flying saucers had been recovered in New Mexico. The document, known as the [[Hottel Memo|Hottel Memo]], is one of the most frequently cited pieces of government paper in UFO research history and was released publicly through FOIA — eventually becoming the FBI's most-viewed public vault document.

## The Hottel Memo

The March 22, 1950 memo relayed information Hottel received from an Air Force investigator — believed to have been affiliated with the [[Air Force Technical Intelligence Center]] (ATIC) — describing the recovery of three flying saucers in New Mexico. Key details as reported:

- Three flying saucers had been recovered.
- Each craft was circular with a raised center, approximately 50 feet in diameter.
- The craft were occupied by humanoid figures approximately three feet tall, dressed in metallic suits.
- A source named "Mr. Carl Howe" claimed the discs were found in New Mexico because high-powered radar in the region interfered with the craft's control mechanisms, causing them to crash.

The memo was addressed to Hoover and is classified within FBI records as a "flying disc" communication. Whether it references the 1947 Roswell crash or a later, separate recovery is debated by researchers; the memo was written three years after Roswell.

## Assessment

Hottel does not appear to have conducted an independent investigation or to have had direct knowledge of the recovered craft himself — he was relaying secondhand information from an Air Force source. His significance lies entirely in the document he authored: as a credentialed FBI official, his transmission of this information to the director of the FBI in an official internal memorandum carries greater evidentiary weight than a civilian claim would. The memo does not evaluate or endorse the claims; it simply transmits them through official channels.
