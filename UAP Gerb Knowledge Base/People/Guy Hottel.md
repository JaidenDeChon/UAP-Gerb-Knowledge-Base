Guy Hottel was a Special Agent in Charge of the FBI's Washington Field Office who on March 22, 1950 wrote a memorandum to FBI Director [[J. Edgar Hoover]] relaying claims that three flying saucers had been recovered in New Mexico. The document, known as the [[Hottel Memo|Hottel Memo]], is one of the most frequently cited government papers in the history of UFO research It was released publicly through FOIA and eventually became the most-viewed document in the FBI's public vault.

## The Hottel Memo

The March 22, 1950 memo passed on information that a source, whose name is redacted, had given to an FBI Special Agent, whose name is also redacted. It did not come to Hottel directly. According to that information, an Air Force investigator had said that three flying saucers were recovered in New Mexico. Gerb guesses the investigator was from the [[Air Force Technical Intelligence Center]] (ATIC); the memo does not say. The memo reports these details:

The memo was addressed to Hoover and is filed in FBI records as a "flying disc" communication. Researchers debate whether it refers to the 1947 Roswell crash or to a later, separate recovery. It was written three years after Roswell.

## Assessment

Hottel does not appear to have investigated independently or to have had direct knowledge of the recovered craft. He was relaying secondhand information from an Air Force source. His importance comes entirely from the document he wrote. Because he was a credentialed FBI official reporting to the FBI director in an official internal memorandum, his account carries greater evidentiary weight than a civilian claim would. The memo does not evaluate or endorse the claims. It only passes them on through official channels.
