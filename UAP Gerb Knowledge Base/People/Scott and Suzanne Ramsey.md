Scott and Suzanne Ramsey are an American couple who research UFOs. They are best known for *The Aztec UFO Incident: The Case, Evidence, and Elaborate Cover-up of One of the Most Perplexing Crashes in History* (2015), written with Frank Thayer. It is the most extensive modern study of the [[Aztec UFO Crash and Recovery|1948 Aztec, New Mexico crash]]. Gerb calls it the one book to buy on the case and praises its thorough sourcing and references. Video captions sometimes spell the name "Ramsay."

## Research on Aztec

Building on [[William Steinman]]'s 1986 book, the Ramseys spent years finding people who were in the Aztec area in 1948 and recording what they remembered. Gerb draws on their work for the account of oil worker [[Doug Noland]], and in 1999 [[Ken Farley]] described the crash site to Scott Ramsey on his deathbed. Rancher [[Valentine Archuleta]] had first told his story to Steinman in 1982; the Ramseys looked for the cliff he said the disc struck, and believe they found it. Their book also names policeman [[Manuel Sandoval]] as one of the officers at the scene, although, as [[Kevin Randle]] pointed out in a 2012 review, they did not interview him: his account reached them through a relative. In 2001 Scott Ramsey interviewed "George," a USAF airman who said he kept personnel records for the recovery at Walker Air Force Base. The couple also met Lee Crane, who recalled Air Force radar personnel describing ducks killed by microwave radar.

Their archival work is just as central to the case as Gerb presents it:

- In [[Frank Scully]]'s archive they found the recording of [[Silas Newton]]'s 1950 University of Denver lecture, Newton's chalkboard drawings, and his unfinished 1954 autobiography.
- They found and reproduced the AFOSI interrogation of radio man [[George Koehler]].
- They pried 289 pages on Newton from the FBI, with more than 100 withheld, and were told that further Army CID records of a 1950 photograph sting had been destroyed.
- They found 1948 [[Bureau of Reclamation]] records of heavy earth-moving equipment, cranes and lowboys in the Aztec area, and hired professionals with no interest in UFOs to reconstruct how the disc could have been moved along the Hart Canyon roads.
- They report that [[Leo GeBauer]] held senior positions at the AiResearch laboratories around 1946.
- In July 2011 they studied the papers of physicist [[John Torrence Tate]] at the University of Minnesota. They concluded he was probably one of the "Dr. G" scientists, and found that he had travelled to the Southwest around the time of the crash.
- Scott Ramsey pressed researcher Karl Pflock about a diary Pflock said Newton had kept, until Pflock admitted that his source was Newton's nephew. Pflock never produced the diary.

The Ramseys argue that the 1953 fraud trial of Newton and GeBauer was punishment for introducing Dr. G to Scully. Gerb, drawing on the same research, holds that the saucer pieces Newton showed journalist [[J. P. Cahn]] were replicas.
