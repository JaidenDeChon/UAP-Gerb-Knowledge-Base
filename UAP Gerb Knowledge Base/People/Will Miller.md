Commander Willard "Will" Miller is a retired U.S. Naval Reserve officer and a former senior analyst at [[SAIC]] who appears throughout the [[Wilson-Davis Memo]]. He attended the April 1997 Pentagon briefing at which [[Steven Greer]] spoke to Vice Admiral [[Thomas Wilson]] about UFOs and crashed craft, and afterwards talked with Wilson privately, which in the notes' account led Wilson to search for a hidden crash retrieval program. A later letter from Miller to [[Eric Davis]] and [[Hal Puthoff]], offering help with research into crash retrieval programs, is what Davis shows Wilson at their alleged 2002 meeting. In 2000 Miller also wrote that the "keepers of the keys" sat in Defense Department middle management and among civilian contractors he named, which Gerb has cited in his videos on [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Northrop Grumman]] and SAIC.

## Career

Miller is a former Navy commander who served in the Naval Reserve. According to Gerb, he worked for SAIC as a "senior analyst in future counter threat planning, directed energy weapons, space systems, and Navy space wargaming". His dates of birth and service could not be verified, so the page gives only the years of the events Gerb's videos record.

## The 1997 Pentagon Briefing

According to the notes, in April 1997 Steven Greer spoke to Wilson, Miller and [[Edgar Mitchell]] in a Pentagon conference room about UFOs, Roswell and crashed craft (see [[1997 Meeting (Wilson, Miller, Mitchell, Greer, Hughes)]]). Afterwards Miller and Wilson spoke privately, and Wilson came away intrigued enough to dig further, which led to his 45-day investigation of special access programs from April to June 1997. Interviewed by researcher Joe Murgia, Miller confirmed that the meeting took place and that Wilson took it at Miller's request, and added that DIA Director [[Patrick M. Hughes]] had also requested the briefing and wanted it expedited. Wilson himself, though he calls the notes fiction, admits meeting Greer and Mitchell. Gerb's *UFO Whistleblowers Vol.1* says that [[Steven Lovekin]] also attended, and dates the briefing to 1998.

## The Letter to Davis and Puthoff

The notes open with Wilson saying that [[Oak Shannon|Oke Shannon]] had asked him to speak to Davis about what Davis had told Commander Miller about crashed and retrieved craft. Before that meeting, Miller had written to Davis and [[Hal Puthoff]] offering his contractual services for research into UFO crash retrieval programs inside and outside the U.S. government. He asked to stay anonymous. In the letter Miller claimed to know:

- specifics on a special team and two key officers focused on crash retrievals (Gerb guesses at programs such as [[Project Moon Dust|Moon Dust]], [[Operation Bluefly|Blue Fly]] or [[Operation Zodiac|Zodiac]]);
- the name and location of a senior officer with firsthand knowledge of U.S. government [[Alien Reproduction Vehicle (ARV)|alien reproduction vehicles]] (ARVs);
- a list of civilian government contractors with knowledge of U.S. work in "alien-derived technologies, crashes, landings, and associated events".

Gerb notes that much of this came to seem "somewhat fact" in the 2020s, through [[David Grusch]] and the UAP provisions of the 2024 National Defense Authorization Act. Asked by Murgia about the letter, Miller said he had no recollection of it, or would not discuss it.

## The "Keepers of the Secrets"

In emails with attorney Peter Gersten, quoted in Patrick Huyghe's 2000 book *Swamp Gas Times*, Miller wrote that senior officials such as the director of the DIA, "with whom I met with recently on the subject", were "protected and isolated from knowledge of this subject." He went on: "If there are any keepers of the keys, they reside in DoD middle management and civilian DoD contractors, BDM, SAIC, Boeing, Lockheed, and the controllers who monitor the flow of money to certain classified" programs. Gerb returns to this list often: in his videos on Lockheed Martin, on Northrop Grumman (which absorbed [[BDM International|BDM]] through [[TRW]]) and on SAIC, Miller's own former employer, which Gerb now wagers is the unnamed contractor of the Wilson-Davis notes. His video on the [[Off-World Technologies Division]] also cites Miller naming SAIC.

## Beliefs on Recovered Craft

Asked by Murgia whether the U.S. has intact craft, Miller said: "It's my firm belief that yes, we, the Russians, probably the Germans, and perhaps the Chinese [have] at least partial technologies and most likely for the US, entire operational craft. I believe that we have our own transluminal velocity craft that can take us anywhere in space and time. Did we develop this technology independently? Probably not."
