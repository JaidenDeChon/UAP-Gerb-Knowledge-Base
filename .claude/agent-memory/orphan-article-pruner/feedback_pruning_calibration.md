Since ALL articles in this KB are single-sentence stubs, standard "stub = delete" logic fails. The calibrated rules are:

**DELETE if the sentence:**
- Uses "the witness," "the speaker," "the subject," "the insider," or other unnamed pronouns with no other identifying anchor
- Is a generic definition that could apply to any wiki ("UAP retrieval is the process of recovering a craft")
- Duplicates another article in the same cluster without adding unique specific detail (named person, date, location, incident)
- References only a source link (## Sources section) with no prose sentence
- Contains "discussed in this context," "referenced in context of," or similarly self-referential non-information
- Describes someone as "mentioned," "referenced," or "noted" without a specific fact about what they did
- Is a fictional character (TV/film)

**KEEP if the sentence contains ANY of:**
- A named person (full name or clear partial name like "Greer," "Herrera," "Wagant")
- A specific date or year
- A specific location (base name, city, country)
- A dollar figure, measurement, or quantitative detail
- A specific named incident, document, or program
- A physical description with measurements
- A named institutional role (e.g., "served as NRO Deputy Director")

**Why:** The KB's value is in specific anchored facts that cross-reference to real events. Generic definitions waste space and confuse readers looking for specific information.

**How to apply:** When evaluating borderline articles, ask: "If I searched for this topic, would this article help me identify which specific incident/person/event is being discussed?" If no, delete.

**Additional deletion signal: Narrator-meta content** — Any article where the content describes what the VIDEO NARRATOR/HOST is doing, planning, or thinking rather than describing a verifiable fact. Examples: "the narrator is actively seeking witnesses," "the speaker gives a presentation," "UAP Gerb attended the hearings," "content creator appeared on these podcasts, resulting in 20,000 new viewers." These are channel production notes mistakenly extracted as KB entities.

- TUO, Reverse Engineering, Crash Retrieval, SAP/USAP, Black Budget, NDA, Legacy Program, NHI, Whistleblower, FOIA, FFRDC/UARC, MRTFB: Each had 2-7 near-identical articles. Keep 1-2 most specific per cluster.
- Indonesia operation: 4 entries (2009 Herrera UAP encounter). Kept 3, deleted 1 generic.
- Wagant testimony: 5+ entries all warranted — different testimony stages (encounter, Greer outreach, signing secrecy docs, interrogation, discharge) each had distinct content.
- Compartmentalization cluster: Keep articles with specific anchors (Janet Airlines, Site 4, Air Force directorates). Delete pure definitions.
- Goldwater/Blue Room cluster (session 2): 4 entries, kept 1 (most specific: LeMay named, angrily refused).
- Montel Williams/Romansky cluster (session 2): 4 entries, kept 1 (most complete: Romansky + children in armed services).
- Japan Airlines cluster (session 2): 3 entries covering different aspects — kept only the most specific (Terachi named, 35,000 ft altitude, speed estimate).
- Event duplicates pattern: Many single events get transcribed multiple times as slightly different article titles. Always keep the version with named people and specific dates/locations.
